  • IRIS Registry Data Analysis

  • Academy members may access the IRIS® Registry (Intelligent Research in Sight) by applying for a research funding opportunity.

    Research fund opportunities open to Academy members (annual deadlines):

      • Research to Prevent Blindness/American Academy of Ophthalmology Award for IRIS Registry Research — Jan. 31
      • Hoskins Center IRIS Registry Research Fund — Extended to June 30
      • Knights Templar Eye Foundation Pediatric Ophthalmology Fund — Extended to June 30
      • Specialty society-funded projects — ongoing submissions

    The Academy’s IRIS Registry represents a remarkable opportunity for knowledge discovery and collaborative studies. As of Jan. 1, 2026, the IRIS Registry has aggregated, deidentified data on over 887 million patient visits and 87 million unique patients. This number continues to grow.

    How It Works

    Electronic health record (EHR) data uploaded to the IRIS Registry is stripped of all unique patient identifiers. Participating physicians retain ownership of their data, while the Academy owns and can analyze the deidentified, aggregated data. The database has strong legal protections and safeguards from legal discoverability, similar to the hospital-based peer-review processes.

    Data Use

    The IRIS Registry’s database provides real-world insights into the natural history of diseases, prevalence of rare diseases, practice patterns, diffusion of technology, comparative effectiveness and more. Data elements include patient demographics, medical and ocular history, examination findings, diagnoses, procedures, and medications.

    As an example, the registry currently contains data on the following disease conditions:

    • The rate of endophthalmitis after cataract surgery, the visual outcome of patients with endophthalmitis and specific risk factors associated with developing endophthalmitis
    • The rate of endophthalmitis after anti-VEGF injections across different agents and the comparative effectiveness of these agents based on visual acuity outcomes
    • The prevalence of myopic choroidal neovascularization in the United States, based on both diagnostic code information and clinical data
    • The treatment patterns for patients with myopic choroidal neovascularization, including the visual acuity outcomes for those treated and those not treated

    IRIS Registry Data Dictionary

    The IRIS Registry database is comprised of data extracted from participants’ electronic health record systems. Review the registry’s data dictionary, which includes data variables accessible for analysis in the IRIS Registry.
