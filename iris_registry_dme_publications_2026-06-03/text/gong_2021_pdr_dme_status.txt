36247812 2024 09 05 2666-9145 1 3 2021 Sep Ophthalmology science Ophthalmol Sci Temporal Trends in the Treatment of Proliferative Diabetic Retinopathy: An AAO IRIS&#xae; Registry Analysis. 100037 100037 100037 10.1016/j.xops.2021.100037 This study examined how treatment patterns for proliferative diabetic retinopathy (PDR) have changed over time using clinical registry data from the AAO IRIS&#xae; Registry (Intelligent Research in Sight). A retrospective cohort analysis using the IRIS Registry database spanning 2013-2017. A total of 141&#x2009;317 patients with newly diagnosed PDR (International Classification of Diseases [ICD], Tenth Revision, codes E08.35, E09.35, E10.35, E11.35, and E13.35 and ICD, Ninth Revision, code 362.02) were included. Comparison analyses were conducted using Tukey and chi-square tests, and time-trend analyses were conducted using Mann-Kendall tests and Theil-Sen slopes. Patient characteristics including age, gender, and laterality; whether patients received intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor injections (IVI) only, panretinal photocoagulation (PRP) only, both IVI and PRP (IVI+PRP), or observation; intravitreal drug data; and diabetic macular edema (DME) status were compared. From 2013-2017, the average age of PDR diagnosis was 59.2 years, with 53.3% of patients being male. Sixty-two thousand one hundred five newly diagnosed PDR patients (43.9%) received IVI, 32&#x2009;293 patients (27.1%) received PRP, 27&#x2009;664 patients (19.6%) received IVI+PRP, and 13&#x2009;255 patients (9.4%) underwent observation. In 2013, more PDR&#xa0;patients undergoing treatment received PRP only (47.5%) than IVI only (37.3%) or IVI+PRP (15.1%). From 2013 to 2017, the percentage of patients treated with PRP only decreased by 5.6% per year ( P &#xa0;= 0.05) and the percentage of patients treated with IVI only increased by 3.9% per year ( P &#xa0;= 0.05). By 2017, most patients received IVI only (52.9%). Patients with PDR with DME were more likely than patients without DME to receive IVI only (64.3% vs. 31.5%; P < 0.001). Among patients receiving IVI and IVI+PRP, bevacizumab (69.8%) was the most common intravitreal medication given followed by aflibercept (18.4%) then ranibizumab (11.7%).
