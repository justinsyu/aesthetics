Purpose
This study aimed to characterize 
long-term treatment patterns 
among patients with diabetic 
macular edema (DME) from a 
large ophthalmology registry
Introduction
• Anti–vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) intravitreal 
therapies (IVT) are the first-line treatment for DME1
• Studies have shown that a number of patients discontinue or 
fail to follow-up appropriately in DME. Patients who exhibit poor 
response are switched to new anti-VEGF agents2-3
• The long-term switching and treatment discontinuation of 
anti-VEGF agents in routine clinical practice remain unclear4
Methods
• A retrospective analysis was conducted among treatment-naïve patients in the U.S. 
with DME (no prior anti-VEGF IVT in the past 12 months) initiating IVT from 
1/1/2015–12/31/2019 (index date) using deidentified electronic medical records 
(IRIS® [Intelligent Research in Sight] Registry)
• Analyzed anti-VEGF agent utilization patterns, including agent type, switches (defined 
as ≥ 3 consecutive injections of a different anti-VEGF agent from the original agent), 
and discontinuations (defined as no anti-VEGF IVT for ≥ 12 months)
• Results were stratified by baseline best visual acuity and initial anti-VEGF agent, 
including on-label (ranibizumab and aflibercept) and off-label (bevacizumab) agents
Discussion
• Over the 6-year follow-up, approximately one-third of patients discontinued anti-VEGF IVT in any given year
• 58% of patients initially received bevacizumab, but its use decreased over time, with an increased use of on-label agents
• Reasons for switching and discontinuation should be further explored
Limitations
• (1) Unable to differentiate between patients discontinuing treatment and those lost to follow-up before 12 months; (2) Treatment patterns 
were derived from electronic health record (EHR) data, which may not capture precise timing of therapy start and stop times; (3) Other 
comorbid conditions that may impact clinical outcomes and treatment persistence may not be captured in EHR data from ophthalmologic 
settings; (4) Larger academic health centers are relatively underrepresented in the data and typically treat more severe and unusual 
ophthalmology cases

• Bevacizumab use decreased by a mean of 5.6% each year and on-label agent use increased by a mean of 6.9% each year 
• 15% of eyes switched anti-VEGF agents after a mean of 53 weeks, of which 74% switched from bevacizumab to an on-label agent 
• 52% of eyes discontinued anti-VEGF treatment after a mean of 24 weeks, of which 33% reinitiated after a mean of 91 weeks
• Rates of discontinuation, switching, and reinitiation were mostly similar regardless of baseline BVA, although discontinuation with no
