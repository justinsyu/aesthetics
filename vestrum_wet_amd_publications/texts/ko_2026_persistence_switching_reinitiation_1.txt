►Faricimab is the only bispecific antibody targeting both angiopoietin-2 and vascular endothelial growth factor 
(VEGF)-A for intraocular use,1 offering a novel therapeutic approach for neovascular age-related macular 
degeneration (nAMD) and diabetic macular edema (DME)2,3
►However, real-world evidence on treatment persistence and long-term use of faricimab in clinical practice 
remains limited

►Faricimab is approved for the treatment of neovascular age-related macular

existing data, which had been deidentified and are unable to be traced

►Data were obtained from the Vestrum electronic health record (EHR) database (Jan 1, 2021–May 31, 2025) 
►Eligible patients had: 
• A diagnosis of nAMD or DME
• ≥ 2 Faricimab (or anti-VEGFa) injections between Jan 1, 2022, and May 31, 2023 (index date: first faricimab 
or anti-VEGF injection)
• ≥ 12 Months of pre-index data and ≥ 24 months of follow-up data
►Treatment persistence was defined as receiving index treatment without a gap in therapy (≥ 180 days) 
►A sensitivity analysis aggregated treatment-persistent eyes with those that reinitiated faricimab after a gap in 
therapy, to account for eyes that received faricimab at extended treatment intervals of ≥ 180 days
►Postgap outcomes were evaluated in nonpersistent eyes
►Results were stratified according to disease (nAMD or DME) and prior anti-VEGF use during pre-index 
(treatment naïve or prior anti-VEGF treated)
►More than 70% of eyes with nAMD and 50% of eyes with DME were persistent with faricimab at 2 years; 18%–37% were persistent with anti-VEGF therapy at 2 years
►Approximately 80% of eyes with nAMD and 65% of eyes with DME were either treatment persistent or had received faricimab after an interval of ≥ 180 days
►Among eyes that were nonpersistent with faricimab, ≤ 50% switched to an anti-VEGF therapy; notably, ~25% reinitiated faricimab and 29%–49% discontinued from faricimab and all anti-VEGF therapies during the 2 years, 
suggesting treatment interval extension with faricimab
►These findings suggest high treatment persistence with faricimab at 2 years in both nAMD and DME, regardless of prior anti-VEGF use
►This study is subject to the common limitations of EHR data, including the lack of certain information in the 
database, missing data, and errors in coding
►Reasons for treatment discontinuation were not captured
►The small sample size of the Vestrum EHR database may limit generalizability of the results
