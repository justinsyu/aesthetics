Currently the only preventative treatment for conversion from non-neovascular to neovascular AMD is Age-Related Eye Disease Study 2 (AREDS2) vitamins which only reduce relative risk by 25%. Therefore new methods to reduce neovascular AMD progression are an unmet need for many patients with non-neovascular AMD. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 3146 infons section_type INTRO type paragraph text Patients of Caucasian race display an increased susceptibility to both non-neovascular and neovascular AMD. Although race is multi-factorial choroidal melanin content is reduced in Caucasian compared to African American patients especially in the macula. Levodopa (L-DOPA) is a by-product of melanin synthesis which can cross the blood-brain and blood-ocular barriers. L-DOPA binds the G-protein coupled receptor GPR143 which is expressed on the apical surface of the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE). GPR143 agonism by L-DOPA increases the expression of pigment epithelial derived factor (PEDF) and decreases the expression of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and exosomes which display key anti- (PEDF) and pro-angiogenic (VEGF and exosomes) activities in the pathogenesis of neovascular AMD. Therefore we hypothesized that less pigmented patients have reduced endogenous L-DOPA production in the choroid leading to lesser GPR143 agonism in RPE cells causing greater VEGF and exosomes and lower PEDF levels. Thus local choroidal L-DOPA levels are a modifiable risk factor for neovascular AMD. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 4260 infons section_type INTRO type paragraph text L-DOPA is an approved treatment for movement disorders such as Parkinson\u2019s Disease effective at L-DOPA doses >600 mg per day. Using the Marshfield Clinic and Truven MarketScan databases patients with a L-DOPA prescription previously showed a delayed age of onset for the diagnosis of both AMD and neovascular AMD compared to patients without a L-DOPA prescription. These data support our hypothesis that L-DOPA is protective against AMD diagnosis and neovascular AMD conversion. Additionally a small prospective uncontrolled clinical trial investigated L-DOPA in patients with new onset neovascular AMD patients. Twelve of 17 patients at Month 1 did not require rescue with anti-VEGF treatment and best-corrected visual acuity improved by 5.4 \u00b1 1.3 letters. Furthermore 11 patients with pre-existing neovascular AMD were treated with L-DOPA and their injection burden was significantly reduced by 50%. These data are uncontrolled and underpowered but suggest the need for further study and support our hypothesis that L-DOPA is protective against neovascular AMD. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 5334 infons section_type INTRO type paragraph text To further examine the role of L-DOPA in neovascular AMD we queried two large independent databases. We investigated the Vestrum Health Retina Database derived from United States (U.S.) retina specialists because it includes high quality ophthalmic information like intravitreal injection data but may lack rigorous systemic information. Additionally we examined the Merative\u2122 MarketScan\u00ae Research Databases which includes high quality systemic drug and dosing information but may lack ophthalmic-specific information like detailed injection histories. Therefore each database compensates for the other\u2019s deficiencies and agreement between databases would further strengthen our hypotheses. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 6034 infons section_type INTRO type paragraph text In the Vestrum Health Retina Database we found that L-DOPA exposure was associated with fewer intravitreal injections and reduced detection of new onset neovascular AMD. We investigated the Vestrum Health Retina Database and the Merative MarketScan Research Databases to determine if L-DOPA was associated with reduced risk and odds respectively of new onset neovascular AMD detection. We found that L-DOPA exposed eyes were associated with reduced risk of conversion to neovascular AMD by 21\u201335% in the Vestrum Health Retina Database. Similarly cases exposed to ~100 to ~300 mg L-DOPA per day were associated with a 15% reduction in the odds while cases exposed to greater than ~300 mg were associated with a 23% reduction in the odds of developing neovascular AMD in the Merative MarketScan Research Databases. These data from two non-overlapping independent databases support our hypothesis that L-DOPA may be protective against conversion to neovascular AMD. The data is further strengthened by the fact that each database has independent strengths ophthalmic information in the Vestrum Health Retina Database and systemic drug and dosing information in the Merative MarketScan Research Databases. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 18272 infons section_type DISCUSS type paragraph text We also identified that eyes exposed to L-DOPA were associated with a reduced injection burden of 11 vs 12 injections over 2 years compared to control eyes. Although statistically significant this effect size is admittedly very small. However this finding supports our hypothesis that L-DOPA is protective in neovascular AMD. This is a minor finding of our study compared to the important reduction in the odds of developing neovascular AMD. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 18716 infons section_type DISCUSS type paragraph text In both Vestrum Health Retina Database cohorts we found that eyes exposed to L-DOPA were significantly older than control eyes. These data agree with prior studies. In our non-neovascular AMD cohort control eyes were 76 years old while L-DOPA eyes were 79 years old. In Brilliant et al control AMD eyes were 71 years old while L-DOPA treated AMD eyes were 79 years old. In our neovascular AMD cohort L-DOPA treated neovascular AMD eyes were older by 2 years 79 vs 81 years of age. In Brilliant et al L-DOPA treated neovascular AMD eyes were older by 5 years 76 vs 81 years of age. Thus our data are in agreement in terms of appropriate age for non-neovascular and neovascular AMD diagnosis and support prior studies that L-DOPA protects from AMD diagnosis. Importantly age has been investigated as a risk factor for conversion from non-neovascular to neovascular AMD in fellow eyes of patients with neovascular AMD in the CATT HARBOR and VIEW studies. In CATT and HARBOR age was not associated with an increased risk of neovascular AMD conversion; however older age was associated with increased risk in the VIEW secondary analysis. We similarly found that older age increased the risk of neovascular AMD conversion by 4\u20135% throughout the Vestrum study (Table 2). However our multivariable logistic regression analysis adjusts for age and other factors thus preventing age from being a confounding factor. Similarly in the MarketScan study cases and controls were matched by age also preventing this variable from confounding our analysis. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 20277 infons section_type DISCUSS type paragraph text We also found that L-DOPA exposed eyes were less likely to be female compared to control eyes in the Vestrum Health Retina Database cohorts. Similar to age sex has also been investigated as a risk factor for conversion to neovascular AMD. In CATT and VIEW studies female sex is associated with an increased risk of fellow eye conversion to neovascular AMD. In HARBOR female sex is not associated with neovascular AMD conversion. We found that male sex reduced the conversion to neovascular AMD by 11\u201316% throughout the Vestrum study. In agreement female sex is associated with 10% increased odds of developing neovascular AMD in the Merative MarketScan Research Databases study (OR 1.10 95% CI 1.08\u20131.13; Table 4). Thus our studies are in strong agreement in terms of the risk of sex and conversion to neovascular AMD and multivariable analysis prevents this from being a confounding factor. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 21180 infons section_type DISCUSS type paragraph text In the Merative MarketScan Research study a cumulative two-year dose of 72 to 241.5 g of L-DOPA (~100 to ~300 mg per day for two years) reduced the odds of developing neovascular AMD by 15%. This dose is far less than the dose of 600 mg per day used in the ELLDOPA clinical trial. Importantly although patients taking 600 mg L-DOPA experience significant side effects only nausea is associated with 300 mg L-DOPA compared to placebo and no side effects are reported in the 150 mg L-DOPA group compared to the placebo. In agreement Figueroa et al. treated new onset neovascular AMD patients with 100 to 300 mg L-DOPA and previously treated neovascular AMD patients with 300 to 600 mg L-DOPA. Twenty two of 28 (79%) patients tolerated 600 mg L-DOPA per day and 5 of 6 (83%) patients tolerated 300 mg L-DOPA per day. Thus 100 to 300 mg per day is expected to be well tolerated in neovascular AMD patients. However elderly AMD patients still require frequent monitoring for unexpected side effects. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 22182 infons section_type DISCUSS type paragraph text The site of action for L-DOPA is the apical surface of the RPE where GPR143 is expressed. L-DOPA is transported across the blood brain barrier by SLC7A5/LAT1. Using published single-cell RNA-sequencing data SLC7A5 is expressed in the RPE and retinal endothelial cells. Therefore L-DOPA is capable of crossing both the blood-retinal and blood-RPE barriers and we hypothesize that 100 to 300 mg L-DOPA is biologically justifiable because of the very high flow within the choroid. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 22664 infons section_type DISCUSS type paragraph text This study has several important limitations. First database studies are always potentially confounded by the quality of input data. This is especially observed in the high percentage of unspecificed dry AMD types in the Vestrum study. Second there is a fairly substantial reduction in sample size from years 1\u20135 in the Vestrum study. This includes both patient dropout and patients whose follow up appointments are in the future. Third although the MarketScan study can inform us regarding dispensing of L-DOPA medication from the pharmacy there is no guarantee that the patient consumed the medication. Fourth patients with diabetes were not excluded and it is possible that neovascular AMD versus diabetic macular edema were incorrectly diagnosed. Fifth patients with Parkinson\u2019s Disease may not return to the eye clinic for follow up appointments due to reduced mobility which would reduce the detection of new onset neovascular AMD. However our Vestrum study had equivalent lost to follow up between groups from Year 1 to Year 5. Sixth the Vestrum database was analyzed using the eye as the experimental unit so bilateral cases could have been included although prior published studies from this database showed no meaningful difference between eye and patient level data. Finally L-DOPA is frequently taken for Parkinson\u2019s Disease this study cannot dissociate L-DOPA medication from Parkinson\u2019s Disease itself reducing the risk and odds of developing neovascular AMD. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 24159 infons section_type DISCUSS type paragraph text In conclusion our data support a positive effect for L-DOPA to inhibit conversion to neovascular AMD which accounts for 90% of vision loss in AMD. There is a possibility of unexpected side effects in people without Parkinson\u2019s disease and we should refrain from easy use of L-DOPA. Thus a randomized controlled clinical trial to test the ability of low dose L-DOPA at 100 to 300 mg per day to reduce the risk of conversion to neovascular AMD in high-risk eyes should be considered. sentences annotations relations bioctype BioCPassage offset 24648 infons section_type DISCUSS type footnote text This is a PDF file of an unedited manuscript that has been accepted for publication.
