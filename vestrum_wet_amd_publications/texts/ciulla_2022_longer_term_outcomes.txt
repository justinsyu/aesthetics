Longer-Term Anti-VEGF Therapy Outcomes
in Neovascular Age-Related Macular
Degeneration, Diabetic Macular Edema, and
Vein Occlusion-Related Macular Edema
Clinical Outcomes in 130 247 Eyes
Thomas A. Ciulla, MD, MBA,1 Rehan M. Hussain, MD,2 Donna Taraborelli, MBE, MPH,3
John S. Pollack, MD,4,5 David F. Williams, MD, MBA5,6
Purpose:
The clinical practice visual acuity (VA) outcomes of anti-VEGF therapy for up to 5 years were
assessed in patients with neovascular age-related macular degeneration (nAMD), diabetic macular edema (DME),
branch retinal vein occlusion-related macular edema (BRVO-ME), and central retinal vein occlusion-related
macular edema (CRVO-ME).
Design:
A retrospective analysis was performed using the Vestrum Health Retina Database.
Participants:
Treatment-naive patients with nAMD, DME, BRVO-ME, or CRVO-ME who received anti-VEGF
injections between 2014 and 2019 and had follow-up data for 12 months.
Methods:
Data on age, sex, the number of anti-VEGF treatments, and VA were analyzed.
Main Outcome Measures:
Mean VA change up to 3 years (BRVO-ME and CRVO-ME) and 5 years (nAMD
and DME).
Results:
At 1, 3, and 5 years, in 67 666, 21 305, and 5208 eyes with nAMD, after a mean of 7.6, 19.5, and 32
injections, there was a mean change of þ3.1, 0.2, and 2.2 letters, respectively. At 1, 3, and 5 years, in 40 832,
7728, and 1192 eyes with DME, after a mean of 6.2, 15.4, and 26.0 injections, there was a mean change
of þ4.7, þ3.3, and þ3.1 letters, respectively. At 1 and 3 years, in 12 451 and 3027 eyes with BRVO-ME, after a
mean of 7.1 and 18.2 injections, there was a mean change of þ9.5 and þ7.7 letters, respectively. At 1 and 3 years,
in 9298 and 2264 eyes with CRVO-ME, after a mean of 7.3 and 18.8 injections, there was a mean change of þ8.3
and þ6.0 letters, respectively (P < 0.01 for all VA changes of > 1 letter). In all 4 conditions, the mean VA increased
with the mean number of anti-VEGF injections, eyes with a baseline VA of 20/40 or better tended to lose VA, and
eyes with progressively worse baseline VA experienced a progressively greater VA gain at 3 years.
Conclusions:
In practice, patients with nAMD, DME, BRVO-ME, and CRVO-ME showed limited visual out-
comes, with patients with nAMD tending to lose VA at 3 and 5 years. Across all 4 disorders, the mean change in
VA correlated with treatment intensity at 1, 3, and 5 years. Patients with better baseline VA are more vulnerable to

Methods
Database
The database used for this analysis has been described in prior
publications.2e5 The Vestrum Health Retina Database is a large
database of aggregated, longitudinal, deidentiﬁed electronic medical
records from a geographically and demographically diverse sample
of patients of retina specialists in the United States. It contains visit
data from approximately 1.5 million unique patients with over 11
million encounters. A panel of 350 retina specialists from the United
States is geographically diversiﬁed in the Southeast (24%), Mid-
Atlantic (24%), West (20%), Southwest (12%), Northeast (8%),
Great Lakes (7%), and North Central (4%) regions. These practices
are based in urban (65%), suburban (32%), and rural (3%) settings.
Aggregated data include in-ofﬁce and outpatient medications; ex-
amination ﬁndings, including VA and intraocular pressure; diag-
nostic test interpretations; systemic and ophthalmic diagnoses;
medical and surgical treatments; and adverse events. A proprietary
deidentiﬁcation process substitutes alphanumeric codes for patient
identiﬁers. The system updates the database weekly. A previously
described ETDRS approximation formula, 85 þ 50  log (Snellen
fraction), is used to derive the VA score.21
Study Design, Dates, and Inclusion and
Exclusion Criteria
A retrospective analysis was performed using data from the Ves-
trum Health Retina Database. Treatment-naive patients with
nAMD, DME, BRVO-ME, and CRVO-ME who received at least 1
anti-VEGF injection between 2014 and 2019 were eligible if they
had follow-up data available through at least 12 months. This
analysis excluded patients with other retinal diagnoses. Information
regarding age, sex, VA, and the use of anti-VEGF agents was
extracted from the database. The Indiana University institutional
review board exempted this study from review because it involved
only the assessment of preexisting, deidentiﬁed data.
Statistical Analysis
The analytical methods used were similar to those used in previous
studies.1e5 The outcomes were analyzed at the patient eye level,
separately for nAMD, DME, BRVO-ME, and CRVO-ME; prior
clinical practice studies, using the same database and methodology,
have demonstrated that there are no meaningful differences in VA
outcomes when patients who receive bilateral treatment for nAMD,
DME, BRVO-ME, or CRVO-ME are excluded.2,3,5 There were 1-,
2-, 3-, 4-, and 5-year distinct cohorts for nAMD and DME, and
each cohort was assessed yearly and stratiﬁed by the number of
anti-VEGF injections. Similarly, there were 1-, 2-, and 3- year
distinct cohorts for BRVO-ME and CRVO-ME, and each cohort
was assessed yearly and stratiﬁed by the number of anti-VEGF
injections. The study analyzed the baseline characteristics using
descriptive statistics, including mean values for baseline age and
VA (letters).
As the main outcome measures for each disorder, the study
assessed the mean 1-, 3-, and 5-year VA change from the baseline
in the 1-, 3-, and 5-year cohorts, stratiﬁed by the number of anti-
VEGF injections, with 95% conﬁdence intervals (CIs) and nomi-
nal P values, using paired t tests. The study assessed the mean and
median injection frequencies. The distribution of injection fre-
quency over these time periods was assessed and charted on his-
tograms. In the 3-year cohort for each disorder, the study stratiﬁed
the outcomes by baseline VA and by injection frequency to assess
the resulting mean VA change from the baseline, with 95% CIs and
nominal P values, using paired t tests. The analysis was not
stratiﬁed by anti-VEGF agents because prior clinical practice
studies, using the same database and methodology, have shown
that ranibizumab, aﬂibercept, and bevacizumab perform similarly
with respect to mean VA change at 1 year; these same studies
demonstrated that there were no meaningful differences in the VA
outcomes when patients who switched between the anti-VEGF
agents were excluded.2,3,5

Demographics
The number of eyes and their baseline characteristics for the nAMD,
DME, BRVO-ME, and CRVO-ME groups (organized into cohorts
of 1-, 3-, and 5-year follow-up) are presented in Table 1. These
cohorts were not mutually exclusive, but the 5-year cohort was the
smallest because of the requirement for a longer follow-up period
within the study inclusion dates as well as more patient dropouts. In
eyes with nAMD, approximately two thirds of the patients were
women, and in eyes with DME and RVO, approximately half were
women. Patients with nAMD were the oldest, with means in the late
70s; patients with DME were the youngest, with means in the early
60s; and patients with RVO showed means in the early 70s. The
mean baseline VA was between 54 and 63 letters (Snellen equiva-
lent, 20/80e20/50) for all the disorders, except for CRVO, which
showed worse baseline VA in the mid-40 letters (20/125). Across all
the disorders, the initial anti-VEGF agents were distributed as 23%

Discussion
This clinical outcome analysis assessed the relationship be-
tween long-term mean changes in VA and injection fre-
quency in patients in the United States injected with currently
used anti-VEGF therapies. This study was limited by its
retrospective design, lack of anatomic data or controls,
dropouts from follow-up over time, and nonstandardized VA
assessment. These limitations may partly account for the
different gains in clinical practice analyses compared with
those in RCTs, which include protocol best-corrected VA
testing and explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria; however,
the strength of this study includes its large sample size, which
facilitated stratiﬁcation to further evaluate the injection fre-
quency and baseline VA. This analysis yields relevant in-
sights into unmet needs related to treatment burden with the
current medications, which is especially important as the
health care system adopts value-based approaches, with
increasing accountability for clinical practice outcomes.

mean VA was still better than that at the baseline
(þ3.3, þ7.7, and þ6.0 letters, respectively), but for eyes
with nAMD, the mean VA returned to the baseline (0.2

The results for eyes with nAMD are similar to those in prior
studies. As noted previously, extension studies of randomized
nAMD clinical trials have shown that VA declines over time
on average, with a mean gain of only 2 letters from baseline by
year 4 (388 eyes, HORIZON),17 a mean loss of 3 letters by
5.5 years (647 eyes, CATT),18 and a mean loss of 8.6 letters
by 7.3 years (65 eyes, SEVEN-UP).19 The trends in these
RCT extensions have been corroborated in several clinical
outcomes studies. A review of the Intelligent Research in
Sight Registry over 3 years found modest mean gains in
vision (<1 letter) after 1 year, followed by a decline despite
continued treatment. More than one third of eyes had
discontinued treatment by the end of year 3.22 The Study to
Observe the Effectiveness and Safety of Ranibizumab
Through Individualized Patient Treatment and Associated
Outcomes (LUMINOUS) 5-year, open-label, observational
study of ranibizumab for nAMD reported a decline in injec-
tion frequency over time, with treatment-naive eyes and eyes
with prior ranibizumab treatment receiving a mean of 4.2 
2.9 and 3.6  2.7 yearly injections, respectively. Regression
analysis conﬁrmed a ﬁrst-year mean VA increase of 3.9 letters
(P ¼ 0.002) for treatment-naive eyes, followed by a slight
decrease of 1.8 letters per year.23 Another retrospective
report of 95 eyes reported the 8-year nAMD clinical prac-
tice outcomes with as-needed ranibizumab treatment. A me-
dian of 6 injections was administered in the ﬁrst year and 3
injections in the eighth year, with a mean VA change of 9.1
letters from baseline, although stable or improved vision was
maintained in 47% of eyes at year 8.24 Similar trends were
reported in two 10-year cohort studies of eyes with nAMD
derived from electronic health record databases in the United
Kingdom. Schwartz et al25 reported an analysis of 15 810 eyes
with nAMD and reported that the mean VA improved from
baseline during the ﬁrst year but decreased thereafter,
resulting in the loss of visual gains. Chandra et al26 reported
a mean VA decline of 2.1 letters from baseline in eyes with
nAMD, with 23.5% of eyes having a decline of 15 letters.
The most common cause of this substantial visual decline
was macular atrophy. Another 12-year retrospective cohort
study of 7802 patients with nAMD reported that the patients
were more likely to experience positive VA outcomes (20/40
vision or 70 ETDRS letters) within 2 years of beginning
treatment and that they maintained this vision for 1.1 years
before it deteriorated to poor vision (20/200 vision or 35
ETDRS letters) within 8.7 years on average.27
The results for eyes with DME and RVO were less
favorable in the current study than in some other long-term
studies with smaller sample size. Approximately two thirds
of patients with DME (317 of 463 eligible patients) from the
Diabetic Retinopathy Clinical Research Network Protocol T
had 5-year follow-up data, for which the subjects were fol-
lowed up for 3 years beyond 2-year Protocol T completion,
during which they were managed at the clinician’s discretion
(simulating clinical practice treatment patterns). Between
years 2 and 5, 217 of the 317 (68%) patients received at least 1
anti-VEGF injection (median, 4 injections). At 5 years, the
mean VA improved by 7.4 letters from baseline (compared
with þ3.1 letters in the current study) but decreased by 4.7
letters between years 2 and 5. Similar to the current study,
DME patients with worse baseline VA (20/50e20/320)
gained more letters than those with better baseline VA (20/
32e20/40), experiencing mean gains of 11.9 letters and 3.2
letters, respectively, at 1 year but losing a mean of 4.8 and 4.6
letters, respectively, between years 2 and 5.20 In a
retrospective, multicenter study of 94 eyes with RVO, the
8-year VA outcomes of anti-VEGF treatment were more
favorable than in the current study. Despite being followed up
for 5 years longer than in the current study, eyes with BRVO-
ME gained a mean of 14.3 letters and eyes with CRVO-ME
gained a mean of 14.4 letters from baseline (compared
with þ7.7 and þ6.0 in the current study, respectively) while
receiving a mean of 4 injections in the eighth year.28
Cross-trial comparisons are limited by differing eligi-
bility criteria, therapeutic regimens, and end point evalua-
tions, including nonstandardized VA assessments in clinical
practice studies. Additionally, clinical practice analyses tend
toward worse therapeutic outcomes, given the more diverse
patient presentations, likely including advanced disease
states, such as ischemia, for DME and RVO, which may be
ineligible for RCTs. Nonetheless, although the precise dif-
ferences in visual gains compared with those in prior studies
may be uncertain, the current study highlights limited VA
outcomes of current anti-VEGF agents, possibly related to
undertreatment with regimens that deviate from frequent
injection schedules and/or noncompliance. Notably, the
baseline VA did not signiﬁcantly affect the treatment fre-
quency decisions for any of the retinal disorders.
Visual Gains Correlate with Treatment Intensity,
with Ceiling Effects Related to Baseline VA
When stratiﬁed by anti-VEGF injection frequency, on
average, the VA gain increased with increasing treatment
intensity, as expected. For both BRVO-ME and CRVO-
ME, at 3 years, the VA gains plateaued at approximately
2 lines of vision in those receiving > 20 injections over
that time span. Compared with eyes with BRVO-ME, pa-
tient eyes with CRVO-ME performed worse for a given
injection frequency and baseline VA, which was not un-
expected, given the generally greater retinal involvement,
with potential for greater ischemia and higher VEGF
levels. For eyes with DME, the 5-year VA gains were
comparatively modest, and there was less variation when
stratiﬁed by injection frequency, with eyes that received
> 44 injections gaining 4.8 letters compared with 1.3 let-
ters in the group that received the fewest ( 9) injections.
The 5-year nAMD VA gains were the least favorable, with
patients receiving < 43 injections losing vision on average.
When stratiﬁed by both anti-VEGF injection frequency at 3
years and by baseline VA, these trends persisted; impor-
tantly, across the disorders, patient eyes with a baseline VA
of 20/40 or better were at the risk of VA loss at 3 years,
regardless of the injection frequency.
In summary, this analysis of 130 247 patient eyes fol-
lowed up for up to 5 years demonstrated that patients with
nAMD, DME, and RVO-ME experience limited visual
outcomes with current anti-VEGF agents in clinical practice.
Patients with nAMD, in particular, tend to lose vision at 3

Additionally, patients who are not treated frequently expe-

a signiﬁcant unmet need for more effective therapy with
durability to address the treatment burden.
