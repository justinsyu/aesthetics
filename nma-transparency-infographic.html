Network Meta-Analysis · The Replication Problem

When the math doesn't add up because the data isn't there.

Network meta-analyses (NMAs) sit at the top of the evidence hierarchy — they pool dozens of trials to compare treatments that have never been tested head-to-head. But when an outside researcher tries to rerun the analysis, the inputs are usually nowhere to be found. This is a story about the gap between published conclusions and the data needed to verify them.

01 · What an NMA actually is

A statistical web that compares treatments that were never compared directly.

A network meta-analysis stitches together every randomized trial it can find on a condition — treatment A vs. B in one study, B vs. C in another — and uses the shared comparators to estimate the effect of A vs. C without a single trial having tested them head-to-head. The result drives guidelines, reimbursement, and clinical practice.

Direct evidence

Trials that tested two treatments head-to-head — the “known” edges of the network.

Indirect evidence

Effect estimates inferred through a shared comparator. If A vs. B and B vs. C are known, A vs. C can be derived.

Coherence assumption

The whole edifice rests on the belief that direct and indirect evidence agree. Checking this requires the underlying numbers, not just the conclusions.

What gets published

A forest plot, a ranking table, a paragraph of methods. Almost never: the per-arm data, the model code, or the variance-covariance structure.

02 · The reproducibility gap

Most published NMAs cannot be re-run by an outside reader.

Empirical audits of the NMA literature keep finding the same pattern: the published artifact is a summary of a computation, not the computation itself. Without the inputs, even the most diligent reviewer is reduced to taking the authors' word for it.

03 · What is actually missing

The gap isn't data — it's everything around the data.

Re-running an NMA is not just a matter of having the studies. It requires a chain of artifacts — the inputs, the model, the assumptions, and the run — and the chain breaks at almost every link.

What an outside researcher would need to actually replicate the analysis:

01

Per-arm trial data

Events and totals (or means and SDs) for every arm of every included study — not just the pairwise contrast.

02

Variance-covariance structure

For multi-arm trials, contrasts within a study are correlated. Without that matrix, the indirect estimates can't be reconstructed.

03

Model specification & priors

Fixed vs. random effects, link function, between-study variance prior, convergence diagnostics — the choices that move the answer.

04

Inclusion & exclusion trail

Which studies were screened, which were dropped, and why — in a form that can be re-applied, not paraphrased.

05

Code & seed

The actual script (R, Stan, JAGS, NetMetaXL, etc.) and the random seed. MCMC results without a seed are not exactly reproducible.

04 · Where the chain breaks

A reproducible NMA is a pipeline, not a paper.

Trace any published NMA backwards from its conclusions, and you'll find a chain of artifacts — each one needing to be open for the next one to be verifiable. Today, most of those links are private.

05 · A path forward

Toward an evidence base that defaults to open.

The fix isn't a new statistical method — it's a cultural one. Treat the data, the model, and the code as first-class outputs of an NMA, equal in standing to the manuscript itself. Below are the four things a transparent NMA should ship with, every time.

A four-part standard

Make the whole pipeline a citable artifact.

01 · DATA

Per-arm, machine-readable

Publish the full extraction table — events, totals, means, SDs, follow-up — as a structured file, not a PDF appendix.

02 · MODEL

Specification, not prose

Share the model file (Stan, JAGS, BUGS, R) with priors and convergence diagnostics. Let readers read the math the computer ran.

03 · CODE

Executable & versioned

Deposit a runnable repository with a fixed seed and an environment lockfile. Re-running the analysis should be one command.

04 · TRAIL

Decisions, logged

Document every screening, exclusion, and sensitivity analysis — including the ones that didn't make it into the final paper.
