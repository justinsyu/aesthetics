Success Metrics05 / 10
Metric shift

Operational speed requires complementary validity measures

“A pipeline that moves data faster is necessary but not sufficient; the data must be complete, validated, and audit‑ready from the point of capture to have regulatory meaning.” C11

Operations

Enrollment speed, first‑dose timing, query resolution, data entry‑to‑lock latency

Safety signal quality

Sensitivity, specificity, false positives, false negatives, alert fatigue, signal latency

Evidence integrity

Traceability, claim validation precision, false acceptance, version lineage, audit trails

Equity and generalization

Subgroup performance, drift, site‑level reporting, external dataset tests, fairness floors

Metric examples are drawn from C05, C08, C09, C11, C12, C13, C14, C15, and C16Bars are visual grouping cues, not quantitative scores
Decision Control06 / 10

For high‑impact AI outputs, commenters propose a staged authorization sequence before clinical reliance

01

Fresh state

Confirm that patient data, cohort state, dose rules, and trial conditions still match the recommendation context.

02

Evidence threshold

Require traceable evidence, uncertainty bounds, and claim‑level validation before the output can move forward.

03

Authority check

Document qualified human review, identity, and accountability before dosing, eligibility, endpoint, or safety reliance.

04

Allow, hold, or deny

Create an audit record explaining why the action was permitted, delayed, or blocked.

Derived from C04 execution admissibility, C14 forensic governance, and C16 fit‑for‑purpose human reviewClinical reliance remains bounded by documented review
Access + Sites07 / 10
Site reality

Sponsor‑level datasets may incompletely capture site operations

1

Screen‑fail rates by criterion, referral‑to‑dose intervals, and source documentation burden

2

Staff bandwidth, protocol amendment burden, and site‑reported operational feasibility

“A pilot that draws only from sponsor and EHR data will optimize for sponsor convenience and produce predictions that fail at sites.” C09

Representation

Equity assessment should be prespecified in the pilot protocol

1

Subgroup performance by age, sex, race, ethnicity, language, insurance status, rurality, and comorbidity

2

Public reporting of drift, failure modes, and plain‑language participant disclosures

“Trust is downstream of transparency, not upstream.” C13

Site measures

Screen‑fail causes, staffing capacity, referral‑to‑dose time

Access measures

Rurality, language, insurance, safety‑net settings

Disclosure

Plain‑language participant artifacts and comprehension testing

Governance

Voting roles for patient and investigator representatives

Architecture08 / 10

System architecture is framed as evidence when AI informs clinical decision pathways

A

Modular by modality

A systemic oncology drug, cell therapy, gene therapy, local ablative therapy, and intratumoral treatment may require distinct AI‑supported workflows.

B

Claim‑level proof

Regulatory interpretability is framed as evidence traceability for individual claims, not only model‑level performance.

C

Controlled evolution

Architecturally enforced PCCPs, deterministic lineage, and trajectory hashes make model change auditable.

D

Mechanistic emulation

One comment proposes mechanistic in‑silico trial emulation as a complementary evidentiary substrate.
