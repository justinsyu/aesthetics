A retrospective real-world study on the efficacy and safety of anlotinib in combination with immunotherapy as maintenance therapy after standard immunochemotherapy for extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ALTER-L059).

Abstract number: 8080
First speaker: Yong Fang
Meeting: 2026 ASCO Annual Meeting
Source: https://www.asco.org/abstracts-presentations/265407
Summary:
Background:

Although immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) combined with chemotherapy have become the standard first-line treatment for extensive-stage small cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC), the use of immunotherapy as a maintenance strategy remains challenging. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy and safety of anlotinib combined with ICIs as first-line maintenance treatment for ES-SCLC.

Methods:

ALTER-L059 was a retrospective, multicenter study conducted at 9 hospitals in China. Eligible patients were 18 years or older with histologically or cytologically confirmed ES-SCLC and had completed 4–6 cycles of first-line immunochemotherapy as induction therapy without disease progression. They subsequently received maintenance therapy with immunotherapy combined with anlotinib. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival from the start of maintenance therapy (PFS2). Secondary endpoints included progression-free survival from the start of induction therapy (PFS), overall survival from the start of maintenance therapy (OS2), overall survival from the start of induction therapy (OS) and safety.

Results:

Between January 2022 and December 2024, a total of 100 patients were enrolled. By the data cutoff date on November 11, 2025, the median follow-up duration was 16.92 months (95% CI: 15.81-18.03). The median PFS2 was 6.05 months (95% CI: 4.83-7.59), and the median PFS was 9.53 months (95% CI: 8.31-11.93). The median OS2 was 17.74 months (95% CI: 15.24-19.71), and the median OS was 21.26 months (95% CI: 18.60-22.60). The incidence of grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) was 30.0%. The most common TRAEs (incidence ≥20%) were hypertension (33.0%), hand-foot syndrome (23.0%), proteinuria (21.0%), and hypertriglyceridemia (20.0%).

Conclusions:

Patients who had been diagnosed with ES-SCLC and had completed 4–6 cycles of immunochemotherapy as induction therapy without disease progression subsequently received maintenance therapy with immunotherapy combined with anlotinib, demonstrating favorable survival outcomes and manageable toxicity.
