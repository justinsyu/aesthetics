# Patient-Reported Outcomes in G-BA/AMNOG and HAS Drug Assessment

Date: July 4, 2026

## Executive Summary

Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) can affect drug assessment in both Germany and France. The practical implication is not that every trial should include every plausible PRO endpoint. It is that every clinical development plan should explicitly test whether symptoms, function, treatment burden, or health-related quality of life are likely to be material to HTA value assessment.

In Germany, G-BA/AMNOG assessment focuses on patient-relevant outcomes: mortality, morbidity, health-related quality of life, and adverse events. PROs can be decisive when they measure symptoms, health status, functioning, or health-related quality of life with suitable methods. In France, HAS is explicit that quality-of-life evidence can contribute to SMR and ASMR, and that lack of quality-of-life evidence can negatively affect ASMR when such evidence is expected, especially in chronic, disabling, or end-of-life conditions.

## Germany: G-BA / AMNOG

### Core Rule

Under AMNOG, the added-benefit assessment is organized around patient-relevant benefit. IQWiG's current methods define this as outcomes concerning how patients feel, function, or survive, including mortality, morbidity, and health-related quality of life. PROs can therefore affect the assessment when they capture patient-relevant symptoms, health status, functioning, or health-related quality of life.

Key source: [IQWiG General Methods 8.0](https://www.iqwig.de/methoden/general-methods_version-8-0.pdf). G-BA process overview: [G-BA benefit assessment of pharmaceuticals](https://www.g-ba.de/english/benefitassessment/).

### Situations Where PROs Can Matter

- Symptom-driven morbidity, such as itch, pain, dyspnea, fatigue, diarrhea, nausea, insomnia, or disease-specific symptom burden.
- Health status, including measures such as EQ-5D VAS.
- Health-related quality of life, including instruments such as EORTC QLQ-C30, DLQI, SGRQ, and SF-36.
- Palliative or chronic settings where maintenance or improvement of quality of life is central to the value judgment.
- Negative patient-reported symptom or quality-of-life findings, which can reduce or negate the added-benefit conclusion.

### Methodologic Conditions

- Instruments should be validated or otherwise suitable for the disease and trial context.
- PRO endpoints should be prespecified and analyzed in a way that supports causal interpretation.
- Randomized evidence is preferred.
- Blinding matters because open-label trials can bias subjective endpoints.
- Responder analyses generally need a clinically meaningful threshold. IQWiG generally expects at least 15% of the instrument's scale range.
- Missing data, short follow-up, and PRO collection only until progression can limit interpretability.

## France: HAS / Transparency Committee

### Core Rule

HAS states that quality-of-life data contribute to assessment of the clinical effect of a drug and may support an ASMR above V when the evidence is rigorous. HAS also states that absence of quality-of-life data can negatively affect ASMR when quality-of-life evidence is expected, particularly in chronic, disabling, or end-of-life conditions.

Key sources: [HAS Transparency Committee doctrine](https://www.has-sante.fr/upload/docs/application/pdf/2021-03/doctrine_ct.pdf) and [HAS note on the role of quality of life in HTA](https://www.has-sante.fr/jcms/c_2883073/fr/evaluation-des-technologies-de-sante-a-la-has-place-de-la-qualite-de-vie).

### Situations Where PROs Can Matter

- Chronic diseases and disabling conditions where symptoms, functioning, or daily-life impact are major components of disease burden.
- End-of-life and metastatic oncology, particularly when survival benefit is uncertain or toxicity is material.
- Symptom-driven conditions such as pruritus, pain, migraine, respiratory disease, dermatologic disease, and cystic fibrosis.
- Pediatric conditions, where patient and caregiver quality of life may be relevant if assessed with adapted tools.

### Methodologic Conditions

- Instruments should be validated and adapted to the disease and population.
- Quality-of-life or symptom objectives and clinical relevance thresholds should be prespecified.
- Double-blind evidence is preferred for subjective endpoints.
- Multiplicity should be controlled.
- The timing and duration of assessment should match the disease and expected treatment effect.
- Missing data should be limited and handled rigorously.
- Exploratory PRO analyses are frequently considered insufficient for formal conclusions.

## Implication for Trial Design

There is a strong HTA rationale to consider PROs at the start of trial design. PROs are most important when the therapy may improve symptoms, preserve function, reduce treatment burden, improve quality of life, or impose tolerability tradeoffs that need patient-level characterization. A weak or exploratory PRO strategy is unlikely to help and may create unfavorable evidence. A well-planned PRO strategy can strengthen the value case in both Germany and France.
