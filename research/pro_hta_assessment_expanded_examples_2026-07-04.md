# Patient-Reported Outcomes in G-BA and HAS Drug Assessment

Date: July 4, 2026

## Summary

Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) can affect drug assessment in both G-BA and HAS reviews. The strongest examples occur when PROs measure patient-relevant symptoms, functioning, health status, or health-related quality of life with validated instruments, prespecified analyses, adequate follow-up, and acceptable missingness.

Across the examples identified, PRO evidence was most influential when it was comparative, disease-relevant, prospectively collected, and interpretable within the assessment comparator context. The absence of credible PRO evidence was a material limitation in chronic, disabling, symptom-driven, palliative, or treatment-burden-sensitive settings.

## Germany: G-BA

### Assessment Framework

German added-benefit assessment is conducted by G-BA. The [G-BA benefit-assessment overview](https://www.g-ba.de/english/benefitassessment/) states that "the G-BA assesses recognition of any additional benefit." The ordinance governing the process states, "Der Gemeinsame Bundesausschuss führt die Nutzenbewertung durch" ["The Federal Joint Committee conducts the benefit assessment"] ([AM-NutzenV Section 7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/am-nutzenv/__7.html)).

The assessment is structured around patient-relevant outcomes. [AM-NutzenV Section 2](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/am-nutzenv/__2.html) defines benefit as a "patientenrelevante therapeutische Effekt" ["patient-relevant therapeutic effect"] and includes "Verbesserung der Lebensqualität" ["improvement in quality of life"] within that concept. The [G-BA Rules of Procedure](https://www.g-ba.de/richtlinien/42/) refer to "patientenrelevanten Endpunkten, insbesondere Mortalität, Morbidität und Lebensqualität." ["patient-relevant endpoints, particularly mortality, morbidity, and quality of life"]. PRO data may therefore contribute to the assessment when the instrument measures symptoms, functioning, health status, or health-related quality of life in a manner that is clinically interpretable and methodologically reliable.

### Situations Where PROs Can Affect the Assessment

- Symptom-driven morbidity, including pruritus, pain, dyspnea, fatigue, diarrhea, nausea, appetite loss, sleep disturbance, disease-specific symptoms, and patient global impressions.
- Health status, including EQ-5D VAS.
- Health-related quality of life, including instruments such as EORTC QLQ-C30, EORTC disease modules, DLQI, KCCQ, SF-12/SF-36, PedsQL, SGRQ, CFQ-R, and disease-specific instruments.
- Palliative settings, where G-BA may explicitly value quality-of-life evidence.
- Chronic disabling disease, where symptoms and function may be central to patient-relevant benefit.
- Negative PRO findings, which can offset or reduce an otherwise favorable efficacy profile.
- Missing, exploratory, or biased PRO findings, which can leave a benefit case dependent on survival, morbidity, or safety alone.

## France: HAS / Transparency Committee

### Assessment Framework

HAS Transparency Committee methods identify quality-of-life evidence as part of the assessment of a medicine's clinical effect. The [HAS Transparency Committee doctrine](https://www.has-sante.fr/upload/docs/application/pdf/2021-03/doctrine_ct.pdf) states, "Les données de qualité de vie contribuent à l’évaluation de l’effet clinique du médicament." ["Quality-of-life data contribute to the evaluation of the clinical effect of the medicine"]. The same document states that "L’absence de données de qualité de vie peut impacter négativement l’ASMR" ["The absence of quality-of-life data may negatively affect the ASMR"] when such evidence is expected, including in chronic, disabling, or end-of-life conditions.

The evidentiary standard is not limited to collecting a questionnaire. HAS describes quality-of-life measurement as reflecting outcomes "du point de vue du patient" ["from the patient's perspective"] ([HAS quality-of-life note](https://www.has-sante.fr/jcms/c_2883073/fr/evaluation-des-technologies-de-sante-a-la-has-place-de-la-qualite-de-vie)). The HAS submission guide further states that these data require the "même rigueur de recueil et d’analyse" ["same rigor in collection and analysis"] as other clinical endpoints. These statements support prospective collection, appropriate instruments, prespecified analyses, and interpretable handling of missing data.

### Situations Where PROs Can Affect the Assessment

- Chronic diseases with substantial daily-life impact.
- Disabling diseases where function and autonomy are material.
- End-of-life and metastatic oncology, especially when survival benefit is modest or uncertain.
- Symptom-driven diseases, including pruritus, pain, migraine, respiratory disease, dermatologic disease, bladder pain, and hereditary angioedema.
- Rare diseases where function, caregiver burden, and quality of life may be central evidence domains.
- Cases where QoL is exploratory, open-label, non-comparative, missing, or not controlled for multiplicity. HAS often explicitly declines to draw formal conclusions in those circumstances.

## Examples Excluded From the Positive-Evidence Tables

The presence of a QoL or PRO instrument in a study does not establish that the resulting data affected the HTA conclusion. Examples were excluded from the positive-evidence tables when the data were not usable, were exploratory, were not aligned with the assessment comparator, showed no clear advantage, or counted against the product. Romosozumab in severe osteoporosis illustrates this distinction: quality-of-life measures were relevant to the evidence plan, but G-BA reported no usable quality-of-life data for the 24-month comparison with alendronic acid. Ixekizumab in plaque psoriasis illustrates a different limitation: HAS reported DLQI and Itch NRS data in the evidence package and assigned an important SMR in a restricted severe-psoriasis population, but the official ASMR was V, meaning no clinical improvement versus COSENTYX. Secukinumab in plaque psoriasis was also removed from the positive-evidence table under the stricter rule because the HAS opinion assigned ASMR IV versus STELARA but stated that the expected impact in terms of morbidity and quality of life could not be assessed. Similar exclusions included G-BA examples with high questionnaire missingness or non-assessable HRQoL data, and HAS examples in which QoL analyses were exploratory, non-comparative, open-label without adequate interpretability, absent, not robust enough for a formal conclusion, not associated with a positive ASMR, or not directly connected to the official assessment rationale.

## Cross-Jurisdiction Findings

1. Favorable QoL/PRO evidence was most useful when it was comparative and interpretable against the comparator used in the assessment.
2. Non-cancer examples were identified in cardiology, cystic fibrosis, amyloidosis, dermatology, migraine, and mastocytosis.
3. Disease-specific instruments often carried clearer interpretive value than generic instruments alone when the endpoint matched the disease burden.
4. Prespecified endpoints, hierarchical testing, clinically meaningful thresholds, and high completion rates were recurrent features of usable positive evidence.
5. G-BA and HAS frequently discounted exploratory, open-label, non-comparative, missing, or poorly justified PRO analyses.
6. A QoL instrument listed in a trial or dossier was not sufficient. The evidence had to be usable in the assessment and favorable for the product.

## Conclusions

The identified examples indicate that QoL and broader PRO evidence can support more favorable G-BA and HAS assessments when the data are usable, comparative, disease-relevant, and favorable for the product. The strongest non-cancer examples include mavacamten, tafamidis, risankizumab, KAFTRIO/KALYDECO, patisiran, pediatric dupilumab in atopic dermatitis, and dupilumab in prurigo nodularis. Evidence was less useful when QoL was only collected but not interpretable, exploratory, non-comparative, affected by missingness, outside the statistical hierarchy, or not aligned with the comparator context used for the assessment.
