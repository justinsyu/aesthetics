# Target Product Profile Template: Slide Deck Outline

## Slide 1: Target Product Profile Template

Key message: A TPP is a living asset strategy tool that translates clinical ambition, evidence needs, patient value, HEOR, market access, and governance decisions into one structured plan.

## Slide 2: Why TPPs Matter

Key message: The TPP keeps teams aligned on the intended label, evidence package, value proposition, and go/no-go criteria before late-stage decisions become expensive to reverse.

## Slide 3: What This Template Is Built To Do

Key message: This template is designed for internal planning, not as a current FDA policy document; it helps teams pressure-test regulatory, clinical, HEOR, access, competitive, patient, CMC, and usability assumptions.

## Slide 4: Start With The Asset Snapshot

Key message: The asset snapshot establishes the strategic context: asset name, modality, mechanism, indication, patient population, development stage, intended regions, current decision gate, update date, and owner.

## Slide 5: Build The Core Attribute Matrix

Key message: Each major TPP attribute should be defined across minimum, target, and aspirational thresholds so the team can separate must-have viability from differentiating upside.

## Slide 6: Define Evidence And Accountability

Key message: For every attribute, the template requires evidence needed, owner, status, and decision implications, turning the TPP from a static wish list into an operating document.

## Slide 7: Align Clinical And Regulatory Plans

Key message: The clinical and regulatory section connects target label claims, population, endpoints, dose, regimen, and FDA engagement questions to explicit evidence gaps and next decisions.

## Slide 8: Make HEOR And Access Central

Key message: The HEOR and market access plan captures burden of illness, economic modeling, value dossiers, HTA readiness, pricing corridor, and coverage-with-evidence options early enough to shape trial design.

## Slide 9: Bring In Stakeholder Evidence

Key message: Patient, clinician, payer, regulator, and site perspectives should each have a defined insight need, method, output, and decision use.

## Slide 10: Keep Competitive Intelligence Active

Key message: The TPP should be updated when competing trials, labels, regulatory decisions, HTA outcomes, literature, guidelines, or stakeholder sentiment change the asset's assumed value or evidence requirements.

## Slide 11: Track Gaps As Decisions

Key message: Evidence gaps should be logged with affected attribute, impact, mitigation, owner, due date, and the decision if unresolved, so risks remain visible at governance gates.

## Slide 12: Use Decision Gates To Govern Progress

Key message: Candidate selection, IND/CTA, end of Phase 2, Phase 3 start, filing, and launch each need defined minimum, target, and aspirational evidence thresholds.

## Slide 13: Practical Operating Model

Key message: The template works best when maintained as a cross-functional source of truth, reviewed before major gates, and refreshed after material regulatory, clinical, payer, or competitive developments.

## Slide 14: What Good Looks Like

Key message: A strong TPP is source-backed, decision-oriented, cross-functional, specific about tradeoffs, and explicit about where the product must meet minimum thresholds versus where it can win through differentiation.

## Slide 15: Close: From Template To Asset Strategy

Key message: The TPP should help teams make better development choices by linking the desired product profile to the evidence, value story, access assumptions, and governance decisions needed to make it real.
