TPP template briefing

Target product profile template for evidence-based asset planning

A structured guide to linking label intent, clinical evidence, patient value, HEOR, market access, competitive context, CMC, and governance.

Planning purpose

Align asset teams on the evidence required for approval, reimbursement, adoption, and portfolio decision-making.

01
Conceptual basis

A target product profile should integrate label planning and value evidence

Regulatory anchor

Translate the intended product label into target claims, evidence requirements, and open agency questions.

Value anchor

Evaluate whether the same profile can address payer, HTA, prescriber, patient, and commercial requirements.

Decision anchor

Define minimum thresholds that trigger continue, pivot, or stop decisions before data are interpreted.

Regulatory note

  • The historical FDA TPP draft guidance was withdrawn in 2019, so this deck treats TPPs as internal planning tools rather than current FDA policy.
  • The template still uses label concepts because that structure keeps development activity tied to the evidence needed for future claims.

Operating model

Begin with a controlled asset snapshot and the relevant decision gate

01

Asset identity

Asset, modality, target, indication, geography, stage, decision gate, and accountable owner.

02

Profile frame

State the patient problem, future standard of care, expected advantage, and minimum viable profile.

03

Threshold set

Use minimum, target, and aspirational columns so tradeoffs are explicit and comparable.

04

Evidence basis

Attach required evidence, source basis, owner, status, and decision implication to each attribute.

05

Refresh trigger

Update after readouts, agency feedback, payer research, HTA changes, and competitor events.

The TPP supports decision-making when it records both the intended profile and the decision rule for evidence that falls below target.

04
Label and evidence linkage

Each target claim requires an evidence path and a gap owner

Assessment questions

  • What claim does the team want the product to support?
  • What type of claim is it: efficacy, safety, convenience, quality of life, economic, or access?
  • What evidence is required and how strong is current support?
  • What is the decision impact if the gap remains unresolved?
Target label claim
Connects trial design to future labeling and positioning.
Primary endpoint
Needs regulatory acceptance and clinical meaning.
Population
Must balance unmet need, feasibility, and generalizability.
Dose/regimen
Links PK/PD, tolerability, usability, and adherence.
Agency questions
Turns the TPP into a briefing tool for formal interactions.
05
Patient and stakeholder evidence

Patient evidence can inform the product profile fields

Target population

Clarifies which patients have the most meaningful unmet need and acceptable benefit-risk fit.

Unmet need

Identifies symptoms, functional limits, burden, and treatment tradeoffs that matter to patients.

Route and frequency

Tests whether dose schedule, setting, and administration burden will enable adherence.

Efficacy endpoints

Links statistical endpoints to outcomes patients recognize as meaningful improvement.

Benefit-risk

Defines tradeoffs patients, clinicians, and regulators may accept for the intended benefit.

Access barriers

Surfaces affordability, geography, insurance, site-of-care, and support-service needs.

The template captures patient, clinician, payer/HTA, regulator, and site/operations insights as separate stakeholder evidence streams.
06
HEOR and market access

A market access-oriented TPP documents payer evidence needs before Phase 3 design is fixed

Comparator

Payer-relevant control

Map the comparator regulators accept against the comparator HTA bodies and payers are likely to require.

Economics

Model readiness

Plan cost-effectiveness, budget impact, utilities, resource use, and uncertainty analyses early.

Access

Coverage logic

Record pricing corridor, restrictions, utilization management, coding, and access assumptions.

Evidence

Value dossier

Align clinical, RWE, PRO/COA, burden, and HEOR outputs into a country-adaptable value story.

Template implication

  • If the clinical trial cannot address payer and HTA decision problems, the access risk should be visible in the TPP while the design can still change.
07
Scenario evaluation

Competitive intelligence tests the TPP against external scenarios

Future standard of care

Track competitor readouts, approvals, guidelines, HTA outcomes, and labels that could change launch-year differentiation requirements.

Adoption behavior

Test whether physicians would prescribe, payers would cover, and patients would choose the product at each evidence level.

Fallback positioning

Define what the product becomes if efficacy, safety, convenience, or cost lands at minimum rather than target.

Competitor event
Refresh comparator, endpoint, and differentiation thresholds.
Payer feedback
Update value story, pricing corridor, access restrictions, and evidence gaps.
Clinical readout
Classify as below minimum, minimum viable, target, or stretch profile.
CMC issue
Reassess formulation, route, cost, stability, device, or supply feasibility.
Patient input
Adjust route/frequency, endpoint strategy, support needs, or benefit-risk stance.
08
Governance

Evidence gaps should be managed as decision risks

Gap register

Every unresolved assumption has an attribute, impact, mitigation, owner, due date, and decision if unresolved.

Decision gates

Candidate selection, IND/CTA, end of Phase 2, Phase 3 start, filing, and launch each need explicit proceed criteria.

Surveillance layer

Living monitoring covers trials, labels, HTA decisions, literature, payer insight, and patient/clinician sentiment.

Quality criteria

  • Each attribute has a status, evidence need, owner, and decision implication.
  • Thresholds are revisited when the asset, evidence base, or market changes.
  • The same profile can support regulatory, HEOR, access, commercial, and portfolio decisions.
