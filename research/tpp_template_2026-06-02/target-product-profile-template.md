# Target Product Profile Template

Use this template as a living planning document for pharma and biotech asset teams. It is organized around intended labeling, evidence generation, patient value, HEOR/market access, competitive positioning, CMC/usability, and governance decisions.

Regulatory note: FDA lists the 2007 "Target Product Profile -- A Strategic Development Process Tool" guidance as withdrawn on 12/13/2019, so this template should be used as an internal planning tool rather than as a claim that the withdrawn guidance is current FDA policy. Sources: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/guidances-drugs/withdrawn-and-expired-guidances-drugs and https://www.fda.gov/media/95015/download.

## 2. Core TPP Attribute Matrix

Status values: Not started, In progress, Evidence gap, At risk, Met, Exceeds, Retired.

| Attribute | Minimum threshold | Target threshold | Aspirational threshold | Evidence needed | Owner | Status | Decision implications |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Target indication and label concept | Approval-supporting population with clear unmet need and feasible diagnostic definition | Label supports clinically meaningful use in the target population and priority subgroup(s) | Broad or earlier-line label supported by robust benefit-risk and evidence package | Regulatory precedent, disease burden, clinical trial eligibility, FDA interaction notes | Clinical/regulatory | [Status] | If minimum is not feasible, reassess indication, population, or development path. |
| Efficacy endpoint | Statistically persuasive effect on accepted primary endpoint versus appropriate control | Clinically meaningful effect size versus current standard of care | Differentiated effect on primary and key secondary endpoints, including durable benefit | Phase 2/3 trials, endpoint validation, estimand, comparator rationale | Clinical | [Status] | Drives go/no-go, sample size, Phase 3 design, and label claims. |
| Safety and tolerability | Benefit-risk acceptable for intended population | Safety profile manageable with routine monitoring and competitive versus alternatives | Safety profile enables preferred use, broader population, or reduced monitoring burden | Nonclinical tox, clinical AE/SAE profile, AESI plan, risk management plan | Safety/regulatory | [Status] | If safety does not meet minimum, revise dose, population, monitoring, or discontinue. |
| Patient experience and COA strategy | Patient-relevant burden and outcomes identified | Fit-for-purpose COA supports endpoint hierarchy and value story | Patient experience data supports differentiated claims and shared decision making | Patient interviews, COA validation, meaningful change threshold, PFDD plan; see https://www.fda.gov/media/159500/download | Patient outcomes/clinical | [Status] | Weak COA strategy can limit label, payer, and prescriber differentiation. |
| RWD/RWE strategy | RWD sources identified for epidemiology, natural history, or external context | Fit-for-purpose RWE plan supports regulatory, HTA, payer, or postapproval needs | RWE supports lifecycle indication expansion, long-term outcomes, or postapproval commitments | Data source feasibility, protocol, confounding plan, data quality checks; see FDA RWE source https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-use-real-world-data-and-real-world-evidence-support-regulatory-decision-making-drug?key=2022 | RWE/epidemiology | [Status] | Determines whether additional studies, registries, or postapproval commitments are needed. |
| Comparator and standard of care | Comparator accepted by regulators or clinically justified | Comparator also credible for HTA and payer decision makers | Comparator strategy demonstrates superiority or clear differentiation versus likely future standard | Guidelines, trial landscape, payer research, HTA precedent, CI monitoring | Clinical/HEOR/CI | [Status] | Misaligned comparator can create regulatory, HTA, or access risk. |
| HEOR value proposition | Basic burden, unmet need, and resource-use rationale documented | Economic and humanistic value case supports expected access and reimbursement | Strong value story supports premium positioning or favorable access restrictions | Burden of illness, cost offsets, utilities, model concept, evidence synthesis; see ISPOR https://www.ispor.org/heor-resources/good-practices | HEOR | [Status] | If value is weak, adjust evidence plan, target population, price assumptions, or launch sequence. |
| HTA and payer decision problem | Priority HTA/payer comparators and outcomes mapped | Evidence package addresses effectiveness, value for money, uncertainty, and access scenarios | Submission-ready model and evidence dossier reduce uncertainty and support broad access | NICE decision problem, economic model, scenario analyses, PAS/CAA assumptions; see https://www.nice.org.uk/process/pmg36/chapter/introduction-to-health-technology-evaluatio and https://www.nice.org.uk/process/pmg36/chapter/economic-evaluation-2 | Market access/HEOR | [Status] | Guides Phase 3 endpoints, subgroup plans, evidence synthesis, and price corridor. |
| Pricing and access assumptions | Preliminary willingness-to-pay and access barriers identified | Price corridor tied to value proof points and target restrictions | Contracting, distribution, and coverage strategy support rapid uptake | Payer research, analogs, budget impact, value dossier, contracting assumptions | Market access/pricing | [Status] | If evidence does not support price/access target, revisit claims, population, or commercial forecast. |
| Competitive differentiation | Current and emerging competitors tracked | Differentiation clear on efficacy, safety, convenience, patient value, or economics | Durable differentiation against future entrants and lifecycle scenarios | Trial landscape, labels, conference updates, SLR/NMA plan, CI dashboard | Competitive intelligence | [Status] | Weak differentiation may trigger asset repositioning, combination strategy, or indication sequencing. |
| CMC readiness | Manufacturable product meeting quality and stability requirements | Scalable process, validated assays, and supply plan aligned to pivotal and launch needs | COGS, shelf life, presentation, and supply resilience support global access | CMC plan, comparability, stability, device/container data, supply risk register | CMC/quality | [Status] | CMC gaps can delay trials, filing, launch supply, or global expansion. |
| Usability and administration | Route, schedule, and setting feasible for target patients and sites | Administration supports adherence and operational fit versus alternatives | Convenience enables differentiation, lower total cost, or expanded site of care | Human factors, site workflow, adherence research, device usability, patient preference | Clinical operations/patient/CMC | [Status] | Poor usability can reduce uptake even if efficacy and safety are strong. |

## 5. Patient and Stakeholder Evidence

| Stakeholder | Required insight | Method | Output | Decision use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Patients/caregivers | Burden, preferences, meaningful benefit, acceptable risk | Interviews, surveys, preference study, advisory board | Patient evidence summary, COA input, endpoint priorities | Endpoint selection, benefit-risk, value story |
| Clinicians | Treatment pathway, adoption barriers, comparator expectations | KOL interviews, chart review, advisory board | Treatment algorithm and adoption map | Trial design, positioning, education plan |
| Payers/HTA bodies | Evidence thresholds, comparator, price/access sensitivity | Payer research, mock HTA, analog review | Payer evidence requirements and value message test | Evidence plan, price corridor, launch sequencing |
| Regulators | Evidence adequacy for intended label and population | Formal meetings, briefing book, TPP discussion | Regulatory feedback log | Development plan and filing strategy |
| Sites and operations | Recruitment feasibility, visit burden, administration workflow | Feasibility survey, site interviews, protocol simulation | Operational risk log | Protocol design and timeline |

## 6. Competitive Intelligence and Evidence Surveillance

Vendor sources emphasize continuous monitoring of literature, trials, labels, regulatory documents, conference abstracts, RWE, payer insights, and value evidence. Relevant sources include Dr.Evidence https://www.drevidence.com/solutions/heor-market-access, Genesis Research Group https://genesisrg.com/evidence-solutions/, Clarivate https://clarivate.com/products/real-world-data/, and RTI Health Solutions https://www.rtihs.org/value-access-and-heor.

| Topic | Watch items | Cadence | Owner | Trigger for TPP update |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Competing trials | New Phase 2/3 starts, endpoints, inclusion criteria, readouts | Monthly; ad hoc for major readouts | CI | Comparator, endpoint, or population assumptions change |
| Labels and regulatory decisions | New approvals, boxed warnings, indication expansions, restrictions | Monthly | Regulatory/CI | Target label or safety expectations change |
| HTA and payer decisions | NICE/HTA outcomes, restrictions, managed access, price/access signals | Monthly/quarterly | Market access | Access threshold or evidence requirement changes |
| Literature and evidence synthesis | SLR updates, NMAs, guideline changes, burden studies | Quarterly | HEOR | Value proposition or comparator evidence changes |
| Patient and clinician sentiment | Patient burden, adherence issues, unmet need, administration barriers | Quarterly or before gates | Patient outcomes/commercial | COA, usability, or positioning assumptions change |

## 8. Decision Threshold Summary

| Gate | Minimum evidence to proceed | Target evidence to proceed | Aspirational evidence | Decision owner | Decision date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Candidate selection | Mechanistic rationale, feasibility, initial TPP | Differentiated target profile and feasible development path | Early value/access rationale and patient-relevant hypothesis | R&D leadership | [YYYY-MM-DD] |
| IND/CTA | Nonclinical safety, CMC readiness, first-in-human plan | Translational biomarkers and patient/endpoint rationale | Early RWE/natural history and payer decision problem mapped | Development leadership | [YYYY-MM-DD] |
| End of Phase 2 | Dose, safety, efficacy signal, Phase 3 design | Target label, comparator, endpoints, HEOR plan, and patient evidence aligned | Payer/HTA validation, RWE plan, and value story tested | Governance committee | [YYYY-MM-DD] |
| Phase 3 start | Pivotal design supports regulatory approval | Pivotal design also supports HTA and payer needs | Phase 3 includes evidence for differentiation, value, and lifecycle expansion | Governance committee | [YYYY-MM-DD] |
| Filing | Complete clinical, safety, CMC, and label package | Evidence package supports launch claims and access strategy | RWE/postapproval plan and HTA submissions ready | Filing team | [YYYY-MM-DD] |
| Launch | Approved label and supply readiness | Value dossier, payer materials, access plan, and field evidence tools ready | Differentiated evidence platform supports rapid uptake and lifecycle studies | Launch leadership | [YYYY-MM-DD] |
