Postdoc Fellowship

United Therapeutics Medical Information

United Therapeutics Corportation (UTC), the UNC Eshelman School of Pharmacy Division of Pharmacotherapy and Experimental Therapeutics (DPET), and the UNC Healthcare Pharmacy Department, offer a 1-year pharmaceutical industry fellowship in Medical Affairs and Medical Information. In the first six months, fellows gain hands-on experience with UNC Healthcare’s Drug Information Services and United Therapeutics’ Medical Affairs teams. The second six months are spent fully immersed in United Therapeutics’ Medical Affairs and Medical Information departments. Throughout the year, fellows also participate in ESOP fellowship program activities, providing a well-rounded professional experience.

United Therapeutics Medical Affairs


Applications open October 1, 2025.
Early application deadline is October 31, 2025. Final application deadline is November 21, 2025.

From Learning to Launch.

Program Goals & Outcomes

This fellowship provides hands-on training in clinical drug information and pharmaceutical medical affairs, covering drug lifecycle management, regulatory compliance, data analysis, and cross-functional collaboration. Fellows gain experience responding to medical inquiries, developing educational materials, contributing to research, and supporting diverse Medical Affairs functions to prepare for leadership roles in the industry.

Division of Pharmacotherapy & Experimental Therapeutics

DPET advances the science and practice of precision therapeutics through innovative, collaborative research that optimizes how drugs are developed, dosed, and utilized. Our faculty and learners ensure safer, more effective treatments tailored to individual patients and their clinical conditions.

Learn more

United Therapeutics Corporation

United Therapeutics Corporation is a biotech company developing innovative therapies for rare, life-threatening diseases like pulmonary arterial hypertension and neuroblastoma, while advancing technologies to expand or preserve the supply of transplantable organs to address critical national shortages.
