## Synthesis: Course White Space

The visible market has credible point solutions but fewer public examples of a single practical curriculum that combines medical affairs role readiness, HEOR/market-access method fluency, integrated evidence generation planning, AI-enabled workflows, and realistic pharma case practice for clinicians changing careers.

The clearest white-space pitch is not "another certification" in isolation. It is a job-realistic readiness program that helps clinicians translate clinical expertise into industry work products:

- Medical affairs operating model: what medical affairs, MSLs, medical information, publications, safety, regulatory, commercial, market access, and HEOR teams each do.
- Evidence generation and IEGP: how teams identify evidence gaps, prioritize evidence questions, align global/regional/local plans, and track evidence impact over the product lifecycle.
- HEOR and market access: how clinical value becomes payer, HTA, pricing, reimbursement, budget impact, and formulary evidence.
- Scientific communication: how to build compliant field-medical narratives, advisory-board objectives, congress plans, medical information responses, and nonpromotional HEOR abstracts.
- AI-enabled workflow: how to use AI for literature triage, evidence mapping, insight synthesis, drafting support, search strategy iteration, medical information workflows, and quality checks while preserving compliance, privacy, validation, and human accountability.
- Practice cases: repeated realistic exercises where learners create or critique a launch evidence plan, KOL engagement plan, payer evidence memo, HTA submission outline, publication/congress abstract, medical information response, advisory-board plan, and AI-assisted evidence synthesis.

## Suggested Modular Structure

1. Pharma Industry Translation for Clinicians
   - Role map, lifecycle map, compliance boundary, nonpromotional exchange, cross-functional handoffs.
   - Sources: ACMA, MAPS, DIA, MSL Society, Rutgers RPIF brochures.

2. Medical Affairs Core Practice
   - MSL role, stakeholder engagement, KOL strategy, medical information, safety reporting, publications, advisory boards, medical governance.
   - Sources: ACMA BCMAS, MAPS Foundations, DIA medical affairs eLearning, MAPS/H1 and IQVIA case materials.

3. Evidence Generation and IEGP
   - Evidence gap assessment, evidence matrix, study concepts, stakeholder evidence needs, prioritization, evidence impact metrics.
   - Sources: MAPS strategic evidence generation guidance, DiMe IEP resource, IQVIA IEP blog, Axtria IEP white paper, Lucid IEGP case.

4. HEOR, HTA, and Market Access
   - Cost-effectiveness, budget impact, outcomes research, payer evidence, HTA methods, market access strategy, reimbursement negotiation.
   - Sources: UW CHOICE syllabi, ISPOR, Rutgers HOPE, Tufts HEOR, NICE, ICER, iDSI, Harvard/UNL/NIH case materials.

5. Regulatory and Development Case Practice
   - Drug development funnel, rare-disease evidence standards, complex innovative trial designs, clinical trial classification, model-informed development.
   - Sources: FDA drug approval case, FDA LEADER 3D cases, FDA CID cases, NIH clinical trial definition cases, FDA MIDD PDF.

6. AI for Medical Affairs and HEOR
   - Literature triage, evidence extraction, claims checking, insight synthesis, prompt design, review workflows, governance, privacy, and quality controls.
   - Sources: ACMA AI framing, MILE/AVAYL/MSL Society position paper, IQVIA AI white paper, CELforPharma AI course, LS Academy AI/compliance course.

7. Capstone Portfolio
   - Learners produce a realistic portfolio: product situation brief, stakeholder map, evidence gap map, IEGP outline, payer evidence memo, KOL engagement plan, medical information response, HEOR abstract, and AI-use audit log.
   - Sources: DIA Student Case Competition, ISPOR impact-case example, MAPS workshop slides, FDA cases, NIH SEED reimbursement/regulatory cases, AMCP Format 5.0, NICE/PBAC submission materials, and HTA manuals.

## Second-Pass Additions to Consider Explicitly

- Pharmaceutical medicine credential spine: compare ACMA/MAPS medical affairs orientation against GMDP/KCL, IFAPP, FPM, PharmaTrain, and ECPM medicines-development/pharmaceutical-medicine training.
- HEOR/RWE method spine: add Jefferson, UNT, JHU, USC, Penn, ISPE, Eu2P, LSHTM, and UW RWE programs as benchmarks for outcomes research, pharmacoepidemiology, drug safety, and real-world evidence.
- Regulatory-science spine: use RAPS RAC-Drugs, RAPS certificates, FDA NCTR/UAMS, Arizona/C-Path, USC, Maryland, and UCSF ACDRS to define what "regulatory literacy" should mean for clinicians entering industry.
- Market-access practice spine: use AMCP Format 5.0, AMCP budget-impact material, PBAC guidance/public summary documents, NICE company evidence submission guidance, and NIH SEED reimbursement cases to make payer evidence concrete.
- AI and evidence-governance spine: use FDA AI guidance/discussion paper, FDA RWE device examples, IQVIA/MILE AI materials, and AI/KOL vendor cases as critique material, with clear separation between regulator guidance, peer-reviewed discussion, and vendor positioning.

## Positioning Implications

Use careful language. Public sources support the claim that existing offerings cover important components of medical affairs and HEOR training. The stronger strategic argument is that a more realistic course can integrate these components into applied, work-product-based practice for clinicians who need to demonstrate readiness, not just pass a knowledge exam.

The differentiator should be framed as:

- Industry-current practice examples.
- Integrated medical affairs + HEOR + market access coverage.
- Evidence planning and AI workflow competence.
- Realistic portfolio outputs.
- Transparent source-backed curriculum design.

Avoid overclaiming that any competitor lacks industry relevance unless supported by documented leadership/current-practice evidence. It is safer and stronger to say the proposed course is designed around current industry work products and public, traceable case materials.
