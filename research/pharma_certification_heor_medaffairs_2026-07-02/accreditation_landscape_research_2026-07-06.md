# Accreditation status of medical-affairs and evidence credentialing programs

Internal strategic reference. Prepared 2026-07-06. This document is research intelligence for planning the BCMAEP credential; it is deliberately kept out of the course content (`BCMAEP_certification_course/`) because it names other programs. Every finding is labeled either independently confirmed via a cited source or claimed by the program only. Where a claimed status could not be independently confirmed, that is stated plainly.

## The distinction that resolves most of the confusion

- **IACET / ANSI-IACET accreditation** accredits a *continuing-education / training provider*. It attests the training organization meets a continuing-education quality standard and may award CEUs. It does not accredit a certification or credential of persons. "ANSI-IACET" means the IACET Standard is itself ANSI-accredited; it does not mean the trainer's credential is accredited as a personnel certification.
- **NCCA accreditation** (National Commission for Certifying Agencies, the accrediting arm of the Institute for Credentialing Excellence, ICE) accredits a *certification program* (certification of persons).
- **ANAB accreditation to ISO/IEC 17024** accredits a *body that certifies persons*.

A program can hold IACET provider accreditation while its credential is not an accredited certification. That is precisely the BCMAS situation.

## Direct answer: accredited, or just following best-practice guidelines?

For the medical-affairs credentials specifically, they mostly follow best-practice guidelines (job-task analysis, item writing, psychometric development) rather than holding formal certification accreditation.

- **BCMAS (ACMA):** the accreditation ACMA actually holds is provider-level continuing-education accreditation from IACET. That is a real, independent accreditation, but it accredits ACMA as a training organization allowed to issue CEUs, not the BCMAS credential as a certification of persons. No NCCA or ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation of BCMAS was found. ACMA's name ("Accreditation Council for Medical Affairs") and its self-styling as an accrediting body are descriptive branding, not an independent personnel-certification accreditation of its credential.
- **MAPS Foundations:** not accredited at all, and MAPS says so plainly (no CE credits offered). It is a competency-framework-based certificate program.
- **MSL-BC (MSL Society):** psychometrically developed, which follows best-practice guidelines, but not accredited. The MSL Society itself answers "Not yet" and notes no NCCA/ANSI-accredited MSL or medical-affairs certification presently exists.
- **CMPP (ISMPP):** a structured, exam-based credential with recertification, but no third-party accreditation is claimed or independently found.
- **RAC (RAPS):** the exception. It actually achieved NCCA accreditation (February 2022) for both RAC-Drugs and RAC-Devices. RAC is regulatory affairs, not medical affairs, but it demonstrates what genuine certification accreditation in this professional space looks like.

Bottom line: among the medical-affairs and publications credentials, the strongest independently verifiable accreditation is BCMAS's IACET provider accreditation, which is continuing-education-provider accreditation, not certification-of-persons accreditation. The others are unaccredited certificate or board-certification programs that describe rigorous, psychometrically informed development but have not obtained NCCA or ISO/IEC 17024 certification accreditation.

## What it takes to achieve certification accreditation

### NCCA accreditation (Institute for Credentialing Excellence)

Eligibility prerequisite: a new certification program may apply only after one year of administration of the assessment instrument, or once at least 500 candidates have been assessed with that instrument, whichever comes first. This is why the MSL Society states it will apply "when eligible."

Standards to be met (2021 NCCA Standards):
1. Governance and impartiality: an autonomous governing body for essential certification decisions, and separation of the certification program from any education or training the organization also provides, so the accredited certification is independent of prep courses.
2. Job / practice analysis: a comprehensive, expert-conducted analysis with data from stakeholders in the occupation, establishing content validity.
3. Exam blueprint / content specifications derived from the job analysis.
4. Item development and exam construction by qualified subject-matter experts, with security and confidentiality controls.
5. A standard-setting (cut-score) study: a defensible passing-score determination.
6. Psychometric and technical documentation: evidence of reliability and validity.
7. Eligibility, recertification, and public-information policies.

Application and review sequence: applications are accepted three times per year (January 31, April 30, August 31); peer review against the Standards; a decision approximately 4 to 5 months from submission; outcomes are Accredit (5-year term), Accredit with Compliance Reporting, or Deny. Accreditation term is 5 years, with maintenance fees. Exact fee amounts are not publicly posted and should be obtained directly from ICE.

### ANAB accreditation to ISO/IEC 17024 (certification of persons)

Prerequisite: the certification scheme must be built to conform to ISO/IEC 17024, including a documented job/task analysis, impartiality and conflict-of-interest controls (separation from training), and demonstrably valid and reliable examinations with time-limited credentials subject to recertification.

Process sequence: obtain the standard (ANAB recommends a two-day Personnel Certification Accreditation Workshop); submit a preliminary application to establish eligibility; submit the full application with supporting documents and fee; document/desk review of the quality system against ISO/IEC 17024; an onsite assessment; closure of any nonconformities; accreditation decision; then maintenance through surveillance and periodic reassessment. This is generally longer than the NCCA cycle because of the onsite audit and nonconformity-closure loop. Fees are provided by quote.

Common thread: both routes require, at minimum, a documented job/practice/task analysis, an exam blueprint derived from it, controlled item development, a standard-setting study, psychometric evidence of reliability and validity, governance with impartiality separation from training, and defined eligibility and recertification policies. NCCA additionally imposes the one-year-or-500-candidate operational threshold before a program can even apply. Doing item writing and psychometric development satisfies parts of these requirements but is not equivalent to holding the accreditation.

## Implication for BCMAEP

BCMAEP already targets ISO/IEC 17024 and the NCCA Standards (personnel-certification accreditation), which none of BCMAS, MAPS, MSL-BC, or CMPP currently holds. Obtaining it would place BCMAEP ahead of the field on the credibility dimension competitors lean on rhetorically but have not achieved. The one-year-or-500-candidate gate is also the concrete reason a version 1.0 design cannot yet be accredited, which supports the honest "not yet accredited, designed for alignment" framing already used in the course.

## Verification limits and honesty caveats

- The IACET directory page for ACMA returned a transient HTTP 503 on direct fetch during research; the listing is indexed in the IACET accredited-providers directory and is corroborated by ACMA's own claims and the PR Newswire award release, so ACMA's IACET provider accreditation is treated as independently confirmed.
- No NCCA or ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation could be independently confirmed for BCMAS, MSL-BC, or CMPP; no such listing was found in accreditor directories and none is claimed by MSL-BC or CMPP. MSL-BC is explicitly stated by its own body to be not yet accredited.
- Exact fee amounts for NCCA and ANAB / ISO 17024 accreditation are not publicly posted in the pages retrieved and are therefore not asserted here. No dollar figures were invented.
- This research was performed by web retrieval on 2026-07-06. Accreditation status can change; reverify against the accreditor directories before relying on any specific claim.
