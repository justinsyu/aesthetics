# Board Certified Medical Affairs and Evidence Professional (BCMAEP)

## Program Handbook and Certification Design

Program brand: **PRIME** (Pharmaceutical Readiness in Integrated evidence and Medical affairs Excellence)
Credential awarded: **Board Certified Medical Affairs and Evidence Professional (BCMAEP)**
Version: 1.0
Prepared: 2026-07-02

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

The BCMAEP program prepares clinicians and other professionals who are moving into the pharmaceutical, biotechnology, and medical-technology industry to perform competently in medical affairs, medical science liaison (MSL), medical information, health economics and outcomes research (HEOR), market access, and evidence-generation roles. It is designed as a board-style certification: a structured curriculum leads to a psychometrically constructed examination and a supervised work-product portfolio, and the credential recognizes demonstrated competency rather than course attendance.

The program is benchmarked against the Board Certified Medical Affairs Specialist (BCMAS) program of the Accreditation Council for Medical Affairs, which describes an online, self-paced, IACET/ANSI-accredited certification built on 13 core competencies and approximately 40 hours of instruction, with a final board examination ([ACMA, BCMAS Program Information](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info)). It is also benchmarked against the Medical Affairs Professional Society (MAPS) Foundations program, which is organized around 42 competencies across seven domains of the MAPS Medical Affairs Competency Framework ([MAPS, Foundations in Medical Affairs](https://medicalaffairs.org/medical-affairs-training/)), and the MSL Society MSL Board Certification (MSL-BC), a psychometrically validated examination requiring a bachelor's degree and one year of MSL experience ([MSL Society, MSL-BC](https://themsls.org/msl-board-certification/)).

BCMAEP is designed to match the role coverage of these programs and to add depth in three areas where publicly documented offerings are comparatively thin for a single integrated curriculum: (1) integrated evidence generation planning across the product lifecycle, (2) HEOR, health technology assessment (HTA), and market access method fluency, and (3) responsible use of artificial intelligence (AI) with an explicit governance and human-accountability framework. It also requires learners to produce realistic industry work products rather than only answer knowledge questions.

## 2. Who the program is for

Primary audience: practicing or recently practicing clinicians (physicians, pharmacists, doctorally prepared nurses, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, dentists, and PhD scientists) and other healthcare or life-science professionals seeking a first industry role or a lateral move into medical affairs, field medical, medical information, HEOR, market access, or evidence generation.

Secondary audience: early-career industry professionals who entered without formal cross-functional training; regulatory, clinical-operations, or commercial staff who interact with medical affairs and want method fluency; and academic researchers moving into industry evidence roles.

This audience mirrors the enrollee profile that BCMAS and MAPS describe: MSLs, medical information and medical communication specialists, medical affairs managers and directors, publication and drug-safety professionals, HEOR liaisons, and doctorally credentialed clinicians ([ACMA, BCMAS Program Information](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info); [MAPS, Foundations in Medical Affairs](https://medicalaffairs.org/medical-affairs-training/)).

## 3. Eligibility

Two eligibility routes, consistent with common credentialing practice for certifications of persons:

- **Credentialed route:** a doctoral or professional clinical or scientific degree (for example MD, DO, MBBS, PharmD, PhD, DNP, NP, PA, BDS, DDS, DVM), or
- **Experience route:** a bachelor's degree plus two years of relevant professional experience in healthcare, life sciences, or a related field.

These routes align with the BCMAS enrollment criteria (a relevant doctoral or professional degree, or a bachelor's degree plus two years of relevant experience) ([ACMA, BCMAS Program Information](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info)) and with the MSL-BC requirement of at least a bachelor's degree ([MSL Society, MSL-BC](https://themsls.org/msl-board-certification/)). The certification examination and the portfolio, not the entry route, determine the credential.

## 4. Design principles

1. **Role readiness and method fluency together.** Medical affairs operations and HEOR/market-access methods are taught in one curriculum so that a candidate can both operate in a field-medical or medical-information role and construct or critique the evidence and value arguments that those roles increasingly require.
2. **Evidence generation as a spine, not a topic.** Integrated evidence generation planning (IEGP) is treated as a core competency with hands-on deliverables, reflecting its position as a named competency in the MAPS Evidence Generation domain, which lists integrated evidence planning, identifying data gaps, and use of real-world evidence alongside HEOR ([MAPS, Foundations in Medical Affairs](https://medicalaffairs.org/medical-affairs-training/)).
3. **Responsible AI throughout, with governance.** AI methods are integrated into each relevant module and are governed by a single AI-use policy that requires validation, source verification, privacy and confidentiality controls, disclosure, and documented human accountability. This mirrors the direction of BCMAS, which now embeds AI literacy across its curriculum ([ACMA, BCMAS Program Information](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info)), and aligns the curriculum with current regulatory thinking on AI (see the AI governance module and `governance/ai_use_policy_and_playbook.md`).
4. **Work products, not only test items.** Candidates build a portfolio of deliverables that resemble first-year industry outputs, assessed with analytic rubrics. This addresses the gap between passing a knowledge examination and being able to produce usable industry work.
5. **Transparent, source-backed content.** Every factual teaching claim drawn from an external source carries a hyperlinked citation to an authoritative primary source, so learners can trace and verify content and can model the same evidence discipline in their own work.
6. **Compliance by construction.** Nonpromotional exchange, fair balance, and separation of medical and commercial functions are taught early and reinforced in every applied exercise.

## 5. Competency framework

The BCMAEP competency framework defines seven domains. Domains A through E map to the 15 instructional modules; domains F and G are assessed primarily through the portfolio and the professional-practice module. The framework is adapted from and cross-referenced to the MAPS Medical Affairs Competency Framework's seven domains (Strategy; Scientific and Technical Knowledge; Business Knowledge; Evidence Generation; Customer Engagement and Scientific Communications; Leadership and Management; Medical Governance and Compliance) ([MAPS, Foundations in Medical Affairs](https://medicalaffairs.org/medical-affairs-training/)).

| Domain | Title | Primary modules | Exam weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Industry orientation and translation | 1, 2, 3 | 15% |
| B | Scientific and clinical foundations | 4, 5, 6 | 20% |
| C | Evidence generation, HEOR, HTA, and market access | 7, 8, 9, 10 | 30% |
| D | Scientific communication and field medical | 11, 12, 13 | 20% |
| E | AI, data, and professional practice | 14, 15 | 15% |
| F | Medical governance and compliance | Cross-cutting; assessed in every module and the portfolio | Embedded |
| G | Strategy, leadership, and evidence-based decision making | Cross-cutting; assessed in the capstone portfolio | Embedded |

Domain C carries the largest examination weight (30%), which is the principal structural difference from BCMAS, where HEOR and pharmacoeconomics are represented as a single core competency within 13 ([ACMA, BCMAS Program Information](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info)).

## 7. Learning model

- **Self-paced online delivery** with defined module sequence, consistent with the BCMAS and MAPS online, self-paced formats ([ACMA, BCMAS Program Information](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info); [MAPS, Foundations in Medical Affairs](https://medicalaffairs.org/medical-affairs-training/)).
- **Each module contains:** learning objectives mapped to competencies; a reading and multimedia set with cited primary sources; a worked example or case; one or more applied activities that produce a draft work product; and a knowledge check. This mirrors the BCMAS module structure of optional pre-quiz, content sections, and post-quiz ([ACMA, BCMAS Program Information](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info)) and adds a required applied deliverable.
- **Case library:** a shared set of anonymized and public-source cases (rare-disease development, complex innovative trial designs, HTA submissions, KOL and evidence planning) reused across modules so learners see the same product move through development, evidence generation, communication, and access. Cases draw on regulator-authored and society-authored public materials (see `assessment/case_library.md`).
- **AI-enabled practice:** each applied activity specifies where AI assistance is permitted, what must be verified by the learner, and what must be recorded in an AI-use log, per the program AI-use policy.

## 8. Assessment and the credential

Two components must both be passed to earn BCMAEP:

1. **Board examination.** A proctored, multiple-choice examination of 150 scored items plus 15 unscored pilot items, drawn from a blueprint weighted to the domains in section 5. The examination tests broad application, not recall of a single course, consistent with the definition of a certification examination as broad in scope and independent of a specific learning event ([MSL Society, MSL-BC](https://themsls.org/msl-board-certification/), citing the Institute for Credentialing Excellence distinction between certificate and certification programs). Candidates are allowed up to four attempts, matching common board-exam retake policy ([ACMA, BCMAS Program Information](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info)). The passing standard is set by a criterion-referenced standard-setting method (modified Angoff), not a fixed percentage. The full blueprint and sample items are in `assessment/exam_blueprint_and_sample_items.md`.
2. **Capstone portfolio.** A set of eight work products assessed against analytic rubrics by trained reviewers, described in `assessment/capstone_portfolio_and_rubrics.md`. The portfolio is the component that most distinguishes BCMAEP from a knowledge-only certification.

The credential is awarded when both components are passed and the eligibility route is verified.

## 9. Accreditation and quality alignment

BCMAEP is designed to be accreditation-ready. It is not accredited at version 1.0; the design deliberately aligns to the standards that certification and continuing-education bodies apply, so that a sponsoring organization can pursue accreditation without redesign. Alignment is documented in `governance/accreditation_and_quality_alignment.md` and summarized here.

A precise distinction matters. Two different kinds of accreditation apply to this field, and they are often conflated:

- **Continuing-education (provider) accreditation** attests that a training organization designs and delivers instruction to a quality standard. The ANSI/IACET 1-2018 Standard for continuing education and training is of this kind; under it, one CEU equals ten contact hours of structured learning ([IACET, ANSI/IACET Standard](https://www.iacet.org/standards/ansi-iacet-2018-1-standard-for-continuing-education-and-training/)). BCMAS is accredited by IACET/ANSI at the provider level ([ACMA, BCMAS Program Information](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info)), and the MSL Society also holds IACET accreditation.
- **Personnel-certification accreditation** attests that a credential validly and reliably measures a person's competency, independent of any course. ISO/IEC 17024:2012 is the international standard for bodies operating certification of persons ([ISO/IEC 17024:2012](https://www.iso.org/standard/52993.html)), and the National Commission for Certifying Agencies (NCCA) Standards, administered by the Institute for Credentialing Excellence, are the US reference; the 2021 NCCA revision contains 23 standards and has been applied to more than 315 accredited programs ([Institute for Credentialing Excellence, NCCA](https://www.credentialingexcellence.org/accreditation/earn-accreditation/ncca)).

BCMAEP targets both: IACET-aligned instructional design for the curriculum, and, for the credential itself, alignment to ISO/IEC 17024 and the NCCA Standards. This is a deliberate step beyond the current benchmarks: BCMAS, MSL-BC, and CMPP do not claim NCCA or ISO/IEC 17024 personnel-certification accreditation on their public pages. Both personnel-certification frameworks require the competency assessment to be independent of the learning event, which is why BCMAEP's examination is blueprinted independently of the modules and its passing standard is set by a criterion-referenced method rather than a course grade.

The certificate-versus-certification distinction, and the accreditation routes for each (the ICE 1100 Standard for certificate programs and the NCCA Standards for certification programs), are used with attribution to the Institute for Credentialing Excellence as presented on the MSL Society page ([MSL Society, MSL-BC](https://themsls.org/msl-board-certification/)).

## 10. Recertification

The credential is valid for three years, matching the MSL-BC recertification cycle ([MSL Society, MSL-BC](https://themsls.org/msl-board-certification/)). Recertification requires 30 hours of documented continuing professional development relevant to the domains, of which at least 6 hours must address evidence generation or HEOR and at least 3 hours must address AI governance or data ethics, plus annual attestation to the code of professional conduct. This structure is more demanding than the BCMAS annual maintenance model, which requires two approved learning activities plus an attestation each year ([ACMA, BCMAS Recertification](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/bcmas-recertification)); the explicit evidence and AI-governance requirements are added because those fields change quickly.

### The wider credential landscape and BCMAEP's integrating position

Beyond the five programs above, the field contains strong specialist credentials that each cover part of the BCMAEP scope. Regulatory affairs has the RAPS Regulatory Affairs Certification (RAC), a post-academic professional certification for regulatory professionals ([RAPS, RAC-Drugs candidate guide](https://www.raps.org/rac-drugs-candidate-guide)). Medical affairs and pharmaceutical medicine are addressed by the GMDP Academy and King's College London certification in medicines development ([King's College London, GMDP Medical Affairs](https://www.kcl.ac.uk/professional-education/catalogue/gmdp-medical-affairs)), the IFAPP training grounded in the PharmaTrain postgraduate education standards ([IFAPP, Introduction to Medical Affairs and Pharmaceutical Medicine](https://ifapp.org/formation/introduction-to-medical-affairs-and-pharmaceutical-medicine/); [PharmaTrain Federation](https://www.pharmatrain.eu/)), and, for physicians, the Faculty of Pharmaceutical Medicine Certificate and Diploma ([FPM, Certificate in Pharmaceutical Medicine](https://www.fpm.org.uk/training-exams/certificate-in-pharmaceutical-medicine/)). HEOR, real-world evidence, and market access are served by university certificates and by industry fellowships, including HEOR and market-access fellowships ([AESARA and University of North Carolina, Health Outcomes and Market Access Fellowship](https://aesara.com/aesara-university-of-north-carolina-health-outcomes-and-market-access-fellowship/); [Jefferson College of Population Health, HEOR fellowships](https://www.jefferson.edu/academics/colleges-schools-institutes/population-health/degrees-programs/fellowships/health-economics-and-outcomes-research-fellowships.html)), and by pharmacoepidemiology and RWE certificates ([USC, Graduate Certificate in Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety](https://mann.usc.edu/program/graduate-certificate-in-pharmacoepidemiology-and-drug-safety/)).

This landscape sharpens rather than weakens the case for BCMAEP. A career changer who wanted the combined competency BCMAEP defines would otherwise assemble it from a regulatory certification, a medical affairs or pharmaceutical medicine credential, an HEOR certificate or fellowship, and separate AI training, at substantial cost and time and with no single assessment that they can work across all of them. BCMAEP's aim is integrated working competency across these areas, validated by one examination and one portfolio, with the depth in each area calibrated to a first industry role rather than to the specialist standard of the corresponding single-domain credential. It is designed to complement, not replace, those specialist credentials: a graduate who later specializes in regulatory or HEOR would pursue the RAC or an HEOR fellowship from a stronger, integrated starting point.

## 12. Positioning statement

BCMAEP is a job-realistic readiness certification that helps clinicians and other professionals translate clinical and scientific expertise into industry work products across medical affairs and evidence generation. Its differentiators are integrated medical affairs plus HEOR and market access coverage in one curriculum, a dedicated integrated evidence generation planning competency, an explicit and governed approach to AI, a required work-product portfolio, and transparent source-backed content. Public materials support the claim that existing programs cover important components of this training; the strategic argument for BCMAEP is integration of those components into applied, assessed, work-product-based practice.
