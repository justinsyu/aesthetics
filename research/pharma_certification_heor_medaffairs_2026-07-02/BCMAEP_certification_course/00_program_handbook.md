# Board Certified Medical Affairs and Evidence Professional (BCMAEP)

## Program Handbook and Certification Design

Program brand: **PRIME** (Pharmaceutical Readiness in Integrated evidence and Medical affairs Excellence)
Credential awarded: **Board Certified Medical Affairs and Evidence Professional (BCMAEP)**
Version: 1.0
Prepared: 2026-07-02

---

## 1. Purpose and scope

The BCMAEP program prepares clinicians and other professionals who are moving into the pharmaceutical, biotechnology, and medical-technology industry to perform competently in medical affairs, medical science liaison (MSL), medical information, health economics and outcomes research (HEOR), market access, and evidence-generation roles. It is designed as a board-style certification: a structured curriculum leads to a psychometrically constructed examination and a supervised work-product portfolio, and the credential recognizes demonstrated competency rather than course attendance.

The curriculum is built around the competencies that medical affairs and evidence roles require in practice, organized into seven competency domains (section 5) and delivered as 15 modules and a capstone. The design covers the full role landscape a career changer enters, from field medical and medical information to HEOR, market access, and evidence generation, in a single integrated program rather than as separate offerings.

BCMAEP adds depth in three areas that are central to modern industry evidence work: (1) integrated evidence generation planning across the product lifecycle, (2) HEOR, health technology assessment (HTA), and market access method fluency, and (3) responsible use of artificial intelligence (AI) with an explicit governance and human-accountability framework. It also requires learners to produce realistic industry work products rather than only answer knowledge questions.

## 2. Who the program is for

Primary audience: practicing or recently practicing clinicians (physicians, pharmacists, doctorally prepared nurses, nurse practitioners, physician assistants, dentists, and PhD scientists) and other healthcare or life-science professionals seeking a first industry role or a lateral move into medical affairs, field medical, medical information, HEOR, market access, or evidence generation.

Secondary audience: early-career industry professionals who entered without formal cross-functional training; regulatory, clinical-operations, or commercial staff who interact with medical affairs and want method fluency; and academic researchers moving into industry evidence roles.

The roles this audience is entering include MSLs, medical information and medical communication specialists, medical affairs managers and directors, publication and drug-safety professionals, HEOR and market-access professionals, and doctorally credentialed clinicians moving into evidence roles.

## 3. Eligibility

Two eligibility routes, consistent with common credentialing practice for certifications of persons:

- **Credentialed route:** a doctoral or professional clinical or scientific degree (for example MD, DO, MBBS, PharmD, PhD, DNP, NP, PA, BDS, DDS, DVM), or
- **Experience route:** a bachelor's degree plus two years of relevant professional experience in healthcare, life sciences, or a related field.

The certification examination and the portfolio, not the entry route, determine the credential.

## 4. Design principles

1. **Role readiness and method fluency together.** Medical affairs operations and HEOR/market-access methods are taught in one curriculum so that a candidate can both operate in a field-medical or medical-information role and construct or critique the evidence and value arguments that those roles increasingly require.
2. **Evidence generation as a spine, not a topic.** Integrated evidence generation planning (IEGP) is treated as a core competency with hands-on deliverables. Planning evidence across the lifecycle, identifying data gaps, and coordinating real-world evidence with HEOR are developed as connected skills rather than isolated topics.
3. **Responsible AI throughout, with governance.** AI methods are integrated into each relevant module and are governed by a single AI-use policy that requires validation, source verification, privacy and confidentiality controls, disclosure, and documented human accountability. The policy is grounded in current regulatory thinking on AI (see the AI governance module and `governance/ai_use_policy_and_playbook.md`).
4. **Work products, not only test items.** Candidates build a portfolio of deliverables that resemble first-year industry outputs, assessed with analytic rubrics. This addresses the gap between passing a knowledge examination and being able to produce usable industry work.
5. **Transparent, source-backed content.** Every factual teaching claim drawn from an external source carries a hyperlinked citation to an authoritative primary source, so learners can trace and verify content and can model the same evidence discipline in their own work.
6. **Compliance by construction.** Nonpromotional exchange, fair balance, and separation of medical and commercial functions are taught early and reinforced in every applied exercise.

## 5. Competency framework

The BCMAEP competency framework defines seven domains, derived from the responsibilities documented across medical affairs and evidence roles (see the [market alignment analysis](market_alignment/medical_affairs_responsibilities_summary.md)). Domains A through E map to the 15 instructional modules; domains F and G are cross-cutting and are assessed primarily through the portfolio and the professional-practice module.

| Domain | Title | Primary modules | Exam weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Industry orientation and translation | 1, 2, 3 | 15% |
| B | Scientific and clinical foundations | 4, 5, 6 | 20% |
| C | Evidence generation, HEOR, HTA, and market access | 7, 8, 9, 10 | 30% |
| D | Scientific communication and field medical | 11, 12, 13 | 20% |
| E | AI, data, and professional practice | 14, 15 | 15% |
| F | Medical governance and compliance | Cross-cutting; assessed in every module and the portfolio | Embedded |
| G | Strategy, leadership, and evidence-based decision making | Cross-cutting; assessed in the capstone portfolio | Embedded |

Domain C, which covers evidence generation, HEOR, HTA, and market access, carries the largest examination weight (30%), reflecting the program's emphasis on evidence and value fluency.

## 7. Learning model

- **Self-paced online delivery** with a defined module sequence, so learners can work through the curriculum on their own schedule while following the intended order.
- **Each module contains:** learning objectives mapped to competencies; a reading and multimedia set with cited primary sources; a worked example or case; one or more applied activities that produce a draft work product; and a knowledge check. The required applied deliverable in each module is what carries a learner's work forward into the capstone portfolio.
- **Case library:** a shared set of anonymized and public-source cases (rare-disease development, complex innovative trial designs, HTA submissions, KOL and evidence planning) reused across modules so learners see the same product move through development, evidence generation, communication, and access. Cases draw on regulator-authored and society-authored public materials (see `assessment/case_library.md`).
- **AI-enabled practice:** each applied activity specifies where AI assistance is permitted, what must be verified by the learner, and what must be recorded in an AI-use log, per the program AI-use policy.

## 8. Assessment and the credential

Two components must both be passed to earn BCMAEP:

1. **Board examination.** A proctored, multiple-choice examination of 150 scored items plus 15 unscored pilot items, drawn from a blueprint weighted to the domains in section 5. The examination tests broad application across the domains rather than recall of a single course, consistent with the defining feature of a certification (as distinct from a certificate of completion): the assessment is independent of any specific learning event. Candidates are allowed up to four attempts. The passing standard is set by a criterion-referenced standard-setting method (modified Angoff), not a fixed percentage. The full blueprint and sample items are in `assessment/exam_blueprint_and_sample_items.md`.
2. **Capstone portfolio.** A set of eight work products assessed against analytic rubrics by trained reviewers, described in `assessment/capstone_portfolio_and_rubrics.md`. The portfolio is the component that most distinguishes BCMAEP from a knowledge-only certification.

The credential is awarded when both components are passed and the eligibility route is verified.

## 9. Accreditation and quality alignment

BCMAEP is designed to be accreditation-ready. It is not accredited at version 1.0; the design deliberately aligns to the standards that certification and continuing-education bodies apply, so that a sponsoring organization can pursue accreditation without redesign. Alignment is documented in `governance/accreditation_and_quality_alignment.md` and summarized here.

A precise distinction matters. Two different kinds of accreditation apply to this field, and they are often conflated:

- **Continuing-education (provider) accreditation** attests that a training organization designs and delivers instruction to a quality standard. The ANSI/IACET 1-2018 Standard for continuing education and training is of this kind; under it, one CEU equals ten contact hours of structured learning ([IACET, ANSI/IACET Standard](https://www.iacet.org/standards/ansi-iacet-2018-1-standard-for-continuing-education-and-training/)).
- **Personnel-certification accreditation** attests that a credential validly and reliably measures a person's competency, independent of any course. ISO/IEC 17024:2012 is the international standard for bodies operating certification of persons ([ISO/IEC 17024:2012](https://www.iso.org/standard/52993.html)), and the National Commission for Certifying Agencies (NCCA) Standards, administered by the Institute for Credentialing Excellence, are the US reference; the 2021 NCCA revision contains 23 standards and has been applied to more than 315 accredited programs ([Institute for Credentialing Excellence, NCCA](https://www.credentialingexcellence.org/accreditation/earn-accreditation/ncca)).

BCMAEP targets both: IACET-aligned instructional design for the curriculum, and, for the credential itself, alignment to ISO/IEC 17024 and the NCCA Standards. Both personnel-certification frameworks require the competency assessment to be independent of the learning event, which is why BCMAEP's examination is blueprinted independently of the modules and its passing standard is set by a criterion-referenced method rather than a course grade.

A certificate program recognizes completion of a specific learning event and is accredited under the ICE 1100 Standard; a certification recognizes competency through an assessment that is independent of any specific learning event and is accredited under the NCCA Standards. BCMAEP is designed as a certification: the credential rests on the independent examination and the portfolio, and the curriculum is the recommended, but not the only, route to competency.

## 10. Recertification

The credential is valid for three years. Recertification requires 30 hours of documented continuing professional development relevant to the domains, of which at least 6 hours must address evidence generation or HEOR and at least 3 hours must address AI governance or data ethics, plus annual attestation to the code of professional conduct. The explicit evidence and AI-governance minimums are included because those fields change quickly and require deliberate maintenance.

## 11. What distinguishes BCMAEP

The design combines elements that are often taught separately into one applied, assessed curriculum. Six features define it:

| Feature | How BCMAEP implements it |
|---|---|
| Integrated scope | Medical affairs operations and HEOR, HTA, and market access methods are taught in one curriculum rather than as separate offerings, so a candidate can both operate in a role and construct or critique the evidence and value arguments those roles require. |
| Evidence generation depth | Domain C (evidence generation, HEOR, HTA, and market access) carries the largest examination weight (30%), with dedicated modules on real-world evidence, economic evaluation, and HTA. |
| Integrated evidence generation planning | A dedicated module (Module 10) and the signature capstone deliverable develop lifecycle evidence planning as a core competency, not a subtopic. |
| Governed AI throughout | AI methods are woven into each relevant module under a single AI-use policy requiring validation, source verification, privacy controls, disclosure, and documented human accountability, and are audited in the capstone. |
| Required work-product portfolio | Eight rubric-scored deliverables that resemble first-year industry outputs, so the credential reflects the ability to produce usable work, not only to answer questions. |
| Dual accreditation target | The design aligns the curriculum to the ANSI/IACET Standard and the credential itself to ISO/IEC 17024 and the NCCA Standards for certification of persons (section 9). |

The unifying aim is integrated working competency across medical affairs and evidence generation, validated by one examination and one portfolio, with the depth in each area calibrated to a first industry role.

## 12. Positioning statement

BCMAEP is a job-realistic readiness certification that helps clinicians and other professionals translate clinical and scientific expertise into industry work products across medical affairs and evidence generation. Its defining features are integrated medical affairs plus HEOR and market access coverage in one curriculum, a dedicated integrated evidence generation planning competency, an explicit and governed approach to AI, a required work-product portfolio, and transparent source-backed content built on primary authoritative sources.
