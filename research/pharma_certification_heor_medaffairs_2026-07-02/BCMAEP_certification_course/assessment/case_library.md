# BCMAEP Case Library

This library provides the shared cases used across modules and the capstone. Cases are built on public, primarily regulator-authored and society-authored materials so that learners work from real, traceable evidence. Each case entry states the source, the modules it serves, the exercise, and the licensing note.

Reuse note: public availability does not grant republication rights. Cases below are used as assigned readings with hyperlinked citations and as prompts for original learner work. Do not copy substantial third-party text or slides into learner-facing materials; link to the source and have learners produce original analysis.

---

## Anchor case A: Rare-disease development and regulatory strategy

Primary sources: the FDA CDER LEADER 3D case series, a set of regulator-authored teaching cases on rare-disease drug development. The series includes odevixibat ([FDA LEADER 3D odevixibat case](https://www.fda.gov/media/186133/download?attachment=)), vutrisiran ([FDA LEADER 3D vutrisiran case](https://www.fda.gov/media/186134/download?attachment=)), tofersen ([FDA LEADER 3D tofersen case](https://www.fda.gov/media/186135/download?attachment=)), avalglucosidase alfa and seladelpar ([FDA LEADER 3D case](https://www.fda.gov/media/186136/download?attachment=)), fosdenopterin ([FDA LEADER 3D fosdenopterin case](https://www.fda.gov/media/185752/download?attachment=)), and olipudase alfa ([FDA LEADER 3D olipudase alfa case](https://www.fda.gov/media/185753/download?attachment=)), with a program user guide ([FDA LEADER 3D user guide](https://www.fda.gov/media/185425/download?attachment=)) and hub page ([FDA, LEADER 3D program](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/accelerating-rare-disease-cures-arc-program/learning-and-education-advance-and-empower-rare-disease-drug-developers-leader-3d)).

Serves: Module 5 (regulatory strategy), Module 4 (endpoints and evidence), Module 10 (evidence planning), capstone item 4.

Exercise: for one assigned drug, map the evidence to the applicable pathway (for example accelerated approval or a single adequate and well-controlled study plus confirmatory evidence), identify endpoint and data limitations, and list the FDA-interaction points a development team would prepare for. FDA states these cases are not product-approval strategies and that rare-disease development is assessed case by case ([FDA, LEADER 3D program](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/accelerating-rare-disease-cures-arc-program/learning-and-education-advance-and-empower-rare-disease-drug-developers-leader-3d)); learners must treat each as a teaching scenario, not a template.

Licensing: US federal government works; cite FDA and preserve the FDA caveat.

## Anchor case B: Complex innovative trial design

Primary sources: the FDA Complex Innovative Trial Design (CID) paired-meeting program, including an external- and synthetic-control case in alopecia areata ([FDA CID alopecia external-control case](https://www.fda.gov/media/188560/download?attachment=)) and a master-protocol case in chronic pain ([FDA CID master-protocol case](https://www.fda.gov/media/155403/download?attachment=)), linked from the program page ([FDA, Complex Innovative Trial Design Meeting Program](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-resources/complex-innovative-trial-design-meeting-program)).

Serves: Module 4 (adaptive, Bayesian, master-protocol, external-control designs), Module 7 (external and synthetic controls).

Exercise: critique whether the disclosed design assumptions are defensible, identify the simulation scenarios a sponsor should run, and translate the FDA-disclosed design categories into a sponsor briefing-package outline. FDA notes each case focuses on the specific submitted design and does not cover the full development-program evidence needs ([FDA, Complex Innovative Trial Design Meeting Program](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-resources/complex-innovative-trial-design-meeting-program)).

Licensing: US federal government works; cite FDA and preserve the scope caveat.

## Anchor case C: Clinical-trial classification drill

Primary source: the NIH clinical-trial definition case studies ([NIH, Clinical Trial Definition Case Studies](https://grants.nih.gov/policy/clinical-trials/CT-Definition-Case-Studies_1-4-18.pdf)).

Serves: Module 4 (design classification), Module 1 (development milestones).

Exercise: for each scenario, decide whether the study meets the clinical-trial definition before revealing the NIH answer, then discuss implications for protocol writing, registration, and regulatory documentation. This is a policy-interpretation teaching resource; adapt scenarios to product-development contexts.

Licensing: US federal government work; cite NIH.

## Anchor case D: HTA and value assessment across jurisdictions

Primary sources: the ICER guide to understanding HTA ([ICER, Guide to Understanding HTA](https://icer.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/ICER-Guide-to-Understanding-Health-Technology-Assessment-6.19.18.pdf)); the NICE health technology evaluations manual ([NICE, health technology evaluations manual, PMG36](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/pmg36/resources/nice-technology-appraisal-and-highly-specialised-technologies-guidance-the-manual-pdf-72286779244741)); the iDSI HTA toolkit ([iDSI HTA Toolkit](https://f1000research.com/documents/8-703/pdf)); and, for a worked payer submission and outcome, the Australian PBAC guidelines and a public summary document ([PBAC, Guidelines for preparing a submission, version 5](https://pbac.pbs.gov.au/content/information/files/pbac-guidelines-version-5.pdf); [PBAC, Public Summary Document, pembrolizumab early triple-negative breast cancer, 2023](https://www.pbs.gov.au/industry/pbac/psd/2023/03/pembrolizumab-early-tnbc-psd-03-2023.pdf?variant=3)).

Serves: Module 9 (HTA and market access), Module 8 (economic evaluation inputs), capstone item 6.

Exercise: build an evidence-submission outline for one product for a chosen jurisdiction (for example NICE in England, ICER in the US, or PBAC in Australia), then identify what would change for a second jurisdiction. The PBAC public summary document lets learners see how a committee actually weighed the clinical and economic evidence and where it identified uncertainty. Note that ICER, NICE, PBAC, and iDSI describe jurisdiction-specific methods that should not be generalized across all HTA bodies.

Licensing: ICER, NICE, and PBAC content is governed by their respective rights; cite and do not reproduce substantial text. The iDSI toolkit states its resource list is not exhaustive or endorsed; use it as a framework.

## Anchor case E: Formulary and payer submission

Primary sources: the AMCP Format for Formulary Submissions, version 5.0, and its pre- and post-approval information-exchange guidance ([AMCP, Format for Formulary Submissions guidance](https://www.amcp.org/resource/amcp-format-formulary-submissions-guidance); [AMCP, Format for Formulary Submissions 5.0 (PDF)](https://www.amcp.org/sites/default/files/2024-04/AMCP-Format-5.0-JMCP-web_0.pdf); [AMCP, pre-approval and post-approval clinical and economic evidence guidance](https://www.amcp.org/resource/amcp-format-formulary-submissions-guidance-submission-pre-approval-and-post-approval-clinical-and)).

Serves: Module 9 (payer submission), Module 3 (pre-approval information exchange), capstone item 6.

Exercise: assemble a payer value dossier outline using the AMCP Format 5.0 structure, distinguishing pre-approval and post-approval communication and mapping where clinical, economic, budget-impact, and real-world evidence sections go.

Licensing: AMCP content; cite AMCP and do not reproduce the format document.

## Anchor case K: Reimbursement and site-of-care strategy across modalities

Primary sources: the NIH SEED reimbursement and regulatory teaching cases, a public set of fictionalized product scenarios developed by NIH to teach how development decisions connect to coverage, coding, payment, and evidence. The set spans a small-molecule drug ([NIH SEED, drug reimbursement case 1, small molecule](https://seed.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2023-12/Drug-Reimbursement-Case-Study-1-Small-Molecule.pdf)), a provider-administered injectable ([NIH SEED, drug reimbursement, physician-administered](https://seed.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2023-12/Drug-Reimbursement-Case-Study-Physician-Administered.pdf)), a self-administered nasal spray ([NIH SEED, drug reimbursement, nasal spray](https://seed.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2024-04/Drug-Reimbursement-Case-Study-3.pdf)), a regulatory development pathway ([NIH SEED, drug regulatory case, ChroKi Biosciences](https://seed.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2024-01/Drug-Regulatory-Case-Study_0.pdf)), a digital decision-support device ([NIH SEED, device reimbursement case](https://seed.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2023-09/Device-Reimbursement-Case-Study-2.pdf)), and a diagnostic test ([NIH SEED, diagnostic reimbursement case](https://seed.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2023-12/Diagnostic-Reimbursement-Case-Study-1.pdf)).

Serves: Module 1 and Module 2 (site of care and lifecycle), Module 5 (the regulatory case), Module 9 (reimbursement and market access), capstone items 5 and 6.

Exercise: for one assigned case, distinguish pharmacy-benefit from medical-benefit reimbursement, identify the coding and payment pathway implied by the site of care, and translate the clinical value into payer-facing evidence assumptions; the regulatory case is used to trace an accelerated-approval and surrogate-endpoint strategy. These are fictionalized NIH educational scenarios, not real products.

Licensing: public NIH educational material; cite NIH SEED and do not reproduce slide figures verbatim without confirming reuse terms.

## Anchor case L: Model-critique and value elements

Primary source: the PBAC public summary document for a cost-effectiveness review of biologics in chronic plaque psoriasis, a worked committee assessment showing stepped economic-evaluation logic and subgroup thresholds in a crowded therapeutic category ([PBAC, biologics for chronic plaque psoriasis cost-effectiveness review, 2020](https://www.pbs.gov.au/industry/pbac/psd/2020/07/biologics-for-the-treatment-of-chronic-plaque-psoriasis-cost-effectiveness-review-psd-july-2020.pdf?variant=3)).

Serves: Module 8 (economic evaluation and critique), Module 9 (HTA).

Exercise: critique the model inputs, comparator choice, subgroup thresholds, and sources of committee uncertainty; state what additional evidence would most change the assessment. Public-summary detail only, Australian context.

Licensing: PBAC public document; cite and do not reproduce substantial text.

## Anchor case F: Pharmacoeconomic critique and communication

Primary sources: the CHEERS 2022 reporting standard and the Second Panel on Cost-Effectiveness reference case (see `references/source_register.md`), and the economic-evaluation methods developed in Module 8. The scenario uses illustrative inputs and is not drawn from a specific product.

Serves: Module 8 (economic evaluation and critique).

Exercise: critique a published economic evaluation against the CHEERS reporting standard, then write a plain-language value memo. Learners produce original critiques.

Licensing: the case uses illustrative inputs; any published economic evaluation a learner selects to critique should be cited to its source.

Licensing: vendor white papers; cite and do not reproduce; use for workflow realism.

## Anchor case H: Scientific communication and nonpromotional abstracts

Primary sources: the ISPOR HEOR impact-case abstract example ([ISPOR, HEOR impact-case abstract example](https://www.ispor.org/docs/default-source/ispor-eu-2025/impact-cases-abstract-example.pdf?sfvrsn=1dfb3b54_3)) and the ISPOR workshop proposal example ([ISPOR, workshop proposal example](https://www.ispor.org/docs/default-source/ispor-eu-2025/workshop-proposal-example_2025.pdf?sfvrsn=7bdad0f5_1)), governed by ISPOR nonpromotional submission rules.

Serves: Module 12 (publications and congresses), capstone item 7.

Exercise: convert an internal HEOR project into a nonpromotional impact-case abstract and a workshop proposal, using generic technology names and no promotional language, as ISPOR requires.

Licensing: ISPOR examples; cite and enforce the nonpromotional standard in learner work.

## Anchor case I: Medical affairs strategy and metrics

Primary sources: the MAPS "Converting Challenges Into Opportunities" workshop slides ([MAPS, Challenges to Opportunities](https://medicalaffairs.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Challenges-to-Opportunities.pdf)); the MAPS/Amedea medical affairs innovation and metrics slides ([MAPS/Amedea, innovation and metrics webinar](https://medicalaffairs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/FINAL-SLIDES-MAPS-Webinar-Mar-10-Amedea-Pharma-Final-Updated-for-download14-Read-Only.pdf)); and the MAPS launch-excellence masterclass brochure ([MAPS, launch-excellence masterclass brochure](https://medicalaffairs.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/MAPS-MasterClass-Singapore-English-Brochure-5.13-min.pdf)).

Serves: Module 2 (operating model and metrics), Module 10 (launch evidence planning), Module 15 (strategy).

Exercise: build a scientific communication platform for a small company with limited data and budget; critique activity-versus-outcome metrics and redesign a KPI logic for field medical or medical information. Learners critique metrics rather than accept them as validated standards.

Licensing: MAPS materials; cite and do not repackage slides.

## Anchor case J: Capstone product scenario

A synthetic product scenario constructed by the program (not drawn from a single proprietary source) that the learner carries through all capstone deliverables. The scenario specifies a therapeutic area, a target product profile, an evidence base with deliberate gaps, and a set of stakeholder evidence needs. It is designed so that the learner must integrate the anchor cases above: regulatory strategy (case A style), evidence design (cases B and C), HEOR and HTA (cases D, E, F), field and communication (cases G, H, I), and an AI-use log throughout.

Serves: the full capstone (all items).

Licensing: original program material; no third-party reuse constraints.
