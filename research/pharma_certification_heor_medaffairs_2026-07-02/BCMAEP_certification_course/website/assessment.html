BCMAEP Board Examination: Blueprint and Sample Items

On this page

  2. 2. Domain blueprint

  4. 4. Item-writing rules
  5. 5. Standard setting and scoring

Companion to Program handbook (section 8) and the competency framework (section 5). This document specifies the examination structure, the domain and cognitive-level blueprint, item-writing rules, the standard-setting method, and a set of sample items with answer rationales. Sample items are illustrative; a live examination requires a formal item bank, pilot testing, and psychometric review.

1. Examination structure

  • Format: proctored, multiple-choice (single best answer), delivered online with remote or center-based proctoring.
  • Length: 150 scored items plus 15 unscored pilot items (165 total).
  • Time: 3 hours.
  • Retakes: up to four attempts.
  • Scope: broad application across the domains, independent of any single module, consistent with the defining feature of a certification examination: it assesses competency independently of any specific learning event.

The examination is one of two required components; the capstone portfolio is the other (Capstone portfolio and rubrics).

2. Domain blueprint

Scored items are distributed by the exam weights in the handbook competency framework.

Domain Content Weight Scored items
A Industry orientation and translation 15% 22
B Scientific and clinical foundations 20% 30
C Evidence generation, HEOR, HTA, market access 30% 45
D Scientific communication and field medical 20% 30
E AI, data, and professional practice 15% 23
Total 100% 150

Domains F (governance and compliance) and G (strategy and decision making) are not separate blocks; compliance and decision-making items are embedded within A to E and tagged so the blueprint can confirm that at least 15% of scored items across the examination assess a compliance or ethics judgment and at least 10% assess an evidence-based decision.

3. Cognitive-level blueprint

Items are classified by cognitive demand so the examination is not recall-dominated.

Level Description Target share
Recall Define or identify a fact or term 25%
Application Apply a concept to a described situation 45%
Analysis and judgment Interpret data, weigh trade-offs, choose a defensible action 30%

Domain C and the embedded decision items are weighted toward application and analysis; a candidate who has memorized definitions but cannot interpret a cost-effectiveness result or a compliance scenario should not pass.

4. Item-writing rules

  • One clearly best answer; distractors are plausible and homogeneous.
  • Clinical, evidence, and compliance scenarios are drawn from realistic industry situations and, where possible, adapted from the public case library.
  • No trick items, double negatives, or "all of the above."
  • Statistical and economic items provide the data needed to reason; they do not require memorized formulas beyond those taught.
  • Compliance items test the boundary between promotional and nonpromotional activity and the correct escalation, not memorized statute numbers.
  • Each item is tagged to a domain, a competency, a cognitive level, and a source of truth (a cited guideline, standard, or primary source).

5. Standard setting and scoring

  • Passing standard is criterion-referenced, set by a modified-Angoff panel of subject-matter experts who estimate, for each item, the probability that a minimally competent candidate answers correctly. The sum of those probabilities defines the cut score.
  • Scores are reported as pass or fail with domain-level performance feedback for candidates who do not pass, so retakes can be targeted.
  • The cut score is reviewed after each item-bank refresh and after any major curriculum update.

6. Sample items with rationales

The following 12 items illustrate the blueprint across domains and cognitive levels. Each shows the domain, competency, cognitive level, the correct answer, and a rationale with a cited source of truth.

Sample item 1 · Domain A, application

A newly hired MSL is asked by a sales representative to share unpublished efficacy data with a prescriber who is a high-volume customer, to support the brand ahead of a formulary review. What is the most appropriate response?

Rationale

MSL scientific exchange is nonpromotional and reactive to unsolicited requests; proactive sharing to support a formulary decision at a sales representative's request crosses the promotional boundary and violates the separation of medical and commercial roles (Module 3). Omitting safety information (D) also violates fair balance.

Sample item 2 · Domain B, analysis

A phase III trial reports a hazard ratio for overall survival of 0.82 (95% confidence interval 0.66 to 1.02). Which statement is best supported?

Rationale

the confidence interval includes 1.0, so the null of no difference is not excluded; the point estimate (0.82) favors treatment but the result is not statistically significant at the conventional threshold, and absence of significance is not proof of no effect (Module 4).

Sample item 3 · Domain B, application

A rare-disease product received accelerated approval based on a surrogate endpoint. What is the sponsor's principal post-approval evidence obligation?

Rationale

accelerated approval based on a surrogate reasonably likely to predict benefit carries an obligation to confirm clinical benefit in post-approval studies; the LEADER 3D cases illustrate this evidence structure (FDA, LEADER 3D program) (Module 5).

Sample item 4 · Domain C, analysis

An intervention has an incremental cost of USD 45,000 and an incremental gain of 0.6 quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) versus standard care. What is the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER), and how should it be interpreted against a USD 100,000 per QALY reference?

Rationale

ICER = 45,000 / 0.6 = USD 75,000 per QALY, which is below a USD 100,000 per QALY reference and would typically be considered cost-effective at that threshold, subject to uncertainty analysis (Module 8). Thresholds are decision aids, not absolute rules (ICER, Guide to Understanding HTA).

Sample item 5 · Domain C, application

A team wants to use a real-world external control arm to support a single-arm trial in a rare cancer. Which is the most important early feasibility question?

Rationale

the validity of an external control depends on comparability of the control population and consistent outcome ascertainment; without these, confounding and misclassification undermine the comparison (Module 7). This is the central issue in the FDA external-control case material (FDA, Complex Innovative Trial Design Meeting Program).

Sample item 6 · Domain C, analysis and judgment

A budget-impact analysis and a cost-effectiveness analysis give different signals: the product is cost-effective at the willingness-to-pay threshold but has a large budget impact. What does this most likely indicate to a payer?

Rationale

cost-effectiveness (value per unit of health gained) and budget impact (total affordability) answer different questions; a product can be cost-effective yet unaffordable at scale, and payers weigh both (Module 8, Module 9).

Sample item 7 · Domain C, application

In an integrated evidence generation plan, what is the primary purpose of an evidence-gap assessment?

Rationale

an evidence-gap assessment compares required evidence (across regulators, HTA and payers, clinicians, and patients) with available and planned evidence, and it drives prioritization of evidence questions in the plan (Module 10).

Sample item 8 · Domain D, application

Under Good Publication Practice (GPP 2022), which statement about the use of a large language model in preparing a manuscript is correct?

Rationale

publication-ethics standards hold that AI tools cannot be authors because they cannot take responsibility for the work; use must be disclosed and human authors remain accountable for verifying content (Module 12, Module 14). See the program source register for the GPP 2022 and ICMJE citations.

Sample item 9 · Domain D, judgment

An advisory board is proposed. Which design element most clearly keeps it nonpromotional and compliant?

Rationale

a compliant advisory board is convened for genuine advisory need, has clear scientific objectives and documented outputs, and compensates at fair market value; a predetermined conclusion or prescriber-based selection signals promotion or inducement (Module 13).

Sample item 10 · Domain E, application

A medical information specialist wants to use a generative AI tool to draft a standard response document from the published literature. Which practice is required by the program AI-use policy?

Rationale

the AI-use policy requires source verification, privacy and confidentiality controls, and documented human accountability; unverified AI output and untraceable claims are prohibited (Module 14, AI-use policy).

Sample item 11 · Domain E, analysis

Why is source verification especially important when using a large language model for literature work?

Rationale

generative models can produce plausible but false references and statements; the mitigating control is verification against primary sources, which is why the curriculum requires a citation-to-source check on all AI-assisted outputs (Module 14).

Sample item 12 · Domain A/F embedded, judgment

During pre-approval, a payer requests health-economic information about an investigational product. Which is the most appropriate action?

Rationale

pre-approval information exchange with payers is permitted within defined guardrails: truthful, nonmisleading, clearly investigational, and consistent with the applicable guidance (AMCP, pre-approval and post-approval clinical and economic evidence guidance) (Module 3, Module 9). Promotion of an unapproved product (C) is not permitted.

7. Form assembly and quality control

  • Each live form is assembled to match the domain and cognitive blueprints within tolerance.
  • Items are reviewed for bias and sensitivity and pilot-tested as unscored items before scoring.
  • Item statistics (difficulty, discrimination) are monitored; poorly performing items are revised or retired.
  • The blueprint and item bank are reviewed at least annually and after any major change to guidance or standards, with particular attention to the fast-moving AI and evidence domains.
