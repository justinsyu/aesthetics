BCMAEP Accreditation and Quality Alignment

This document maps the BCMAEP design to the standards that certification and continuing-education bodies apply, so a sponsoring organization can pursue accreditation without redesign. BCMAEP is not accredited at version 1.0; it is built to be accreditation-ready. See also the handbook, section 9.

1. Two kinds of accreditation, kept distinct

The field conflates two different accreditations. Keeping them distinct is itself part of the design.

  • Continuing-education (provider) accreditation certifies that a training organization designs and delivers instruction to a quality standard. The ANSI/IACET 1-2018 Standard for continuing education and training is of this kind; under it, one CEU equals ten contact hours of structured learning, and providers reapply every five years (IACET, ANSI/IACET 1-2018 Standard).
  • Personnel-certification accreditation certifies that a credential validly and reliably measures individual competency, independent of any course. ISO/IEC 17024:2012 is the international standard for bodies operating certification of persons (ISO/IEC 17024:2012); the NCCA Standards administered by the Institute for Credentialing Excellence are the US reference, with 23 standards in the 2021 revision and more than 315 accredited programs (Institute for Credentialing Excellence, NCCA).

BCMAEP targets both kinds of accreditation: IACET-aligned instructional design for the curriculum, and ISO/IEC 17024- and NCCA-aligned certification for the credential itself. Pursuing personnel-certification accreditation (ISO/IEC 17024, NCCA), in addition to provider-level continuing-education accreditation, is a deliberate design choice so that the credential is validated as a measure of competency independent of the curriculum.

2. Design features that follow from ISO/IEC 17024 and NCCA

Both personnel-certification frameworks share requirements that shaped the BCMAEP design:

  • Independence of assessment from training. The examination is blueprinted from a competency framework, not from the modules, and can be passed by a candidate who learned the material elsewhere. This is why the handbook separates the PRIME curriculum (the learning program) from the BCMAEP credential (the assessment).
  • Job-relevant competency basis. The competency framework (handbook, section 5) is derived from documented industry practice, including the responsibilities catalogued in the market alignment analysis. A formal job-task analysis would be conducted before accreditation to confirm the blueprint weights.
  • Criterion-referenced standard setting. The passing standard is set by a modified-Angoff panel, not a fixed percentage or a curve, so the standard reflects minimally competent performance (see Examination blueprint).
  • Psychometric quality control. Items are piloted as unscored, monitored for difficulty and discrimination, and reviewed for bias; forms are assembled to the blueprint.
  • Impartiality and governance. An accredited body would separate the exam-development and appeals functions from the training-delivery function and would document conflict-of-interest controls.
  • Recertification. A defined three-year recertification cycle with continuing-education requirements maintains currency (handbook, section 10).

3. Design features that follow from IACET

For the PRIME curriculum:

  • Learning objectives are measurable and mapped to competencies (see Curriculum and syllabus).
  • Instructional design includes assessment of learning at the module level (knowledge checks) and at the program level (the board exam and portfolio).
  • Contact hours are defined per module (handbook, section 6), supporting CEU calculation at the IACET ratio of ten contact hours per CEU (IACET, About the CEU).
  • Content is reviewed and updated on a defined cycle, with particular attention to the AI and evidence domains.

4. The certificate-versus-certification distinction

The Institute for Credentialing Excellence distinguishes a certificate program (which recognizes mastery of specific learning outcomes and is assessed in connection with a learning event, accredited under the ICE 1100 Standard) from a certification program (which recognizes previously acquired competency through an assessment independent of a specific learning event, accredited under the NCCA Standards). BCMAEP is designed as a certification: the credential rests on the independent board examination and the portfolio, and the PRIME curriculum is the recommended, but not the only, route to competency.

5. Roadmap to accreditation

A sponsoring organization pursuing accreditation would, in order: establish an independent certification governance body; conduct a formal job-task analysis to confirm the blueprint; develop and pilot a full item bank with psychometric review; conduct a formal standard-setting study; document the management system and impartiality controls; and apply to the relevant body (NCCA or an ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation body such as the ANSI National Accreditation Board) for the credential and to IACET for the training program. The version 1.0 materials in this package are structured to feed each of these steps.

6. Honest statement of current status

At version 1.0, BCMAEP is a fully specified, accreditation-ready design with complete curriculum, an examination blueprint with sample items, a portfolio with rubrics, and a governed AI-use policy. It has not undergone job-task analysis validation, item-bank piloting, formal standard setting, or accreditation review. Claims about the credential should reflect this status: it is designed to meet ISO/IEC 17024 and NCCA expectations and to be delivered by an IACET-aligned provider, and it is not yet accredited.
