BCMAEP Capstone Portfolio and Rubrics

Companion to Program handbook (section 8) and Curriculum and syllabus (capstone). The capstone is the second required component of the credential and the feature that most distinguishes BCMAEP from a knowledge-only certification. Candidates build a portfolio of eight work products around one assigned product scenario (case J in Case library) and present an executive summary. Reviewers score each work product against the analytic rubric below.

1. Purpose and principle

The board examination confirms breadth of knowledge. The portfolio confirms that a candidate can produce usable, compliant industry work. The two together support the claim that a BCMAEP holder is ready to contribute in a first industry role, not only to answer questions about one.

The portfolio is built on the applied deliverables produced across modules, refined and integrated around a single case so the work products are internally consistent (the same product, evidence base, and stakeholders run through all eight).

2. Required work products

Plus a capstone executive summary (Module 15) that presents the portfolio to a simulated cross-functional leadership audience, delivered as a written brief and a five-minute recorded or scripted presentation.

3. Submission requirements

  • All eight work products plus the executive summary, submitted as a single portfolio referencing one assigned product scenario.
  • Every factual claim drawn from an external source carries a hyperlinked citation to a verifiable authoritative source. Unsupported factual claims are marked and penalized.
  • The AI-use log (work product 8) documents every material AI contribution: the tool, the task, what was verified and how, and who is accountable. A portfolio that used AI without a log is returned.
  • No confidential or proprietary third-party material is included; all cases use public or program-provided sources.
  • The scenario is synthetic (case J), so no real patient data is involved.

4. Analytic rubric

Each work product is scored on five dimensions, each on a 0 to 4 scale. A work product passes at a total of 14 of 20 with no dimension below 2. The full portfolio passes when all eight work products pass and the executive summary passes.

Dimension 0 to 1 (below standard) 2 (approaching) 3 (meets) 4 (exceeds)
Technical accuracy Contains material errors of fact or method Some errors, none disqualifying Accurate; methods correctly applied Accurate with sophisticated, well-justified choices
Compliance and ethics Crosses the promotional or privacy boundary Minor compliance ambiguity unaddressed Nonpromotional, fair-balanced, privacy-aware Anticipates and resolves compliance risks proactively
Evidence and citation Claims unsupported or sources not verifiable Some claims uncited or weakly sourced Claims cited to verifiable authoritative sources Citations precise, primary, and critically weighed
Integration and strategy Disconnected from the scenario and other products Loosely connected Consistent with the scenario and other products Demonstrably integrated; trade-offs reasoned
Communication Unclear or inappropriate for the audience Understandable but not audience-adapted Clear and audience-appropriate Concise, persuasive, and decision-ready

5. Work-product specifications and specific criteria

Work product 1: Product situation brief and lifecycle map

Specification: a two-page brief describing the assigned product, its therapeutic area and unmet need, its development stage, and a lifecycle map marking evidence and access decision points.
Specific criteria: correct placement of development phases and milestones; identification of where medical affairs and evidence functions attach; realistic unmet-need framing without promotional language.

Work product 2: Compliance decision memo

Specification: a memo classifying a set of six proposed activities (provided with the scenario) as promotional, nonpromotional, or requiring medical-legal-regulatory review, each with rationale and the correct escalation.
Specific criteria: correct classification; correct application of fair balance, unsolicited-request handling, and pre-approval information-exchange limits; correct privacy handling.

Work product 3: Critical appraisal and evidence table

Specification: a structured appraisal of one assigned pivotal publication plus an evidence table summarizing design, population, endpoints, results, and limitations.
Specific criteria: correct interpretation of effect sizes and uncertainty; identification of bias and confounding; accurate extraction with values traceable to the source; a plain-language summary suitable for internal use.

Work product 4: Regulatory strategy brief

Specification: a brief mapping the product's evidence to an applicable regulatory pathway (for example accelerated approval or orphan pathway), with agency-interaction points and evidence risks.
Specific criteria: correct pathway logic; realistic identification of evidence gaps and confirmatory obligations; correct use of the rare-disease evidence concepts from the LEADER 3D cases.

Work product 5: Integrated evidence generation plan

Specification: an evidence-gap assessment and an integrated evidence matrix mapping prioritized evidence questions to study concepts, data sources, timing, owners, decision points, and impact metrics, aligned across global, regional, and local needs.
Specific criteria: stakeholder evidence needs correctly identified (regulators, HTA and payers, clinicians, patients); defensible prioritization; a matrix that is internally consistent and connects to work products 3, 4, and 6; defined impact metrics and a governance cadence. This is the signature deliverable and is weighted most heavily in the portfolio.

Work product 6: HTA or payer submission outline with cost-effectiveness summary

Specification: a submission outline for one jurisdiction plus a one-page summary of an introductory cost-effectiveness analysis (the spreadsheet model from Module 8) with a sensitivity analysis and a note on what changes for a second jurisdiction.
Specific criteria: correct value argument structure; correct interpretation of the ICER and uncertainty; realistic anticipation of committee concerns; appropriate use of the AMCP Format structure and a named HTA jurisdiction's methods.

Work product 7: KOL engagement plan and nonpromotional congress abstract

Specification: a compliant KOL engagement plan with segmentation, objectives, and an insight-capture template, plus a nonpromotional congress abstract in HEOR impact-case format.
Specific criteria: compliant, nonpromotional objectives; realistic insight questions tied to the evidence plan; an abstract that uses generic technology names and meets nonpromotional standards.

Work product 8: AI-use plan and audit log

Specification: a plan describing where AI was used across the portfolio and an audit log with, for each use, the tool, the task, the verification performed, the data-handling decision, and the accountable person; plus a short evaluation of one AI workflow for fitness, bias, and reproducibility.
Specific criteria: completeness and honesty of the log; correct application of the program AI-use policy; evidence that AI outputs were verified against primary sources; correct data-privacy decisions.

Capstone executive summary

Specification: a two-page executive brief and a five-minute presentation summarizing the product situation, the evidence strategy, the value and access argument, and the recommended next steps for a cross-functional leadership audience.
Specific criteria: synthesis across all eight work products; a clear, defensible recommendation; audience-appropriate communication; no promotional framing.

6. Reviewer calibration and integrity

  • Reviewers are trained and calibrated against anchor portfolios at each score point before scoring live submissions.
  • Each portfolio is independently scored by two reviewers; discrepancies beyond one point on any dimension are adjudicated by a third.
  • An academic-integrity check confirms original work; portfolios are screened for undisclosed reuse of third-party material and for undisclosed AI use, consistent with the disclosure requirement in the AI-use policy.
  • Candidates who do not pass receive dimension-level feedback and may revise and resubmit the failing work products within the enrollment window.

7. Why the portfolio matters

Certification examinations, by design, assess broadly and independently of a specific learning event. That is appropriate for confirming knowledge, but it does not confirm that a candidate can produce a compliant evidence plan, a defensible cost-effectiveness summary, or a nonpromotional abstract. The portfolio closes that gap by requiring the candidate to build the work products a first-year industry professional is expected to produce, assessed against explicit standards. This is the primary way BCMAEP aims to exceed knowledge-only certification.
