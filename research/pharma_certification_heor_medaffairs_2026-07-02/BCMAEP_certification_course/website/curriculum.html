Curriculum and syllabus

Fifteen modules in five instructional domains (A through E), plus two cross-cutting domains and a capstone portfolio. Approximately 90 to 110 hours of structured effort over a 6-to-12-month window. For each module: competencies, learning objectives, topic outline, applied deliverable, AI-use focus, and assessment linkage.

Program-level learning outcomes

On completion, a BCMAEP holder can:

  1. Describe the structure of the pharmaceutical, biotechnology, and medical-technology industry and locate medical affairs and evidence functions within the drug development and product lifecycle. (A, B)
  2. Operate within the compliance and ethics boundary that separates nonpromotional medical exchange from commercial promotion. (F)
  3. Critically appraise clinical and observational evidence, including trial design, statistics, and bias. (B)
  4. Explain regulatory approval pathways and the evidence standards that support them. (B)
  5. Distinguish and apply real-world data sources and real-world evidence methods. (C)
  6. Construct and critique HEOR analyses, including cost-effectiveness and budget-impact models and outcomes research. (C)
  7. Explain how HTA bodies and payers evaluate evidence and make coverage, pricing, and reimbursement decisions across jurisdictions. (C)
  8. Build an integrated evidence generation plan that aligns evidence questions, study concepts, stakeholders, and impact metrics across the lifecycle. (C, G)
  9. Plan and conduct compliant field-medical engagement, insight collection, scientific communication, publications, advisory boards, and medical information responses. (D, F)
  10. Use AI responsibly in medical affairs and evidence workflows, with validation, disclosure, privacy controls, and documented human accountability. (E, F)
  11. Produce industry-standard work products and communicate evidence and value to scientific, payer, and internal audiences. (D, G)

Module 1 · Domain A

The pharmaceutical, biotechnology, and medtech industry and the drug development lifecycle

Open module 1

Competencies: A, B. Approx. 5 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Map the phases of drug discovery and development from target identification through post-marketing, and place regulatory and evidence milestones on that map. (A, B)
  • Distinguish pharmaceutical, biologic, cell and gene therapy, device, diagnostic, and combination-product development pathways. (B)
  • Describe the roles of sponsors, contract research organizations, regulators, payers, health systems, and patients across the lifecycle. (A)
  • Explain how public research funding and private development connect in the therapeutic ecosystem. (A)

Topic outline: industry segments and business model; the development funnel and attrition; phases 0 to IV; investigational new drug and new drug/biologics license applications at a high level; modality-specific pathways (small molecule, biologic, cell and gene therapy, device, diagnostic, combination product); stakeholders and money flow; where medical affairs and evidence functions attach to each phase.

Applied deliverable: a one-page lifecycle map for an assigned case product, marking evidence-generation and access decision points.

AI-use focus: using AI to summarize a development landscape and generate a first-draft lifecycle map, then verifying every date and claim against primary sources.

Source anchors: FDA drug development and approval overview; NIH translation-of-research proceedings; EUPATI discovery-and-development overview; FDA model-informed drug development materials.

Module 2 · Domain A

The medical affairs operating model and cross-functional ecosystem

Open module 2

Competencies: A. Approx. 5 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Define the functions of medical affairs: field medical (MSL), medical information, publications, medical communications, HEOR, medical operations, and medical strategy. (A)
  • Explain the interfaces between medical affairs and clinical development, regulatory, safety, commercial, and market access, and the handoffs between them. (A)
  • Describe the medical affairs strategic planning cycle and the medical strategy or medical plan. (A, G)
  • Articulate the value and impact measures used for medical affairs functions and their limitations. (A, G)

Topic outline: the seven-domain competency view of medical affairs; medical affairs organizational structures (global, regional, affiliate); the medical plan and its inputs; interfaces and firewalls with commercial; activity versus outcome metrics; the evolving strategic role of medical affairs.

Applied deliverable: a cross-functional responsibility map for a launch scenario, identifying which function owns each evidence and communication task.

AI-use focus: using AI to draft a stakeholder map and a metrics framework, with human review for accuracy and compliance.

Source anchors: medical affairs professional-society practice and benchmark materials; IQVIA and Worldwide medical affairs perspectives; industry innovation and metrics materials.

Module 3 · Domain A

Compliance, ethics, and the nonpromotional boundary

Open module 3

Competencies: A, F. Approx. 6 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Distinguish nonpromotional scientific exchange from promotional activity and apply fair-balance principles. (F)
  • Explain key legal and ethical frameworks: the US Anti-Kickback Statute and False Claims Act at a working level, the Physician Payments Sunshine Act, off-label communication rules, the PhRMA Code, and good practice (GxP) systems. (F)
  • Describe how medical, legal, and regulatory review (MLR) works and the medical reviewer's role. (F)
  • Apply data privacy and confidentiality requirements (for example HIPAA and GDPR at a working level) to medical and evidence activities. (F)

Topic outline: promotional versus nonpromotional exchange; fair balance and substantiation; unsolicited requests and scientific response; transparency and aggregate spend reporting; interactions with HCPs and payers (pre-approval information exchange); privacy and confidentiality; the compliance role of the medical reviewer.

Applied deliverable: a compliance decision memo classifying a set of proposed activities as promotional, nonpromotional, or requiring review, with rationale.

AI-use focus: the compliance constraints on AI use itself (confidential data handling, disclosure, no fabrication), setting up the program AI-use policy.

Source anchors: AMCP Format and pre- and post-approval information exchange guidance; APGO responsible-interaction case materials; ISPOR nonpromotional communication standards.

Module 4 · Domain B

Clinical trial design, biostatistics, and critical literature appraisal

Open module 4

Competencies: B. Approx. 8 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Classify study designs (randomized controlled trials, observational designs, single-arm and externally controlled trials, adaptive and master-protocol designs) and state their strengths and threats to validity. (B)
  • Interpret common statistical measures: effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values, hazard ratios, number needed to treat, and multiplicity. (B)
  • Evaluate endpoints, including surrogate endpoints, patient-reported outcomes, and clinical outcome assessments. (B)
  • Critically appraise a publication and identify bias, confounding, and overinterpretation. (B)

Topic outline: the clinical-trial definition and classification; hierarchy of evidence and its limits; randomization, blinding, and control; estimands; superiority, noninferiority, equivalence; adaptive and Bayesian designs; master protocols and external controls; endpoint selection and COA/PRO; critical appraisal frameworks.

Applied deliverable: a structured critical appraisal of an assigned trial publication, including a validity assessment and a plain-language summary suitable for internal use.

AI-use focus: using AI to extract study characteristics and draft an evidence table, then verifying every extracted value against the source article.

Source anchors: NIH clinical-trial definition case studies; FDA complex innovative trial design case materials; FDA model-informed drug development materials; peer-reviewed reporting standards.

Module 5 · Domain B

Regulatory affairs and the drug approval pathway

Open module 5

Competencies: B. Approx. 7 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Describe the FDA and EMA approval pathways and the major application types. (B)
  • Explain expedited and special pathways (breakthrough therapy, accelerated approval, priority review, orphan designation, conditional marketing authorization). (B)
  • Explain the evidence standards for rare-disease and small-population development. (B)
  • Describe the label and its role, and how medical affairs supports regulatory interactions. (B, F)

Topic outline: FDA centers and application types; EMA procedures and the European regulatory network; expedited programs; orphan and rare-disease frameworks; the single adequate and well-controlled study plus confirmatory evidence concept; labeling and the target product profile; regulatory milestones and agency-meeting types.

Applied deliverable: a regulatory strategy brief for an assigned rare-disease case, mapping evidence to the applicable pathway and identifying agency-interaction points.

AI-use focus: using AI to compare pathway requirements across two jurisdictions and to draft a briefing outline, with verification of every regulatory fact.

Source anchors: FDA LEADER 3D rare-disease case studies (odevixibat, vutrisiran, tofersen, avalglucosidase alfa, seladelpar, fosdenopterin, olipudase alfa); FDA drug approval overview; FDA CID materials.

Module 6 · Domain B

Pharmacovigilance, drug safety, and risk management

Open module 6

Competencies: B, F. Approx. 5 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Describe pharmacovigilance systems, adverse-event reporting, and signal detection at a working level. (B, F)
  • Explain benefit-risk assessment and risk management, including Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategies (REMS). (B, F)
  • Describe the medical affairs and MSL responsibilities in safety reporting and the timelines that govern them. (F)

Topic outline: adverse-event definitions and seriousness; individual case safety reports and reporting timelines; signal detection and periodic safety reporting; benefit-risk frameworks; REMS elements; the field-medical duty to report; safety in real-world data.

Applied deliverable: an adverse-event handling flow for a field-medical scenario, showing what to capture, escalate, and document.

AI-use focus: constraints on AI in pharmacovigilance (no automated adjudication without validation and human sign-off; confidentiality of case data).

Source anchors: FDA pharmacovigilance and REMS overviews; ICH pharmacovigilance references; DIA safety-reporting curriculum.

Module 7 · Domain C

Real-world data and real-world evidence

Open module 7

Competencies: C. Approx. 7 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Distinguish real-world data (RWD) sources (claims, electronic health records, registries, patient-generated data) and their fitness for purpose. (C)
  • Explain real-world evidence (RWE) study designs and their acceptability to regulators and HTA bodies. (C)
  • Identify threats to validity in RWE (confounding, selection, misclassification) and the methods that address them. (C)
  • Describe when RWE can support regulatory and access decisions and when it cannot. (C)

Topic outline: RWD taxonomy and data quality; target-trial emulation; comparative-effectiveness RWE; external and synthetic control arms; causal inference basics; transparency and reproducibility; regulator and HTA positions on RWE.

Applied deliverable: an RWE feasibility note for an assigned evidence question, selecting a data source and design and stating the main validity risks.

AI-use focus: using AI for code-list and phenotype drafting and literature triage, with validation of every definition against source documentation.

Source anchors: comparative RWE case analyses in regulatory and HTA decision making; FDA RWE program materials; HAS real-world studies guidance.

Module 8 · Domain C

HEOR methods: economic evaluation, modeling, and outcomes research

Open module 8

Competencies: C. Approx. 9 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Distinguish types of economic evaluation (cost-effectiveness, cost-utility, cost-benefit, cost-consequence, cost-minimization) and select the appropriate type. (C)
  • Build and interpret a simple decision-analytic model (decision tree and Markov cohort model), including incremental cost-effectiveness ratios and QALYs. (C)
  • Conduct and interpret sensitivity and uncertainty analysis (one-way, probabilistic) and budget-impact analysis. (C)
  • Critique a published economic evaluation using CHEERS and a structured critique tool. (C)

Topic outline: the foundations and perspectives of economic evaluation; the Second Panel on Cost-Effectiveness in Health and Medicine reference case; decision trees and Markov models; costs and health-state preferences; QALY estimation; probabilistic sensitivity analysis; value frameworks and elements of value; budget-impact analysis; the CHEERS reporting standard and structured critique.

Applied deliverable: an introductory spreadsheet cost-effectiveness model for an assigned intervention with a one-way sensitivity analysis and a short interpretation memo.

AI-use focus: using AI to structure a model and draft documentation, with independent verification of model logic and inputs; when to use spreadsheet versus programmed models.

Source anchors: standard economic-evaluation method topics; CHEERS reporting standard; the Second Panel on Cost-Effectiveness in Health and Medicine reference case.

Module 9 · Domain C

Health technology assessment and global market access

Open module 9

Competencies: C. Approx. 8 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Explain the purpose and process of HTA and compare major agencies (for example NICE, ICER, and Canada's Drug Agency). (C)
  • Explain decision rules, thresholds, and innovative access agreements. (C)
  • Describe pricing and reimbursement instruments, international price referencing, and the US Medicare drug price negotiation program. (C)
  • Assemble the clinical and economic value arguments for a payer or HTA submission and anticipate the main uncertainties a committee will raise. (C, G)

Topic outline: history and principles of HTA; evidence sourcing, synthesis, and appraisal for HTA (systematic review, meta-analysis, Bayesian network meta-analysis, causal inference, transferability); thresholds and decision rules; managed-entry and outcomes-based agreements; pricing and reimbursement; international reference pricing; US price negotiation under the Inflation Reduction Act; the AMCP dossier format.

Applied deliverable: an HTA submission outline for an assigned product for one jurisdiction, plus a short note on what would change for a second jurisdiction.

AI-use focus: using AI to compare agency methods and draft a submission skeleton, with verification against each agency's published manual.

Source anchors: ICER guide to understanding HTA; NICE health technology evaluations manual; iDSI HTA toolkit; AMCP Format for Formulary Submissions.

Module 10 · Domain C

Integrated evidence generation planning (IEGP)

Open module 10

Competencies: C, G. Approx. 8 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Define integrated evidence generation planning and explain how it aligns clinical development, medical affairs, HEOR, market access, and regulatory evidence needs across the lifecycle. (C, G)
  • Conduct an evidence-gap assessment and translate stakeholder evidence needs into prioritized evidence questions. (C, G)
  • Build an integrated evidence matrix mapping questions to study concepts, data sources, timing, owners, and decision points. (C, G)
  • Define evidence-impact metrics and a governance model for plan maintenance. (C, G)

Topic outline: from siloed study planning to integrated evidence planning; stakeholder evidence needs (regulators, HTA and payers, clinicians, patients); evidence-gap and landscape assessment; prioritization methods; the integrated evidence plan structure; global, regional, and local alignment; governance, review cadence, and impact measurement; the role of AI in evidence mapping.

Applied deliverable: a full integrated evidence generation plan outline for the assigned capstone product, including an evidence-gap assessment and an evidence matrix (this is a core capstone component).

AI-use focus: using AI for evidence-landscape mapping and gap detection, then human prioritization and verification; documenting AI contributions in the plan.

Source anchors: professional-society strategic evidence-generation guidance; DiMe integrated evidence resources; peer-reviewed integrated evidence generation literature (see Source register); IQVIA and Axtria integrated evidence planning materials.

Module 11 · Domain D

Medical science liaison excellence and field medical strategy

Open module 11

Competencies: D. Approx. 7 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Describe the MSL role, its boundaries, and its position in the medical plan. (D, F)
  • Plan compliant KOL and stakeholder engagement, including identification, mapping, and objective setting. (D)
  • Collect, structure, and route medical insights so they inform strategy. (D, G)
  • Prepare for and conduct a scientific exchange interaction, including objection and conflict management. (D)

Topic outline: the MSL competency set; KOL and digital-opinion-leader identification and mapping; engagement planning and objectives; scientific narrative and reactive exchange; insight definition, capture, and synthesis; field metrics; the MSL in the era of AI.

Applied deliverable: a KOL engagement plan and an insight-capture template for the capstone product, with compliant objectives.

AI-use focus: using AI for KOL landscape drafting and insight theme synthesis, with human validation and privacy safeguards.

Source anchors: professional-society and IQVIA field-medical materials; MAPS/H1 KOL intelligence case.

Module 12 · Domain D

Scientific communications, publications, congresses, and medical information

Open module 12

Competencies: D. Approx. 7 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Build a scientific communication platform and publication plan. (D)
  • Apply Good Publication Practice (GPP 2022) and authorship and disclosure standards, including standards for AI use in manuscripts. (D, F)
  • Plan congress activity and nonpromotional scientific presentations, including HEOR impact-case abstracts. (D)
  • Design and deliver compliant medical information responses. (D, F)

Topic outline: the scientific communication platform and lexicon; publication planning and GPP 2022; authorship, contributorship, and disclosure; predatory-journal avoidance; congress planning and abstract writing; the nonpromotional standard; medical information systems and standard-response documents; enduring materials.

Applied deliverable: a nonpromotional congress abstract (HEOR impact-case format) and a standard medical-information response document for the capstone product.

AI-use focus: permitted and disclosed AI assistance in drafting and literature search; the GPP and ICMJE positions on AI authorship; verification of all references.

Source anchors: GPP 2022; ICMJE recommendations; professional-society scientific-communication materials.

Module 13 · Domain D

Advisory boards, investigator-sponsored research, and medical education

Open module 13

Competencies: D. Approx. 5 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Design a compliant advisory board with clear objectives and outputs. (D, F)
  • Explain investigator-sponsored and investigator-initiated research governance. (D, F)
  • Describe independent and accredited medical education and the firewall between funding and content. (D, F)

Topic outline: advisory-board objectives, selection, agenda, and documentation; needs versus promotion; investigator-sponsored study review and support; independent medical education and grants; documenting fair-market value and avoiding inducement.

Applied deliverable: an advisory board objectives-and-agenda document for the capstone product with a compliant output plan.

AI-use focus: using AI to synthesize advisory-board pre-reads and draft summaries, with human validation and attribution.

Source anchors: professional-society advisory-board practice materials; responsible-interaction guidance; compliance frameworks from Module 3.

Module 14 · Domain E

AI and data fluency for medical affairs and evidence generation

Open module 14

Competencies: E, F. Approx. 7 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Explain, at a working level, how large language models and common machine-learning methods function and where they fail. (E)
  • Apply the program AI-use policy: permitted uses, validation, source verification, privacy, disclosure, and human accountability. (E, F)
  • Situate AI use within current regulatory and professional guidance (for example FDA and EMA positions on AI, ISPOR good practices for machine learning and generative AI in HEOR, and publication-ethics positions on AI). (E, F)
  • Evaluate an AI tool or workflow for fitness, bias, reproducibility, and governance. (E, F)

Topic outline: foundations of machine learning and generative AI; hallucination, bias, drift, and reproducibility; prompt design and retrieval; validation and human-in-the-loop; privacy, confidentiality, and intellectual property; the regulatory and guidance landscape; building an AI-use log; evaluating vendors.

Applied deliverable: an AI-use plan and audit log for the capstone, and an evaluation of one AI workflow used in the portfolio.

AI-use focus: this module defines the standard applied everywhere else.

Source anchors: FDA AI draft guidance (2025); EMA reflection paper on AI (2024); peer-reviewed working-group reports on machine learning and generative AI in HEOR; GPP 2022 and ICMJE positions on AI; professional-society AI position materials (see Source register).

Module 15 · Domain E

Professional practice, strategic communication, and career transition

Open module 15

Competencies: E, G. Approx. 5 hours.

Learning objectives:

  • Communicate evidence and value to scientific, payer, patient, and internal executive audiences. (D, G)
  • Apply strategic thinking to a medical or evidence problem and defend a recommendation. (G)
  • Translate a clinical or scientific background into an industry-ready professional profile and prepare for role-specific interviews. (G)
  • Commit to a professional code of conduct and a personal continuing-development plan. (F, G)

Topic outline: audience-adapted communication and executive presence; structured problem solving and recommendation framing; the transition narrative for clinicians; interview and case-presentation preparation; ethics and professional identity; the recertification and development plan.

Applied deliverable: a capstone executive summary and a five-minute recorded or written stakeholder briefing; a personal transition and development plan.

AI-use focus: using AI to rehearse and refine communication while preserving authenticity and disclosure.

Source anchors: professional-society strategy and leadership materials; documented industry role-competency descriptions.

Capstone

integrated evidence and medical affairs portfolio

Open capstone specification

Competencies: F, G, plus integration of A to E. Approx. 12 hours.

The capstone binds the applied deliverables into a coherent portfolio built around one assigned product scenario. Required work products (assessed with the rubrics in Capstone portfolio and rubrics):

  1. Product situation brief and lifecycle map (Module 1, 2)
  2. Compliance decision memo (Module 3)
  3. Critical appraisal and evidence table (Module 4)
  4. Regulatory strategy brief (Module 5)
  5. Integrated evidence generation plan with evidence-gap assessment and evidence matrix (Module 10, drawing on 7, 8, 9)
  6. HTA or payer submission outline with cost-effectiveness summary (Module 8, 9)
  7. KOL engagement plan and nonpromotional congress abstract (Module 11, 12)
  8. AI-use plan and audit log spanning the portfolio (Module 14)

A capstone executive summary (Module 15) presents the portfolio to a simulated cross-functional leadership audience.
