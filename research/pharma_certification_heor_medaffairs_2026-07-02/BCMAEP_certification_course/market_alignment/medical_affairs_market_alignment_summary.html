Medical Affairs RolesWhat the job market asks for, and whether the curriculum matches

This brief ranks the responsibilities that appear in real medical affairs and evidence job descriptions, from most to least common, and maps them against the 15-module BCMAEP curriculum to test whether the course prepares candidates for the work they will actually do. It is a reference for people moving into pharma and a design check for the program. Every figure is an approximate thematic tally across the collected postings, not an exact measurement; the full link index and analysis are in the companion files.

Prepared: 2026-07-02  |  Evidence base: approximately 75 medical affairs and evidence job postings across more than 33 companies, plus 6 role-definition references, collected 2026-07-02  |  Method: thematic frequency coding of listed responsibilities, mapped to the BCMAEP module map

Job-market evidence and method

What the postings say

Evidence base Five parallel searches collected roughly 75 real postings across five role families: field medical and medical science liaison (MSL); medical affairs management and medical director; medical information, communications, and publications; HEOR, market access, and real-world evidence; and biotech and mid-size company roles. Companies represented include Pfizer, Merck, Bristol Myers Squibb, Eli Lilly, AbbVie, Amgen, Gilead, Regeneron, Vertex, Johnson & Johnson, Novartis, Roche and Genentech, AstraZeneca, GSK, Sanofi, Bayer, Takeda, Boehringer Ingelheim, Novo Nordisk, Biogen, UCB, Astellas, Jazz, and the biotechs Alnylam, Insmed, Ultragenyx, BioMarin, Incyte, argenx, Apellis, Sarepta, Blueprint Medicines, and Neurocrine. Durable role definitions were drawn from the MSL Society, the MAPS competency framework, and an APPA, IFAPP, MAPS, and MSL Society position statement.

Method and limits The counts are thematic tallies, not exact measurements. Responsibilities are worded differently across postings, so each theme groups several phrasings, and one posting can contribute to many themes. About one third of the postings were fetched in full; the rest were captured from search-result summaries, which under-report longer duty lists. The approximate count shown for each theme is the number of the roughly 75 postings in which the theme clearly appeared. Read the numbers as prevalence tiers, not precise frequencies.

How the BCMAEP curriculum aligns

Coverage is rated by how directly a module addresses each responsibility: direct a module is built around it, substantial covered within a module, partial introduced but not a focus.

  • Cross-functional collaborationdirectModule 2 (operating model and cross-functional ecosystem).
  • Compliance, ethics, governance, safety reportingdirectModule 3 (compliance and the nonpromotional boundary) and Module 6 (pharmacovigilance).
  • KOL engagement and insight gatheringdirectModule 11 (MSL and field medical strategy), including insight capture and synthesis.
  • Scientific exchange and educationdirectModule 11, with the nonpromotional boundary from Module 3 and evidence interpretation from Module 4.
  • Evidence generation (trials, RWE, HEOR)directModule 10 (integrated evidence generation planning), Module 7 (real-world evidence), Module 4, and Module 13 (investigator-sponsored research).
  • Scientific communications, publications, medical informationdirectModule 12 (publications under GPP 2022, congresses, and medical information).
  • Congress and advisory board supportdirectModule 12 (congresses) and Module 13 (advisory boards).
  • HEOR methods, value dossiers, market access, RWDdirectModule 8 (economic evaluation), Module 9 (HTA and market access), and Module 7 (real-world data).
  • Strategy and medical plandirectModule 2, Module 10, and Module 15 (strategy and recommendation framing).
  • Medical, legal, and regulatory review of materialssubstantialThe medical reviewer role in Module 3, applied in Module 12.
  • Therapeutic-area and scientific expertisesubstantialModule 4 (critical appraisal) and Module 1. Product-specific depth is employer-provided by design.
  • Clinical trial site identification and feasibilitysubstantialModules 4, 5, and 11.
  • AI and digital toolsdirectModule 14 (AI and data fluency) plus AI woven through every module and a program-wide AI-use policy. The course is ahead of current posting frequency here.
  • Project, budget, and vendor managementpartialIntroduced in Module 15; not a dedicated project-management course.
  • People and team management, leadershippartialModule 15 and the leadership domain; introductory, appropriate for an entry-transition credential.
  • CRM, documentation, and territory operationspartialModule 11 covers the field operating model; tool-specific training (Veeva) is employer-provided.
What the analysis shows
  • FindingThe curriculum covers the responsibilities that dominate the field.
    EvidenceAll six near-universal (tier 1) themes and all five very-common (tier 2) themes are covered directly by a dedicated module. The three functions in almost every posting, cross-functional collaboration, compliant nonpromotional exchange, and KOL engagement with insight gathering, are the subjects of Modules 2, 3, and 11.
    Readalignment Strong on the high-frequency work. A graduate is prepared for what the job asks most.
  • FindingThe course is ahead of the current market on two trending themes.
    EvidenceIntegrated evidence generation planning is a named requirement in only a minority of today's postings but is the explicit function of senior evidence roles. AI and digital tools appear explicitly in only about 5 of 75 postings, yet the course makes AI governance a dedicated module and a program-wide policy.
    Readforward-looking Positions graduates for where medical affairs is moving, appropriate for a credential meant to last. Revisit the AI theme's market frequency at each curriculum review.
  • FindingThree areas are lighter, by design rather than omission.
    EvidenceProject, budget, and vendor management and people management are introduced in Module 15 but not to management depth; these concentrate in director-and-above roles a first hire grows into. Employer-specific tooling (CRM such as Veeva) is taught conceptually in Module 11, not as tool training. Product-specific scientific depth is left to the employer.
    Recommendationoptional additions A short field-medical operating-tools and metrics unit (generic, not one vendor) in Module 11 or 15, and a brief project-and-vendor-management segment in Module 15, would close the visible gaps without expanding the entry-transition scope.
Notes for candidates by target role
  • MSL or field medical: emphasize KOL engagement, compliant scientific exchange, insight gathering, and trial-site support (themes 3, 4, 7, 12), covered in Module 11 with compliance from Module 3. Expect substantial travel and CRM documentation.
  • Medical information, communications, or publications: emphasize inquiry response, content development, publication planning under GPP 2022, and material review (themes 11, 13, 10, 15), covered in Module 12 with standards from Module 3.
  • HEOR, market access, or real-world evidence: emphasize economic methods, value dossiers and HTA, market access, and real-world data (themes 16, 18, 19, 20), covered in Modules 7, 8, and 9 with integrated evidence planning from Module 10. This is the course's deepest area and clearest differentiator.
  • Medical affairs management or medical director: emphasize strategy ownership, evidence-generation leadership, cross-functional leadership, and material review (themes 8, 6, 1, 15), plus the emerging people and budget management (theme 21).

Confidence and caveats. The tier assignments are robust: the same near-universal themes recur across independent searches and across the durable role-definition sources, which lends confidence that Modules 2, 3, and 11 address the core of the work. The exact counts are approximate and skewed downward for postings captured by snippet rather than full fetch, so a theme's true prevalence may be higher than shown. Job postings expire; the link index records each with its date and title for re-finding. Full data and the underlying postings are in job_description_links.md and medical_affairs_responsibilities_summary.md.
