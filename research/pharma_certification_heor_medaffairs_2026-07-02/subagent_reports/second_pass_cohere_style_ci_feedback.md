## Decision

One genuinely new narrow reusable lesson was found after the second-pass search direction.

The prior update already covers the need to archive certification, training, and course-market claims separately from analyst-derived curriculum implications. The second-pass research added a distinct risk: public third-party syllabi, slide decks, PDFs, brochures, case packets, examples, and toolkits may be useful as evidence or design references, but public availability does not establish permission to republish or package them as course assets.

## Change made

Added one bullet to `_skills_to_install/cohere-style-ci/SKILL.md` near the existing certification/training/course-market rule.

The new guidance requires curriculum, certification, training, or case-study CI work to:

- Distinguish public accessibility from reuse permission.
- Treat archived third-party materials as evidence, assigned-reading links, or design references unless visible license or terms explicitly allow reuse.
- Record visible copyright, license, permission, gated-access, participant-only, or `not for sale/distribution` restrictions in source logs or methods notes.
- Avoid describing materials as reusable course assets, or copying substantial text, slide images, assignments, or case prompts into paid or distributed courseware, without confirmed permission.

## Why this was new

The second-pass research repeatedly surfaced permission and reuse caveats: the README states that public availability does not imply permission to republish content inside a paid course, and the case-study report flags sources that are public but copyrighted, brochure-only, summary-only, participant-only, or explicitly not for sale or distribution.

That lesson is separate from the existing source-stated-claim versus analyst-synthesis rule. It governs how CI artifacts characterize public course and case-study materials as evidence, references, design inputs, or reusable assets.

## Reference-file decision

No edit was made to `_skills_to_install/cohere-style-ci/references/style-guide.md`.

This is an evidence/source-handling rule, not a visual style or slide-grammar rule. The style guide already covers audience framing and source-observed facts versus analyst implications, so adding this there would duplicate rather than sharpen the guidance.
