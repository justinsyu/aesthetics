# Competitive Exam, Accreditation, and AI Guidance Gap Research

Research date: 2026-07-02. Method: direct fetches of program pages and candidate handbooks (PDF text extracted locally), web search where pages blocked automated fetch, and PubMed metadata lookups for all DOIs. All PubMed-derived citations below are based on articles retrieved from PubMed; DOI links are included for each. Items that could not be fetched directly are flagged.

## 1. ACMA BCMAS: exam and module specifics

### Current curriculum (13 modules as of 2026-07-02)

The live [BCMAS program information page](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info) and the [ACMA homepage](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/) list 13 curriculum modules, quoted exactly as displayed:

1. AI Foundations for Medical Affairs Professionals
2. Medical Affairs Strategy, Tactics, Launch Excellence and Readiness
3. Clinical Trial Design and Drug Development
4. Medical Devices, Diagnostics, and Digital Innovation
5. Regulatory Affairs
6. Scientific Communications and Publication Strategy
7. HEOR & Pharmacoeconomic Models
8. How to Interpret Clinical Trial Data and Evidence in Medicine
9. Drug Safety, Pharmacovigilance and Predictive Analytics
10. Medical Science Liaison Excellence & Field Strategy
11. Medical Information Essentials: Process, Standards, and Practice
12. Compliance and Data Privacy in Medical Affairs
13. Building Medical Affairs Business Acumen Skills

The homepage describes the program as "This comprehensive, 13 core competencies, self-paced, e-learning, online program trains you on the core competencies crucial for success as a medical affairs or medical science liaison (MSL) professional" ([medicalaffairsspecialist.org](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/)).

Note on the "20 modules" figure: search-engine indexed text for the same [program-info page](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/program-info) describes BCMAS as "a comprehensive, 20-module, self-paced, online program" (not directly fetched; the live page fetched 2026-07-02 shows 13 modules). Third-party descriptions repeating the 20-module count include a [PharmCMed course listing](https://pharmcmed.com/blog/13-courses/44-board-certified-medical-affairs-specialist-program) and an archived [ACMA BCMAS information packet](https://docplayer.net/77560976-Information-packet-board-certified-medical-affairs-specialist-bcmas-program-independent-credentialing-in-medical-affairs.html). The most defensible statement: ACMA historically marketed BCMAS as approximately 20 modules; the current public curriculum consolidates content into the 13 named modules above.

### Exam format

- The [BCMAS exam policy page](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/policies/exam) states: "The BCMAS Certification Exam is a proctored, three-hour exam administered online with ProctorU."
- Question formats listed on the same page: multiple choice questions, case studies, and true or false questions. Required materials: calculator, pencil/pen, and web camera. The total number of questions is not stated on ACMA's public pages.
- The [homepage](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/) states "4 attempts provided (online)" for the board exam.
- Program logistics: six months of platform access; average completion approximately 40 hours (2-3 months); price $2,399 in full or 3 installments of $839 ([medicalaffairsspecialist.org](https://www.medicalaffairsspecialist.org/bcmas)).

### Eligibility

The [BCMAS enrollment criteria page](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/policies/program) requires either an MD, PharmD, PhD, DO, MBBS, DNP, NP, PA, BPharm, PsyD, or other relevant degree from an accredited institution, or a bachelor's degree plus 2 years of relevant professional experience.

### Accreditation

- ACMA states the program is "Accredited by IACET/ANSI" with IACET and ANSI logos displayed ([medicalaffairsspecialist.org](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/)); CME/CE credits are referenced in the policies section.
- ACMA appears in IACET's accredited provider directory: "[Accreditation Council for Medical Affairs is an IACET Accredited Provider!](https://dev.iacet.org/affiliates/accredited-providers-list/accredited-provider-overview/?providerID=120374)"
- IACET named ACMA its Exemplar Training Organization in 2020 ([PR Newswire release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/accreditation-council-for-medical-affairs-acma-wins-top-honors-at-iacet-awards-301131412.html)).
- Note: IACET accreditation is a continuing-education provider accreditation (course quality), not a personnel-certification accreditation such as NCCA or ISO/IEC 17024 (see section 4). ACMA's public pages do not claim NCCA or ISO/IEC 17024 accreditation for the BCMAS credential itself.

### Recertification

The [BCMAS recertification page](https://medicalaffairsspecialist.org/certifications/bcmas/bcmas-recertification) states: "Upon successfully completing the BCMAS program, your board certification is valid for one year. To maintain your active status and credential, you must recertify annually." Requirements: complete any 2 approved learning activities (courses, webinar recordings, or podcasts, with topics including patient-reported outcomes and drug literature evaluation) plus a final attestation module. Cost: $99 USD per year. The page warns that "BCMAS certifications that are not maintained every year will be suspended."

## 2. MSL Society MSL-BC (Board Certification for MSLs)

Source pages: [MSL-BC overview](https://themsls.org/msl-board-certification/), [exam content page](https://themsls.org/msl-board-certification-exam-info/exam-content/), and the [MSL-BC Candidate Handbook PDF](https://www.themsls.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/MSL-BC-Candidate-Handbook-Final-8921.pdf) (downloaded and text-extracted).

### Eligibility

- Bachelor's degree or higher from a regionally or nationally accredited institution, plus one (1) year of cumulative full-time experience working as an MSL (or equivalent title) or leading an MSL team ([themsls.org](https://themsls.org/msl-board-certification/)). The certification is explicitly not available to aspiring MSLs.

### Exam structure

- 140 multiple-choice questions: 125 scored plus 15 unscored pretest items; 4-hour time limit; administered virtually through the MSL Society learning management system with live ProctorU proctoring ([themsls.org](https://themsls.org/msl-board-certification/)).
- Passing standard (candidate handbook): "you must correctly answer a minimum 86 of the 125 scored questions" (68.8%), a criterion-referenced standard rather than a curve.
- Fees: first attempt $450 (members) or $550 (non-members); retakes $300/$400. Failed candidates must wait 60 days before the second and third attempts and 3 months thereafter; after four attempts, evidence of additional professional development is required.

### Detailed content outline (effective February 23, 2020)

The exam covers 3 content areas, 14 tasks, and 60 steps, derived from a Job Task Analysis meeting held March 24-25, 2017 in Denver plus a JTA survey ([MSL-BC Candidate Handbook](https://www.themsls.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/MSL-BC-Candidate-Handbook-Final-8921.pdf)). Domain weights (percentage of scored items):

- A. Engaging External Stakeholders: 52% (Develop KOL Engagement Plans 7.2%; Collect Insights 8%; Identify Clinical Investigators 8%; Communicate Medical/Scientific Data 8%; Connect Internal and External Stakeholders 4.8%; Provide Conference Support 5.6%; Support External Stakeholder Research Activities 4.8%; Respond to Medical Information Requests 5.6%)
- B. Collaborating with Internal Stakeholders: 21% (Provide Scientific Education and Training 10.4%; Support Clinical Research Team 4.8%; Serve as an SME on Internal Cross-Functional Teams 5.6%)
- C. Maintaining MSL Expertise: 27% (Remain Current on Scientific Knowledge 17.6%; Maintain Knowledge of Pharmacoeconomics 4.8%; Coordinate Activities with other Field Personnel in Territory 4.8%)

### Recertification

3-year recertification cycle. Options: documented professional development activities (formal education, publications, presentations) or re-sitting the exam; credential holders must also re-attest annually to the Code of Ethics for Medical Science Liaisons and MSL Leaders and pay recertification fees (exam-based recertification seating fee: $450 members / $550 non-members; $50 late fee within a 30-day grace period) ([MSL-BC Candidate Handbook](https://www.themsls.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/MSL-BC-Candidate-Handbook-Final-8921.pdf)).

## 3. ISMPP CMPP certification

Source pages: [ISMPP CMPP overview](https://www.ismpp.org/cmpp), [exam details page](https://www.ismpp.org/exam-details), and the [January 2025 CMPP Candidate Handbook PDF](https://www.ismpp.org/assets/docs/Certification/2025%20CMPP_handbook_v3_FINAL.pdf) (downloaded and text-extracted).

### Structure

- 150-item, multiple-choice, computer-based examination; 3 hours; delivered by Pearson VUE at test centers worldwide or via OnVUE remote proctoring; offered in two month-long windows per year (March 1-31 and September 1-30). Next window: September 1-30, 2026; application deadline August 1, 2026 ([ismpp.org](https://www.ismpp.org/cmpp)).
- Scoring: criterion-referenced standard setting with statistically scaled scores; failing candidates (scaled score below 650) receive performance feedback by content domain ([2025 Candidate Handbook](https://www.ismpp.org/assets/docs/Certification/2025%20CMPP_handbook_v3_FINAL.pdf)). The [exam details page](https://www.ismpp.org/exam-details) reports a pass rate of approximately 80%.

### Exam blueprint (three duties)

Quoted from the examination blueprint in the [2025 Candidate Handbook](https://www.ismpp.org/assets/docs/Certification/2025%20CMPP_handbook_v3_FINAL.pdf):

1. Develop Publication Plan (research the disease and therapeutic landscape; identify data to be published; assess audience needs; define scope; manage Publication Steering Committee; apply scientific communication platforms; develop tactical plan; monitor evolving trends)
2. Implement Publication Plan (identify and engage authors/contributors; follow writing, review, and approval processes; manage administrative aspects; track metrics; monitor evolving trends)
3. Foster Ethical & Compliant Behavior in Publications (maintain knowledge of standards, guidelines, and position statements; apply ethical conduct standards; ensure disclosures; acknowledge contributors; monitor evolving trends)

### Eligibility and maintenance

- Eligibility ([exam details](https://www.ismpp.org/exam-details)): bachelor's degree plus 4,000 hours (about 2 years full-time) of verifiable relevant employment, or high school diploma plus 10,000 hours (about 5 years).
- Credential valid 5 years; recertify by continuing education credits or by retaking the exam ([ismpp.org/cmpp](https://www.ismpp.org/cmpp)). Retake fee $195; non-member application administrative fee $235 (includes one year of ISMPP membership).
- The handbook's study resource list anchors the exam to GPP 2022, ICMJE recommendations, COPE, CONSORT, and EQUATOR (relevant to BCPIS curriculum design).

## 4. Certification-industry accreditation standards

**NCCA (National Commission for Certifying Agencies), Institute for Credentialing Excellence.** The NCCA, operated by the Institute for Credentialing Excellence (I.C.E.), accredits professional certification programs against the NCCA Standards for the Accreditation of Certification Programs; the 2021 revision (implemented beginning January 31, 2023) contains 23 standards covering governance, program characteristics, public information, and recertification, reduced from 24 in the prior edition during the 2019-2021 revision ([Standards Revision page](https://www.credentialingexcellence.org/Accreditation/Earn-Accreditation/NCCA/Standards-Revision)). NCCA uses peer review and has accredited more than 315 programs from more than 130 organizations across healthcare and other sectors ([credentialingexcellence.org NCCA page](https://www.credentialingexcellence.org/accreditation/earn-accreditation/ncca)). Neither BCMAS, MSL-BC, nor CMPP appears to claim NCCA accreditation on the pages reviewed; NCCA remains the reference standard a new BCPIS program could target.

**ANSI/ISO/IEC 17024:2012 (personnel certification).** ISO/IEC 17024:2012, "Conformity assessment: General requirements for bodies operating certification of persons," specifies requirements for bodies certifying individuals, including general, structural, resource, records, certification scheme, certification process, and management system requirements, with emphasis on impartiality (independence from training activities) and competence-based examinations ([ISO listing](https://www.iso.org/standard/52993.html); the iso.org page returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch, so scope details were verified via the [IAS personnel certification accreditation page](https://www.iasonline.org/services/personnel-certification-bodies/) and the [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO/IEC_17024)). In the US, ANSI National Accreditation Board (ANAB) accredits certification bodies to this standard; the [ANAB page](https://anab.ansi.org/credentialing/personnel-certification/) also blocked automated fetch (HTTP 403, not directly fetched).

**IACET continuing education standard.** The ANSI/IACET 1-2018 Standard for Continuing Education and Training defines a model for developing, delivering, and administering continuing education programs; only IACET Accredited Providers may award IACET CEUs, where one CEU equals ten contact hours of structured learning, and providers must re-apply for accreditation every five years ([IACET standard page](https://www.iacet.org/standards/ansi-iacet-2018-1-standard-for-continuing-education-and-training/); [About the CEU](https://www.iacet.org/standards/ansi-iacet-2018-1-standard-for-continuing-education-and-training/continuing-education-unit-ceu/about-the-ceu/)). This is the accreditation ACMA holds for BCMAS; it certifies training quality rather than the psychometric validity of a personnel credential.

## 5. Authoritative AI guidance relevant to pharma, medical affairs, and HEOR training content

- **FDA draft guidance (January 2025).** "Considerations for the Use of Artificial Intelligence To Support Regulatory Decision-Making for Drug and Biological Products," issued by CDER with CBER, CDRH, CVM, OCE, OCP, and OII; the availability notice was published in the Federal Register on January 7, 2025 with comments due April 7, 2025 ([Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/07/2024-31542/considerations-for-the-use-of-artificial-intelligence-to-support-regulatory-decision-making-for-drug); [FDA guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/considerations-use-artificial-intelligence-support-regulatory-decision-making-drug-and-biological), fda.gov blocked automated fetch, verified via search results and the Federal Register). It is FDA's first guidance on AI in drug and biologic development and proposes a 7-step risk-based credibility assessment framework for AI models tied to a defined context of use, spanning nonclinical, clinical, postmarketing, and manufacturing phases.
- **EMA reflection paper (2024).** "Reflection paper on the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the medicinal product lifecycle" (EMA/CHMP/CVMP/83833/2023), first published September 30, 2024; it states principles for applying AI and machine learning at any step of the medicine lifecycle, from drug discovery through the post-authorisation setting, for human and veterinary medicines ([EMA page](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/use-artificial-intelligence-ai-medicinal-product-lifecycle)).
- **ISPOR good practices and working group reports (all verified via PubMed):**
  - Padula WV, Kreif N, Vanness DJ, et al. "Machine Learning Methods in Health Economics and Outcomes Research: The PALISADE Checklist: A Good Practices Report of an ISPOR Task Force." Value in Health. 2022;25(7):1063-1080. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2022.03.022) (PMID 35779937). Identifies 5 ML application areas in HEOR and provides the PALISADE transparency checklist.
  - Fleurence RL, Bian J, Wang X, et al. "Generative Artificial Intelligence for Health Technology Assessment: Opportunities, Challenges, and Policy Considerations: An ISPOR Working Group Report." Value in Health. 2025;28(2):175-183. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2024.10.3846) (PMID 39536966). Reviews generative AI for systematic reviews, real-world evidence, and health economic modeling, with policy suggestions for HTA agencies emphasizing human oversight.
  - Fleurence RL, Wang X, Bian J, et al. "A Taxonomy of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Health Economics and Outcomes Research: An ISPOR Working Group Report." Value in Health. 2025;28(11):1601-1610. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jval.2025.04.2167) (PMID 40348011). Defines generative AI concepts for HEOR (prompt engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, fine-tuning, agents) and current limitations.
- **MAPS (Medical Affairs Professional Society).** MAPS published a white paper, "Vision for Generative Artificial Intelligence in Medical Affairs," endorsed by members of the MAPS Executive Consortium (contributors from Teva, Kyowa Kirin, UCB, Baxter, Lilly, Janssen, and others), addressing adoption of generative AI in Medical Affairs; full text is member-gated or purchasable ([medicalaffairs.org](https://medicalaffairs.org/position-paper-vision-gen-ai/)). MAPS also released the 2024 "Digital, Advanced Analytics & Artificial Intelligence in Medical Affairs" benchmark report, surveying 34 representatives from 32 pharmaceutical, biotech, and device companies with Boston Consulting Group ([report page](https://medicalaffairs.org/benchmark-report-2024-medical-affairs/); [public PDF](https://medicalaffairs.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/MAPS-Digital-Advanced-Analytics-Artificial-Intelligence-Report-2024.pdf)). No MAPS formal standard on AI was identified on public pages as of 2026-07-02.
- **GPP 2022 and ICMJE.** According to PubMed: DeTora LM, Toroser D, Sykes A, et al. "Good Publication Practice (GPP) Guidelines for Company-Sponsored Biomedical Research: 2022 Update." Annals of Internal Medicine. 2022;175(9):1298-1304. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.7326/M22-1460) (PMID 36037471); GPP 2022 covers ethics, transparency, and compliant incorporation of "new and emerging publication tools." The ICMJE Recommendations state that chatbots "should not be listed as authors because they cannot be responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and originality of the work," require disclosure of AI-assisted technologies at submission, and place responsibility for AI-assisted content (including plagiarism and accuracy review) on human authors ([ICMJE, Defining the Role of Authors and Contributors](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html)).

## 6. Integrated evidence generation planning (IEGP)

PubMed search for "integrated evidence generation" (2020-2026) returned 52 records, but most matches occur in author affiliations (for example Bayer's "Integrated Evidence Generation" departments across multiple 2025-2026 papers), indicating the term is institutionalized in pharma organizational structures. Peer-reviewed references addressing the concept directly (all verified via PubMed):

1. Schneeweiss S, Miksad R. "Bench to Budget: Integrated Evidence Generation for Medications." Clinical Pharmacology and Therapeutics. 2025;117(4):869-871. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpt.3603) (PMID 40110751). Editorial framing integrated evidence generation across the medication lifecycle from development through payer decision-making.
2. Wang T, McAuslane N, Goettsch WG, Leufkens HGM, De Bruin ML. "Challenges and Opportunities for Companies to Build HTA/Payer Perspectives Into Drug Development Through the Use of a Dynamic Target Product Profile." Frontiers in Pharmacology. 2022;13:948161. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2022.948161) (PMID 35924050). Survey of 11 top pharmaceutical companies; concludes the TPP can serve as an iterative tool "to enhance an integrated evidence generation plan" supporting HTA submissions.
3. Vreman RA, Leufkens HGM, Kesselheim AS. "Getting the Right Evidence After Drug Approval." Frontiers in Pharmacology. 2020;11:569535. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2020.569535) (PMID 33013409). Proposes conditioning value-based pricing and reimbursement on a prospective post-approval evidence generation plan.

## 7. Other BCMAS-like "pharma industry readiness" certifications

1. **ACMA PACS (Prior Authorization Certified Specialist):** IACET/ANSI-accredited certification for prior authorization and market access professionals, developed with over 30 subject matter experts ([priorauthtraining.org](https://www.priorauthtraining.org/); [ACMA Marketplace PACS](https://www.acmamarketplace.org/certification/pacs)).
2. **ACMA PRC (Pharmaceutical Representative Certification):** 15 e-module training and certification covering technical competencies, ethics and compliance, and soft skills, aimed at US jurisdictions that require pharmaceutical representative credentialing such as Illinois and Washington DC ([ACMA Marketplace PRC](https://www.acmamarketplace.org/certification/prc)). ACMA also lists additional certificate programs (for example a Regulatory Affairs Expert Program and Clinical Development Expert Program) on [acmalifesciences.org](https://acmalifesciences.org/about-acma).
3. **CfPIE certification programs:** the Center for Professional Innovation and Education offers 12 life-science certifications tied to course completion (for example GRACP in regulatory affairs, PDCP in pharmaceutical development, CMDCP in device compliance); no dedicated medical affairs board certification was identified ([cfpie.com](https://www.cfpie.com/certifications/Certification-Programs)).
4. **UCSD Drug Development Product Management Specialization (Coursera):** 3-course specialization (Drug Discovery, Drug Development, Drug Commercialization) from the UC San Diego Skaggs School of Pharmacy covering discovery through commercialization, including pharmacoeconomics and market access content ([coursera.org](https://www.coursera.org/specializations/drug-development-product-management)).
5. **From Science to Pharma BC-MSLcert:** commercial "board certification" platform marketed to aspiring MSLs, including CV, cover letter, and interview preparation ([fromsciencetopharma.com](https://www.fromsciencetopharma.com/mslboardcertification)).
6. **The MSL Academy, Excel Into MSL / MSL-BCS credential:** commercial training program offering an "MSL Board Certified Specialist" designation for candidates transitioning into MSL roles ([themslacademy.com](https://www.themslacademy.com/excel-into-msl)).
7. **MSL Mastery, Aspire MSL:** self-paced training and coaching program for aspiring and current MSLs, with resume, LinkedIn, and interview preparation components ([mslmastery.com](https://www.mslmastery.com/aspiremsl)).
8. **Academy of Pharmaceutical Affairs CMSL Certificate Program:** certificate program covering MSL role fundamentals, clinical trial appraisal, presentation, medical strategy, and KOL engagement ([aapaconsult.com](https://aapaconsult.com/msl-live-program-1/)).

Context note: a third-party comparative review of medical affairs and MSL board certification options is available at [PharmExec](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/medical-affairs-and-medical-science-liaison-msl-board-certification-comprehensive-review) (not independently verified for accuracy; useful as a landscape reference). Entries 5-8 are commercial certificate or coaching products without third-party accreditation claims on the pages reviewed, which distinguishes them from BCMAS (IACET/ANSI provider accreditation) and MSL-BC/CMPP (psychometrically developed exams with job task analyses).
