AMCP real-world evidence standards: Overcoming barriers to using real-world evidence in US payer decision-making

Abstract

BACKGROUND:

US payers are interested in incorporating real-world evidence (RWE) into their pharmaceutical coverage and reimbursement decisions. One barrier to using RWE to inform decisions is the lack of standards for RWE assessment and interpretation specific to US payer needs.

OBJECTIVE:

To develop RWE standards supporting US payer decision-making through a framework outlining study types and potential endpoints important to US payers throughout a product’s life cycle and a set of criteria, tailored to US payer needs, for assessing and applying RWE.

METHODS:

The Academy of Managed Care Pharmacy (AMCP) Research Institute, in partnership with IQVIA, convened a multistakeholder group of RWE experts to complete a survey and participate in a series of workshops with the goal of developing RWE standards tailored to payer decision-making needs. Informed by a targeted literature search, we created an initial list of RWE assessment criteria relevant to payers and refined by consensus and a framework describing RWE appropriate to different product life cycle stages from pre- to postapproval.

RESULTS:

A total of 36 payers completed the survey, and few (18%) reported regularly using RWE as part of their decision process; however, most (80%) were interested in using it. There was consensus on the need for a set of payer-specific standards. Through 2 focus groups and an AMCP Partnership Forum, participants vetted, refined, and finalized this payer-specific framework and developed a checklist that comprised a total of 29 RWE assessment criteria across 6 categories. This framework and set of criteria became the final AMCP RWE standards.

CONCLUSIONS:

The AMCP RWE standards provide a tool to facilitate payer-specific assessments of RWE and guidance for designing RWE studies and communicating results that will be most impactful for payer formulary and coverage decisions.

Plain language summary

Payers value real-world evidence (RWE) that shows how drugs and other treatments impact their members’ outcomes and costs in the real world. However, they do not regularly use RWE in their decision-making because of a lack of standards that help them understand how to use the information. The Academy of Managed Care Pharmacy (AMCP) Research Institute partnered with IQVIA, payers, pharmaceutical companies, and others to design payer-focused RWE standards. These standards include (1) a framework that outlines what RWE study types and endpoints might be available to support their decisions and (2) a checklist of payer-focused criteria that will serve as a template for pharmaceutical companies to use in sharing RWE with payer decision-makers.

Implications for managed care pharmacy

AMCP RWE standards support payer decision-making based on RWE by specifying the information most relevant to them for coverage, formulary, and reimbursement decisions. In addition, the standards provide pharmaceutical companies and other researchers with a tool to inform RWE study design and a guide for communicating their results to payers to include in their decision-making process.

Real-world evidence (RWE) is generated from analyses of real-world data (RWD) that are collected during routine health care encounters.1 Over the last decade, RWE has become a more prominent part of the totality of evidence, reflecting the real-world safety and effectiveness in patients treated during routine clinical care.2–5 Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) remain the gold standard of evidence for demonstrating safety and treatment effect for new products, not only for regulatory approval but also to support payer decisions. Payers are interested in RWE to better understand how a drug performs in the real world.

Since the issuance of the 21st Century Cures Act in 2016, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has developed guidance for using RWE for initial drug approval and subsequent indications, driving an increase in RWE as part of submissions packages, both for substantive and supportive evidence.6–11 However, there has not been a similar effort to develop guidance or best practices for assessing and incorporating RWE as part of payer coverage and reimbursement decisions. Existing guidelines focus primarily on best practices for designing and conducting RWE studies12 or on reporting standards for publication13 and do not include criteria specifically relevant to US payer decision-making. Thus, the focus of this initiative was to create a set of RWE standards to frame expectations and guide RWE generation to meet payer evidentiary needs.

Research conducted by the RWE Leadership Forum14 in 2023, facilitated by IQVIA, showed that 100% of US payers interviewed believe RWE has value for informing reimbursement decision-making.15 Despite recognizing its value, none of the payers interviewed routinely used RWE in their decision-making processes and few understood how to gain access to RWE for consideration in decision-making. Thus, the Academy of Managed Care Pharmacy (AMCP) Research Institute,16 in partnership with IQVIA, convened RWE experts to inform the development of a set of RWE standards that would include (1) a customized checklist reflecting relevant criteria for RWE study design, assessment, and decision-making processes specifically to address payer needs and (2) a framework to describe RWE study types and US payer-specific endpoints, reflecting AMCP dossiers, at different facets of the product life cycle.

Methods

The AMCP Research Institute, in partnership with IQVIA, convened a multistakeholder group of RWE experts representing multiple stakeholders across the United States to complete a survey and participate in a series of engagements including 2 focus groups and an AMCP Partnership Forum to inform the development of the AMCP RWE standards. Participants were selected to be representative of national and regional health plans, integrated delivery networks (IDNs), pharmaceutical companies, pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), consultants, researchers, and government payers. Before the first focus group, the participants completed a brief survey developed by the AMCP Research Institute and IQVIA (Supplementary Exhibit 1 (434.2KB, pdf) , available in online article) to share current knowledge and experience using RWE in decision-making processes. The results of this survey informed, in part, the first focus group that included 11 experts representing a variety of payers (eg, small and large health plans, PBMs, and IDNs) and 14 individuals representing pharmaceutical companies. This focus group was hosted by the AMCP Research Institute and IQVIA in New Orleans, LA, in April 2024 with the goal of defining evidentiary needs and process considerations for payers to incorporate RWE into their decisions. Following the first focus group, a similar survey was administered to a larger group of 36 payer respondents to determine whether focus group findings resonated with a broader audience.

To build on findings from the first focus group, a targeted literature search was conducted using Google, PubMed, and Microsoft Copilot and Perplexity using the keywords “RWE,” “real-world evidence,” “checklists,” and “criteria” to identify publications or reports associated with RWE guidelines that could be relevant to payer evaluations for health care decision-making. From the literature search, 19 relevant publications were identified and reviewed. From these articles, a total of 550 RWE assessment criteria were distilled into a preliminary list of 26 for further evaluation and refinement. A table listing the final publications assessed from the literature search is available as supplementary material (Supplementary Exhibit 2 (434.2KB, pdf) ).

Based on findings from the literature search and the initial focus group, the AMCP Research Institute drafted the AMCP RWE standards including criteria for evaluating RWE and a framework describing study types and example endpoints relevant to payers.

Participants from the first focus group met virtually for a second time in September 2024 to refine the draft AMCP RWE standards (framework and criteria). Subsequently, a revised AMCP RWE standards document was prepared for final refinement at the AMCP RWE Partnership Forum held in Alexandria, VA, in November 2024. The Partnership Forum included an expanded group of 30 invited participants (previous focus group participants plus additional stakeholders from payers, PBMs, IDNs, consultancies, and government organizations) to provide a comprehensive critique of the proposed AMCP RWE Standards and how they may be implemented. Attendees participated in 5 workshops over 1.5 days to refine the payer-focused RWE criteria. Through the workshops, the Partnership Forum participants also refined the framework to describe RWE study types and payer-relevant endpoints potentially available across the 4 relevant stages of the drug life cycle listed below. The final recommendations from the Partnership Forum were built by group consensus, reviewed by all participants, and led to the final AMCP RWE standards (Supplementary Exhibit 3 (434.2KB, pdf) ).

Results

IDENTIFYING PAYER RWE NEEDS

A total of 36 individuals completed the initial survey, primarily from the perspective of health plans (n=17, 47% of respondents) or PBMs (n=19, 42%). Few respondents (n=6, 18%) reported regularly using RWE as part of their decision process; however, most (n=30, 80%) were interested in using it. Respondents stated the largest barrier to using RWE was their lack of understanding and experience interpreting and applying RWE, and this confirmed the need for standards to streamline how payers could include RWE studies in coverage and reimbursement decision-making processes.

Focus group participants reiterated the need for standards to facilitate communication and to ensure RWE studies are designed to provide the most useful information for payer decision-makers. They emphasized the importance of employing clearly defined analytical methods, including appropriate endpoints and clearly defined metrics such as disease prevalence, medication persistence/adherence, switch rates, and major safety events. Payers also suggested the need for educational materials to better understand the strengths and limitations of RWE studies and guidance on how to apply RWE to their decisions.

When considering what information should be included in a set of RWE standards, participants’ top priorities included identifying relevant RWE to inform coverage and reimbursement decisions; verifying fitness of the data source to the study question; presenting clear study objectives and designs, including appropriate analyses, timeframes, confounders, subgroups, and sensitivity analyses as appropriate; describing the relevance of the study population, including generalizability with a well-defined patient selection process, and the impact the inclusion and exclusion criteria may have on the representativeness of the final study population for the disease of interest; and having an adequate cohort size to detect a meaningful difference in well-defined outcomes. Respondents agreed that the limitations of every study should be well-described, along with each author’s contributions to the work and a clear statement of funding sources and conflicts of interest.

RWE CRITERIA

This AMCP Initiative, comprising the literature review, focus groups, and workshops as described above, resulted in 29 criteria across 6 categories: (1) General Study Questions, (2) Data Source, (3) Outcomes Measured, (4) Analytics, (5) Results, and (6) Limitations. The final criteria are shown in Table 1. These criteria ask pharmaceutical companies to explain fundamental aspects of the study such as how the results of a study are applicable to a payer’s population, how and why the data source was chosen, and what limitations a study might have. These criteria, described by category, were redesigned into a checklist to apply to RWE study design and assessment.

RWE FRAMEWORK

The final RWE framework clarifies study types and endpoints that could be available and useful at 4 stages of the drug life cycle: (1) preapproval, (2) approval, (3) postapproval, and (4) expansion into new indications. The endpoints emphasize value drivers for payers and their members: unmet need, health care resource utilization, drug effectiveness and safety (ideally comparative), and medication adherence, among others. Discussion

RCTs continue to be the gold standard for demonstrating the safety and efficacy of new pharmaceuticals. However, payers regularly find that RCT-driven evidence may not fully reflect their insured populations or the outcomes they see in real-world clinical care. This potential difference between RCT outcomes and outcomes measured in a real-world setting drives both payer interest in better understanding how to incorporate RWE in their decisions and investment by pharmaceutical companies and other researchers in generating RWE that is most useful for payer decisions.

In this initiative, we found that payers view RWE as a potentially important addition to the totality of evidence that could enhance coverage and reimbursement decisions, but few are currently using it. There is consensus that establishing payer RWE expectations will support RWE assessment and facilitate communication between payers, pharmaceutical companies, and others who produce RWE intended for payers as well as encourage payer RWE adoption as part of coverage and reimbursement decisions. The AMCP RWE standards, composed of the RWE Framework and Checklist, address this need by defining elements most important to payers when assessing RWE as part of their decision-making processes. These standards help payers understand the relevance of the study objectives to their populations; how data sources align to the study objectives; the outcomes measured; the analytics, study design, and study methods used to determine relevant outcomes to the membership of a health plan; the validity and value of the results; and the limitations of the study. The AMCP RWE standards will increase transparency in study reporting, and, most importantly, facilitate meaningful and impactful dialogue between pharmaceutical companies and other RWE producers and payers regarding RWE.

The framework in the AMCP RWE standards highlights how many types of RWE studies, with a variety of available endpoints, align to different stages of the product life cycle. This framework will help payers identify RWE studies that provide important information from pre- to postmarketing and inform how they might use the available results. The framework also aligns with AMCP dossier categories (preapproval, approved product, and expansion into new indications). This is particularly relevant as payers and pharmaceutical companies (who often generate RWE) rely heavily on AMCP dossiers to communicate evidence that can guide decisions.17

This initiative revealed that the type of RWE payers value most is comparative real-world safety and effectiveness, although there are other study types that are equally relevant. The Preapproval Information Exchange Act, signed into law in 2022, has facilitated preapproval product discussions between pharmaceutical companies and health care payers for new drugs.18 However, no mechanism currently encourages a similar exchange of this important postapproval information to inform short- and longer-term coverage and reimbursement decisions. We included a postapproval category in the AMCP RWE Framework because that reflects a time when product-specific RWD are often available. Pharmaceutical companies and other researchers generating RWE for payer decisions can use this framework to inform their evidence strategies, ensuring that they are generating meaningful evidence for payers. Ideally, this framework will define RWE-related payer needs to guide RWE study design and execution (eg, patient populations, priority therapeutic areas, and endpoints). Relevant RWE-generated postapproval offers an opportunity for payers to reconsider coverage, discounts, and formulary tiering based on how the products are performing in their real-world populations.19 We propose this postmarketing category be added to a future version of the AMCP Format for Formulary Decisions to facilitate sharing new data that demonstrate real-world use and outcomes in patients undergoing routine clinical care.

Where the framework informs how to shape the totality of evidence to payer needs, the AMCP RWE Criteria enable payer assessments of individual RWE studies generated by pharmaceutical companies. The 29 criteria, tailored to payer needs, provide a guide for pharmaceutical companies and other researchers designing studies intended for payer decision-making. The criteria outline elements needed regarding general study questions, relevant data sources, outcomes that should be measured, and appropriate analytical methods. Using the format of a checklist, the criteria also provide space to explain how results relate to prior studies, details on decisions that drove the study design and data selection, and the specific limitations a study may have. Pharmaceutical companies and other researchers can use the checklist as a cover sheet for studies submitted to payers, giving the payers a brief overview and context of the study.

The next component of the AMCP RWE initiative will be a comprehensive education program on RWD and RWE. The education programing will be tailored to RWE producers, including pharmaceutical companies, and payers with a focus on demonstrating the practical application of these RWE standards. Payers who participated in the focus groups and Partnership Forum indicated that further education was needed before they would be comfortable using RWE in their decision-making processes. The goal of this training will be to enhance understanding and facilitate effective implementation of RWE principles. Complementary training will be delivered to pharmaceutical manufacturers and will be available for other researchers, guiding how to use the standards to shape overall evidence strategies and individual study design and how to use the checklist as part of their best practices for communicating RWE generated at different stages of the product life cycle to a payer audience. The AMCP Research Institute is committed to advancing the broader use of relevant RWE and will continue facilitating demonstration projects with payers, academia, consulting groups, RWE researchers, and pharmaceutical companies to support the establishment of the AMCP RWE standards as an essential tool for RWE study design and assessment.

Conclusions

Payers recognize the value of RWE to support health care decision-making. Through collaboration and consensus among payers, pharmaceutical companies, and other RWE researchers, we created the AMCP RWE standards specifically to facilitate 2-way communication between RWE producers and payers to incorporate RWE throughout the product life cycle as part of the totality of evidence supporting payer coverage and reimbursement decisions. These standards will help researchers design and execute RWE studies to produce the information most important to payers. The AMCP RWE standards provide guidance on best practices for the design, assessment, and application of RWE studies to improve health care decisions.
