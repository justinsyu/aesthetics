## A. CLINICAL SLR — PROCESS, REPORTING AND PRISMA COMPLIANCE

### INFO-Merck-1
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Provision of Appendix D (clinical SLR / RWE SLR methods and results)
- **Question:** Confirm that Appendix D — referenced in Sections B.2.1, B.2.5, B.2.8 and B.2.10 as containing the full clinical SLR methodology, real-world evidence SLR methodology, PRISMA flow diagrams and risk-of-bias outputs — has been submitted to NICE in unredacted form. If the document is not currently in the EAG's possession, provide it together with all sub-appendices and date-stamped search audit trails.
- **Rationale:** PRISMA 2020 (item 7) and PRISMA-S (items 1–16) require that the full review protocol, search strategies and database outputs be publicly available; NICE Methods Guide PMG36 (Section 3.1) requires the EAG to be able to verify the SLR methods used to populate the network.

### INFO-Merck-2
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Protocol registration of the clinical SLR
- **Question:** Provide the PROSPERO (or equivalent) registration number and full date-stamped protocol (and any amendments) for the original 2018 clinical SLR and for each of the five subsequent updates referenced in Section B.2.10.1. If no prospective registration exists, explicitly state this and provide a contemporaneous review protocol for each iteration.
- **Rationale:** PRISMA 2020 item 24a explicitly requires registration details; Cochrane Handbook Chapter 1 §1.5 and CRD guidance (2009, Stage 1 Box 1.1) both require an *a priori* protocol to mitigate post hoc decisions on inclusion.

### INFO-Merck-3
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Line-by-line search strategies — clinical SLR
- **Question:** Provide the complete Boolean search strategy used for every database searched (at minimum MEDLINE, Embase, the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials [CENTRAL] and the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews [CDSR]) for: (a) the original 2018 search; (b) each of the five updates; (c) the most recent 13 May 2024 search; and (d) the 4 June 2024 search referenced in Section B.2.1. Strategies should be reported line-by-line, with field tags (.ti., .ab., .tw., /), MeSH/Emtree terms (with explosions noted), free-text terms, truncation, proximity operators, Boolean combination, and the number of hits per line. Confirm the host interface used (Ovid SP, EBSCO, Wiley etc.).
- **Rationale:** PRISMA-S items 6–8 require full presentation of search strategies for each database/interface; Cochrane Handbook Chapter 4 §4.4.4 mandates verbatim, line-by-line strategy reporting so the search is reproducible.

### INFO-Merck-4
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Trial registry and grey-literature searching
- **Question:** Confirm and document searches of ClinicalTrials.gov, EU Clinical Trials Register (EU CTR/CTIS), WHO ICTRP and ISRCTN. For each registry, provide: (a) the precise search terms; (b) date of search; (c) date range; (d) number of records identified; (e) screening outcomes. Repeat the request for conference proceedings — at minimum ASCO, ASCO GU, ESMO, ESMO Asia, IKCS — including the year ranges and follow-up procedure for abstracts whose full publications were subsequently identified.
- **Rationale:** PRISMA-S items 5 and 11; Cochrane Handbook Chapter 4 §4.3.3 — handsearching and registry searching are required to mitigate publication bias in NMAs of immuno-oncology evidence where pivotal data are often first reported at congress.

- **Rationale:** PRISMA 2020 item 7; Cochrane Handbook Chapter 4 §4.4.9 advises updating searches within 12 months of submission; NICE PMG36 requires the EAG to assess currency of evidence base.

### INFO-Merck-6

- **Rationale:** PRISMA-S item 9; Cochrane Handbook Chapter 4 §4.4.5 — language restrictions in oncology SLRs can introduce systematic bias particularly for Asian / Eastern European trials relevant to the aRCC NMA (e.g., RAVE-Renal, J-DART).

### INFO-Merck-7

- **Rationale:** PRISMA 2020 items 8 and 10a; Cochrane Handbook Chapter 5 §5.4 — single-reviewer screening is associated with a documented loss of 8–13% of eligible records.

## B. NMA INCLUSION CRITERIA, FEASIBILITY AND TRANSITIVITY

### INFO-Merck-14
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** PICOS for the NMA — distinct from the SLR
- **Question:** Explicitly tabulate the PICOS inclusion criteria applied to the NMA stage (which may legitimately be narrower than the SLR PICOS). For each criterion (population — IMDC risk classification; intervention; comparator; outcome — including censoring rule, BICR vs investigator assessment; study design — RCT only or quasi-experimental; minimum follow-up), state the criterion, the rationale, and reference the supporting NICE DSU TSD or ISPOR guidance.
- **Rationale:** PRISMA-NMA item 6; ISPOR Jansen et al. 2014 — the criteria for inclusion in the synthesis should be transparent and pre-specified; the EAG cannot otherwise audit which of the 75 SLR studies were eligible for the network and which were excluded *post hoc*.

### INFO-Merck-15
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Trial-by-trial NMA inclusion/exclusion table
- **Question:** Provide a trial-by-trial table for every study identified by the clinical SLR (n = 75) showing — for each — whether the study was included in the NMA and, if not, the specific reason mapped to the NMA PICOS in INFO-Merck-14. In particular, justify the exclusion from the network of: KEYNOTE-426 (pembrolizumab + axitinib — TA650); studies of tivozanib (TA512) and pazopanib (TA215) which are listed in the final scope as comparators; and any pembrolizumab + axitinib studies in the intermediate-/poor-risk subgroup.
- **Rationale:** Final scope (September 2024) lists pazopanib, sunitinib and tivozanib as comparators across all risk groups, and additionally cabozantinib, nivolumab + ipilimumab, lenvatinib + pembrolizumab and cabozantinib + nivolumab for the intermediate-/poor-risk subgroup. The NICE scope must be addressed in full unless a fully justified exclusion is provided (PMG36 §3.1.6).

### INFO-Merck-16
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Feasibility / transitivity assessment
- **Question:** Section B.2.10.2 reports a feasibility assessment but the document presents only a one-paragraph PH discussion and a network diagram. Provide a full transitivity / feasibility assessment table comparing — across the five trials in the network — distributions of: (a) IMDC risk subgroup composition (% intermediate vs % poor in each pooled "intermediate-/poor-risk" arm); (b) prior nephrectomy rate; (c) histology (clear cell vs non-clear cell); (d) sarcomatoid features; (e) PD-L1 status; (f) ECOG PS distribution; (g) sites of metastatic disease; (h) censoring rules for PFS (BICR vs investigator); (i) outcome definitions; (j) minimum follow-up at the data-cut used; and (k) subsequent therapy patterns. Discuss the implication of differences for the exchangeability assumption.
- **Rationale:** PRISMA-NMA item S2; NICE DSU TSD 2 §2.4 and TSD 3 §2 — transitivity is the foundational assumption of indirect comparison; differential pooling of intermediate and poor risk across trials is a known source of heterogeneity in aRCC.

### INFO-Merck-17
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Proportional hazards assessment
- **Question:** Provide the full PH assessment results referenced as Appendix N. For each outcome (OS, PFS) and each trial × arm, provide: (a) log–log plots; (b) Schoenfeld residuals or equivalent test; (c) p-values for tests of the PH assumption; (d) a justification for whether PH holds. Where PH is uncertain, justify why a fractional-polynomial NMA (Jansen 2011), accelerated-failure-time NMA, or piecewise-constant-hazard NMA was not used as in TA858, TA780 and TA964.
- **Rationale:** NICE DSU TSD 14; NICE DSU TSD 19 (FP NMA); TA858 Committee preferred PH but accepted that the assumption was uncertain — the EAG must be able to verify the company's choice.

### INFO-Merck-18
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Fixed-effects vs random-effects model
- **Question:** Section B.2.10.5 reports identical DICs of 9.99 for FE and 9.97 for RE (PFS) — clarify these values and confirm that this is correctly reported (the OS DICs are also reported as 9.99 for both models, which appears improbable). Provide: (a) the full DIC table for OS and PFS, FE and RE; (b) the posterior distribution of the between-study standard deviation τ for the RE models; (c) the prior distribution for τ; (d) the residual deviance vs number of unconstrained data points; and (e) trace plots and Brooks–Gelman–Rubin diagnostics.
- **Rationale:** NICE DSU TSD 2 §4; the EAG must be able to verify model fit and convergence claims.

### INFO-Merck-19
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Inconsistency / loop assessment
- **Question:** Section B.2.10.3 reports that inconsistency cannot be assessed because "there were no loops in the network". Confirm the star-shape of the network (sunitinib at the centre) and acknowledge explicitly that the indirect estimates rest entirely on the transitivity assumption with no statistical test of inconsistency available. State how the EAG should weight this limitation in the cost-effectiveness analysis.
- **Rationale:** NICE DSU TSD 4 §4.3 — inability to test inconsistency is a major limitation that should be explicitly flagged for decision-makers; PRISMA-NMA item S6.

### INFO-Merck-20
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** WinBUGS / R code and convergence outputs
- **Question:** Provide the gemtc R script (or equivalent WinBUGS/JAGS code) used for the NMA, full chain-output summaries, posterior densities and full forest-plot numerical results (the document reports only abbreviated point estimates and credible intervals).
- **Rationale:** NICE DSU TSD 2 §6 — full code and outputs are required for reproducibility.
