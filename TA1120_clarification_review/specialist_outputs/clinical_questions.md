# TA1120 Avelumab with axitinib for untreated advanced renal cell carcinoma

## Clinical clarification questions from the EAG

Conventions: CLIN-Merck-N (single recipient — Merck)

---

## A. DEVIATIONS FROM THE NICE FINAL SCOPE

### CLIN-Merck-1
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Population framing — base case restricted to favourable-risk
- **Question:** The NICE final scope specifies the population as "Adults with untreated advanced renal cell carcinoma", with favourable-risk, intermediate-/poor-risk and PD-L1 status considered "if the evidence allows" only as subgroups. The submission instead frames the cost-effectiveness base case around the favourable-risk subgroup (Section B.3.2.1), with intermediate-/poor-risk presented as a subgroup analysis and the full ITT population relegated to Appendix O. Please (a) justify, with reference to the marketing authorisation wording, why the company considers the IMDC-stratified analyses primary rather than the ITT population specified in the scope; (b) confirm whether the company is seeking a positioning restricted by IMDC risk or the full licence; and (c) present base-case clinical and cost-effectiveness results for the ITT population in the main body of the submission for the appraisal committee.
- **Rationale:** The NICE scope is the reference document for the appraisal. The submission's structure (favourable-risk base case; intermediate-/poor-risk and ITT in appendix) reverses the hierarchy the scope sets out and would constrain how the committee weighs the evidence. The marketing authorisation is for first-line aRCC without IMDC restriction.

### CLIN-Merck-2
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Omission of an ITC versus favourable-risk TKI comparators (pazopanib, tivozanib)
- **Question:** Section B.2.10 states that "an ITC was not deemed necessary for the favourable-risk or ITT populations" because TKI monotherapies are assumed to have broadly equivalent efficacy, citing Manz et al. 2019 and precedent from TA645/TA512. Please (a) provide the full reference and methodological details of the Manz NMA (population, included trials, outcomes, model fit); (b) confirm whether the assumption of TKI equivalence was tested in the IMDC favourable-risk subgroup specifically (where outcomes differ markedly from the ITT) or only in mixed-risk populations; and (c) provide a within-trial or subgroup-level comparison of pazopanib and tivozanib outcomes in favourable-risk patients sufficient to support the assumption.
- **Rationale:** The favourable-risk subgroup is the company's base case. Assuming pazopanib and tivozanib are equivalent to sunitinib (TTD, PFS, OS, RDI all assumed equal — Section B.3.3.2.1; Table 55; Table 63) drives the entire favourable-risk comparator analysis. The EAG requires explicit justification, particularly because tivozanib (TA512) and pazopanib (TA215) were each appraised on their own evidence.

### CLIN-Merck-3
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Exclusion of PD-L1 subgroup from the cost-effectiveness analysis
- **Question:** The NICE final scope lists PD-L1 status as a pre-specified subgroup. The submission presents efficacy results for PD-L1+ patients (Table 20) but states (Section B.3.2.2) that PD-L1 was not explored in cost-effectiveness analyses on the basis of clinical expert opinion. Please (a) provide unredacted PFS and OS hazard ratios with 95% CIs by PD-L1 status in both IMDC favourable-risk and IMDC intermediate-/poor-risk subgroups; (b) confirm whether the PD-L1- (negative) subgroup has been characterised, and present those data; and (c) explain why the PD-L1+ primary endpoint population — for which the trial was powered — is not used to test the robustness of conclusions in the cost-effectiveness model.
- **Rationale:** PD-L1 status was the powered primary endpoint population in JAVELIN Renal 101. Excluding it from the cost-effectiveness analysis on the strength of unspecified expert opinion is a scope deviation that requires committee-visible evidence.

### CLIN-Merck-4
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Marketing authorisation wording vs trial eligibility
- **Question:** Please provide the exact UK marketing authorisation wording for avelumab + axitinib in first-line aRCC, and (a) map it against the JAVELIN Renal 101 eligibility criteria (Table 6), specifically identifying any restriction by histology (clear-cell component required in the trial), ECOG (0–1 in the trial), prior treatment, brain metastases, or IMDC risk; (b) confirm whether the marketing authorisation requires a clear-cell component (since the trial excluded patients without a clear-cell component); and (c) describe the proportion of UK aRCC patients in real-world data who would fall outside the JAVELIN eligibility criteria but inside the licence.
- **Rationale:** The licence may be broader than the trial population (e.g. non-clear-cell histology, ECOG 2). The committee needs to know whether the submission's cost-effectiveness extrapolations are applicable across the full licensed indication.

## B. TRIAL DESIGN, CONDUCT AND INTERNAL VALIDITY

### CLIN-Merck-5
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Switch from BICR- to investigator-assessed PFS after IA2
- **Question:** Table 7 and Section B.2.6.1.2 indicate that PFS was assessed by BICR for IA1 (DCO 20 June 2018) and IA2 (DCO 28 January 2019), but "BICR activities subsequently ended" and PFS was assessed by investigator for IA3 and the FA. Please (a) explain the prospective rationale for terminating BICR after IA2 and whether this was protocol-specified; (b) provide the concordance statistics (e.g. absolute and proportional disagreement rates, HR with and without BICR) between BICR and investigator-assessed PFS at IA1 and IA2, in the FAS and by IMDC subgroup; (c) confirm whether the open-label investigators were aware of treatment allocation at the time of investigator-assessed progression decisions used in the FA; and (d) provide an FA PFS estimate using the BICR rules retrospectively where data permit (e.g. for the IA2 BICR cohort).
- **Rationale:** Open-label investigator assessment of PFS is at high risk of detection bias, particularly where pseudo-progression on immunotherapy and dose interruption on TKIs may differ. The submission relies on investigator-assessed FA PFS in both subgroups, including for the economic model.

### CLIN-Merck-6
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Censoring rules for PFS
- **Question:** Please specify (a) the exact censoring rules applied for PFS in the FA (including for missed assessments, start of subsequent therapy, lost to follow-up, withdrawal of consent, and clinical progression in the absence of radiographic progression); (b) whether an EMA-aligned or FDA-aligned censoring approach was used; and (c) provide a sensitivity analysis of PFS in the IMDC favourable-risk and intermediate-/poor-risk subgroups under at least one alternative censoring rule (for example, treating start of subsequent therapy as an event).
- **Rationale:** PFS is sensitive to censoring rules; in long-follow-up open-label studies, the choice has a material effect on HR. The submission does not document this transparently.

### CLIN-Merck-7
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Loss to follow-up and consent withdrawal patterns by arm
- **Question:** Table 11 (patient disposition at FA) contains several redacted entries (non-compliance, protocol deviation, eligibility, withdrawal by participant, lost to follow-up). Please unredact these figures by arm and (a) provide the IMDC favourable-risk and intermediate-/poor-risk specific patient disposition tables; (b) characterise any imbalance in withdrawal of consent or loss to follow-up between arms (overall and within subgroups); and (c) discuss the implications for OS analysis, given that censored patients in long-term follow-up disproportionately affect tail estimates.
- **Rationale:** The numerical OS benefit in favourable-risk patients is concentrated in the tail (after ~30 months — Section B.2.6.1.1). Differential censoring between arms could bias these tail estimates.

### CLIN-Merck-8
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** OS data maturity in IMDC subgroups
- **Question:** Please confirm the OS event count, percentage maturity, and median follow-up by treatment arm at DCO 31 August 2023 for (a) the IMDC favourable-risk subgroup (the base case population), (b) the IMDC intermediate-/poor-risk subgroup, and (c) the IMDC favourable-risk × PD-L1+ subgroup. For the favourable-risk subgroup, the submission reports 44/94 (46.8%) and 52/96 (54.2%) deaths with median OS of 79.4 vs 65.5 months — please confirm whether these maturity levels meet pre-specified criteria for reliable median OS estimation, and present a sensitivity analysis with restricted follow-up matched between arms.
- **Rationale:** OS maturity in the favourable-risk subgroup is low (~50% of patients still alive). The 79.4-month median is informed by a small number of late events with wide CIs.

### CLIN-Merck-9
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Stratification and multiplicity in IMDC subgroup analyses
- **Question:** Section B.2.13.2.1 notes that randomisation was stratified only by ECOG performance status and region, not by IMDC risk; that IMDC subgroup analyses were pre-specified but exploratory; that intermediate and poor subgroups were pooled post hoc; and that no multiplicity adjustment was applied. Please (a) confirm whether the IMDC favourable-risk subgroup was specified a priori in the original SAP (including the cut-off date of the pre-specification) and provide the relevant SAP excerpt; (b) provide all available subgroup-by-treatment interaction p-values for PFS and OS; and (c) quantify the impact of imbalance in baseline male sex between arms in the favourable-risk subgroup (noted in Section B.2.3.3, both currently redacted) by providing an adjusted HR for OS and PFS controlling for sex.
- **Rationale:** The economic case rests on a subgroup that the trial was not powered to detect a significant difference in. Imbalances in baseline characteristics within an exploratory subgroup further weaken the inference.

## C. POPULATION AND EXTERNAL VALIDITY

### CLIN-Merck-10
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** UK representation in JAVELIN Renal 101
- **Question:** Table 6 records 32 patients recruited in the UK out of 886 (3.6%). Please (a) confirm the number of UK patients in each IMDC risk subgroup of the FAS; (b) provide outcomes (PFS, OS, ORR, discontinuation) for the UK subset, with and without the avelumab + axitinib stratification; and (c) discuss the generalisability of the trial findings to UK NHS practice given that the trial was dominated by US (n=261), Russian (n=138) and Canadian/Western European centres.
- **Rationale:** A 3.6% UK representation is small; the company nonetheless relies heavily on the trial as the source of UK utility values (Section B.3.4.1), subsequent therapy distribution (Table 69), and RDI (Table 63).

### CLIN-Merck-11
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Baseline characteristics vs UK practice
- **Question:** Please provide, for the IMDC favourable-risk subgroup of JAVELIN Renal 101, the previous nephrectomy rate, ECOG distribution, time from diagnosis to treatment, sites of metastasis, and proportion with sarcomatoid features. Compare these directly to the UK ROC dataset (McGrane 2024) favourable-risk subset (n=294) and the SACT CDF favourable-risk cohort, and comment on the implications of any differences (e.g. very high nephrectomy rate in JAVELIN — ~80% — vs UK practice).
- **Rationale:** The favourable-risk subgroup baseline characteristics in JAVELIN (Table 8) are largely redacted but appear enriched (higher nephrectomy rate, ECOG 0, single tumour site) compared to UK real-world practice. This has direct implications for external validity.

### CLIN-Merck-12
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Sunitinib comparator dosing schedule vs UK practice
- **Question:** JAVELIN Renal 101 used sunitinib 50 mg once daily 4-weeks-on/2-weeks-off (Schedule 4/2). UK clinical practice has substantially moved towards 2-weeks-on/1-week-off schedules to improve tolerability. Please (a) state what proportion of UK favourable-risk sunitinib patients in the UK ROC and SACT datasets receive 4/2 vs 2/1; (b) discuss whether the 4/2 schedule used in JAVELIN may underestimate sunitinib effectiveness (or overestimate toxicity) relative to current UK practice; and (c) provide any sensitivity analysis for the impact of this assumption on the cost-effectiveness comparison.
- **Rationale:** If UK clinical practice predominantly uses an alternative sunitinib schedule with comparable efficacy and better tolerability, the JAVELIN comparator arm may not represent the true UK first-line TKI standard of care.

### CLIN-Merck-13
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Place of avelumab + axitinib given evolution of the treatment landscape
- **Question:** The submission proposes positioning of avelumab + axitinib in routine commissioning across all IMDC risk groups (Section B.1.3.6). Pembrolizumab + axitinib was not recommended (TA650) and avelumab + axitinib has been in the CDF since 2020. Please (a) provide the proportion of UK first-line patients who would, in 2024 practice, receive avelumab + axitinib vs IO+IO (nivolumab + ipilimumab), IO+TKI (lenvatinib + pembrolizumab, cabozantinib + nivolumab) or TKI monotherapy, by IMDC group; (b) confirm whether avelumab + axitinib in the intermediate-/poor-risk group is intended as a substitute for, or supplement to, the existing reimbursed IO+TKI/IO+IO options; and (c) provide UK-specific clinical opinion on the relative clinical positioning of avelumab + axitinib among the now-multiple IO-containing options.
- **Rationale:** The treatment landscape has changed materially since TA645 (TA780, TA858, TA964 all post-date it). The committee needs to know whether avelumab + axitinib remains a clinically relevant first-line option in 2025, particularly in intermediate-/poor-risk.

### CLIN-Merck-16

The NMA in Section B.2.10 also explicitly cites heterogeneity within the pooled subgroup as a limitation.

## E. SAFETY, DOSE INTENSITY, DISCONTINUATIONS

### CLIN-Merck-18
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Relative dose intensity and dose modification rates
- **Question:** Table 63 reports RDI values of 91.7% for avelumab, 83.7% for axitinib and 81.9% for sunitinib (from the ITT population). Please (a) provide RDI values for the IMDC favourable-risk subgroup and intermediate-/poor-risk subgroup separately (the base case uses ITT-derived RDI); (b) unredact the rates of dose reduction, interruption, and discontinuation of avelumab and axitinib (Table 31, currently largely redacted); and (c) provide the cumulative dose actually received per patient over the entire treatment duration for avelumab and axitinib, by IMDC subgroup.
- **Rationale:** RDI feeds directly into drug cost. The submission uses ITT-derived RDI in a favourable-risk base case, which may misrepresent costs and tolerability in that subgroup, particularly given the long treatment duration.

### CLIN-Merck-19
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Treatment-related discontinuation
- **Question:** Section B.2.11.3.1 reports that 34.3% of avelumab + axitinib patients vs 17.5% of sunitinib patients discontinued at least one study drug due to TEAEs, and that the treatment-related TEAE discontinuation rate for any study drug was 25.3% vs 9.3%. Please (a) provide these discontinuation rates by IMDC subgroup; (b) provide the rate of discontinuation of axitinib alone, of avelumab alone, and of both together; (c) characterise outcomes (PFS and OS) for patients who discontinued one but not both drugs; and (d) describe how these patterns are reflected in the economic model TTD curves and post-discontinuation costs.
- **Rationale:** Differential discontinuation patterns affect both effectiveness and costs. With the avelumab two-drug regimen, partial discontinuation is common and is not transparently handled in the submission.

### CLIN-Merck-20
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Immune-related adverse events and corticosteroid use
- **Question:** Section B.2.11.4.1 reports irAEs in 50.7% of avelumab + axitinib patients (14.7% Grade ≥3). Please (a) provide unredacted incidence by irAE cluster (currently redacted: rash, hepatitis, type 1 diabetes mellitus); (b) provide rates of systemic corticosteroid use, hospitalisation, and permanent endocrine sequelae (hypothyroidism on long-term replacement); and (c) characterise long-term irAEs occurring after treatment discontinuation given the median follow-up of >6 years.
- **Rationale:** Late-onset irAEs and lifelong endocrine replacement carry both clinical and cost implications that are not captured in the submission's single one-off AE cost (Table 67, £801.11 for all events).

### CLIN-Merck-21
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Cardiac toxicity
- **Question:** Section B.2.11.4.3 cites multiple redacted figures for cardiac TEAEs in the avelumab + axitinib arm, including ejection fraction decreased, troponin T increased, and myocardial necrosis marker increased. Acute myocardial infarction also appears in Table 35 as a serious TEAE more frequent in the avelumab + axitinib arm. Please unredact these figures and discuss whether the cardiac signal warrants any change in the AE costing or selection in the economic model.
- **Rationale:** Cardiac toxicity is a known class effect of VEGFR-TKIs and immune checkpoint inhibitors; a redacted signal in a long-follow-up study warrants visibility.

## F. TREATMENT SWITCHING AND SUBSEQUENT THERAPY

### CLIN-Merck-22
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Crossover and subsequent IO confounding
- **Question:** Section B.2.3.1 confirms that crossover was not permitted, but Section B.2.6.3 reports that 64.6% of the sunitinib arm favourable-risk subgroup and 55.2% of the sunitinib intermediate-risk subgroup received subsequent PD-1/PD-L1 therapy. Please (a) confirm whether any treatment-switching adjustment (RPSFTM, two-stage estimation, IPCW) was conducted for OS in the favourable-risk and intermediate-/poor-risk subgroups; (b) if not, justify why an adjustment was not undertaken given the substantial subsequent IO exposure in the comparator arm; and (c) provide a switch-adjusted OS HR with 95% CI as a sensitivity analysis for each subgroup.
- **Rationale:** When 64.6% of the comparator arm subsequently receives the same drug class being tested in the experimental arm, naïve OS may substantially understate the experimental OS benefit — or, equivalently, may reflect the benefit of subsequent IO rather than first-line avelumab + axitinib. NICE DSU TSD 16 is the relevant methodological reference.

### CLIN-Merck-23
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Subsequent therapy distribution applied in the model
- **Question:** Table 69 presents the subsequent therapy distribution used in the favourable-risk base case, with subsequent nivolumab removed from the avelumab + axitinib arm and re-allocated. Please (a) provide the unadjusted JAVELIN Renal 101 subsequent therapy distribution by IMDC subgroup; (b) provide UK-specific subsequent therapy distributions from the UK ROC, SACT CDF and Nathan 2024 datasets for patients who progressed on first-line avelumab + axitinib, by IMDC risk; (c) justify the assumption that no patient on avelumab + axitinib receives a subsequent IO in NHS practice (clinical experts unnamed); and (d) confirm whether the re-allocation of the nivolumab proportion to TKIs in the avelumab + axitinib arm is consistent with actual UK second-line prescribing patterns.
- **Rationale:** Subsequent therapy assumptions are pivotal to the differential subsequent treatment cost (Table 72: £38,287 avelumab + axitinib vs £74,883 sunitinib), which materially favours the experimental arm. The assumptions need to be empirically grounded in UK practice.

## H. INDIRECT TREATMENT COMPARISONS / NMA

### CLIN-Merck-27
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Network composition and proportional hazards
- **Question:** For the intermediate-/poor-risk NMA (Section B.2.10), please (a) provide the proportional hazards assessment per comparison (log-cumulative hazard plots, Schoenfeld residual tests) for each connection in the network; (b) confirm whether any non-PH method (fractional polynomials, restricted mean survival time, time-varying hazards) was explored, and present results; (c) provide the unredacted forest plots (Figures 16 and 17) with HRs and 95% CrIs; (d) confirm whether the IMDC intermediate-/poor-risk subgroup data from each comparator trial (CABOSUN, CheckMate 9ER, CheckMate 214, CLEAR) used the same definition of the intermediate-/poor-risk pooled subgroup as JAVELIN; and (e) discuss any imbalance in baseline characteristics across the NMA's intermediate-/poor-risk subgroups.
- **Rationale:** PH violations in long-follow-up IO vs TKI trials are well known; the company's brief statement that PH "is likely reasonable" is insufficient. Subgroup definitions and baseline differences across the NMA also drive bias.

### CLIN-Merck-28
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** Fixed vs random effects choice in a single-study-per-comparison network
- **Question:** Section B.2.10.5 selects the fixed-effects model on the basis of DIC and the fact that each connection contains a single study. Please (a) confirm that no between-study heterogeneity can be estimated in such a network and that the random-effects credible intervals are dominated by the prior; (b) provide a sensitivity analysis with an informative half-Normal prior on the heterogeneity SD (as per NICE DSU TSD 2) and report the impact on point estimates and uncertainty; and (c) discuss the impact on the credibility of the indirect comparison given the substantial heterogeneity in IMDC composition and baseline characteristics across CABOSUN, CheckMate 9ER, CheckMate 214 and CLEAR.
- **Rationale:** A single-study-per-comparison network with fixed effects under-represents uncertainty; the random-effects results are needed to bound the plausible range.

### CLIN-Merck-29

Provide any sensitivity NMA including KEYNOTE-426 to assess the robustness of relative effect estimates.
- **Rationale:** Excluding the most structurally similar comparator (another PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor + axitinib vs sunitinib trial) requires justification, particularly because its inclusion would alter the network connectivity and potentially the conclusions.

## K. ECONOMIC-MODEL-RELATED CLINICAL DATA

### CLIN-Merck-33
- **Recipient:** Merck
- **Topic:** TTD vs PFS curves and clinical plausibility
- **Question:** Section B.3.3.3 presents TTD curves modelled separately for avelumab and axitinib using a generalised gamma extrapolation, based on data with most numerical inputs redacted (Tables 49–54).
