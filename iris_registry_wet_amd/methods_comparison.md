## Common Methods Pattern

Across the core wet AMD/nAMD cohorts, the dominant design is a retrospective, eye-level or patient-eye-level cohort study using deidentified EHR data from the AAO IRIS Registry. The most common patient definition is age >=50 or >=55 with an ICD-coded nAMD/wet AMD diagnosis linked by laterality to the study eye and anchored to an anti-VEGF injection. Drug-specific studies define the index as first use of the drug of interest (brolucizumab, faricimab, anti-VEGF generally); conversion studies define baseline from dry/nonexudative AMD status.

The strongest recurring design choices are:

- Specify laterality and analyze at the eye level, with clustering or sensitivity handling for two eyes from the same patient.
- Use an explicit index date: first anti-VEGF injection, first drug-of-interest injection, first nAMD presentation, or dry AMD cohort entry.
- Define baseline VA windows and follow-up windows before analysis. Most VA outcomes convert Snellen to logMAR or approximated ETDRS letters.
- Distinguish treatment-naive from treatment-experienced eyes, especially for anti-VEGF burden and switch studies.
- Require minimum follow-up and treatment exposure when studying treatment response; examples include >=2 injections within 45 days, >=1.5 years follow-up, >=2 years follow-up, or 12 months of exclusive drug exposure.
- Predefine treatment-pattern endpoints: number of injections, injection intervals, treatment gaps, treatment discontinuation, switching, and persistence/LTFU.
- Use multivariable models with demographic and clinical covariates; use Cox models for conversion/time-to-event outcomes; use Poisson/logistic models for initiation/exposure outcomes; use matching/weighting for trial emulation.

## Outcomes Previously Studied

Prior IRIS wet AMD/nAMD work has studied:

- Visual acuity at diagnosis, at 1 year, at 2 years, and up to 6 years.
- VA change by baseline VA, injection frequency, treatment exposure, anti-VEGF drug, and patient characteristics.
- Anti-VEGF treatment burden: injection frequency, injection interval, gaps, switching, discontinuation, persistence, and LTFU.
- Brolucizumab-specific outcomes: initiation profiles, 1-year VA/interval outcomes, extension success, and ocular safety events.
- Faricimab-specific outcomes: early and 1-year real-world outcomes after initiation.
- Safety outcomes: IOI, retinal vasculitis, retinal vascular occlusion, and infectious endophthalmitis.
- Conversion outcomes: nonexudative/dry AMD to exudative/wet AMD, fellow-eye conversion, and cataract-surgery-associated conversion.
- Disparities: presentation VA, treatment initiation, treatment exposure, and outcomes by race/ethnicity, insurance, age, and care factors.
- Trial emulation: VIEW 1/2 and CATT eligibility, treatment arms, and primary VA endpoints.

## Practical Takeaway

For a new IRIS Registry wet AMD study, the most defensible approach is a retrospective eye-level cohort with a diagnosis-code-and-injection-based nAMD definition, strict laterality, an index date tied to first qualifying anti-VEGF or drug-specific injection, baseline VA captured in a predefined window, and outcomes at 12 months plus longer follow-up when feasible. The most common core endpoints are VA change in ETDRS-equivalent letters, injection burden/intervals, treatment gaps or persistence, and anti-VEGF switching. For conversion questions, use Cox/time-to-event methods with dry/nonexudative AMD baseline status and conversion to exudative/nAMD as the endpoint. For treatment-response questions, adjust for baseline VA, age, sex, race/ethnicity, insurance, treatment exposure, provider/specialty, and comorbid ocular factors; account for inter-eye correlation.
