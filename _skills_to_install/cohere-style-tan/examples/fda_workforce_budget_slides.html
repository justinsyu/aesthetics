User fees are almost half of FDA funding

Oversight reports identify capacity stress

Headcount and budget growth do not prove that FDA has sufficient capacity. GAO and HHS OIG connect staffing, funding-model limits, data quality, training, travel burden, and resource constraints to inspection and recall gaps.[5][6][7][8]

Drug inspections
-36%

GAO found FY2023 drug manufacturer inspections were 36% below FY2019, due in part to reduced investigator capacity. Investigator vacancy reached 16% in Jun. 2024.[5]

Food inspections
3-5 yr

HHS OIG found FDA was not inspecting enough domestic food facilities to meet required future timeframes and had data-quality issues in its facility inventory.[6]

Device recalls
3,934

GAO found FDA oversaw 3,934 medical device recalls from FY2020 to FY2024 and could not meet its 3-month termination goal due to resource constraints.[7]

Role mix matters: losing experienced inspectors, reviewers, recall staff, scientists, or lab personnel can affect capacity more than the same aggregate headcount change in lower-criticality roles. GAO's 2026 review also identifies user-fee dependence as a constraint on flexible oversight funding decisions.

Interpretation

The official data point to a mixed FDA capacity picture. FDA's requested program level rises in FY2027, while OPM's Feb. 2026 active-employee file is materially below the Sep. 2024 count. FTE requests, user fee continuity, and process modernization can support key work. Capacity risk remains because FDA's budget is split across restricted funding streams, salary costs remain high, global facility oversight remains large, and GAO/OIG reports identify inspection and recall gaps. A useful dashboard should track total employees, dollars, vacancies, role mix, inspection completion, recall termination, review timelines, and foreign oversight workload.[1][3][4][5][6][7][8]
