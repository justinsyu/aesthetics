## Header line rhythm
- When reviewers request matched header line counts across sibling cards, panels, tables, or other same-page peer components, revise wording and/or component-specific width/type settings so headings share the requested line rhythm without filler or meaning changes.
- For matched two- or three-line headings in peer components, choose the intended rendered line count for the row or page, then use explicit line breaks, concise wording changes, or clarifying expansions of abbreviations when needed to match rhythm while preserving the original meaning.
- When review feedback asks for a specific title or header word to move to the next line, use an explicit line break that keeps meaningful noun phrases together, then verify the rendered PDF shows the requested wrap without clipping or overlap.
- When one peer header is longer than the others, first shorten or abbreviate that label to match the row's intended line count before changing shared typography.
- Same-row or same-page peer headings should use the same rendered font size; do not shrink only the longest peer heading unless the whole peer group uses a shared adjusted heading size.

# Cohere Style CI

## HEOR/RWE framing

- For HEOR/RWE decks, avoid describing evidence as directly supporting commercialization unless the evidence is specifically real-world effectiveness, safety, or utilization evidence for the sponsor's drug. Otherwise, frame HEOR evidence around patient burden, unmet need, disease-state awareness, treatment patterns, and access or value context.
- For HEOR, RWE, and market-access deck titles, prefer simple, direct, evidence-bound wording over launch-specific or over-engineered phrasing. Titles should state the evidence role plainly, such as how evidence clarifies disease burden, unmet need, treatment patterns, access context, or value questions, without implying product-launch use unless the cited evidence directly supports that use.
- For HEOR and market-access decks, avoid reader-facing terms such as `web-scan` or generic `scan` when describing evidence review. Prefer domain-appropriate terms such as public-source review, source review, evidence review, landscape review, source-observed workflows, and methods or source notes as appropriate.
- For HEOR and market-access decks, avoid unclear metaphorical headings such as `domain spine` and overly abstract phrases such as `domain framework` when describing professional-organization source material. Prefer plain wording such as `defines HEOR and access topics`, `sets the HEOR and access context`, or `organizes the evidence areas`.
- For HEOR/RWE AI-adoption, FDE-style, or workflow-modernization proposals, start from the team's existing approved AI tools, governance constraints, evidence workflows, and adoption barriers before recommending new software. Separate opportunity discovery, implementation support, training on existing tools, procurement decision support, workflow use cases, and day-to-day adoption so the proposal does not collapse into a vendor-buy recommendation.
- For DTC pharma commercial reviews that connect lifestyle, daily-functioning, or patient-experience messages to HEOR, PRO, or RWE support, keep the ad creative/source and inferred message separate from label-supported efficacy claims and from public outcomes evidence. For each candidate, preserve the ad source URL and observed date, exact message cue, indication and company, HEOR evidence support level, evidence URLs such as PubMed, PMC, label, trial, or other official sources, and a compliance caveat when evidence is descriptive, post hoc, exploratory, non-brand-specific, or supports clinical outcomes rather than patient functioning.
- For DTC drug-advertising CI artifacts, separate patient-functioning, quality-of-life, convenience, adherence, administration, and treatment-burden messages from direct label-supported endpoint claims. Record whether the ad message is a source-observed creative cue, a verified verbatim ad claim, a brand-page statement, a patient narrative, or analyst synthesis; do not recast lifestyle or treatment-burden creative as an efficacy, safety, superiority, or PRO claim unless the exact ad or label text directly supports that claim.
- For HCP product-website PRO-vs-label audits, keep the website claim, label text, and independent PRO or outcomes evidence as separate fields. Preserve the exact website passage, page or section, URL, access date, product and indication, label version or effective date, label section, endpoint or instrument, population, comparator, and time horizon; do not classify an HCP-site quality-of-life, symptom, function, tolerability, convenience, or treatment-burden statement as label-supported PRO evidence unless the cited label passage directly supports that same endpoint, population, and framing. When expanding an existing audit, append to the existing run package when appropriate, retain examples only with clean unobscured live-browser screenshots plus the label-search basis, move obstructed captures or label-present candidates into screen-outs or excluded working paths, and split dense disease-area tables rather than overriding PDF overflow.
- For patient-facing product-website PRO-vs-label audits, use the same claim-label-evidence separation as HCP audits while preserving the patient audience and source type. Treat patient-site copy, patient stories, symptom or daily-life framing, treatment-burden language, and plain-language summaries as patient-facing website claims unless the exact page and label passage support the same endpoint, population, comparator, time horizon, and framing; record whether each item is a brand-page statement, patient narrative, educational disease-state statement, affordability or support-program message, or analyst synthesis. Do not merge patient-facing and HCP-site examples without clearly labeling audience, URL, access date, page or section, label-search basis, and screenshot status.
- For investor or asset-manager competitive-intelligence research involving HEOR, RWE, or market access, separate verified investor strategy, personnel, mandate, and portfolio evidence from inferred HEOR interest. Rank similar investors by strategy fit and portfolio evidence rather than generic AUM, brand prestige, or fund size. Translate the research into source-backed networking topic areas, such as burden, unmet need, evidence gaps, payer or access dynamics, RWE feasibility, endpoint strategy, and launch or access risk. Do not state or imply that an investor uses HEOR unless public evidence directly supports that claim.
- When DTC pharma commercial reviews compare ad messaging with HEOR, PRO, or RWE evidence, preserve short verbatim commercial, website, and literature excerpts with source URLs in separate fields; avoid long reproduced passages and do not quote song lyrics. For user-requested exact advertisement, commercial, or website quotes, use verified verbatim claim text only; do not present ad titles, brand or campaign names, scene labels, truncated phrases, or paraphrases as verbatim claims. Preserve qualifiers, comparators, time horizons, and lead-in clauses that materially constrain the claim. Use archive captions only when the status clearly labels them as captions rather than official transcripts. Label each quote/status as `FDA-quoted TV ad`, `official transcript`, `official brand page not ad`, `trade-press quote`, `archive caption`, `source inaccessible`, `access-dependent`, or `needs source-line check`; mark unavailable rather than filling gaps.
- For DTC claim quotes, use binary exact-quote status: `VERIFIED VERBATIM` only after inspecting accessible source text and matching the quote 100%, preserving casing, punctuation, ellipses, trademark symbols, comparators, time horizons, and lead-in clauses. If the source text cannot be accessed, or the quote is inferred, guessed, normalized, truncated, rebuilt from snippets, or memory-derived, mark `CANNOT PULL EXACTLY` or unavailable; do not use confidence levels for exact quotes.

## Core Contract

Create a fixed 16:9 HTML slide report and exported PDF using the tan Cohere-style CI format. Include only factual, evidence-backed information from primary sources published within the last 7 days inclusive of the current date.

If the user explicitly requests a broader market scan, vendor capability inventory, regulatory/HTA/professional-organization guidance compilation, or current landscape compilation on AI, follow the requested time scope instead of the last-7-days default. These runs may produce evidence artifacts without a slide report, such as vendor evidence inventory JSON, website AI-capability screenshots with bounding boxes around supporting text, and a labeled screenshots PDF.

Do not include recommendations, opinions, strategic advice, or unsupported interpretation unless the user explicitly requests an audience-facing advisory, playbook, or decision-support deck. In those cases, frame guidance as source-supported implications, actions, risks, or questions for the intended audience; cite the supporting evidence at the claim or advice level; and state material evidence limits. Every substantive statement in the output must have an inline citation hyperlinked to the source URL.

Viewer-facing report language must be scientific, objective, neutral in tone, and update-oriented, with concise NEJM-like prose for CI deliverables. Avoid casual attribution such as "says"; use formal verbs such as "states," "reported," "listed," "describes," or "documented" as appropriate. Avoid engineering jargon unless the topic is computer science or software infrastructure, including computer-science-like phrases such as "programmatic patterns" when analyzing PCORI or other public-program data, and translate technical workflow terms into plain scientific or business language when writing for biomedical, market-access, HEOR, payer, regulatory, or commercial audiences.

Reader-facing decks must be framed for the intended audience and decision context. Do not describe what source material, training programs, curricula, or workshops say about an audience as a substitute for analysis; translate cited evidence into audience-relevant implications, actions, risks, decisions, or open questions. Avoid semicolons in reader-facing slide copy. Split dense clauses into shorter sentences or bullets instead. Keep source mechanics and program-description details in source logs, methods notes, or reference material unless they are themselves the substantive finding.

- In viewer-facing CI copy, start every sentence with a capital letter, including complete sentences that begin after a colon.

Reader-facing decks must not refer to the requestor, user, person giving instructions, prompt, production premises, or artifact-production process. For executive-facing decks, use neutral, executive-appropriate wording focused on decision-relevant facts, implications, risks, and open questions. For HEOR and market-access decks, use scientific, Value in Health-like wording that is evidence-bound, methods-aware, and appropriate for payer, access, outcomes-research, and value-evidence readers.

For biomedical and scientific decks, write for NEJM-style medical/scientific readers: use objective, neutral, evidence-bound language and avoid hype, promotional, consulting, or marketing phrasing. Do not use nonstandard synthesis labels such as "claim-cited synthesis"; describe the specific cited evidence, source status, methods, population, endpoint, or limitation instead.

For competitive-intelligence or evidence-assessment decks intended for general pharma audiences, do not use consulting, outreach, lead-generation, business-development, hook, or pitch language unless the user explicitly requests that framing. Use objective, neutral, scientific wording appropriate for an NEJM-style medical/scientific reader.

For general-pharma CI or evidence-assessment decks, avoid reader-directive framing that tells audiences how to interpret the evidence, including phrases such as "should," "need," "require," "central question," "relevant assessment," "hook," "guardrail," or "what not to overcount," unless the user explicitly requests a playbook or recommendation; prefer descriptive, source-bound statements about observed claims, evidence alignment, limitations, and uncertainty.

For regulatory CI decks, avoid assertive or retrospective blame phrasing such as "should have," "red-team," "weak claim," "strong claim," "preventable," "foreseeable event," or "approval risk event." Prefer measured language such as "regulatory uncertainty," "evidence limitations," "could have informed," "may have reduced uncertainty," and "not determinable from public information."

For regulatory CI decks, avoid uncommon or academic shorthand such as "determinable counterfactual outcome" when describing whether earlier mitigation would have changed an FDA action. Use conventional scientific or regulatory wording, such as "public information does not establish whether earlier mitigation would have changed FDA's final action."

Use direct affirmative framing instead of rhetorical contrast constructions such as "not X, but Y" or "not X. It is Y" when the contrast is not analytically necessary. Prefer precise standalone statements that describe the finding, implication, or operating principle without editorial setup.

Use formal competitive-intelligence language in viewer-facing text. Avoid casual freshness framing such as "new" or "newly" when a precise source status is available; prefer terms such as "first-posted," "reported," "published," "during the reporting period," "source-supported," or the exact source date. Avoid conversational explanation clauses such as "because the same sentence also covers..."; when scope qualification is required, state it formally as a source-scope limitation, such as "cited only for the subgroup explicitly named in the source."

Do not coin uncommon shorthand labels in viewer-facing CI language; use explicit source-supported phrasing such as "bluebird bio coverage" or "outcomes-based agreement coverage" instead of compressed labels like "bluebird access." Avoid vague company-specific access shorthand and use standard payer/access terms such as outcomes-based agreement, commercial payer coverage agreement, warranty program, managed access agreement, or reimbursement agreement when supported by the source.

Define unfamiliar accounting, pricing, policy, or industry terms in plain English near their first prominent viewer-facing use. Do not rely on technical source terms, such as "retained share," as standalone reader-facing language for general audiences; clearly distinguish spending shares, margins, receipts, and retained amounts from profit.

## Required Workflow

1. Create a run folder for every output, for example `competitive_intelligence_reports/<topic_slug>/<YYYY-MM-DD_HHMM>/`.
2. In mixed repositories that also contain apps, websites, or product code, limit writes to the requested CI run folder, generated support artifacts, and explicitly requested skill files. Do not modify app or product files, such as Field Intelligence Engine files, unless the user explicitly asks for that app work.
3. Inside the run folder, create:
   - `screenshots/date-verification/`
   - `screenshots/evidence/`
   - `screenshots/reference/`
   - `screenshots/render-review/`
   - `assets/`
   - `sources/`
   - `report.html`
   - `[disease]-ci-report-[MM.DD.YY].pdf`
   - `[disease]-ci-screenshots-[MM.DD.YY].pdf`
4. Copy `assets/tan_slide_background.png` from this skill into the run folder's `assets/` directory before building `report.html`.
5. Use the Codex Chrome plugin to open every webpage used as evidence. Load Chrome tools through the Chrome skill or tool discovery when needed. Screenshots must be from the actual open browser page, not from URL previews, search results, downloaded HTML, or non-browser renderers.
6. Verify each source publication date from visible webpage text in Chrome. Save a screenshot showing the visible publication date in `screenshots/date-verification/`, and ensure the exact date text has a visible highlight or bounding box in the saved screenshot. Do not rely on URL text, search snippets, page metadata, JSON-LD, file names, browser history, or LLM memory as the date source.
7. Define the requested topic, disease, population, product, geography, and evidence scope before extracting facts. Exclude adjacent or mixed-scope sources unless the in-scope subgroup, indication, result, or record is separately visible and cited.
8. Read primary sources directly before extracting facts. Do not identify or extract included facts from deterministic search outputs, snippets, secondary summaries, answer boxes, cached descriptions, or `web.run` results. Search may only help locate candidate pages; included facts must come from opened primary-source webpages or primary documents.
9. Before taking any webpage screenshot, close or reject privacy-choice overlays when available, and close or accept all popups, modals, cookie banners, newsletter prompts, chat widgets, interstitials, sticky overlays, and other page chrome that obscures evidence or dates. Do not save screenshots while any such overlay remains visible.
10. Draw a visible bounding box around the exact supporting text on each webpage before capturing evidence screenshots when a box can tightly frame the text without obscuring it. Use true text highlighting only when a bounding box would be inaccurate, unreadable, or technically infeasible. The boxed or highlighted supporting text must visibly appear in the saved screenshot itself, with enough surrounding context to validate the fact; an evidence box that merely frames a darkened, dimmed, or otherwise obscured region is not acceptable. Save these screenshots in `screenshots/evidence/`. If a page cannot show the relevant date and marked supporting text visibly, exclude that source.
11. Visually confirm each saved webpage screenshot before using it: the page must be unobscured, visible dates must be readable and marked with a highlight or bounding box, and evidence screenshots must clearly show highlighted or boxed supporting text. If a saved screenshot shows a privacy or cookie overlay, dimmed page state, modal backdrop, or any other obscuring layer, close or reject the overlay on the source page and retake the screenshot. Retake any screenshot that fails this check.
12. Build the HTML deck using the style reference in `references/style-guide.md`, which tracks the current example report, and the copied background asset at `assets/tan_slide_background.png`. Open and follow the current example report before building or revising a deck. The current reference example is `examples/aml_reference_report/report.html`.
13. If sources or slides are removed during revision, renumber citations and any visible slide/page numbering, prune `report.html`, `sources/source-log.md`, `sources/reference-screenshots.csv`, and any numbered source extracts, and rebuild the screenshots PDF so final artifacts contain only cited references. When review feedback selects a whole slide/page and asks to delete it, remove the entire slide element rather than emptying its contents, then renumber visible slide/page numbers, re-export, and confirm the exported PDF page total matches the current slide count so no blank page or stale slide number remains. If review feedback asks to remove a source-proof appendix, thumbnail slide, or other evidence-preview slide from the main deck, omit that slide from `report.html` and regenerate the main report PDF without deleting the companion source screenshots, labeled screenshots PDF, source log, or reference-screenshot manifest unless the user explicitly asks to delete those artifacts. Any remaining numbered support files such as `source-05-browser-text.txt`, `source-05-date.png`, and `source-05-evidence-01.png` must match the final report reference number; move excluded or superseded candidate materials into clearly labeled working/excluded paths instead of leaving them under final reference numbers.
14. Export the main report PDF with `scripts/export_html_slides_pdf.mjs` and name it `[disease]-ci-report-[MM.DD.YY].pdf`, where `[disease]` is the disease/topic slug and `[MM.DD.YY]` is the run date.
15. Export every date-verification and evidence screenshot used to support cited report references into one separate labeled screenshots PDF named `[disease]-ci-screenshots-[MM.DD.YY].pdf`. Each included screenshot page must show the reference/citation number(s) it supports, such as `Reference 3 - date verification` or `Reference 3 - evidence 1`.
16. Save the screenshot-PDF assembly manifest at `sources/reference-screenshots.csv` when using more than a few screenshots, with `label,path,caption` columns matching the labels in the screenshots PDF.
17. Review rendered screenshots of the exported main report PDF before finalizing. Fix any overlap, clipping, off-page content, excessive padding, table headers that wrap to more than one row, reference sections that spill beyond one page, or text below the minimum readable size.
18. Visually inspect the final labeled screenshots PDF before delivery. Confirm every included screenshot is readable, unobscured by overlays, free of hidden or partially visible privacy/cookie popups anywhere on the appendix page, correctly labeled, and visibly contains a highlight or bounding box around the source date or supporting evidence text.
19. Audit generated report and PDF hyperlinks for live resolution. Replace broken or dead source URLs with working canonical source URLs when available; otherwise remove the clickable annotation rather than leaving a broken link.
20. Validate the final report against the saved screenshots and the labeled screenshots PDF before delivery.

When revising an existing report and the user asks to follow the current instructions, re-apply the current workflow, language, style, evidence, export, and QA requirements to the revised artifact as a whole, not only to the explicitly changed copy or slide.

When adapting an existing CI report into a slide deck, treat user-requested section exclusions as controlling the main-deck narrative and visible slide set. Preserve screenshots for the retained cited evidence in the companion screenshots PDF, source log, and reference-screenshot manifest; do not reintroduce excluded report sections solely to satisfy a request to include all screenshots.

When revising an existing deck on a later date, update visible report-date, run-date, and cover-date labels to the current requested date while preserving source publication, effective, posting, and access dates as separate source metadata.

When creating year-over-year sibling artifacts from a prior validated report, preserve the validated layout patterns, link styling, citation styling, and export QA approach, but rebuild the data, evidence, source log, screenshots, citations, and reference artifacts from the requested year's current primary sources. Do not create the new artifact by retitling or date-shifting a prior report.

## Evidence Rules

- Use only information published in the last 7 days, counting the current date as day 0 and including both endpoints.
- For explicitly requested broader market scans or vendor inventories, use the user's requested time scope and document that scope in the source log or JSON output.
- For KOL mapping, expert-intelligence, HCP-network, vendor capability, or build-vs-buy CI scans, separate public-source facts or proxies that can be independently rebuilt; non-public or proprietary data layers such as claims, Rx, labs, EHR, referrals, patient mix, private communities, identity graphs, contact databases, CRM records, engagement history, or proprietary models; and blended workflow or service layers such as entity resolution, scoring, curation, alerts, CRM/API integration, analyst review, compliance workflow, and refresh operations. Do not present public-source replicability as exact replication of vendor coverage, refresh cadence, identity resolution, compliance controls, or proprietary methods.
- When using public KOL or HCP signals, frame them as evidence of public expertise, visibility, roles, relationships, or ranking hypotheses. Do not infer private beliefs, prescribing intent, endorsement, availability, willingness to advise, or likely engagement behavior unless a cited source directly states those facts.
- For build-vs-buy implications in CI artifacts, start from the decision context, evidence need, geography, disease area, use case, and operational requirements. Treat transparency, explainability, and repeatable public evidence trails as build-favoring conditions; treat proprietary clinical activity, patient mix, referral networks, global identity resolution, compliant engagement workflow, private HCP access, and maintained platform operations as buy or partner drivers when those layers are material to the decision.
- For downloaded datasets, portfolio exports, registries, grants databases, or other time-series analyses, preserve the raw export and record the download date, source URL, visible record count when available, and any filter state in the source log or methods note. In viewer-facing charts and summaries, label partial current years explicitly with the extraction date, avoid presenting year-over-year comparisons as complete when the current year is incomplete, and state when apparent increases or decreases may reflect partial-year accrual, data-lag, posting-date, or export-refresh effects rather than a true full-year trend. Document blank fields, impossible dates, future-dated records, and other source-export anomalies rather than silently correcting them; if they are excluded from a trend or denominator, state that treatment near the relevant chart or in the methods note.
- For registry, claims, EHR, or other RWE epidemiology audits, define the unit of analysis, denominator, case definition, index date, lookback or washout window, follow-up and attrition rules, and code-list or algorithm availability before interpreting incidence, prevalence, conversion, treatment-burden, or persistence patterns. Keep patient-, eye-, event-, injection-, visit-, practice-, and geography-level measures visibly distinct; do not compare rates or trends across sources unless the denominator, timeframe, capture setting, and inclusion/exclusion rules are compatible or the limitation is stated near the finding.
- For AI-answer, search-answer, chatbot-response, or similar answer-surface audits based on screenshots, OCR, or visible browser captures, preserve the prompt bank, prompt strata, prompt origin, screenshot path, OCR text path, run surface, run date, and any reconstructed or partial-response flags at row level. Use metric-specific denominators that exclude in-progress, failed, blank, or insufficient visible responses only for affected measures, and label rates as visible-capture or OCR-visible findings rather than complete model behavior. For competitor-visibility comparisons, evaluate prompt-frame fit, evidence/source ecosystem depth, attribute clarity, neutral-source compatibility, and safety/medical-caveat coupling, and keep verified citation/source observations separate from inference about why retrieval happened. State when OCR can miss hidden answer text, collapsed citations, full URLs, lower-page content, or browser DOM content, and treat source-quality labels such as authoritative, manufacturer, consumer, commercial, news, or SEO as visible source signals rather than verified source-grade citations unless each cited source has been opened and checked.
- For explicitly requested regulatory, HTA/payer, professional-organization, methods, standards, or current AI landscape compilations, source scope may extend beyond the last 7 days when needed to capture currently operative guidance or landscape context. Classify each source by authority type, such as regulator, HTA/payer, professional/methods/standards, industry, or vendor; distinguish binding guidance from draft, nonbinding, position-statement, consensus, or reporting-standard sources; and state the date and current status for each source.
- For regulatory artifact discovery runs that produce raw JSON inventories, include only sourceable agency, sponsor, or recognized authority records with a working source URL. Preserve the user-requested field list and field count exactly, such as a 12-field schema; classify agency decisions, authorizations, assessment records, and similar artifacts as `regulatory`; and use notes fields for omitted regions, not-found searches, access limits, or search coverage rather than inventing records or adding unrequested fields.
- Prefer primary sources: company press releases, SEC filings, regulator pages, clinical trial registry records, conference abstracts, journal articles, product labels, payer documents, and official organization pages.
- Independently verify each cited source's legitimacy and claim fit before relying on it: confirm the source owner or authority, canonical URL, publication context, and that the cited passage directly supports the exact statement, population, geography, timeframe, product, comparator, endpoint, and status being reported. Exclude sources that cannot be validated or only support adjacent claims.
- For competitive-intelligence deliverables that use mixed evidence types, classify each source-supported finding in the source log or evidence table as officially confirmed, grey-literature corroborated, or inferred/best-guess. Reserve officially confirmed for source-owner or recognized authority records; use grey-literature corroborated for reputable but non-official corroboration; use inferred/best-guess only when the source set supports a transparent analyst inference rather than a directly confirmed fact. Pair each classification with a concise confidence label and basis.
- For competitive-intelligence audits of promotional, commercial, or patient-impact claims, preserve a claim-to-evidence matrix in the source log, evidence table, or equivalent audit artifact. Separate promotional or ad-source claim text from peer-reviewed, regulatory, registry, or other independent support; distinguish direct PRO, QoL, symptom, function, treatment-burden, or patient-experience evidence from efficacy, safety, biomarker, or utilization evidence that only plausibly supports patient-impact narratives; and avoid upgrading plausible support into direct claim substantiation.
- For KOL, advisory-board, expert-persona, or HCP influence mapping in CI deliverables, define the advisory objective and inclusion criteria before ranking experts. Keep public source-backed facts separate from analyst-derived hypotheses, cite the facts that support each hypothesis, and do not treat authorship, trial participation, payment disclosure, speaking history, or committee membership as private beliefs, endorsements, prescribing intent, or willingness to advise unless a source explicitly supports that interpretation.
- For social-media or KOL-based conference monitoring, treat posts, profiles, reactions, and speaker activity as discovery signals until validated against primary abstract, poster, program, or organizer records. Do not treat a monitored social signal as useful CI unless the underlying conference evidence has been checked, cited, and caveated for missing, embargoed, withdrawn, or nonpublic abstract data.
- For CI artifacts adapted from promotional, social-media, or viral pipeline graphics, preserve the original product or company item set and original ordering as source-audit metadata, not as evidence by itself. Re-verify and correct each row's sponsor, indication, regulatory status, geography, and development stage against current source-owner or recognized authority records; caveat unresolved mismatches in the source log and viewer-facing limitations when material. Do not present a ranked "most promising" or similar list as an evidence-derived ranking unless the source provides a transparent ranking method or the artifact documents a reproducible, cited scoring method.
- For CI artifacts adapted from provided social-media images, media posts, or viral graphics, keep the source artifact context in the source log or audit notes only; reader-facing slide language must not mention the provided image, viral graphic, or artifact context, must not instruct readers how to interpret the material, and after fact-checking must state source-verified information objectively in neutral NEJM-like prose.
- In reader-facing CI decks, avoid internal artifact or source-set mechanics such as "evidence set," "source set," "entries," "15-entry set," or unexplained "set" language when the deck should read as standalone original research. Also avoid "named" list framing such as "the named therapies" or "named products" when it implies a prior artifact or list unknown to the reader. Use standalone product, program, therapy, cohort, or source-scope wording appropriate to original research-style documents.
- For LinkedIn-derived people or team datasets used in biotech CI deliverables, treat profile and company-page data as public, user-maintained evidence rather than definitive employment, responsibility, seniority, headcount, endorsement, or intent records. Preserve row-level profile or company URLs, capture dates, visible source text or screenshot paths where feasible, inclusion and exclusion criteria, deduplication and role-normalization rules, and stale or incomplete-profile limits in the dataset notes or source log. Use neutral scientific language for team summaries; aggregate roles, experience, affiliations, and movement patterns only from cited fields, and do not infer private relationships, capability gaps, hiring intent, or individual motivations unless directly supported by source-owner disclosures.
- For CI reports and infographics, pair displayed claims and aggregate findings with source screenshots and a source-log/evidence-log entry identifying the supporting source, evidence text, screenshot path, and any dataset row or field used.
- When source screenshots are embedded directly in CI report or deck slides, use only screenshots captured after cookie, privacy, permission, modal, and popup overlays have been closed, rejected, or accepted, and crop or scale them so the important source text remains visible and readable in the exported deck.
- For source collection runs affected by anti-bot, rate-limit, access-denied, blank-page, or challenge-page responses, slow the retry cadence before continuing, such as waiting between attempts, reducing concurrency to one source at a time, or using a later manual browser retry after the condition clears. Document the retry timing and unresolved failures in the source log, run summary, or blockers notes.
- Treat consent, CCPA, cookie, privacy-choice, access-denied, blank, blocked, and anti-bot challenge pages as failed captures, not valid source pages. Do not count them as captured sources, do not extract facts from them, and record the affected URL and failure reason.
- Before finalizing an evidence inventory, validate that each successful capture contains readable source-owner body text, a source title or context, and the evidence passage needed for extraction. Exclude or mark as failed any capture that only contains overlay, consent, challenge, blank-page, or navigation text.
- When later retries recover previously blocked sources, update downstream coverage metrics, including captured-source counts, failed-source counts, evidence inventory totals, use-case or insight table denominators, deck coverage statements, blockers, and run-summary files so final artifacts reflect the recovered source set.
- For knowledge-graph, relationship-mapping, or entity-network CI research, define the node types, edge types, inclusion criteria, geography, time scope, and evidence thresholds before extraction. Record provenance at the node and edge level, including source URL, evidence text, source date, screenshot path where applicable, and confidence/classification. Keep directly source-stated relationships separate from analyst-inferred relationships; label inferred edges explicitly with the basis for inference, and do not imply undisclosed affiliation, influence, endorsement, investment, partnership, prescribing intent, or causality unless the source directly supports it.
- For pharma CI/insights role scans, organize findings by recurring workflows and responsibilities. Explicitly separate evidence-backed role responsibilities, automatable or semi-automatable tasks, and human-judgment tasks such as strategic recommendations, go/no-go decisions, investment choices, and compliant claim approval.
- For analyst-coverage CI reports, collect public source URLs for each covered product or asset and summarize the cited analyst stance by product, separating direct source-stated views from analyst-derived synthesis. When outreach opportunities are requested, tie each HEOR, pharmacy, pharma, or market-access opportunity to a visible evidence gap, access question, payer or provider concern, or positioning issue supported by the cited analyst coverage; do not infer private intent or recommend outreach beyond the source-backed opportunity frame.
- For market or competitive-intelligence reviews of PDF conversion, document-processing, extraction, OCR, or related workflow categories, include developer-facing APIs, SDKs, open-source libraries, command-line tools, and self-hosted frameworks alongside consumer SaaS products, PDF editors, browser tools, and office-suite features. - For local conference-data archive apps or abstract-corpus dashboards, preserve source manifests, source URLs, download or generation timestamps, source-file hashes, record-level source links, data dictionaries, validation summaries, extracted tables or sections, and role-specific queues as separate audit artifacts. Label scoring, classifications, and role queues as triage aids rather than analyst-approved conclusions unless a human review step is explicitly documented.
- For interactive CI archive or dashboard pages that load local JSON, CSV, or generated datasets in the browser, include a concise visible loading state, replace placeholder metric text after successful load, and show a clear nontechnical error state if data loading fails. While data is loading, use the page's established loading style and do not render section headers, KPI cards, or empty chart containers until the corresponding data is ready. Do not leave permanent "Loading" labels, empty chart panels, or hidden failures in the delivered page.
- When the user asks to review findings more easily, produce a self-contained HTML review artifact that preserves the source findings faithfully, states source/date/scope metadata clearly, and uses readable opportunity maps, tables, or cards for scanning. Avoid external dependencies unless the artifact requires them, and keep provenance or support data available in the HTML or companion source logs rather than relying on hidden local context.
- Exclude sources without a visibly verified publication date.
- Treat scope as part of the evidence standard. If a source covers a broader or adjacent topic than requested, include only facts that are explicitly about the requested scope; otherwise leave it out.
- For local venue, restaurant, or listing intelligence, treat Google Maps, popular-times, business-profile, and directory data as potentially partial, dynamic listing data unless the page itself provides a stable, cited source statement. Do not invent unavailable fields; label confidence or coverage limits in working notes or source logs; and distinguish listing/map observations from primary-source evidence such as venue websites, menus, filings, permit records, official announcements, or owner-published pages.
- For journal articles, abstracts, conference posters, white papers, or observational studies, treat study design details as evidence-bound scope facts: data-collection period, platform or data source, sample size, geography, population, and inclusion/exclusion criteria must be visible in the source and cited before they appear in the report.
- For ClinicalTrials.gov sources, do not treat `Last Update Posted` as sufficient evidence that the record contains a substantive update. Inspect the Record History and, when needed, compare record versions to identify what actually changed. Include a registry item only when the changed fields are substantively relevant to the report audience, such as medical affairs, market access, commercial, clinical development, regulatory, or similarly specified leadership readers. Exclude records whose recent activity is limited to administrative date updates, site/contact maintenance, or other low-substance changes.
- Treat all statements as claims requiring evidence. Use citations on the same sentence or clause as the supported claim.
- When source-backed claims are requested in executive or audience-focused synthesis decks, cite public source URLs as inline hyperlinks at the claim level. Do not use local files, screenshots, or uncited synthesis as viewer-facing substitutes for public-source support.
- Attach citations only to claims directly supported by the cited source. Do not place article citations after internal workflow or count statements such as article URLs identified, captures saved, use-case rows, or deck methodology metrics; remove citations from self-evident labels, metadata, and unsupported synthesis.
- Use hyperlink citations such as `<a class="cite" href="https://example.com/source">1</a>`.
- When review feedback requests lighter inline citation markers, use a consistent outlined treatment across inline citations: white fill, blue outline, and blue number. Avoid filled blue citation circles when they read as visually too bold.
- When multiple inline citations appear together, separate them with commas so they render as `1,2,3`, not `123`.
- Ensure adjacent citation punctuation is part of the superscript citation run. Adjacent citations should render as superscript `3,4`, with no baseline comma or space between superscript numbers.
- Visible reference sections or slides in the main report must not show local file paths or local screenshot filenames. Use source names, visible publication dates, citation/reference numbers, and/or external source URLs only.
- Maintain a source log in `sources/source-log.md` with citation/reference number, source URL, visible publication date, screenshot path for date verification, screenshot path for each evidence screenshot, and facts extracted.
- When a candidate source is excluded for scope reasons, record the reason briefly in the source log or working notes so later revisions can distinguish "contains in-scope participants" from "supports an in-scope report fact." Keep excluded-candidate screenshots, browser-text extracts, and other working files clearly labeled as excluded or stored outside the final numbered reference sequence.
- Do not paraphrase beyond what the screenshot-supported evidence can substantiate.

## Report Language Rules

- State the report date range once on the first slide, using plain wording in the eyebrow chip or subtitle.
- First-slide report titles should follow the convention `[Disease] competitive intelligence update`, with the disease stated in title case and the phrase `competitive intelligence update` in lowercase unless the user explicitly requests a different title.
- When the user supplies an exact cover title, use it verbatim unless it creates an accuracy issue or cannot be made to fit cleanly; after export, verify the rendered cover title for fit, wrapping, line-height, and overlap.
- Spell out the disease state in the report title unless doing so would force the title beyond two lines; only then use a clear abbreviation.
- Report titles and headlines must distinguish the intended scan universe from observed evidence clustering. If research searched broadly for value-based contracts, outcomes-based agreements, warranty programs, managed access, or similar arrangements, do not title the report as a gene-therapy-only scan merely because public findings clustered around gene therapies.
- For AI, market-access, payer, HEOR, or commercial CI decks, do not title or frame the deck as direct evidence of AI-driven payer engagement, production deployment, scaled implementation, or "what has already been done" unless the source set contains direct evidence for those claims. When evidence is mostly prior conference programming, adjacent implementation examples, frameworks, or vendor-reported cases, state those limitations in the cover title, subtitles, slide headlines, source notes, and recommendations.
- For AI medical affairs or evidence-synthesis decks, separate publications curation, retrieval, summarization, and synthesis workflows from deployable implementation proof. When the source set is mainly adjacent computer-science or data-science analogues, prototypes, benchmarks, or test environments, describe them as analogues or examples and do not present them as production deployment, tested implementation, or scaled adoption unless the cited sources explicitly document live use in the intended medical-affairs or evidence-synthesis setting.
- Avoid ambiguous executive jargon in report titles, slide headlines, takeaway statements, and callouts. Do not use phrases such as "accountable scale" or "AI scale" unless the phrase is clearly defined by the audience or is the dominant terminology in the target source set. Prefer commonly understood executive language such as "responsible adoption," "AI adoption," "AI deployment," "AI rollout," "AI implementation," or "AI governance and adoption" when supported by the deck topic and evidence.
- Avoid ambiguous deictic shorthand in CI deck titles, subtitles, callouts, and source notes, including phrases such as "the topic," "this language," "those themes," "the first," and "this proposal" when the referent is not explicit on the slide. Name the evidence area, workshop proposal, audience, claim, or source-supported finding directly.
- In conference-content decks, avoid ambiguous terms such as "programming" or "GenAI programming" when readers could confuse them with software programming; use precise terms such as "conference sessions," "educational content," "workshops," "short courses," or "prior meeting content."
- When a competitive intelligence deck title wraps awkwardly, preserve the intended visual hierarchy with neutral copy tightening and modest width, type, or spacing adjustments. Do not introduce stronger claims, marketing language, or new substantive interpretation just to make the title fit.
- Viewer-facing executive or audience deck headlines should avoid odd internal phrasing such as "executive jobs" or jobs-to-be-done language; prefer familiar executive language such as decisions, priorities, questions, agenda, or actions when supported by the evidence.
- For executive-facing labels and section headers, avoid preachy or exclusionary framing such as "why this is executive work"; prefer neutral accountability, governance, decision-context, risk, funding, or operating-model labels when those accurately describe the evidence-supported role.
- When review feedback requests no conclusions in a cover title, describe the analysis or scan scope objectively and avoid findings or evidence-pattern conclusions in the title; place findings in summary bullets or body slides instead.
- Objective or non-conclusion cover titles should still sound professional and publication-like. Avoid generic phrases such as "public-source scan" when a more formal analysis title is possible.
- When review feedback says the cover should only state the drug, product, company, disease, or other named subject, remove sponsor, context, deck-topic, report-type, and analysis-scope phrasing from the main cover title. Keep any necessary context in the subtitle, eyebrow, metric cards, metadata, or source log instead.
- When a cover title should include both a named product and topic or context, avoid phrasing that makes the sponsor or company name appear to be part of the product name. Prefer formats such as `[drug/product]: [topic/context]`, or move sponsor/company context into the subtitle, eyebrow, metric cards, or metadata.
- Define all abbreviations used in viewer-facing report or infographic text on first use, especially program, dataset, trial, endpoint, source, and measure abbreviations.
- First-slide subtitles should use the same content width as the title unless the layout is intentionally asymmetric for a user-provided design reference.
- First-slide structure should follow the current example report unless a user-provided design reference overrides it: title and date chip at top, metric cards directly under the title, then a dark `Executive summary` callout with substantive bullets and inline citations.
- For hero or cover slides, do not place a chip above the main title. Put context or date boxes below the subtitle, and vertically center the title, subtitle, context, and component group within the available slide area unless a user-provided reference requires a different composition.
- For cover provenance or retrieval boxes, omit generic labels such as `CONTEXT`; when retrieval timing is needed, write the sentence directly beginning with `Data retrieved...` and style the box as a Cohere-style component rather than a default labeled note.
- For executive or audience-focused synthesis decks, let the cited evidence and the audience's decision needs determine the narrative sequence. Do not force a prior deck structure when the evidence points to a different ordering, emphasis, or grouping.
- When a transition or lead-in slide introduces multiple following example slides, preview the full set of example categories that follows. Do not let a small table or short list imply a narrower scope than the subsequent slides actually cover. Remove bottom coverage strips, pills, or repeated category bands when they crowd the slide or duplicate what the title, roadmap, or following section already communicates.
- For audience-facing implication or action slides, write from the audience's decision need rather than from the source artifact's self-description. Convert source-backed findings into concise implications, recommended actions, risks to monitor, or decision questions, and attach citations to the evidence supporting each implication or action.
- First-slide elements must not repeat the same information across the subtitle, metric boxes, summary callouts, or other visible components. Give each element a distinct analytical role, such as scope/date framing, quantified activity, concrete findings, or implications directly supported by cited evidence.
- Do not over-index on tactical usage examples, workflow details, or feature catalogs when the audience needs strategic governance, evaluation, adoption, investment, risk, or operating-model decisions. Use tactical examples only when they support the cited strategic question being answered.
- For executive AI, market-access, HEOR, payer, or commercial proposal decks, avoid preachy or generic transformation language. Build confidence with concrete, source-supported examples, named decision scenarios, sample artifacts, and tangible workshop outputs such as draft payer evidence questions, scenario matrices, stakeholder maps, claims/evidence checklists, pilot charters, or decision logs. Present these artifacts as examples or workshop products with clear assumptions and evidence limits, not as uncited recommendations.
- For proposal-recommendation slides in conference, workshop, AI, HEOR, market-access, payer, or commercial CI decks, ground each proposal in specific prior-program whitespace, current evidence sources, and concrete workshop artifacts or exercises rather than generic strategic themes. Keep engineering principles, framework or acronym support, source-owner deployment evidence, and payer/evidence standards in separate evidence fields or slide sections so implementation logic does not substitute for real deployment proof or domain evidence requirements.
- Title the first-slide summary block "Executive summary." Make it substantive enough to start a discussion by naming the concrete updates or findings, not merely stating that updates occurred.
- Clearly distinguish summary, explainer, and takeaway sections when more than one appears in the same artifact. Use summary sections for concise reported findings, explainer or methods sections for scope and measurement context, and takeaway sections for source-supported implications or synthesis; do not let labels, placement, or copy make these roles interchangeable.
- On the first slide, place "Executive summary" and the heading of any adjacent key callout in the same top row, with their supporting details, bullets, or evidence text below those headings rather than beside or above them.
- Viewer-facing executive-summary and metric-card subtext must use update-specific wording such as "this update" rather than generic production, audit, or deliverable language such as "the final report."
- In viewer-facing executive-summary bullets, do not mix a company name into a list of product names in a way that makes ownership or category ambiguous; use product-only lists, explicit company/product pairings, or clear possessive/company-name phrasing consistently.
- In pharma, HEOR, or CI decks, when a displayed product line pairs brand and generic names, format the second name in parentheses, such as `BRAND (generic)`, and apply this in obvious product lines and timeline text.
- Do not use an "Access note" label or imply a market-access issue when the cited update does not affect access, reimbursement, coverage, price, or availability. Use a neutral content-specific label instead, or omit the note.
- Do not use generic time-window labels such as "evidence window," "date window," "in the window," or "inside the window" anywhere in viewer-facing report text. When dates matter after the title slide, use concrete phrasing such as "between May 6, 2026 and May 12, 2026" or cite exact source dates.
- After the first slide, avoid non-actionable date wording in slide titles. Titles should communicate the substantive update, not merely that something happened during the report date range.
- Slide titles should state concrete source facts or update outcomes. Avoid weak connective verbs such as "highlighted" or "added" when they do not explain the actual update, and do not use compound titles that imply one source/action caused or highlighted an unrelated source fact.
- CI report slide titles, takeaway statements, callouts, and metric labels should be substantive and formal. Do not use process/source-mechanics constructions such as "[source type] sources reported," "[source type] sources did," or "[source type] sources said" unless the source artifact itself is the update being reported.
- Metric-card labels should describe the substantive activity being counted, such as disclosure type, development stage, meeting activity, access signal, or market signal. Avoid weak deliverable mechanics such as "sources cited" when a content-specific label is available.
- Hero or summary chips should use reader-facing plain-language labels. Avoid internal pipeline terms such as "captures saved," "evidence rows," or "use-case families" unless the audience has already been told what they mean; prefer fewer, clearer chips such as "articles reviewed," "AI examples found," or topic-specific opportunity areas.
- When viewer-facing tables show row counts, define the counted row unit in plain language, such as articles, source pages, claims, providers, or records.
- In viewer-facing table labels, subtitles, and body copy, avoid internal file, capture, extraction, browser, screenshot, or process wording; use reader-facing subject-matter labels instead.
- Do not use semicolons in slide titles. When a title contains two independent source facts or update outcomes, split the idea across title and subtitle or rewrite the title into one clear source-bound statement.
- Do not put periods at the end of slide titles, section titles, card headings, or other title-like labels unless the punctuation is part of a verified source quote or formal name.
- Viewer-facing slide titles, takeaway panels, and callout text must be source-content specific. Avoid vague labels such as "items" or "context"; name the concrete trial, program, source type, update, or finding supported by the cited evidence.
- In competitive-intelligence slide decks, titles and subtitles must be substantive and content-specific, not structural placeholders such as "Items X-Y" or generic section labels. Subtitles should add factual scope, evidence status, or an analytic distinction instead of restating grouping mechanics.
- When a trial, program, source, or other entity is first mentioned in viewer-facing text, include enough cited factual context for a leader reader to understand what it is, such as phase, population or scope, intervention, platform, sponsor, or source owner when those details are visible in the cited source.
- Do not include source publication-date or date-display commentary in main body slides. The report date range belongs on the first slide, while source/date verification belongs in `sources/source-log.md`, reference tables, or the labeled screenshots PDF.
- Do not use process-oriented viewer-facing sections such as "Factual scope," "Source construction," or "evidence construction." These are audit details, not report content.
- Keep source construction, screenshot provenance, date-verification process, and audit details in `sources/source-log.md`, the labeled screenshots PDF, or appendix material unless the user explicitly asks to show methodology in the report.
- Do not give URL audits, source-validation caveats, broken-link checks, or capture-quality notes their own reader-facing slide unless the user explicitly requests that validation detail as report content. Preserve those caveats in methodology notes, source logs, sources/reference materials, run summaries, blockers notes, or working notes as appropriate.
- Audience-facing CI slides should not include internal capture, scraping, browser/tool retry details, or failed-capture counts unless they are necessary methodological caveats; keep those details in methods notes, source logs, run summaries, blockers notes, or working files as needed.
- When methodology notes are appropriate for viewer-facing artifacts, place them inside available whitespace with concise wording, preserving slide fit, readable type, and existing visual hierarchy; do not add or expand a methodology area if it creates crowding, clipping, or smaller evidence text.
- Include a standalone methodology slide only when the user requests it or when the methods materially help the reader interpret the findings, scope, or limitations. Otherwise keep caveats and methods concise in speaker notes, source notes, source logs, run summaries, or other non-primary-deck support artifacts.
- Remove nonessential source-matrix, process-summary, methodology-summary, and audit-summary slides when they duplicate a final References slide or do not add substantive competitive-intelligence insight.
- Keep data-source limitations, confidence labels, missing-field caveats, and map/listing-data methodology out of viewer-facing slides unless the user explicitly asks for methodology or source-quality notes; preserve those details in `sources/source-log.md`, working notes, or appendix material instead.
- Do not create viewer-facing source/extraction process slides, file-audit slides, extraction-discrepancy slides, or local file inventory slides unless explicitly requested. Keep extraction discrepancies, dataset mechanics, and local file inventories in metadata, source logs, or working notes; use cover and summary space for substantive key insights supported by citations.
- Do not add arbitrary numeric scores, grades, or method ratings to viewer-facing report slides unless the user explicitly asks for a scored framework or the score comes directly from a cited source. Prefer concise factual labels or cited source facts over invented scoring systems.
- Do not display qualitative guidance, unordered categories, subjective utility labels, or non-measured priorities as quantitative-looking charts, such as bar charts, ranked bars, percentages, or score-like visual encodings, unless actual numerical data supports the encoding. Use cards, grouped lists, matrices, or clearly labeled qualitative frameworks instead.
- Order quantitative bar charts by the displayed metric from highest to lowest unless the chart has a clear rationale for another sequence, such as time, process stage, geography, or a user-specified category order.
- When the user or review feedback asks to remove visible source notes, process notes, methodology notes, or explanatory labels from a viewer-facing artifact, remove them from the visible design entirely. Preserve necessary support in citations, source logs, reference slides, appendix material, or screenshot PDFs instead.
- Do not add subtitles, body blurbs, or intro text that merely repeats the slide title. Omit them unless they add distinct analytical detail supported by cited evidence; when used, give enough explanatory context to clarify the scope, method, comparator, population, outcome, or why the update matters.
- When a slide uses section-head subtext directly under the title, keep that subtext on one line whenever the available width allows; adjust title/subtext width, spacing, or title-specific type size before accepting a two-line wrap.
- Do not use redundant callout text that repeats metrics or section summaries already visible on the same slide. If a callout is used, it must add objectively stated findings with inline citations.
- Avoid headings that describe obvious data-handling mechanics, such as "Recorded brief-summary facts." Prefer content-specific headings such as "Brief-summary facts," "Regulatory updates," "Registry activity," or "References."
- Keep role labels, legend labels, speaker labels, and entity labels concise. Prefer short functional labels over sentence-like descriptors unless the longer wording is necessary for evidence accuracy.
- Do not allow one-word lines in titles or headers. During page-by-page screenshot review, fix title and header widows with concise rewrites, adjusted width/type, or deliberate line breaks; apply the same orphan-line check to display copy such as callouts, captions, labels, and slide statements.
- In interpretation and takeaway boxes, treat rendered one-word widow/orphan lines as final-copy defects. Revise the wording or adjust the box width, type size, or line-height, then visually verify the exported PDF render before delivery.
- Keep inline citations visually attached to the statement they support; do not let citation markers sit alone in a corner, at a distant flex edge, or detached from the final word. Methods notes should describe the analysis scope, data basis, and limitations objectively rather than saying the artifact answers the user's question or restating the prompt.
- In slide footers, source notes, and citation lines, do not append redundant process phrases such as "screenshot appendix included separately" when the appendix, source log, or reference screenshot manifest already exists. If review feedback asks to delete that phrase, remove the phrase entirely rather than replacing or rewording it.
- On cover slides, omit or delete scope footer notes such as "Scope: historical source-backed analysis..." when the deck title, subtitle, date range, citations, or source log already make the scope clear. If review feedback says to delete a cover-scope footer, remove it entirely and preserve any necessary scope detail in `sources/source-log.md` or working notes.
- For slide-title or hero-title orphan fixes, first use the available horizontal space with title-specific width, max-width, grid, font-size, or line-height adjustments before changing global heading styles.
- For hero-title orphan fixes, use intentional line breaks when needed so the final title line contains at least two words, including moving a word to the next line if that creates a cleaner balanced wrap.
- When reviewers specify the intended line count for a main slide title, make the rendered title fit that count by widening the title block, adjusting title-specific font size or line-height, adding deliberate line breaks, or lightly rewriting while preserving meaning.
- When review feedback requests a long slide title to wrap to two lines, balance the line lengths with title-specific width, type, wording, or deliberate line breaks, and do not accept a one-word first or final line.
- When review feedback asks for a slide title to fit on one line, shorten wording or adjust title-specific width, font size, or spacing before accepting a wrapped title.
- During final render review, keep slide titles and infographic titles to no more than two lines unless a user-provided design reference requires otherwise. Shorten wording, rebalance title width, or adjust title-specific size and line-height before accepting a three-line title.
- Multi-line titles and headlines must have enough line-height and inter-line spacing that characters, ascenders, descenders, strokes, and punctuation never touch or visually collide between lines. Verify rendered browser/PDF output and increase line-height or spacing rather than accepting touching title lines.
- After adjusting slide or infographic title line-height, preserve clear vertical spacing below the full title block so the next object does not sit too close to the title's lowest descenders, punctuation, or citation markers.
- When review feedback flags a large multi-line title as touching or too tight and also asks not to bold it, increase title line-height or inter-line spacing and reduce the title font-weight; do not rely only on shrinking the title text.
- In cards, callouts, and compact evidence panels, prevent body text, inline citations, and adjacent rows from touching or overlapping after PDF export. Preserve the Cohere visual rhythm by increasing local line-height, row gaps, or card padding first; only shrink text after spacing fixes still cannot fit the content cleanly.
- Do not use "says" anywhere in the report. Use formal attribution: "states that," "reported," "listed," "describes," "documented," or similar scientific phrasing.
- Avoid informal slide-title verbs and shorthand such as "puts," "moves into," "broadens," or "new..." in viewer-facing competitive-intelligence reports. Use formal source-bound wording such as "defines," "documents," "is under evaluation," "is identified as," "describes," or "reports."
- In viewer-facing scientific or business copy, do not make the source artifact the subject of the sentence unless the artifact itself is the relevant update. Avoid phrases such as "The PMC XML...", "The PubMed article...", "The abstract reports...", "The article reports...", "The record lists...", or "The release states..." in slide body text; instead describe the supported fact directly with an inline citation. Source-mechanics phrasing belongs in `sources/source-log.md`, reference tables, screenshot labels, or methodology notes.

Treat every efficacy, safety, access, economic, RWE, comparative, investigational, and forward-looking statement as a claim requiring source substantiation, audience-appropriate context, and clear limitations. Preserve fair-balance or risk context where product benefits are discussed, avoid adapting payer or scientific-exchange language for patient/HCP promotional use without explicit user direction, and never state or imply that Codex has made a document "MLR-compliant," "MLR-approved," or legally cleared; instead describe the artifact as evidence-backed and suitable for human MLR/legal/regulatory review.
- If the user or review feedback requests no terminal periods in visible display statements, remove final periods from headings, chips, labels, captions, and callouts. Keep punctuation only where grammar, citations, abbreviations, or sentence clarity require it.
- The final citation slide should follow the current example report unless a user-provided design reference overrides it: use a `References 1-N` eyebrow, title the slide "References", and use a one-page grid/table with source-agnostic columns such as `Ref`, `Source`, `Date / Status / Source Owner`, and `Evidence Used in Report`. For HEOR/value-evidence reference or evidence tables where the field is sponsorship or funding rather than source authority, use `Sponsor` or `Sponsor/Funder` instead of `Source Owner` and verify the actual role from the source.
- References, source-list, and citation-summary slides in competitive intelligence decks should use the deck's panel, card, table, and accent styling rather than plain document-like blue link lists, while preserving readable numbered citations and working hyperlinks.
- On References, source-list, or citation-summary slides, omit bottom notes that merely restate evidence screenshots, appendix PDFs, source logs, or screenshot bounding boxes already provided elsewhere. - In compact source blocks, source chips, or condensed reference panels, keep each reference to one line whenever possible: use a concise source name, date or status, and citation marker or URL; move longer evidence descriptions to the source log, reference table, or screenshot appendix.

## Chrome Screenshot Requirements

Use the Codex Chrome plugin for source pages. For each source:

Open and keep Chrome/browser windows behind the user's current apps, or run source review and screenshot capture in a non-disruptive background/headless browser context that does not steal focus or interrupt the user's workflow.

1. Open the URL in Chrome.
2. Close or reject privacy-choice overlays when available, and close or accept all popups, modals, cookie banners, newsletter prompts, chat widgets, interstitials, sticky overlays, and any other obstructing page elements before capturing any webpage screenshot.
3. Confirm the visible publication date is within the last 7 days, or within the user's documented broader scope for an eligible broader-scope run.
4. Capture a date-verification screenshot from the open browser page with the source date visibly highlighted or boxed.
5. Draw a visible bounding box around the source text used in the report on the actual page when a box can tightly frame the relevant words or lines. If bounding boxes fit poorly, obscure text, or are technically infeasible, use true text highlighting or low-opacity tints that keep the letters readable; do not use broad yellow rectangles as approximate boxes. When post-processing screenshots, use OCR/manual word coordinates or native browser selection/text-fragment highlighting so the mark closely follows the relevant text. Mark only the exact text that confirms the claim, not adjacent domain or context text that does not support it.
6. Capture an evidence screenshot showing the highlighted or boxed text and enough surrounding context to validate the fact. The marked supporting text must be plainly visible in the saved image; do not rely on a bounding box around a darkened, dimmed, or obscured page region.
7. Visually confirm the saved screenshot file before relying on it. If a screenshot contains a privacy or cookie overlay, modal backdrop, dimmed page state, or other obscuring layer, close or reject the overlay on the source page and retake the screenshot. Retake screenshots that contain obstructing overlays, unreadable dates, missing date boxes, missing evidence highlights or boxes, clipped evidence, or hidden supporting text.
8. Save screenshots with stable names such as `source-03-date.png` and `source-03-evidence-01.png`.
9. Include every date-verification and evidence screenshot for cited sources in the final labeled screenshots PDF. Do not include uncited source screenshots unless they are explicitly marked as uncited appendix material.

If highlighting with browser selection is unreliable, inject a temporary highlight style into the open page or use the browser’s find/selection tools, then screenshot the actual page.

## Exported PDF layout guardrails
- When composing use-case cards or similarly dense card grids for competitive intelligence decks, keep card copy short enough to avoid clipping, overlap, or bottom-edge crowding in the exported PDF.
- When page or slide feedback says metric rows, summary cards, repeated cards, or other component blocks sit too close to the bottom edge or page chrome, or extend below the slide or page, first check whether excess subtitle-to-component gap or page-specific header margin can be reduced before shrinking components. If the header spacing is already tight, reduce component heights, padding, min-height, row gaps, or redistribute vertical space so the blocks clear the page edge. For related same-row or same-section components, decrease all affected component heights by the same amount so alignment and visual rhythm are preserved.
- When conclusion, executive-summary, or other summary bullets sit inside a lower or bottom panel, verify the exported PDF at final size and keep the bullet text visibly readable. Do not let these bullets inherit tiny footer, note, caption, or global list styles; enlarge the bullet text and rebalance panel height, padding, or surrounding spacing instead.
- When review feedback selects a cover metric, stat-pill row, or similar cover-summary row and says to remove it, delete the whole row and rebalance the cover title/subtitle/content spacing so the removed row does not leave an empty band.
- When review feedback asks for cleaner number-led stat, metric, or chip treatments, remove decorative dot or bullet markers before the number and let the number lead the chip.
- For paired or adjacent slide components, balance internal content heights visually. When one component appears underfilled or overfilled relative to its pair, adjust component widths, column proportions, padding, or copy density so content lands at comparable visual depths without filler.
- For stacked CI card rows after typography increases, verify the exported PDF for overlap between rows; when row content differs, rebalance individual row heights instead of forcing equal-height rows.
- When side-by-side CI components are intended as a paired comparison, align their outer heights, including evidence screenshot panels placed beside tables, unless the slide clearly uses asymmetric emphasis.
- When adding bottom padding or breathing room to a stacked right-side component, resize the paired left evidence, screenshot, chart, or proof component at the same time so the row preserves matched outer heights and aligned bottoms.
- When matching side-by-side component heights and a reviewer has separately specified table-header formatting, preserve the approved header row and adjust the peer component height, crop, padding, or container sizing instead.
- When a callout or container is over-tall for its text, reduce height, min-height, or padding until top and bottom internal padding appear visually equal.
- Avoid awkward one-word lines in cards by tightening copy, widening the text measure, or splitting overloaded cards across additional space before export.
- Apply the same one-word-line check to card body bullets and list items, not only card titles, headings, or subtitles. Fix awkward bullet wraps with concise wording, adjusted text measure, shared list typography or spacing, or a different card split, then verify the exported PDF render.
- Apply the one-word-line check to cover, hero, and section-header subtitles and other prominent display text after PDF export; fix orphan words with width, line-height, spacing, or concise wording changes before final delivery.
- Keep CI table header rows content-fit: avoid excessive fixed height, padding, or blank space below header labels, while preserving readable label alignment.
- For related tables within the same CI deck, keep header-row vertical padding, label alignment, and above/below text spacing consistent across slides unless a table has a clearly different hierarchy or density requirement.
- Size slide table columns to content; when review flags excessive right-side blank space, reduce over-wide final/rightmost columns instead of letting them dominate the table width.
- Verify the exported PDF, not only the browser view, because pagination and font rendering can expose card crowding that is not obvious in HTML.
- For long slide titles, prefer explicit line breaks that keep meaningful noun phrases together, especially when a requested wrap preserves slide count and existing formatting fixes.
- When cover or section-header feedback flags title/date crowding, rebalance the full header stack together: adjust title width, explicit line breaks, eyebrow/date chip length, line-height, and spacing below the title so the date remains readable and no header text presses into the first content block.
