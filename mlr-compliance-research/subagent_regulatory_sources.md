# Regulatory Sources for MLR Review

Access date: June 3, 2026

| title | publisher | date/version if known | relevance | checklist implications |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| [Office of Prescription Drug Promotion (OPDP)](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/cder-offices-and-divisions/office-prescription-drug-promotion-opdp) | FDA CDER OPDP | Content current as of Sept. 9, 2025 | Establishes OPDP's role in reviewing prescription drug advertising and promotional labeling for truthful, balanced, accurate communication. | Confirm all promotional pieces are truthful, balanced, non-misleading, and consistent with labeling; route potential false or misleading claims to regulatory/legal review; maintain readiness for OPDP inquiries or complaints. |
| [Advertising and Promotion Guidances](https://www.fda.gov/animal-veterinary/guidance-industry/advertising-and-promotion-guidances) | FDA | Content current as of Nov. 19, 2024 | Index of FDA advertising and promotion guidances, including risk disclosure, social media, misinformation, CFL, DTC quantitative claims, and SIUU topics. | Use as the MLR source map; confirm channel-specific guidance applies before approving social, DTC, HCP, payor, misinformation, or risk-heavy content. |
| [OPDP eCTD](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/center-drug-evaluation-and-research-cder/opdp-ectd) | FDA CDER OPDP | Content current as of Sept. 9, 2025; final electronic submissions guidance issued June 24, 2019; eCTD required for in-scope submissions effective June 24, 2021 | Explains OPDP promotional submission formats, Form FDA 2253 resources, advisory submissions, grouped submissions, and response submission resources. | Build submission readiness into final MLR approval: current PI, Form FDA 2253 where required, correct eCTD placement, submission type, cover letter, and archive package. |
| [21 CFR 202.1 - Prescription-drug advertisements](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/part-202/section-202.1) | eCFR / FDA, HHS | eCFR title 21 displayed up to date as of June 1, 2026; title 21 last amended June 1, 2026 | Core prescription drug advertising rule covering established name presentation, fair balance, brief summary, risk information, and misleading presentations. | Check proprietary and established name prominence; ensure claims are not false or misleading; verify fair balance and risk prominence across layout, audio, visuals, supers, links, and adjacent context. |
| [21 CFR 314.81 - Other postmarketing reports](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/part-314/section-314.81) | eCFR / FDA, HHS | eCFR title 21 displayed up to date as of June 1, 2026; title 21 last amended June 1, 2026; source 50 FR 7493, Feb. 22, 1985 | Requires submission of prescription drug promotional labeling and advertising specimens at first dissemination/publication with Form FDA-2253 and current professional labeling. | Add launch/publication gate: capture first-use date, final specimen, current PI, FDA-2253, product/application identifiers, and evidence of timely submission. |
| [21 CFR 601.12 - Changes to an approved application](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/part-601/section-601.12) | eCFR / FDA, HHS | eCFR title 21 displayed up to date as of June 1, 2026; title 21 last amended June 1, 2026; source 38 FR 32052, Nov. 20, 1973 unless otherwise noted | Governs BLA changes, including labeling changes, prompt revision of promotional labeling and advertising after certain labeling changes, and biologics ad/promo submission cross-reference to 21 CFR 314.81(b)(3)(i). | For biologics, trigger MLR re-review after labeling updates; confirm promotional materials match current approved labeling and that ad/promo submissions follow required FDA pathways. |
| [Medical Product Communications That Are Consistent With the FDA-Required Labeling - Questions and Answers](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/medical-product-communications-are-consistent-fda-required-labeling-questions-and-answers) | FDA | Final guidance, June 2018; content current as of Nov. 12, 2024 | Explains FDA's evaluation of product communications containing information not in FDA-required labeling that may still be consistent with that labeling. | Require CFL analysis for extrapolations, endpoints, populations, dosing context, comparative framing, and omitted limitations; document evidence and why the communication does not create a new intended use or conflict with labeling. |
| [Drug and Device Manufacturer Communications With Payors, Formulary Committees, and Similar Entities](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/drug-and-device-manufacturer-communications-payors-formulary-committees-and-similar) | HHS Guidance Portal / FDA | Draft guidance for industry; issue date June 2, 2026; comments close Aug. 3, 2026; date published Nov. 30, 2024 | Guidance for communications with payors, formulary committees, and similar entities, including healthcare economic information and product information for coverage/reimbursement decision makers. | Confirm audience qualification; label materials as payor/formulary use where appropriate; substantiate economic claims; separate approved, investigational, and anticipated information; include limitations and context. |
| [Communications From Firms to Health Care Providers Regarding Scientific Information on Unapproved Uses of Approved/Cleared Medical Products](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/communications-firms-health-care-providers-regarding-scientific-information-unapproved-uses) | FDA | Final Level 1 guidance, January 2025; content current as of Jan. 6, 2025; not for current implementation pending OMB collection decision | Describes FDA enforcement policy for certain firm-initiated scientific information about unapproved uses shared with HCPs. | For SIUU materials, verify HCP audience, scientific rigor, source integrity, disclosures, approved-use context, non-promotional tone, separation from promotional claims, and implementation status before release. |
| [Presenting Quantitative Efficacy and Risk Information in DTC Promotional Labeling and Advertisements](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/presenting-quantitative-efficacy-and-risk-information-direct-consumer-dtc-promotional-labeling-and) | FDA | Final Level 2 guidance, December 2023; content current as of Dec. 11, 2023 | Provides recommendations for numerical efficacy and risk information in DTC prescription drug and biologic promotions. | Check absolute numbers, denominators, timeframes, baseline risk, comparator clarity, visual accuracy, balanced risk/benefit quantification, and consumer comprehension. |
| [Presenting Risk Information in Prescription Drug and Medical Device Promotion](https://www.fda.gov/media/76269/download) | FDA | Draft guidance, May 2009 | Describes FDA factors for evaluating risk disclosure content and format, including consistency, signals, framing, hierarchy, quantity, materiality, and prominence. | Evaluate risk presentation as a design/content issue: comparable prominence, readable formatting, proximity to benefit claims, avoidance of distracting visuals/audio, complete material risks, and channel-specific constraints. |
| [Health Products Compliance Guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance) | FTC | Replaces 1998 dietary supplement guide; page resource current as accessed | FTC staff guidance on truthful, non-misleading health product advertising and adequate substantiation for express and implied objective claims. | For consumer-facing health claims, identify express/implied claims; require competent and reliable scientific evidence; review testimonials, images, omissions, disclosures, and substantiation before dissemination. |
| [16 CFR Part 255 - Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-255) | eCFR / FTC | eCFR title 16 displayed up to date as of June 1, 2026; title 16 last amended May 14, 2026; source 88 FR 48102, July 26, 2023 | FTC guides on endorsements, testimonials, expert endorsements, organizations, and disclosure of material connections. | For KOL, influencer, patient, ambassador, speaker, or testimonial content, verify truthful experience, typicality or clear qualification, expertise fit, independence, material connection disclosure, and platform-specific visibility. |
| [General Compliance Program Guidance](https://oig.hhs.gov/compliance/general-compliance-program-guidance/) | HHS OIG | GCPG; voluntary, nonbinding guidance | General compliance program reference covering federal laws, compliance infrastructure, risk management, and the seven compliance program elements. | Tie MLR SOPs to compliance infrastructure: written policies, training, monitoring/auditing, escalation, corrective action, records, and governance for high-risk promotional practices. |
| [Code on Interactions With Health Care Professionals](https://www.phrma.org/resources/code-on-interactions-with-health-care-professionals) | PhRMA | Updated Aug. 6, 2021; revised code effective Jan. 1, 2022 | Voluntary industry code for ethical pharmaceutical company interactions with HCPs related to product marketing and medical information. | Review HCP-facing programs for business courtesy limits, speaker program controls, venue/meal appropriateness, bona fide services, educational purpose, no inducement, and documentation of legitimate need. |
