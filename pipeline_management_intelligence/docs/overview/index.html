Pipeline Intelligence walkthrough

Using a pipeline dashboard for P&T committee planning

This overview shows how the dashboard helps formulary and market access teams identify upcoming therapies, triage disruption risk, review cited evidence, and prepare P&T committee workstreams before launch pressure arrives.

01 Planning surface

The first view tells a committee what needs attention

The dashboard opens with the current active pipeline scope, high-risk count, near-term count, launch-timing distribution, and disruption heatmap. For P&T planning, this is the top-of-meeting orientation: what is in scope, how soon it may matter, and which assets combine higher budget impact with meaningful clinical differentiation.

Committee use

Use the summary row to size agenda load, the launch chart to identify lead-time pressure, and the heatmap to flag assets that may require budget scenarios, prior authorization language, or evidence review before launch.

02 Therapeutic-area focus

Filter the pipeline to the specialty area being planned

Therapeutic-area filtering turns a broad pipeline view into a committee-specific work queue. In the cardiometabolic view shown here, the asset count, launch timeline, heatmap, table, and selected detail pane all update together so the committee can discuss one service line or disease area without losing source traceability.

Committee use

Use this mode before a diabetes, obesity, retina, oncology, or immunology review cycle to identify which assets belong in the committee packet and which can remain on watch.

03 / 07
03 Risk triage

Convert risk scoring into an agenda queue

Risk is defined as a formulary-planning prioritization signal, not a clinical safety risk. The filter rail keeps the definition visible while the table can be filtered to high-risk assets and sorted by a planning field such as expected launch. This separates assets that need immediate committee preparation from assets that can be monitored.

Committee use

Use high-risk filtering to decide which assets need near-term evidence synthesis, utilization-management modeling, budget-impact review, and medical-policy drafting before the next committee cycle.

04 / 07
04 Evidence basis

Open an asset view before making assumptions

Selecting an asset opens its manufacturer, indication, CT.gov study links, research status, mechanism, launch timing, risk, action, clinical profile, cost, evidence gaps, and management tools. Basis controls reveal how each estimate was derived, while inline source links keep the page compact.

Committee use

Use the detail pane to distinguish official or source-derived facts from estimates. The source-backed rationale helps a team document why an asset entered the committee queue and what uncertainty still needs review.

05 / 07
05 Committee packet review

Sort the table for pre-work, ownership, and follow-up

Column headers sort the review table directly, so teams can organize by asset, manufacturer, indication, phase, launch, clinical differentiation, annual cost, risk, or action without leaving the dashboard. The sticky filter rail and table scrolling keep the review context in view while the detail pane remains tied to the selected asset.

Committee use

Use sortable columns to assign owner follow-ups by manufacturer or indication, group assets by phase, and identify which records should be exported or summarized for the next P&T meeting packet.

06 Planning map

How the dashboard maps to P&T planning work

The dashboard is designed to support committee preparation, not replace clinical or payer judgment. It gives the team a structured way to turn public pipeline signals into a planning backlog with traceable assumptions.

Dashboard signal
Planning question
P&T committee output
Launch timeline bucket
How soon could the asset affect coverage, contracting, or policy operations?
Committee calendar placement, evidence-review start date, and policy drafting lead time.

Evidence gaps
What uncertainty should be resolved before a coverage decision?
Data requests, manufacturer questions, label-watch items, and post-launch evidence needs.
Good use

Formulary planning aid

  • Prioritize committee pre-work from source-backed pipeline signals.
  • Track which assumptions are source-direct, source-derived, or heuristic.
  • Use CT.gov and primary-source links to support internal review notes.
Boundary

Not a final coverage decision

  • Does not replace final label, clinical guideline, comparative-effectiveness, rebate, or net-cost review.
  • Does not infer access impact from trial existence alone.
  • Should be refreshed and re-reviewed as source evidence changes.
