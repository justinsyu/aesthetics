# Tildrakizumab research note

Date checked: 2026-05-27

## Recommended dashboard values

### Consolidated Tildrakizumab / ILUMYA asset

- mechanism: Humanized IgG1/k monoclonal antibody that selectively binds the p19 subunit of interleukin-23 (IL-23) and inhibits interaction with the IL-23 receptor.
- marketed_launch_timing: Marketed in the US as ILUMYA; original FDA approval for adult moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis on 2018-03-20. Latest located FDA label is the 2025 label for BLA761067 supplement 24.
- clinical_profile: Established adult plaque-psoriasis biologic dosed 100 mg SC at Weeks 0 and 4, then every 12 weeks. Labeled safety concerns include serious hypersensitivity, infections, TB evaluation before treatment, and avoiding live vaccines. Adult pivotal evidence supports PASI/PGA benefit versus placebo; newer label evidence includes scalp and nail psoriasis data. Current pipeline records should be treated as post-approval evidence or label-expansion monitoring rather than initial launch.
- annual_cost_range: 65700-82100 USD WAC drug-only estimate. Rationale: manufacturer WAC disclosure lists $16,416.64 per 100 mg syringe; labeled maintenance is every 12 weeks after two loading doses. Maintenance-year approximation is 4 doses ($65,666.56) to 4.33 doses ($71,100+), and first 52-week/loading year is 5 doses ($82,083.20). Excludes administration, rebates, discounts, wastage, and site-of-care differences.
- budget_impact_risk: Medium overall; Medium-High for NCT06611163 if genital psoriasis is added to the label or payer criteria because genital/groin involvement is commonly treated as a high-impact or "crucial body area" even with lower BSA; Medium for NCT03997786 because pediatric expansion would add a new covered population but likely smaller volume; Low-Medium for NCT06488170 because it is observational real-world QoL evidence.
- recommended_action: Prepare for label-expansion review on genital psoriasis and pediatric plaque psoriasis; monitor real-world QoL evidence. Do not treat as a new product launch.
- evidence_gaps: No posted ClinicalTrials.gov results for NCT06488170, NCT06611163, or NCT03997786 as of the check date; no FDA-approved pediatric indication in current label; no genital psoriasis indication in current label; no head-to-head evidence versus current IL-23/IL-17 preferred agents in genital psoriasis; net payer cost depends on rebates and buy-and-bill/site-of-care economics.
- management_tools: Medical-benefit PA/precertification; diagnosis and severity criteria; specialist prescribing or consultation; TB screening documentation; no concomitant biologic or targeted synthetic therapy for same indication; quantity limits aligned to 100 mg at Weeks 0, 4, then q12w; continuation criteria based on BSA/sign/symptom improvement; site-of-care or buy-and-bill controls where applicable.

### Trial-specific field suggestions

#### NCT06488170

- clinical_differentiation: Low
- budget_impact_level: Low-Medium
- disruption_risk: Low-Medium
- recommended_action: Monitor
- rationale: Prospective observational cohort in routine practice; 42 actual participants; QoL/WHO-5 primary outcome through Week 28; not FDA-regulated and not an interventional label-enabling US study.

#### NCT06611163

- clinical_differentiation: Meaningful
- budget_impact_level: Medium-High
- disruption_risk: High
- recommended_action: Prepare
- rationale: Phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in moderate-to-severe genital psoriasis; 206 actual participants; primary completion 2026-01-21; primary endpoint is modified sPGA-G clear/minimal with at least 2-point improvement at Week 16. If positive, could support a high-impact site-specific label or payer-policy expansion.

#### NCT03997786

- clinical_differentiation: Meaningful
- budget_impact_level: Medium
- disruption_risk: Medium-High
- recommended_action: Prepare / monitor pediatric label decision
- rationale: Phase 2/3 pediatric study in ages 6 to <18 with placebo and etanercept comparator components plus long-term extension; 135 actual participants; primary completion 2025-04-07; estimated study completion 2031-08-05. Could expand use into pediatrics, but final long-term evidence remains immature.
