The RFI 02 / 10

FDA is testing whether AI can improve early trial decisions without relaxing regulatory standards.

Where FDA points the pilot

Trial efficiency, safety monitoring, dose selection, and early go/no‑go decisions.

RFIRFI asks for structure, metrics, and governance feedback.
Who is in scope

Sponsors pursuing early‑phase trials through CDER, CBER, and the Oncology Center of Excellence.

Priority Use Cases 04 / 10

The strongest pull is toward safety‑critical oncology and dose decisions, with recruitment and Alzheimer’s as notable branches.

Oncology dose escalation

Metrics 05 / 10

Commenters want the pilot judged by reliability and downstream behavior, not only speed.

Speed, but bounded

Operational velocity

Enrollment speed, data entry‑to‑lock latency, query resolution, time from data availability to AI flag.

Safety

Signal quality

Sensitivity, specificity, false positives, false negatives, alert fatigue, signal latency, concordance with human safety assessment.

Integrity

Audit readiness

Completeness, validation status, GxP compliance, traceability, version lineage, forensic chain of custody.

Equity

Subgroup behavior

Pre‑specified subgroup performance, site‑level reporting, generalization testing, fairness definitions, drift monitoring.

“A pipeline that moves data faster is necessary but not sufficient; the data must be complete, validated, and audit‑ready.” C11

Governance 06 / 10

The most distinctive comments converge on a “permission to act” layer between AI output and clinical action.

1

Execution admissibility

Before an AI‑supported recommendation is acted on, the system must verify current state, authority, and evidence.

“Whether an AI‑supported recommendation is admissible at the moment it is acted upon.”

2

Human signed action

High‑impact AI outputs should require documented, qualified human review before they influence participant safety, eligibility, endpoints, or dosing.

“AI‑enabled approaches should augment, not replace, necessary clinical, scientific, safety, quality, and operational oversight.”

3

Forensic traceability

Audit records should capture the output, model state, data freshness, identity/authority, and why an action was allowed, held, or denied.

“Explainability shouldn’t be a post‑hoc ‘narrative,’ but a pre‑requisite for execution.”

Quote sources: C04, C16, C14.Theme: NIST-aligned trustworthiness operationalized as gates, not just documentation.
Equity + Sites 07 / 10

Several comments warn that AI can accelerate existing trial access problems unless site and subgroup data become first‑class inputs.

Site operating reality

Sponsor EDC, EHR networks, and ClinicalTrials.gov miss screen‑fail criteria, staff bandwidth, source‑document burden, and time from referral to first dose.

“A pilot that draws only from sponsor and EHR data will optimize for sponsor convenience and produce predictions that fail at sites.” C09

Representation by design

Veritas asks FDA to reserve at least one‑third of pilot slots for safety‑net, rural, and Medicaid‑heavy settings and to make fairness a pre‑specified primary endpoint.

“Trust is downstream of transparency, not upstream.” C13

Architecture 08 / 10

Architecture is treated as part of the evidence: modularity, lineage, traceability, and mechanistic reasoning.

C07

Modular AI

Therapy‑specific modules for radiology extraction, lesion targeting, safety monitoring, and dose‑planning support.

C03

Trial orchestration

AI as an operating layer across trial matching, navigation, site workflows, real‑time data, and analytics.

C12

Claim validation

Evidence traceability rate, claim validation precision, and false acceptance rate at the individual claim level.

C15

Controlled evolution

Architecturally enforced PCCPs, deterministic lineage, trajectory hashes, and mechanistic in‑silico trial emulation.

“The architectural framing of regulated AI matters more at this RFI stage than after the resulting rule is drafted.” C15
