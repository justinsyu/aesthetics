WELCOME AND INTRODUCTIONS 
The meeting was called to order by Dr. Capparelli stated that Committee members are volunteers, have been 
appointed according to Public Act (TCA 71-5-2401) which established the PAC. Committee members have signed 
conflict of interest statements on file, and Dr. Capparelli announced that there are no conflicts of interest for 
this meeting. Dr. Capparelli confirmed that a quorum had been established. Members of the Committee, 
TennCare Team, and OptumRx Account Team were introduced.  
Dr. Capparelli explained that the August 2025 meeting was canceled due to lack of quorum and discussed that 
most of those reviews were carried to this PAC meeting. Dr. Capparelli asked the committee if any of the 
abbreviated or low utilization reviews should be extracted for discussion. Dr. Phares extracted the Potassium 
Sparing Diuretics class. Dr. Rhea asked what page in the packet the abbreviated reviews started on. Dr. 
Capparelli confirmed these started on page 159. Dr. Phares asked if the Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension (PAH) 
class was going to be reviewed as it was on the agenda, but the review was not in the packet. Dr. Phares stated 
that he used to have a conflict of interest with the PAH class but no longer does. Dr. Lovett informed Dr. Rhea 
that she could see the list of drug classes for review in the agenda on page 3 of the packet. Dr. Rhea extracted 
the abbreviated drug classes for further review: Atypical Antipsychotics and Antidepressants: SNRI drug classes.

TENNCARE UPDATE 
Ronya Green, MD, MPH, MBA presented an update on the TennCare program. Dr. Green discussed the new 
Dental Benefits Manager. Renaissance assumed care as the new dental provider for TennCare members on 
November 1, 2025. This program will support TennCare Medicaid and CoverKids CHIP dental members, and the 
providers who care for them. No action is required for members. Dental providers should respond to outreach 
and credential with Renaissance. Dr. Green shared a brief explanation of the TennCare Diaper Benefit and 
provided an update for the impact of this program. As of October 16, 2025, 78, 604 unique members have 
accessed the diaper benefit. Diapers are being distributed in 92 counties across the state and in over 420 
pharmacies. 39.9 million diapers have been dispensed since the initiation of the program. On February 15, 2025, 
TennCare enhanced the reimbursement of covered diaper brands for pharmacies. TennCare now reimburses 
covered UPCs based on the brand and size of the dispensed product. Effective February 28, 2025, TennCare 
allows pharmacist to submit claims for diapers not on the Covered UPC list utilizing a Generic Diaper UPC. The 
reimbursement is based on the size of the dispensed product. 
Dr. Capparelli asked if there is a size limitation for the diapers allowed to be dispensed. Dr. Green explained that 
there has been an increase in the diaper size allowed for members. Dr. Williams-Clark noted that there is no 
prior authorization process in the system and that pharmacists should use their best judgement in dispensing 
diapers to the member/family. Dr. Williams-Clark stated that there are quantity limits in place. Dr. Rhea asked 
why the diaper program is being discussed with the formulary committee. Dr. Williams-Clark noted that these 
TennCare updates are intended to provide a broad TennCare perspective to make the Committee aware of the 
new programs and recent changes. Dr. Williams-Clark explained that pharmacists are more accessible typically 
than a general practitioner, allowing the pharmacy to be the best place for program utilization. Dr. Rhea asked if 
infant formula is covered by TennCare. Dr. Williams-Clark explained that some nutrition and supplementation is 
covered for members that have certain health conditions. Dr. Williams-Clark explained that the WIC program 
typically covers formula for infants that don’t have underlying conditions or illnesses that would fall under the 
pharmacy benefit. Dr. Capparelli asked if there were any plans to address the current federal political discussion 
around lowering drug pricing. Dr. Williams-Clark stated that these changes will likely start at the federal/national 
level and that she currently has not been made aware of a direct engagement from a Medicaid program 
perspective. Dr. Williams-Clark noted that the TennCare team is closely monitoring updates and will provide 
additional information as it becomes available.

with other antidepressants, noting that the presentation only mentioned lithium and valproate. Dr. Pollack 
stated that currently the labeled indication for bipolar depression is either for monotherapy or adjunct therapy 
with lithium or valproate, confirming that the indication does not include use with other antidepressants.

Regarding Zepbound, Dr. Capparelli asked for confirmation that there is not currently an indication for diabetes. 
Dr. Lubelcyzk stated that Zepbound is not indicated for diabetes but that it is indicated for obstructive sleep 
apnea (OSA). Dr. Rhea asked if Eli Lilly is funding insurance companies to cover Zepbound for OSA and stated 
that she has noticed other commercial insurance plans not covering Zepbound for the OSA indication. Dr. 
Lubelcyzk stated that they are working with insurances to provide information regarding the OSA indication and 
working on obtaining broader access for coverage for patients. Dr. Capparelli stated that the GLP-1 RA class has 
been shown to reduce opioid or alcohol use for patients with substance use disorder in some studies and asked 
if Eli Lilly has any current plans to examine this use. Dr. Lubelcyzk stated that there are different molecules in 
this class that are being studied currently in phase two trials for different indications and that there should be 
more information by the end of the year. Dr. Alston asked how closely diet was monitored in the clinical trials 
for Zepbound. Dr. Lubelcyzk explained that patients in the study were counseled on diet and exercise but there 
is no data to determine how closely patients were monitored.

TennCare Weight Management Agent Introduction 
Dr. Lovett provided an explanation of the addendum sent out this morning and reiterated that the addendum 
will replace the previous criteria in the PAC packets. As of August 1, 2025, these agents and associated criteria 
have been in effect as interim criteria. Preferred oral weight management agents are available at POS without a 
prior authorization. Dr. Lovett discussed that the increase in utilization rate over the past three months has 
caused concern for exceeding budgetary restrictions. The proposed amended criteria is in response to these 
agency discussions. Dr. Williams-Clark stated that the criteria for indications other than weight loss for these 
agents are not being updated. Dr. Williams-Clark stated that the criteria presented will be in reference to weight 
loss management only. Dr. Williams-Clark addressed the current national discussion surrounding GLP-1 RA 
coverage. Dr. Williams-Clark stated that TennCare is trying to allow access to those members who are going to 
see the most benefit. Dr. Williams-Clark mentioned that other states have rolled out similar initiatives and are 
analyzing results from those states to help update TennCare’s policy. For example, Dr. Williams-Clark noted that 
Virgina rolled out a more general policy that end up being taken back due to budget restrictions. Dr. Rhea asked 
when the proposed criteria would be implemented. Dr. Williams-Clark stated that TennCare started covering 
these agents 8/1/25 as interim criteria but any changes presented would go into effect 1/1/26.

Discussion and Approval: 
Dr. Capparelli asked why the criteria was changed to BMI 30-35 when there is a large population of patients with 
a BMI greater than 35. Dr. Lovett stated that this change is to try to maintain coverage as a benefit for members 
and noted that guidelines mention consideration for weight loss surgery for those above BMI 35. Dr. Lovett 
noted that members with a BMI of over 35 will qualify for coverage if they have a concomitant comorbid 
condition. Dr. Capparelli noted that many patients are not candidates for surgery. Dr. Lovett reiterated that oral 
weight loss medications are covered without prior authorization at this time.  
Dr. Close asked for clarification on the criteria point “Medication will not be used with combination with other 
medications for obesity/weight loss” stating that obesity treatment guidelines address adding on agents if one is 
not effective. Dr. Lovett explained that medications FDA-approved for weight loss are excluded from use with 
these agents. Dr. Williams-Clark states that members should not be on more than one GLP-1 RA. Dr. Close 
explains that Qsymia and Zepbound could clinically be used together and agreed that duplicate GLP-1 RA 
therapy is not appropriate.  
Dr. Rhea stated that it takes some patients several dose titrations until they lose weight and asked if the 
proposed criteria took titration schedules into consideration. Dr. Williams-Clark stated that this was considered 
but is open to any modifications made by the Committee. Dr. Alston asked if TennCare is going put a duration 
limitation on these medications. Dr. Alston mentioned that some members are obese due to other underlying 
factors (hormonal, psychiatric conditions, etc.) and asked if there have been considerations on addressing these 
other variables. Dr. Williams-Clark stated that if the Committee feels these issues can be addressed in the 
criteria, modifications are welcome.  
Dr. Phares asked what would be acceptable for the criteria point requiring “lifestyle modification” and how 
aggressively this will be monitored. Dr. Williams-Clark stated that this was left generalized to try allow the 
physician to make patient-specific decisions. Dr. Phares asked what TennCare would consider recommended 
programs and asked how specifically it would be monitored. Dr. Williams-Clark noted that there is a limitation 
for what can be accepted. Dr. Phares suggested creating a contract for weight loss between the provider and 
patient or within the criteria. Dr. Phares also noted that whatever is required should try to minimize the amount

REVIEW OF DECISIONS FROM PREVIOUS MEETING (GRAYED-OUT PACKET) 
Dr. Boldin presented the modifications approved by PAC last meeting that were rejected by TennCare. In regard 
to the Androgens class, PAC approved the criteria with modification to state “attestation” instead of 
“documentation” of low testosterone levels. TennCare rejected PAC’s recommendation. This decision was based 
on Optum’s current prior authorization process, which interprets “documentation broadly. Acceptable forms 
include verbal documentation via phone, PA fax forms, prescriber attestation, and chart notes. This approach 
allows for greater flexibility in meeting PA requirements. Stricter requirements—such as the submission of 
medical records or chart notes—are only applied when clinically appropriate. These cases typically involve 
medications that may be used off-label or for rare diseases, where additional clinical justification is necessary. 
TennCare limits these burdensome requirements to ensure they are used judiciously and do not unnecessarily 
impact provider workflow. 
Next, for the GLP-1 Agonists criteria, PAC approved the criteria with modification to state “Prescriber attests 
patient is participating in a weight management program”. TennCare rejected PAC’s recommendation. This 
decision was based on clinical guidelines, which recommend that effective weight management programs 
include three essential components: behavioral intervention, dietary changes, and physical activity. These 
elements are considered critical for achieving successful treatment outcomes.
