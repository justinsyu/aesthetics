# Group E Buried P&T/DUR/PDL Insights

Scope: Group E materials under `pnt-committee-monitoring/decision-rationale/group-e`, covering South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Vermont, Virginia, Washington, West Virginia, Wisconsin, and Wyoming. The strongest validated findings came from Tennessee, Wyoming, West Virginia, Wisconsin, South Dakota, Vermont, Washington, and Utah. Texas and Virginia materials were reviewed but did not add stronger buried details than the findings below.

## Top Findings

1. **Final decision mechanics matter as much as committee votes.** Tennessee shows PAC modifications can be rejected by TennCare after the vote; Wisconsin similarly sends committee recommendations to the DHS Secretary for adoption, modification, or referral back.

2. **GLP-1 policy is being shaped by budget pressure, indication control, and renewal thresholds.** Tennessee explicitly tied tighter weight-management GLP-1 criteria to recent utilization growth and budget concerns, with documented 3% month-3 and 5% later renewal thresholds. Vermont reported $16.9 million in SFY25 GLP-1 gross spend while maintaining no weight-loss drug coverage under its State Plan. South Dakota added Wegovy step therapy before Rezdiffra for MASH patients with BMI 27 or higher.

3. **Rare disease and specialty criteria are becoming highly document-driven.** TennCare's Vykat XR pathway requires methylation-analysis evidence plus hyperphagia documentation and response-based renewal. Wyoming limited Elevidys to ambulatory patients despite safety, logistics, and evidence concerns. West Virginia's Cytokine/CAM antagonist policy ties grandfathering to labeled use and availability of more cost-effective biosimilars.

4. **Manufacturer participation rules are narrow and evidence-specific.** Wyoming excludes posters, package inserts, and late materials. Wisconsin looks for new information and head-to-head peer-reviewed superiority evidence, caps written testimony at three pages, and bars direct outreach to committee members on upcoming topics. Washington excludes new drug information that DERP has not reviewed.

5. **Hidden access barriers often sit outside the headline PDL status.** West Virginia excludes samples from prior therapy history, requires more than convenience arguments for nonpreferred combination products, and routes non-reviewed drugs to medical-director appeal only. TennCare's wording distinction between documentation and provider attestation can materially change provider burden.
