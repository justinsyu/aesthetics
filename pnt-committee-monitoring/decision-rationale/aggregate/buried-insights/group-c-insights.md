# Group C Buried P&T/DUR/PDL Insights

Scope: Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, Nevada, New Hampshire, and New Jersey. Massachusetts produced no source-backed findings because the local Group C collection shows only HTTP 403 blocks.

## Top Findings

1. **Anti-obesity access is tightening and fragmenting.** Michigan will limit anti-obesity GLP-1 authorization to morbid obesity on January 1, 2026 while honoring existing PAs only through the remainder of their 6-month term. Montana requires objective MASH fibrosis documentation plus long-cycle renewal proof for Wegovy, Nevada added a specialist-justification pathway around Wegovy step logic for Rezdiffra, and Mississippi's July 2026 notice would prefer Foundayo and Zepbound pending approvals.

2. **Biosimilar displacement is explicit in multiple states.** Minnesota recommended moving Humira to nonpreferred while preferring multiple adalimumab and ustekinumab biosimilars. Mississippi similarly moved several biosimilars to preferred with grandfathering.

3. **Public-comment rules are strategically important and easy to miss.** Montana requires written clinical material at least 7 days before meetings, limits it to 2 pages, and says not to provide pricing information. Mississippi opens registration 30 days before meetings and limits industry to 3 minutes per drug. Missouri DPAC gives 3 minutes per medication, with manufacturers capped in aggregate. New Hampshire requires 48-hour notice and caps written material at 5 pages.

4. **Cost logic is usually process-level, not product-level.** Michigan is the clearest value-based-contracting source, naming active outcomes-based agreements and saying it prefers claims-trackable outcomes and MDHHS-staff tracking. Mississippi explicitly pairs committee recommendations with net cost and supplemental rebates. Nebraska requires quarterly rebate-agreement, compliance, and savings reports.

5. **Preferred does not mean open access.** Montana's PDL says samples, patient assistance, or cash pay cannot be used to bypass criteria or PDL placement for grandfathering. Missouri's SmartPA depends on paid MO HealthNet claim history and other data elements. Nebraska's CGRP and opioid criteria show how preferred/nonpreferred placement translates into step edits and hard utilization limits.
