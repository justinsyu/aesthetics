## Highest-Value Patterns

- **Access criteria often hide the real decision:** Many findings are not meeting-date facts; they are buried PA/PDL implementation details such as diagnosis thresholds, renewal requirements, specialist attestations, prior therapy, quantity limits, and non-covered indication carve-outs.
- **Manufacturer action windows matter before the meeting:** Submission deadlines, written-comment requirements, public testimony rules, and contact restrictions are actionable because they define when evidence can realistically influence a review.
- **Final access signals are downstream artifacts:** Minutes or agendas may flag discussion, but provider notices, PDL updates, PA criteria, effective dates, and agency notices are where access impact becomes confirmable.
- **Cost and rebate logic is visible unevenly:** Some states expose cost-effectiveness, supplemental rebate, budget impact, or cost-analysis referrals; other states hide net-cost details, which should be recorded as an evidence gap rather than inferred.
- **Therapeutic-area criteria differ sharply:** GLP-1/obesity, rare disease, oncology, immunology/biosimilars, CNS, migraine, OUD/pain, respiratory, and infectious disease documents show different evidence burdens and utilization-control logic.
