New Drugs for Review 
1. Alhemo (concizumab-mtci) pens – a tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) antagonist indicated for routine 
prophylaxis to prevent or reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes in adult and pediatric patients 12 years of 
age and older with hemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency) with FVIII inhibitors or hemophilia B 
(congenital factor IX deficiency) with FIX inhibitors. [Not a PDL class - Add to MPPL with PA] Note: Falls in an 
existing MHP pharmacy carveout class 
Proposed Criteria: 
• 
Patient is ≥12 years of age; AND 
• 
Diagnosis of congenital FVIII deficiency (hemophilia A) or congenital FIX deficiency (hemophilia B); AND 
• 
Prescriber attests that the patient has FVIII or FIX inhibitors respectively; AND 
• 
Patient will discontinue use of other prophylactic therapies (on-demand use may be continued); AND 
• 
Treatment will be initiated with a weight-based loading dose of 1 mg/kg on Day 1 followed by a once-daily 
dose of 0.2 mg/kg until individualization of the maintenance dose needed to achieve concizumab (Alhemo) 
plasma concentrations ≥200 ng/mL. 
• 
Length of approval: 1 year 
2. Alyftrek (vanzacaftor/tezacaftor/deutivacaftor) tablets – a combination of deutivacaftor, a CFTR potentiator, 
tezacaftor, and vanzacaftor indicated for the treatment of cystic fibrosis (CF) in patients aged 6 years and older 
who have at least one F508del mutation or another responsive mutation in the CFTR gene. [Not a PDL class - Add 
to MPPL with PA] Note: Falls in an existing MHP pharmacy carveout class 
Proposed Criteria: 
• Patient is ≥6 years of age; AND 
• Diagnosis of cystic fibrosis; AND 
• Patient has one or more F508del mutation or other mutation responsive to 
vanzacaftor/tezacaftor/deutivacaftor (Alyftrek) in the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator 
(CFTR) gene, as confirmed by genetic testing (copy of test results must be faxed); AND 
• Baseline FEV1 and BMI measurements obtained or attempted (measurements must be documented); AND 
• Liver function tests (e.g., alanine aminotransferase [ALT], aspartate aminotransferase [AST], alkaline 
phosphatase, bilirubin) have been assessed prior to initiation of Alyftrek and will be monitored regularly 
during treatment; AND 
• Must be prescribed by or in consultation with a pulmonologist experienced with the treatment of cystic 
fibrosis; AND 
• For 4-20-50 mg tablets, patient age is 6 years to 11 years and patient weight is less than 40 kg; OR 
• For 10-50-125 mg tablets, patient is ≥12 years or weighs ≥40 kg 
• For renewal, attestation of continued therapeutic effectiveness. 
• Length of approval: 1 year

6. Hympavzi (marstacimab-hncq) pen – a tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) antagonist indicated for routine 
prophylaxis to prevent or reduce the frequency of bleeding episodes in adult and pediatric patients 12 years of age 
and older with hemophilia A (congenital factor VIII deficiency) without factor VIII inhibitors or hemophilia B 
(congenital factor IX deficiency) without factor IX inhibitors. 
[Not a PDL class - Add to MPPL with PA] Note: Falls in an existing MHP pharmacy carveout class 
Proposed Criteria: 
• 
Patient is ≥12 years of age; AND 
• 
Diagnosis of congenital FVIII deficiency (hemophilia A) or congenital IX deficiency (hemophilia B); AND 
• 
Prescriber attests that the patient does not have FVIII or FIX inhibitors respectively; AND 
• 
Patient will discontinue use of other prophylactic therapies (on-demand use may be continued); AND 
• 
Treatment will be initiated with a loading dose of 300 mg followed by a maintenance dose of 150 mg weekly or 
clinical rationale provided for the maintenance dose of 300 mg weekly. 
• 
Length of approval: 1 year 
7. Tryngolza (olezarsen) autoinjector – an APOC-III-directed antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) indicated as an adjunct 
to diet to reduce triglycerides in adults with familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS). [Not a PDL class - Add to 
MPPL with PA] Note: Under review for carveout 
Proposed Criteria: 
Initial request 
• 
Patient is 18 years of age or older; AND 
• 
Patient has a diagnosis of familial chylomicronemia syndrome (FCS) as confirmed by ONE of the following: 
o Genetic confirmation of biallelic pathogenic variants in affected genes; OR 
o Genetic test results are inconclusive or not available and the patient has ONE of the following 
 
Patient has a familial chylomicronemia syndrome score ≥ 10; OR 
 
Patient has a North American familial chylomicronemia syndrome (NAFCS) score ≥ 45; OR 
 
Patient has a history of pancreatitis; OR 
 
Patient has a history of eruptive xanthomas; OR 
 
Patient has a history of lipemia retinalis; AND 
• 
Patient has fasting triglyceride (TG) levels ≥880 mg/dL; AND 
• 
Patient has not responded to conventional lipid lowering therapies (e.g. fibrates, niacin, statins, omega-3 fatty 
acids, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors); AND 
• 
Secondary causes of hypertriglyceridemia have been ruled out (e.g. alcohol use, chronic kidney disease, 
hypothyroidism, uncontrolled diabetes, medications [e.g., atypical antipsychotics, beta-blockers, 
corticosteroids, oral estrogens]); AND 
• 
The medication will be used concomitantly with a low-fat diet; AND 
• 
Medication is prescribed by a cardiologist, an endocrinologist, or a physician who focuses on the treatment of 
disorders related to severe hypertriglyceridemia. 
• 
Quantity Limit: 1 auto-injector (80 mg/0.8 mL) every 28 days 
• 
Length of approval: 6 months 
Renewal request 
• 
Prescriber attests that the patient has demonstrated a positive clinical response (e.g. reduction in TG level from 
baseline, reduction in the episodes of acute pancreatitis) 
• 
Length of approval: 1 year

Off-Cycle Review 
1. Anti-Obesity Agents – PA criteria revision 
Initial Request 
• 
Prescriber attests that the patient will not use more than one weight loss medication in this drug class concurrently; 
AND 
• 
Prescriber attests that the patient will not use an anti-obesity GLP-1 agonist (Wegovy, Saxenda or Zepbound) 
concurrently with a medication that contains a DPP-4 inhibitor (alogliptin, linagliptin, saxagliptin or sitagliptin); AND 
• 
Provider provides clinical documentation including the patient’s current height, weight, and BMI; AND 
• 
Patient ≥ 18 years of age; OR 
• 
Patient age ≥12 years (Wegovy, Xenical, Saxenda, phentermine/topiramate); OR 
• 
Patient age ≥17 years (phentermine); AND 
• 
Patient age ≥12 years to <18 years must have an initial BMI per CDC growth charts at the 95th percentile or greater for 
age and sex (obesity); OR 
• 
Patient age ≥12 years to <18 years with BMI in the 85th – 94th percentile (overweight) per CDC growth charts and has 
at least one of the following weight-related coexisting conditions: 
o diabetes, sleep apnea, hypertension, or dyslipidemia; OR 
• 
Patient age ≥18 years (benzphetamine, diethylpropion, phendimetrazine, Zepbound®); AND 
• 
Patient age ≥18 years must have an initial body mass index [BMI] ≥ than 30 kg/m2; OR 
• 
Patient age ≥18 years must have an initial body mass index [BMI] ≥ than 27 kg/m2 but <30 kg/m2 and at least one of 
the following risk factors: 
o hypertension, coronary artery disease, diabetes, dyslipidemia, or sleep apnea; OR 
o This medication is being prescribed for cardiovascular risk reduction in patients with prior myocardial 
infarction, prior stroke or peripheral arterial disease (Wegovy); 
• 
For patients with an eating disorder, prescriber attests that treatment has been optimized and conﬁrms the safety 
and appropriateness of this anti-obesity treatments; AND 
• 
Prescriber attests that metabolic or other reason(s) for obesity/symptoms have been ruled out or diagnosed and 
treated (e.g., thyroid dysfunction, diabetes, sleep apnea, etc.); AND 
• 
Prescriber attests to patient’s absence of any contraindications to use of the requested product, including pregnancy, 
lactation, a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or multiple endocrine neoplasia type II; AND 
• 
Prescriber attests medication therapy is part of a total treatment plan including diet and exercise/activity as 
appropriate for the patient’s ability; AND 
• 
Prescriber attests that patient has been informed weight may return with cessation of medication unless healthy 
lifestyle diet and activity changes, as appropriate for the patient’s ability, are permanently adopted. 
MDHHS recommends that prescribers consider the beneﬁts of a diabetes prevention program for their patients.

MDHHS Updates 
Ms. Bouck reported that the Governor’s FY2026 Recommendations were released in February and are posted at 
https://www.michigan.gov/budget/-/media/Project/Websites/budget/Fiscal/Executive-Budget/Current-Exec-Rec/FY25-
General-Omnibus.pdf . She noted that no changes in either pharmacy benefit copayments or dispensing fees were 
included in the Recommendations. Ms. Bouck also provided a summary of some Medicaid-related recommendations. 
The Governor’s FY 2026 Executive Budget recommends $136.8 million Gross ($53.3 million General Fund) to support 
expanded access to health care coverage for high-risk and high-need individuals who rely on coverage for access to 
critical health services including Vulnerable Adults, Expanded Coverage for Children and Reentry Support for 
incarcerated beneficiaries. The budget will not be finalized until July at the earliest. 
Ms. Bouck noted that the current Single PDL and Brand over Generics, effective 2/1/2025 are posted on the website at 
https://mi.primetherapeutics.com > Providers > Documents. Quarterly, the MCO Common Formulary workgroup 
provides input and recommendations on Single PDL coverage for P&T Workgroup consideration before each full P&T 
Committee meeting. The P&T Committee makes clinical recommendations for both the Michigan Pharmaceutical 
Product List (MPPL) and the subset of drugs on the Single PDL. Ms. Bouck stated that a quarterly Summary of PDL 
changes update is available following the P&T Committee meetings at https://mi.primetherapeutics.com > Providers > 
Recent Changes - MI Single Preferred Drug List (Single PDL). The next quarterly update will list the changes effective 
5/1/2025. 
Ms. Bouck reported that State Plan Amendment 25-0001: Coverage of Prescribed Drugs Authorized for Import During a 
Recognized Shortage was submitted to CMS of 1/22/2025. This amendment would allow the department to cover drugs 
authorized for import during a drug shortage recognized by the US FDA. The deadline for CMS approval is 4/22/2025. A 
Tribal Notice Letter of Intent to a SPA was published on 11/21/2025. 
Ms. Bouck announced two final policies. MMP 24-60 Consolidated Appropriations Act compliance (Juvenile Justice) 
provides coverage of screening, diagnostic and targeted case management (TCM) services for eligible juveniles who are 
within 30 days of their scheduled date of release from a public institution. It reduces gaps in treatment or medication 
coverage for Medicaid and CHIP eligible juveniles. The effective date was 1/1/2025 but facility readiness may vary. A 
phased rollout approach is being implemented that includes assistance with Medicaid enrollment. MMP-25-01 
Clarification of Enrollment Requirement for Prescribers clarifies the limitations that may apply to pharmacies’ ability to 
use Submission Clarification code (SCC) = 13 (Payer Recognized Disaster). This refers to the Medicaid Provider

Ms. Bouck noted two updates to the Medicaid Provider Manual. In accordance with MMP 24-62, Pharmacy Chapter, 
Section 14.15 Physician-Administered Injectable Drugs was updated to include injectable drug products covered under 
the medical benefit and carved out of MHP coverage. This pertains to HIV PrEP and PEP products. Furthermore, 
Pharmacy Chapter, Section 2.2 – Prescriber Identification was updated to add that the only exceptions to the provider 
enrollment requirement must be in compliance with policy and include that the prescriber enrollment in another state 
Medicaid program has been validated, and the prescription is being provided during a declared natural disaster or 
emergency services. The language added points to MMP-25-01 for further details. 
Ms. Bouck announced that the department is streamlining the Fee-For-Service pharmacy PA process for multi-ingredient 
compound coverage. Over 200 additional NDCs have been added to the covered API & Excipient List located at 
https://mi.primetherapeutics.com >>Provider >> Documents >> Other Drug Information >> Active Pharmaceutical 
Ingredients & Excipient Coverage List. Also, a new PA Fax form is being created for multi-ingredient compound claims. 
Once finalized, it will be published on the pharmacy website at https://mi.primetherapeutics.com >> Provider >> Forms 
>> Prior Authorization Forms. 
MDHHS received CMS approval in October 2018 to pursue Outcomes/Value-Based Purchasing Agreements with drug 
manufacturers to address high-cost drugs. MDHHS currently has four active agreements with others also under review. 
In August 2020, MDHHS executed the outcomes-based contract with Novartis Gene Therapies for the gene therapy drug 
Zolgensma. The contract with Abbvie for the drug Mavyret was executed in April 2021 as part of the “We Treat Hep C” 
initiative. In August 2023, an agreement was entered with BlueBird Bio for Zynteglo. And in January 2024, the 
agreement was executed with BlueBird Bio for Lyfgenia. MDHHS continues to review potential agreements with a couple 
drug manufacturers nearing finalization. Ms. Bouck noted that the list of Outcomes/Value-Based Contract agreements 
is posted on their pharmacy website. Agreements that allow MDHHS staff to track outcomes instead of by a third-party 
data aggregator are preferred. MDHHS also prefers contracts where the outcomes can be easily tracked using claims 
data such as orphan drugs where the eligible patient population is small or for drugs where the eligible patient 
population is large, and outcomes are based on volume and adherence. Contact Rita Subhedar 
(SubhedarR1@michigan.gov) or MDHHS-MSAIndustryRequests@michigan.gov to start discussions (See Procedure). 
Lastly, Ms. Bouck provided the website links to additional resources such as MDHHS policy bulletins, web 
announcements, DUR Board and P&T Committee.
