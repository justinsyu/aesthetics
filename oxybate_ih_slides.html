Product landscape and approval status

The three branded oxybates differ on regulatory positioning for IH and on the type of IH evidence that has been published. The framing here is descriptive: positions vary across formulation, dosing schedule, indication scope, and depth of public IH-specific data.[1][2][3]

Evidence and regulatory milestones

The public trajectory of branded oxybate IH evidence has been asymmetric. Xywav progressed from registration trial through approval, extension follow-up, and phase 4 confirmation. Xyrem accumulated later academic evidence without an IH label. Lumryz remains between development and public IH readout in the reviewed sources.[1][4][6][12]

Study-level evidence: Xywav

The Xywav adult IH dataset is the only one in the class that combines a registration-grade randomized-withdrawal trial, an open-label extension, and a phase 4 prospective cohort with objective sleep measures. Effect estimates are concentrated in responder-enriched and uncontrolled designs, which is methodologically relevant when interpreting magnitude.[1][4][9][10]

Study Design & n Key endpoints & results Methodological notes
Pivotal phase 3[1]
NCT03533114; Study 2
 Multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized withdrawal after open-label titration (10+ wks) and stable-dose period; n=154 enrolled, 115 randomized/evaluable.[1] Median ESS change SDP→DB-RWP: +8 placebo vs 0 Xywav (p<0.0001). PGIc worsening: 88.1% placebo vs 21.4% Xywav. Median IHSS change: +14 vs 0 (p<0.0001).[1] Enriched randomized-withdrawal estimates maintenance among responders/tolerators rather than de novo average treatment effect in unselected IH.[1]
Open-label titration[9]
Thorpy et al., 2022
 OL analysis from phase 3 program; safety pop. 154; mITT 115.[9] Reported clinically meaningful improvements on ESS, IHSS, PGI-C, FOSQ-10, WPAI:SHP during OL phase. Early TEAEs decreased from 56.5% (wks 1–4) to 31.7% (wks 13–16).[9] OL design with no control group and response-enriched population; useful for tolerability trajectory rather than causal effect estimation.[9]
24-week OLE[10]
Morse et al., 2023
 Open-label extension after pivotal RCT; 106 entered OLE.[10] PGI-C "very much improved" rose from 36.7% (OLE wk 2) to 53.8% (OLE end); FOSQ-10 and WPAI:SHP stable; new TEAEs decreased over OLE.[10] Uncontrolled extension with survivor bias; supports durability narrative without confirming it under blinding.[10]
DUET (phase 4)[4]
Plante et al., 2026; NCT05875974
 Prospective, multicenter, single-arm, OL multi-cohort; n=46 enrolled, 40 completed; ICSD-3 IH; once-/twice-nightly.[4] ESS LS mean Δ−8.4 (p<0.0001); IHSS Δ−15.5; PGI-C overall improvement 94.6%; sleep inertia 81.1%; actigraphy nocturnal TST −24.6 min (p=0.0035); FOSQ-10 +3.3.[4] Single-arm, small sample limits multiplicity beyond primary/key secondary; LST subtype not separately analyzed.[4]

Study-level evidence: Xyrem and Lumryz

Xyrem has direct adult IH efficacy data, but the body remains small and is not embedded in an IH-specific regulatory package. Lumryz has identifiable IH protocol-level information without public efficacy results in the reviewed sources, leaving its IH-specific certainty currently insufficient.[2][6][8][12]

Study Design & n Key endpoints & results Methodological notes
SODHI (Xyrem)[6]
Dauvilliers et al., 2025; NCT03597555
 Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled (registry indicates crossover elements); ICSD-3 adult IH; sodium oxybate 4.5–9 g/night vs placebo.[6] Class I evidence at 8 weeks: IHSS LS mean Δ−11.61 (95% CI −16.63 to −6.59, p<0.0001); MWT latency +14.75 min (95% CI 9.98–19.52, p<0.0001); ESS improved (between-group estimate not visible in accessible snippets).[6] TEAEs 81.8% vs 26.1% placebo; single published RCT, no broader confirmatory program identified.[6]
Leu-Semenescu (Xyrem)[8]
Retrospective, 2016
 Specialist-center chart review; n=41 IH; mean dose 4.3 ± 2.2 g/night.[8] ESS Δ−3.5 ± 4.5; severe morning inertia improved in 24/34 (71%); mean follow-up 15.8 months.[8] Side effects in 67%; 53% discontinuation over follow-up; pre-approval-era diagnostic phenotyping; confounding inherent.[8]
AASM guideline (Xyrem)[11]
2021 practice guideline
 Evidence synthesis; not a trial.[11] AASM suggests sodium oxybate for adult IH; supports off-label use.[11] Downstream evidence; does not substitute for an IH regulatory package.[11]
REVITALYZ (Lumryz)[5]
NCT06525077
 Phase 3, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized withdrawal; ~150 planned; adults with IH whether or not on prior oxybates/alerting agents.[5][12] No public IH efficacy results identified in the reviewed sources. Primary endpoint described as ESS at week 14; secondary endpoints include PGI-C, IHSS, FOSQ.[5][12] Top-line readout described in company communications as expected in 2026; certainty for IH currently insufficient pending public data.[12]

Evidence gaps

Across the branded oxybate class, several IH-specific evidence gaps appear repeatedly in the reviewed sources. They are the same patterns regardless of product, which suggests structural opportunities for class-level evidence generation rather than only product-level work.[1][4][6][8][12]

Population & phenotype

Who is studied vs. who is treated
Pediatric IH absentLST not separately analyzed

What the evidence shows

Registration-grade studies focus on adults; pediatric IH evidence was not identified. The long-sleep-time (LST) subtype and prior oxybate exposure are inconsistently reported.[1][4]

Possible directions

  • Bayesian-extrapolated adolescent PK/PD and safety programs.[1]
  • Pre-specified LST subgroup analyses in future trials.[4]
  • Stratification by prior oxybate exposure and cardiometabolic risk.[1]

Outcomes &
objectivity

What is measured vs. what matters
Limited objective sleep physiologySparse driving/cognition data

What the evidence shows

Most registration-grade evidence relies on patient-reported sleepiness scales; objective measures (MWT, PSG, actigraphy, psychomotor vigilance) appear unevenly across the dataset, with DUET adding actigraphy in a small single-arm cohort.[1][4]

Possible directions

  • Mechanistic trials nested in routine treatment initiation, capturing MWT, PSG, vigilance, driving simulator, digital cognition.[4]
  • Standardized actigraphy and sleep-inertia capture across products.[4]

Comparative & long-term safety

Across products and over time
No head-to-head trialsRare-event capture limited

What the evidence shows

No head-to-head IH trials between branded oxybates were identified. Long-term, rare-event, and psychiatric/sleep-disordered-breathing safety data come mostly from narcolepsy datasets and short IH extensions.[1][2][3]

Possible directions

  • Multi-product expert-center prospective registries focused on IH.[8]
  • Comparative-effectiveness designs once Lumryz IH efficacy is public.[12]

Product-specific evidence generation considerations

The directions below describe study designs identified in the reviewed sources as candidates for closing specific gaps. They are presented as options for stakeholders to consider in light of their priorities, rather than as recommendations.[1][4][6][12]

Xywav

Mature IH dataset; specific gaps remain
Pediatric program optionMechanistic outcomes option

Gap A — Pediatric IH absent

Design option: Bayesian extrapolation plus open-label adolescent PK/PD and safety program, expanded only if signal and tolerability are acceptable. n: 40–60 adolescents with adult borrowing. Duration: 26-week primary, 52-week extension.[1]

Gap B — Objective performance outcomes

Design option: Mechanistic trial nested in routine initiation. Outcomes: MWT, psychomotor vigilance, driving simulator, 24-hour PSG, actigraphy, digital cognition. n: 40–60 adults. Duration: 12–18 months.[4]

Feasibility

Pediatric: moderate to difficult given rarity and phenotype heterogeneity; mechanistic: moderately feasible at specialist centers.[1][4]

Xyrem

Strong single RCT; replication and long-term gaps
Confirmatory RCT optionRegistry option

Gap A — Replication needed

Design option: Multinational, double-blind, placebo-controlled adult IH RCT (parallel-group preferred; otherwise randomized withdrawal with mandated reporting of pre-randomization response). Co-primary: ESS and IHSS. n: 120–140 for superiority; ~260–320 for active-comparator non-inferiority. Duration: 12-week blinded + 40-week extension.[6]

Gap B — Long-term safety

Design option: Prospective expert-center IH registry. Outcomes: discontinuation, falls, depression/suicidality, sleep apnea, ER visits, weight, BP. n: 300–500. Duration: 3–5 years.[8]

Feasibility

Scientifically straightforward; commercially harder absent an IH-label sponsor pathway. Registry feasibility is moderate via consortia.[6][8]

Lumryz

Pipeline asset for IH
REVITALYZ readout optionComparative-effectiveness option

Gap A — No public IH efficacy yet

Design option: Complete REVITALYZ reporting and publish full CONSORT dataset, including subgroup analyses by prior oxybate use and LST phenotype. Trial already sized at ~150.[12]

Gap B — Comparative value

Design option: Open-label, rater-blinded comparative-effectiveness trial vs twice-nightly oxybate. Outcomes: ESS/IHSS non-inferiority; adherence/persistence superiority; satisfaction; nighttime awakening burden; productivity. n: 150–180. Duration: 24–26 weeks.[12]

Feasibility

High once REVITALYZ is public; comparative-effectiveness logical when payer-facing differentiation is desired.[12]

Sample size and feasibility considerations

Planning estimates for two-arm superiority designs are summarized for orientation only. They are intended to support discussion among potential sponsors and investigators, and would be refined using variance from full original datasets prior to protocol-ready power calculations.[1][4][6]

Consideration 01

Per-arm sizing for symptom endpoints

For a two-arm superiority trial at α=0.05, 90% power:

  • ESS Δ=3 points, SD=5 → ~59 per arm
  • IHSS Δ=6 points, SD=10 → ~59 per arm
  • MWT Δ=8 minutes, SD=12 → ~47 per arm

A 120–140 patient adult IH RCT is therefore not excessive for superiority on conventional symptom endpoints after dropout.[6]

Consideration 02

Timeline ranges by program type

Indicative durations identified in the reviewed sources:

  • Pooled reanalysis of existing Xywav data: ~6–9 months if data access exists.[1]
  • New adult Xyrem confirmatory RCT: ~3.5–4 years end-to-end.[6]
  • Lumryz head-to-head burden/adherence study post-REVITALYZ: ~2.5–3 years.[12]
  • Pediatric IH program: ~3–4.5 years; staged adolescent-first design recommended in source materials.[1]
Consideration 03

Operational levers and design choices

Frequently cited feasibility levers for a rare-disease class:

  • Specialist hypersomnia consortia for enrollment and safety registries.[8]
  • Pre-specified LST and prior-oxybate subgroup analyses to limit interpretive ambiguity.[1][4]
  • Active-comparator non-inferiority margins agreed with regulators in advance.[6]
