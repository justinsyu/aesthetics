Ophthalmology outcomes in public NHANES data

A descriptive analysis of disease prevalence, patient-reported functioning, visual acuity, retinal imaging, and visual-field outcomes using the latest public NHANES release and the most recent ophthalmology-specific modules.

Scope distinction

The latest public cycle, August 2021-August 2023, supports current estimates for vision difficulty and blurred-vision symptoms. Public NHANES ophthalmology examination measures for cataract, glaucoma, AMD, diabetic retinopathy, visual acuity, retinal imaging, and FDT visual fields are historical, with the most recent disease-specific modules in 2005-2008.12

Patient-reported outcome signal

Among adults with severe seeing difficulty, the weighted prevalence of PHQ-9 score ≥10 was 32.0% (95% CI, 25.5%-38.6%); among adults without severe seeing difficulty, it was 12.0% (95% CI, 10.4%-13.6%).

Measurement interpretation

The functioning questionnaire measures activity difficulty even with glasses or contact lenses. It does not distinguish refractive error, cataract, retinal disease, glaucoma, or neurologic causes. The balance item asks about blurred vision with head movement, a vestibular-symptom context rather than an ophthalmology diagnosis.34

Estimates use `WTINT2YR` for interview measures and `WTMEC2YR` for PHQ-9 estimates, with `SDMVSTRA` and `SDMVPSU` for variance estimation.

Correctable impairment signal

Presenting acuity impairment was 7.15%, compared with 2.73% after objective refraction. This contrast is consistent with a measurable correctable component, but the analysis is descriptive and does not assign cause.

Field-test interpretation

The FDT component used a Humphrey Matrix N-30-5 screening protocol with two tests per eye and a final 2-2-1 algorithm incorporating reliability indicators. Positive FDT status should be interpreted as visual-field loss by protocol, not as a standalone clinical diagnosis of glaucoma.8

Visual acuity estimates use NHANES vision examination variables for presenting acuity and objective-refraction acuity.6

Outcome type

These are participant-reported activity and concern outcomes, not utility measures or preference-based health-state values.

Denominator

The displayed estimates use adults aged 40 years or older to align with the retinal imaging and FDT analytic frame.

Use in value research

The items can support burden-of-illness context, but comparisons across eye diseases require prespecified disease definitions and complete-case handling.

Vision questionnaire items were administered in the household interview and include eyesight rating, worry, activity difficulty, cataract operation, glaucoma, and AMD history.5

Methods
07 / 09

Methods were specified to preserve NHANES design and component denominators

Data acquisition

Public CDC XPT files were downloaded from NHANES for August 2021-August 2023 and for the 2005-2006 and 2007-2008 ophthalmology cycles. Files were linked by `SEQN`.

Weights and variance

Interview outcomes used `WTINT2YR`; examination outcomes used `WTMEC2YR`. For 2005-2008, two-year weights were divided by 2. Standard errors were Taylor-linearized with `SDMVSTRA` and `SDMVPSU`.9

Outcome definitions

Visual acuity impairment was defined as better-seeing-eye Snellen denominator >40. FDT visual-field loss used the NHANES final eye status positive category. Retinopathy and ARM estimates used worse-eye derived retinal-imaging variables.

Eligibility and missingness

Analyses used component-eligible age groups and non-missing outcome data. Ungradable, insufficient, unreliable, and missing records were not coded as disease-negative unless the NHANES derived variable represented a valid negative state.

Current-cycle constraints

The August 2021-August 2023 public release was used only for measures available in that release: seeing difficulty, disability indicators, blurred vision with head movement, diabetes status, and PHQ-9.

Replication files

The analysis folder includes scripts for historical ophthalmology modules and current-cycle eye-related measures, raw downloaded XPT files, output CSVs, and this HTML report.

Estimates are weighted percentages with 95% confidence intervals using a normal 1.96 multiplier. The analysis is descriptive and cross-sectional.

Interpretation
08 / 09

Findings are nationally representative within component-specific historical frames

Supports

Descriptive national estimates for selected ophthalmology outcomes among the US civilian, noninstitutionalized population within age-eligible NHANES components.

Requires caution

Historical data years, noninstitutionalized sampling, nonmydriatic imaging, gradability, reliability, and component-specific exclusions may affect interpretation.

Should not imply

Current prevalence of ophthalmic disease, causal relationships, clinical diagnosis from screening findings alone, or longitudinal progression.

Scientific interpretation

Retinopathy and ARM estimates are examination-based historical measures. Self-reported glaucoma and AMD reflect prior communication from an eye doctor and may differ from examination-based case definitions. FDT visual-field loss is a protocol-defined functional outcome.

Value and outcomes research relevance

The data support burden and outcomes context across disease domains, including prevalence, visual function, activity limitation, concern about eyesight, depression screening among severe seeing difficulty, and measurement constraints relevant to real-world evidence synthesis.

CDC notes that NHANES stopped collecting vision and eye health data in 2008 and that ophthalmologic exams were limited and did not use gold-standard dilated examinations.2
