# Literature-corroboration citation notes

Reviewed deck: `current_2021_2023_ophthalmology_findings.html`

## Recommendation

Keep the deck's current CDC/NCHS citations as the primary evidentiary base. Add no more than 4 to 5 literature-corroboration references, and place them only where the deck interprets patterns rather than where it reports NHANES estimates. The cleanest approach is to extend the references slide from 7 sources to roughly 11 or 12 sources, with one compact superscript on each relevant interpretive slide.

## Best places to add citations

### Slide 04 - Age Gradient

Suggested placement: add one citation at the end of the "Interpretation" paragraph, after "other causes."

Why here: this is the first slide that interprets severe seeing difficulty as a functional population measure with likely heterogeneous etiologies. A single corroborating citation can support the broader point that visual acuity loss and blindness are commonly stratified by age, sex, and race/ethnicity in US surveillance work, without implying the NHANES item is an acuity test.

Best citation candidate: Flaxman et al., JAMA Ophthalmology 2021, US visual acuity loss/blindness Bayesian meta-analysis. Use cautiously because it is about best-corrected acuity loss/blindness, not self-reported seeing difficulty.

Neutral citation language if adding a reference entry: "Flaxman AD et al. Prevalence of visual acuity loss or blindness in the US: a Bayesian meta-analysis. JAMA Ophthalmol. 2021;139(7):717-723. doi:10.1001/jamaophthalmol.2021.0527."

### Slide 05 - Subgroups

Suggested placement: add one citation at the end of the "Race/ethnicity interpretation" paragraph.

Why here: the deck is appropriately descriptive and warns against overinterpreting subgroup differences. A self-reported-vision-difficulty paper using the same general question wording can corroborate that sociodemographic patterning exists in other US survey data while preserving the deck's caution.

Best citation candidate: Moayad et al., American Journal of Ophthalmology 2023, 2021 NHIS analysis of self-reported vision difficulty.

Neutral citation language if adding a reference entry: "Moayad L et al. Association between sociodemographic factors and vision difficulty in the National Health Interview Survey: a population-based analysis. Am J Ophthalmol. 2023;255:30-42. doi:10.1016/j.ajo.2023.06.015."

### Slide 06 - Patient-Reported Outcomes

Suggested placement: add one citation at the end of the "Interpretation" paragraph, after "disease-specific mechanisms." Optionally add a separate PHQ-9 methods citation on slide 09 instead of here.

Why here: this is the strongest place for literature corroboration. The deck reports a cross-sectional association between severe seeing difficulty and PHQ-9 >=10; a systematic review/meta-analysis can show that vision impairment and depression are commonly co-observed in population-based studies, while still respecting the slide's noncausal language.

Best citation candidate: Virgili et al., Journal of Clinical Medicine 2022, systematic review/meta-analysis of population-based studies.

Neutral citation language if adding a reference entry: "Virgili G et al. The association between vision impairment and depression: a systematic review of population-based studies. J Clin Med. 2022;11(9):2412. doi:10.3390/jcm11092412."

Optional methods-only citation: Kroenke et al. for the PHQ-9 >=10 threshold. If included, put it on slide 09's "Clinical context variables" card rather than slide 06, so the patient-outcome slide does not become citation-heavy.

### Slide 07 - Risk Context

Suggested placement: add one citation at the end of the "Ophthalmology relevance" paragraph, after "not current diabetic-retinopathy prevalence."

Why here: the slide already makes the right distinction between diabetes history and diabetic-retinopathy prevalence. A clinical guideline citation can support why diabetes is ophthalmology-relevant without turning the NHANES contrast into a disease-prevalence claim.

Best citation candidate: ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, Retinopathy, Neuropathy, and Foot Care.

Neutral citation language if adding a reference entry: "American Diabetes Association Professional Practice Committee for Diabetes. Retinopathy, neuropathy, and foot care: Standards of Care in Diabetes-2026. Diabetes Care. 2026;49(Suppl 1):S261-S276. doi:10.2337/dc26-S012."

Alternate epidemiology citation: Lundeen et al., JAMA Ophthalmology 2023, prevalence of diabetic retinopathy in the US in 2021. Use only if the slide keeps the caveat explicit, because this is not based on the 2021-August 2023 public NHANES questionnaire files and should not be treated as validation of the deck's diabetes-seeing-difficulty contrast.

### Slide 08 - Blurred Vision Symptom

Suggested placement: add one citation at the end of the "Measurement caveat" paragraph, after "not as an ophthalmology-specific diagnosis."

Why here: the item is embedded in a balance questionnaire and asks about vision blurring with head movement. A vestibular/dynamic-visual-acuity citation can corroborate the interpretation that this belongs in a dizziness/balance context rather than a disease-specific ophthalmology outcome.

Best citation candidate: Roberts et al., Annals of Otology, Rhinology & Laryngology 2006, dynamic visual acuity in vestibular dysfunction.

Neutral citation language if adding a reference entry: "Roberts RA et al. Computerized dynamic visual acuity with volitional head movement in patients with vestibular dysfunction. Ann Otol Rhinol Laryngol. 2006;115(9):658-666. doi:10.1177/000348940611500902."

More current alternative: Geisinger et al., Frontiers in Neurology 2024, functional impact of bilateral vestibular loss and oscillopsia. This is useful for modern wording but less compact than the Roberts citation.

### Slide 09 - Methods

Suggested placement: add a small citation after "PHQ-9 >=10 was used as the moderate-or-greater depression-screening threshold."

Why here: this would support a scoring convention, not a substantive ophthalmology interpretation. Add only if the reference slide has room.

Best citation candidate: Kroenke et al., Journal of General Internal Medicine 2001.

Neutral citation language if adding a reference entry: "Kroenke K, Spitzer RL, Williams JBW. The PHQ-9: validity of a brief depression severity measure. J Gen Intern Med. 2001;16(9):606-613. doi:10.1046/j.1525-1497.2001.016009606.x."

## Places to avoid adding literature citations

- Slide 01: keep this as a clean summary; extra superscripts would make the opening feel less current-data-driven.
- Slide 02: already does the measurement-boundary work with CDC/NCHS sources; adding literature here would duplicate slide 10's source function.
- Slide 03: this is mostly deck-specific weighted output. Extra literature citations could confuse the distinction between NHANES questionnaire estimates and outside visual-impairment prevalence estimates.
- Individual metric cards and bar labels: avoid adding superscripts inside numeric callouts. Put citations at paragraph ends or in source notes only.
