What is measured
02 / 12

The 2021-August 2023 public files capture eye-related function and symptoms, not diagnoses

Functioning questionnaire

The adult item asks whether participants have difficulty seeing even with glasses or contact lenses. The file also includes Washington Group disability indicators for adults and children.2

Balance questionnaire

The blurred-vision item asks about blurring of vision when moving the head in the context of dizziness, light-headedness, or balance symptoms among participants aged 20-69 years.3

Diabetes and depression

The diabetes questionnaire provides diagnosed diabetes status. The DPQ file supports PHQ-9 scoring among adults with public examination-component data.45

Not publicly available in 2021-August 2023

Visual acuity, retinal imaging, FDT visual fields, cataract operation history, glaucoma, age-related macular degeneration, and diabetic retinopathy are not available as current public ophthalmology outcomes in this release.1

Analytic implication

The findings should be interpreted as patient-reported seeing function, symptom burden, risk context, and co-occurring patient-reported outcomes. Prior work comparing national survey sources found that self-reported and exam-based vision estimates can differ, supporting cautious interpretation.14

CDC VEHSS documents that NHANES collected vision and eye-health data through 2008; the 2021-2023 public release contains functioning and symptom measures but not ophthalmology examination modules.

Contrast

The weighted prevalence of severe seeing difficulty was 8.23% among adults aged 80 years or older and 2.13% among adults aged 18-39 years, an absolute difference of 6.10 percentage points (95% CI, 3.79-8.42).

Interpretation

The age gradient is consistent with seeing difficulty as a population-level functional measure. A nationally representative NHATS study also found a high objective vision-impairment burden among US adults aged 71 years or older.16

Age estimates use adults with valid `FNQ410` responses and `WTINT2YR`. Adult age ≥80 is top-coded in NHANES 2021-August 2023; the weighted mean age for the top-coded group is documented by NCHS.6

Sex contrast

Severe seeing difficulty was 4.57% among female adults and 2.81% among male adults, an absolute difference of 1.77 percentage points (95% CI, 1.05-2.49). Prior US survey studies similarly reported sociodemographic patterning in vision difficulty.89

Race/ethnicity interpretation

Race/ethnicity estimates are descriptive and should be interpreted with attention to sample size and confidence intervals. The current public dataset does not identify the underlying ophthalmic disease process for observed functional differences.

Race/ethnicity categories use `RIDRETH3`; estimates use `FNQ410`, `WTINT2YR`, `SDMVSTRA`, and `SDMVPSU`.

Functional-affect items showed similar direction

Daily depressed feelings with intensity reported as a lot were 14.94% with severe seeing difficulty and 3.82% without severe seeing difficulty. Daily worried, nervous, or anxious feelings with intensity reported as a lot were 26.06% and 6.40%, respectively.

Interpretation

These are cross-sectional associations between patient-reported seeing function and patient-reported mental-health outcomes. Published NHANES and NHIS studies have reported similar associations between self-reported vision impairment or visual function loss and depression or psychological distress.1011

PHQ-9 score was calculated from `DPQ010`-`DPQ090`; estimates use `WTMEC2YR`. Functional affect items use `FNQ510`-`FNQ540` and `WTINT2YR`.52

Key contrasts

Blurred vision with head movement was 12.58% among female participants and 7.61% among male participants, an absolute difference of 4.96 percentage points. It was 17.79% among Other Hispanic participants and 8.37% among non-Hispanic White participants.

Measurement caveat

This item is embedded in the balance questionnaire and refers to blurring of vision when moving the head. Earlier national studies used related symptoms to characterize vestibular and balance disorders, so the item is best framed as a balance-context visual symptom.1819

Blurred vision with head movement: `BAQ321B`; eligible participants were aged 20-69 years. Estimates use `WTINT2YR`.3

Literature context
09 / 12

Published studies corroborate the direction of the current-cycle signals

Vision difficulty and mental health

In NHANES 2005-2008, PHQ-9 depression was 11.3% among adults with self-reported visual function loss and 4.8% among those without; visual function loss remained associated with depression after adjustment.10

Self-reported impairment and distress

Using 2016-2017 NHIS data, a CDC-authored study found that adults with general vision impairment had higher adjusted odds of mild/moderate and serious psychological distress than adults without general vision impairment.11

Subgroup patterning

A 2021 NHIS analysis reported that self-reported vision difficulty was associated with multiple sociodemographic factors, including female sex in univariable analysis and non-Hispanic Black race in multivariable analysis.8

Severe visual impairment

A BRFSS analysis reported that 5.17% of respondents self-identified as blind or having serious difficulty seeing even with glasses, supporting that severe self-reported vision difficulty is a measurable US population-health construct.9

Diabetes context

NHANES visual-acuity and retinal-image studies support diabetes as an ophthalmology-relevant risk context, even though the current public NHANES files do not measure diabetic retinopathy.171213

Interpretive use

The literature does not validate the current AI-generated analysis directly. It provides external consistency checks for the observed directions: vision difficulty co-occurs with mental-health burden, subgroup heterogeneity is expected, diabetes remains clinically relevant, and head-movement blurred vision has a balance-symptom literature.19

Methods
10 / 12

Detailed methods for replication

Data files

Downloaded public CDC XPT files: `DEMO_L`, `FNQ_L`, `BAQ_L`, `DIQ_L`, and `DPQ_L`. Records were linked by `SEQN`.

Population and denominators

Adult seeing difficulty and disability estimates used adults aged ≥18 years. Youth seeing difficulty used ages 5-17. Blurred vision with head movement used ages 20-69. PHQ-9 analyses used adults with public DPQ data.

Outcome definitions

Adult severe seeing difficulty: `FNQ410` = 3 or 4. Some-or-more seeing difficulty: `FNQ410` = 2, 3, or 4. Youth severe seeing difficulty: `FNQ021` = 3 or 4. Blurred vision: `BAQ321B` = 1.

Clinical context variables

Diagnosed diabetes: `DIQ010` = 1. PHQ-9 score was summed from `DPQ010`-`DPQ090` using values 0-3; PHQ-9 ≥10 was used as the moderate-or-greater depression-screening threshold.

Survey design

Interview outcomes used `WTINT2YR`; PHQ-9 estimates used `WTMEC2YR`. Standard errors were Taylor-linearized over `SDMVSTRA` and `SDMVPSU`; 95% CIs used a normal 1.96 multiplier.7

Missing data and scope

Refused, do not know, missing, and invalid response values were excluded from each outcome-specific denominator. The analysis does not impute missing values and does not estimate disease prevalence for unavailable ophthalmology diagnoses.

Replication scripts: `scripts/analyze_nhanes_current_eye_related.py` and `scripts/analyze_nhanes_2021_2023_deep_dive.py`. Output tables: `output/current_eye_related_estimates.csv`, `output/current_2021_2023_deep_dive_estimates.csv`, and `output/current_2021_2023_key_contrasts.csv`.
