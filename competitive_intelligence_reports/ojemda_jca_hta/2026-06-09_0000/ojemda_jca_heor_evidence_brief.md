# Ojemda / tovorafenib EC HTA JCA HEOR evidence brief

## Executive read

The JCA report uses a narrow comparative evidence base. For the main included comparative question, PICO 5 in patients >1 year with BRAF V600E-mutant paediatric LGG, the assessment relies on an unanchored MAIC comparing FIREFLY-1 tovorafenib data with Bouffet 2023 dabrafenib + trametinib data. The report also documents broader HEOR-relevant evidence activities: systematic information retrieval, trial registry and PubMed completeness checks, a patient-registry search, attempted external control-arm feasibility work, PV/EM identification using literature review/SLR inputs, HRQoL and fatigue outcomes requested in scope, and safety/effectiveness analyses.

The usable comparative evidence is limited. PFS, ORR, and safety outcomes are the main outputs carried into the report. OS, HRQoL, disease symptoms, symptomatic disease control, fatigue, and several composite outcomes were requested but had no relative results. External controls were attempted but not used. STC was discussed as an alternative to MAIC but was not performed. PICO 7 MAIC evidence was submitted but excluded because TRAM-01 documentation was insufficient.

## Detailed extraction by report area

### Background, epidemiology, and burden

- The medical-condition overview frames paediatric LGG as a heterogeneous grade 1/2 tumor group and gives contextual survival and incidence estimates. The report states a 10-year OS range of 85% to 96%, estimates paediatric LGG as 30% of childhood brain tumors based on US data, and cites an HTD-estimated EU incidence of BRAF-altered paediatric LGG of 0.33 per 100,000 among ages 0-19. See p. 15, Section 2.1.
- The burden content is clinically oriented rather than economic. It lists tumor-related morbidities such as seizures, hemiparesis, focal neurologic findings, behavioral changes, visual disturbance, cognitive dysfunction, and endocrine dysfunction. See p. 15, Section 2.1.
- Patient/carer input adds patient-relevant burden themes: chronic multi-line treatment, chemotherapy toxicities, QoL, growth concerns, discontinuation/tapering, rebound effects, access variability, and missing long-term patient-relevant safety discussion. See pp. 92-93, Appendix A.1 and pp. 104-106, Appendix A.4.

### Assessment scope and outcomes

- The PICO table shows comparative results submitted and included only for PICO 5. PICO 7 results were submitted but not included, and PICOs 1-4, 6, and 8 had no included comparative data. See pp. 19-20, Table 10.
- The requested outcome scope is broad and HEOR-relevant: safety, OS, PFS, objective response, generic HRQoL, disease-specific HRQoL, symptoms, symptomatic disease control, response/stable-disease composites, and fatigue if not captured by other instruments. See pp. 21-22, Table 11.
- The report specifies that fatigue should be measured by an instrument capable of generating QALY weights when fatigue is not otherwise captured. See p. 21, Table 11.

### Information retrieval and evidence identification

- The JCA summarizes HTD evidence retrieval sources: HTD-sponsored studies, bibliographic searches for tovorafenib/comparators, study registries/results databases, HTA reports, EMA submission files, and patient registries for paediatric LGG. Search dates are reported for several components but missing for HTD-sponsored studies and EMA submission files. See p. 25, Section 4.1.
- Assessors did not identify additional studies and confirmed no RCTs or anchored indirect-comparison networks for any PICO. Three studies were identified through this process: FIREFLY-1, Bouffet 2023, and TRAM-01. See p. 26, Section 4.1.
- Appendix B documents assessor concerns: missing lists of excluded registry studies with reasons, missing search dates for certain source types, and inappropriate/insufficient exclusion rationales for some tovorafenib program studies. See pp. 109-110, Appendix B.
- Appendix B.1 gives assessor verification searches in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU Clinical Trials Register, CTIS, and PubMed/MEDLINE. See pp. 110-113.

### Included and excluded study evidence

- For PICO 5, FIREFLY-1 and Bouffet 2023 are included as non-randomized, non-comparative, open-label, multi-center studies providing indirect evidence. FIREFLY-1 is the pivotal study for EMA authorization in this JCA. See p. 27, Table 13.
- TRAM-01 is excluded because available evidence consisted of a design publication and abstracts, with insufficient documentation to assess study methods/results and insufficient patient-characteristic reporting for the PICO 7 subpopulation. See p. 28, Table 14 and surrounding text.
- For PICOs without comparator data, single-arm FIREFLY-1 outputs are acknowledged but not discussed because they do not estimate relative effects against scoped comparators. See p. 24, Section 3; p. 28, Section 4.1.1; p. 45, Section 4.3.1.

### Real-world evidence and external controls

- For PICOs 1-4, 6, and 8, the HTD attempted to build external control arms using patient data acquired outside clinical studies. The report says one US database initially appeared feasible but was unsuitable because patient counts with BRAF alterations and adequate R/R follow-up were insufficient. See p. 24, Section 3.
- No external-control estimate is used in the JCA. The HTD concluded that no fit-for-purpose reliable comparator source could be found. See p. 24, Section 3.
- Patient/carer feedback explicitly flags the exclusion of partial or mixed real-world comparator data as limiting relevance for families because paediatric LGG treatment is individualized. See p. 104, Appendix A.4.

### MAIC, STC, and evidence synthesis methods

- With no direct comparative evidence or connected network, the HTD used unanchored indirect treatment comparisons for PICO 5. MAIC was selected because comparator IPD were unavailable and only aggregate comparator data from Bouffet 2023 were accessible. See pp. 52-54, Section 4.3.2.2.1.
- The MAIC reweighted FIREFLY-1 IPD to match Bouffet 2023 summary baseline characteristics. The report emphasizes the strong assumptions required for validity: complete adjustment for prognostic/effect-modifying variables, sufficient overlap, comparable outcome definitions/assessment methods, and related conditions. See pp. 53-54.
- STC is discussed as another possible population-adjusted method, but the HTD did not perform STC because the post-MAIC ESS reduction threshold of over 75% was not met. Assessors note that both MAIC and STC require all relevant PVs/EMs to be adjusted in the unanchored setting. See p. 54.
- MAIC limitations include limited common baseline characteristics, incomplete adjustment for identified PVs/EMs, uncertainty about covariate overlap, small ESS, statistical imprecision, and uncertainty about internal/external validity. See pp. 49-57 and pp. 62-67.
- PV/EM identification drew on literature review/SLR inputs and expert ranking, but assessors could not determine whether the process was comprehensive because reporting was limited. See pp. 54-57.

### Relative effectiveness results

- Table 25 shows that included PICO 5 relative-effect results are all based on unanchored MAIC methods. Included comparative outputs were PFS by RANO-LGG IRC, ORR by RANO-HGG IRC/INV, and selected safety outcomes. See pp. 74-76, Table 25.
- OS, HRQoL, disease symptoms, symptomatic disease control, CR/PR/SD duration composites, and fatigue had no included relative-effect results. See pp. 74-80, Tables 25-26.
- PFS results are presented as 6- and 12-month PFS relative risks and as time-to-event HR/RMST outputs. In Table 27, HR estimates are above 1 for tovorafenib vs dabrafenib + trametinib, with the table footnote specifying HR <1 would indicate longer PFS with tovorafenib. See pp. 78-81, Tables 26-27.
- ORR results vary by assessor method. INV-assessed RANO-HGG results numerically favor dabrafenib + trametinib, whereas IRC-assessed RANO-HGG results numerically favor tovorafenib; confidence intervals are wide and include the null. See pp. 78-82, Table 26 and ORR discussion.
- The JCA excludes the RANO-LGG ORR analysis because FIREFLY-1 and Bouffet 2023 used inconsistent definitions around minor response, creating bias in favor of tovorafenib. See pp. 57-60 and p. 82.
- PFS evidence is affected by possible informative censoring, missing censoring-reason breakdowns in Bouffet 2023, lack of provided RANO-HGG INV PFS comparison despite feasibility, and nominal/non-prespecified p-values. See pp. 60-64 and pp. 82-83.

### Safety results

- Table 28 provides descriptive safety rates for PICO 5: any AE 100% in both groups; serious AE 59.1% with tovorafenib vs 41.7% with dabrafenib + trametinib; Grade >=3 AE 90.9% vs 61.1%; death related to AE 9.1% vs 0%; discontinuation due to AE 13.6% vs 22.2%; interruption due to AE 68.2% vs 72.2%. See p. 84, Table 28.
- Relative safety results are in Appendix C.3. The MAIC base-case OR for Grade >=3 AE is 12.65 [2.30, 69.58], serious AE 3.04 [0.77, 12.10], interruption due to AE 1.15 [0.28, 4.76], and discontinuation due to AE 0.58 [0.11, 3.03]. Any AE and death related to AE do not have relative estimates due to 100% event rates or zero comparator events. See pp. 122-123, Table 34.
- The assessors requested corrected safety analyses because they could not reproduce several naive comparisons and suspected switched event rates for discontinuation/interruption. The HTD later confirmed a data-input error and supplied corrected analyses. See p. 138, Appendix D.2.1; pp. 151-152, Appendix D.2.2; p. 154, Appendix D.2.3.
- Safety certainty is limited because treatment exposure duration differences could not be adjusted for using available Bouffet 2023 data. See p. 62 and p. 83.

### HRQoL, utilities, and patient-relevant outcomes

- Generic HRQoL and disease-specific HRQoL are requested outcomes, preferably PedsQL 4.0/SF-36 and PedsQL 3.0/EORTC QLQ-C30. See p. 21, Table 11.
- Table 25 footnote states FIREFLY-1 assessed HRQoL using PedsQL 4.0 Generic Core Scales, PedsQL 3.0 Cancer Module domains/global scale, and PROMIS Pediatric/Parent Proxy Profile, but results were not available for Population 2. See pp. 74-76, Table 25.
- HRQoL relative-effect rows in Table 26 are explicitly unavailable. See pp. 78-80, Table 26.
- Fatigue and cognitive function were assessed in FIREFLY-1 through PROMIS instruments, but Population 2 results were unavailable; Bouffet 2023 did not report these outcomes. See pp. 74-76, Table 25.
- Carer feedback calls the absence of QoL data a major evidence gap for patients and carers, especially given the chronic nature of paediatric LGG and importance of functional/cognitive outcomes. See pp. 104-106, Appendix A.4.

## Extraction implications for a later infographic

- The strongest evidence-supported infographic message would be about evidence architecture and gaps, not product benefit: one included MAIC for PICO 5; multiple PICOs without comparative evidence; attempted but unused external controls; broad HRQoL/patient-relevant outcomes requested but not available; safety analyses corrected after assessor request.
- Any visual treatment-effect display should foreground uncertainty: unanchored non-randomized evidence, small ESS, residual confounding, inconsistent ORR direction across IRC/INV analyses, nominal p-values, and exposure-duration limitations for safety.
- RWE/external-control language should be framed as attempted feasibility work, not an accepted JCA evidence input.
- HRQoL/utility language should be framed as requested and instrumented but not available for the comparative Population 2 analysis.
