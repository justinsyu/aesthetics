Evidence architecture

Eight PICO questions came from three patient groups and their comparator choices

PICO is the JCA's question format: Patient population, Intervention, Comparator, and Outcomes. The report defined three pediatric LGG populations, then split them into eight comparator questions; only PICO 5 produced comparative evidence included in the JCA.4

PICO 1-4

Population 1: full claimed indication

Patients 6 months or older with pediatric LGG and a BRAF fusion, rearrangement, or V600 mutation. Four comparator questions covered individualized treatment, carboplatin/vincristine, and vinblastine; no comparator data were available.4

PICO 5-6

Population 2: BRAF V600E, older than 1 year

PICO 5 compared tovorafenib with dabrafenib plus trametinib and was the one MAIC included in the report. PICO 6 used an individualized-treatment comparator but had no comparator data.4

PICO 7-8

Population 3: BRAF fusion/rearrangement or non-E V600

PICO 7 compared tovorafenib with trametinib, but its MAIC was not included because comparator-study information was insufficient. PICO 8 had no comparator data.48

HEOR-related outputs

Six evidence-building outputs shaped the assessment narrative

The report did not present cost-effectiveness or budget-impact results; the HEOR relevance sits in evidence generation, evidence synthesis, patient-relevant outcomes, and comparator feasibility.

Epidemiology
Burden and incidence framing

The background reported a submitting-developer estimate of 0.33 BRAF-altered pediatric LGG cases per 100,000 individuals aged 0 to 19 in EU member states.2

Systematic retrieval
Structured searches and completeness checks

Assessors reviewed the submitting developer's search strategies and checked completeness against registries and PubMed.19

Literature review
Variables that could bias the comparison

The submitting developer used literature review, systematic-review records, grey literature, and expert ranking to identify variables that could bias the comparison.11

Indirect comparison
Unanchored MAIC

With comparator patient-level data unavailable, MAIC and simulated treatment comparison (STC) were identified as possible methods to compare separate studies.9

Real-world feasibility
External control attempt

The submitting developer attempted external control arms using patient data outside clinical studies, but no reliable comparator source was possible.5

Patient relevance
HRQoL and real-world comparators

Patient/carer feedback highlighted absent HRQoL results and real-world comparator relevance for individualized pediatric LGG care.17

The practical HEOR takeaway is that the JCA tested whether evidence synthesis and comparator-source work could support relative clinical effects, not whether economic value claims were demonstrated.

Evidence synthesis

The MAIC was used, but the report framed it as a high-uncertainty indirect comparison

MAIC means matching-adjusted indirect comparison: it reweights one study population to resemble another when no head-to-head trial exists. Here it was unanchored, meaning there was no common comparator arm linking the studies.

Why MAIC was used

ConditionReport evidence
No head-to-head or common-link comparisonUnanchored indirect treatment comparisons were described as non-randomized evidence with higher risk of bias than randomized evidence.9
Comparator patient-level data unavailableWith Bouffet 2023 patient-level data unavailable, MAIC and simulated treatment comparison were potential approaches.9
Alternative simulation method not carried outSimulated treatment comparison would have been used only if the effective sample size reduction exceeded 75%; it did not, so that method was not carried out.10

Why confidence remained limited

RiskAssessment concern
Completeness of bias-variable selectionAssessors reported that the process for identifying bias-related variables could not be established as comprehensive and not all identified variables were included.12
Sensitivity analysesSensitivity analyses provided insufficient information to understand remaining uncertainties.13
Nominal testingNo formal hypothesis testing was described, so p-values were interpreted as nominal.15
Effective sample size after reweighting
Small analysis sets limited adjustment

Small FIREFLY-1 analysis sets limited how many bias-related variables could be adjusted, creating imprecision.10

Outcome comparability
Definitions required careful alignment

Additional requests asked for ORR analyses based on comparable RANO-HGG criteria.24

Statistical uncertainty
Sensitivity analyses did not resolve uncertainty

Limited confounding-variable information meant sensitivity analyses did not fully explain remaining uncertainty.13

RWE and HRQoL

Real-world comparator and quality-of-life evidence mattered because they were largely absent

External comparator means using outside-study or non-trial patients as the comparison group. The clearest HEOR evidence gap was that external, real-world, and quality-of-life material could not be translated into robust relative-effect evidence.

Assessment implications

Assessor requests show where the evidence package needed clarification or correction

Additional information requests focused on comparable response definitions, pooled FIREFLY-1 analyses, and corrected safety analyses. ORR means objective response rate; SDV means source data verification.

Response definitions
Comparable response-rate analysis requested

The submitting developer provided objective response rate analyses based on comparable RANO-HGG response criteria after the second request.24

Safety data checks
Corrected safety analyses were provided

The second request asked for corrected analyses on all safety outcomes for PICO 5 because submitted analyses appeared incorrect.2224

Data quality boundary
Arm 1 + 2 pooling contested

The submitting developer argued pooled analyses would mix source-data-verified and non-source-data-verified datasets, while assessors did not agree this should prevent requested analyses.2324

Readout for evidence planning

The strongest comparative pathway can still narrow to a single PICO when comparator evidence is sparse.
External comparator work must be designed as assessable evidence, with transparent feasibility and endpoint logic.
Literature reviews supporting MAIC assumptions need traceable Variables that could bias the comparison and explicit handling of unmeasured confounding.
Quality-of-life and real-world comparator relevance can shape uncertainty even when they do not enter main relative-effect tables.
