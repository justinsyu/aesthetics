Bezuclastinib | Company and registry sources

Cogent reported GIST launch planning, and ClinicalTrials.gov listed a bezuclastinib expanded-access program12

The Cogent source was dated May 5, 2026, and the expanded-access record displayed a May 8, 2026 last-update date.12

Launch planning

Bezuclastinib

Cogent stated that it was planning for dual launches of bezuclastinib in systemic mastocytosis and GIST.1

ASCO

PEAK

Cogent stated that pivotal data from the Phase 3 PEAK trial in GIST patients had been selected for oral presentation at the 2026 ASCO Annual Meeting.1

Funding

Cash runway

Cogent reported ending first-quarter 2026 with $866.4 million in cash, which it stated was sufficient to fund operations into 2028.1

Expanded access record

  • ClinicalTrials.gov listed NCT06948955 as available expanded access sponsored by Cogent Biosciences for bezuclastinib coadministered with sunitinib in GIST.2
  • The registry summary stated that the program provides investigational bezuclastinib with sunitinib to GIST patients with no comparable or satisfactory alternative therapy options.2
  • The registry summary stated that the combination provides broad inhibition of primary and secondary KIT mutations that commonly occur in GIST.2

Published evidence | Case report

A peer-reviewed case report described high-risk gastric GIST in a large hiatal hernia7

The International Medical Case Reports Journal article displayed a May 7, 2026 publication date.7

Presentation

Large hiatal hernia

The report stated that CT showed a large hiatal hernia with an 8.4 x 6.5 cm heterogeneous mass arising from herniated and non-herniated gastric wall and extending into the esophageal lumen.7

Treatment

Adjuvant imatinib

The report stated that surgical resection confirmed high-risk GIST and that the patient received adjuvant imatinib 400 mg daily.7

Follow-up

Recurrent lesion

The report stated that follow-up CT on August 10, 2025 showed a 6.2 x 6.0 cm recurrent lesion.7

Article conclusion

  • The article concluded that a hiatal hernia with mass should prompt suspicion for malignancy and that GIST should remain an important differential diagnosis.7
  • The article stated that immunohistochemistry was essential for definitive diagnosis in the case and that complete surgical resection, consideration of adjuvant therapy, and radiological surveillance were needed for high-risk disease.7
