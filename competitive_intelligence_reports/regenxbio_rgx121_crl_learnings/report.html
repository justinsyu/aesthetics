Regenxbio's RGX-121 CRL was a foreseeable evidence-risk event

FDA's February 7, 2026 Complete Response Letter for the Hunter syndrome gene therapy focused on phenotype definition, external-control comparability, and the evidentiary bridge from CSF HS D2S6 to cognitive benefit.

Decision
CRL, not approval

BLA STN BL 125840/0 for clemidsogene lanparvovec, announced by Regenxbio on February 9, 2026.

Pivotal package
13 patients

Single-arm RGX-121-101 Part 2, with a novel CSF biomarker and natural-history external control.

Judgment
Foreseeable

Not guaranteed, but FDA says it had warned on the same concerns through multiple development meetings.

Source basis: FDA public CRL, FDA guidance, company releases, and precedent program releases.June 2, 2026
Finding 1 | What FDA rejected

The CRL was not mainly a manufacturing or safety rejection; it was an efficacy-evidence rejection.

FDA could not conclude the single-arm study was adequate and well controlled.The agency evaluated the totality of data but said the package did not provide substantial evidence of effectiveness.
The three central gaps were population, control, and surrogate endpoint.These same issues were raised before the BLA and were explicitly characterized as potential review issues.
Safety was a future-resubmission concern, not the core approval blocker.The CRL includes a vector-integration safety update request related to RGX-111, but labels it as additional comment.

Finding 2 | Specific deficiencies

FDA's objections were mutually reinforcing: weak phenotype definition made the control and biomarker threshold less interpretable.

Finding 3 | Company framing vs FDA language

Regenxbio disclosed the same issue cluster, but the FDA CRL is materially sharper than the company summary.

Regenxbio's release accurately identified the CRL topics: neuronopathic eligibility, natural-history comparability, and CSF HS D2S6. The FDA letter adds the review-history context, the specific threshold-validity concern, and the conclusion that even the narrower neuronopathic indication lacked substantial evidence.

CI learning: use the FDA CRL where available. Company releases often describe the issue cluster but soften the regulatory standard and the sequence of prior warnings.

Finding 4 | Regulatory playbook

FDA's public guidance already highlighted the two hardest parts of RGX-121's evidence plan.

Finding 5 | Same-disease precedent

Hunter syndrome CNS outcomes were already known to be difficult even in a controlled trial.

Shire's intrathecal idursulfase program missed its cognition and adaptive-behavior endpoints.This was a controlled, randomized, open-label, assessor-blinded Phase II/III study in children with Hunter syndrome and cognitive impairment.
That precedent should have raised the evidentiary bar for a biomarker-first, single-arm RGX-121 filing.The lesson was that neurodevelopmental benefit would be hard to infer without robust controls and longer observation.

Finding 6 | Broader precedent

Other programs showed that rare-disease flexibility and prior alignment do not lock in approval.

S7. Atara release, January 12, 2026. FDA may reappraise single-arm adequacy and interpretability after earlier alignment.

S8. BioMarin release, August 19, 2020. AAV gene-therapy packages can face late demands for stronger durability and follow-up evidence.

The learning is structural: a small single-arm package with a novel surrogate is vulnerable if FDA cannot independently verify the control match, endpoint threshold, and clinical-meaning bridge.

What the sponsor could have done

The avoidable risk was not one missing paper; it was filing before the weakest assumptions were stress-tested.

Risk area
Earlier mitigation
Why it could have mattered
Phenotype
Use an adjudication committee and multiple pre-defined factors to classify neuronopathic disease.
FDA explicitly recommended this path to improve interpretability in a future study.
External control
Prospectively match or restrict untreated controls by age, stage, cognitive test, disease features, and sampling windows.
The proposed natural-history control was undermined by non-comparability.
CSF HS D2S6
Externally validate the threshold with more MPS II samples and show relationship to cognitive trajectory.
FDA said the threshold was based on too few relevant external MPS II patients and lacked validation.
Follow-up
Continue neurodevelopmental observation to ages where plateau or decline can be evaluated.
FDA said most Part 2 patients had only 52 weeks of follow-up, too short for cognitive interpretation.
Confounding
Pre-specify ERT, immunosuppression, CSF route, hearing, and missing-covariate analyses.
The CRL calls out all of these as interpretability problems or future-resubmission issues.

Could this have been unknowable?

No. The exact CRL outcome was uncertain, but the risk pattern was visible before filing.

Not foreseeable
Weak claim

FDA says the relevant concerns were raised through Pre-IND, IND, and BLA interactions.

Foreseeable
Strong claim

Public guidance and precedents pointed to the same issues: external controls, surrogate endpoints, and single-arm evidence.

Preventable
Partly

No sponsor can eliminate rare-disease uncertainty, but it could have reduced the filing-risk profile materially.

Best concise answer: the CRL was not inevitable, but it was foreseeable enough that a sponsor-side red-team should have highlighted it as a major approval risk before submission.
