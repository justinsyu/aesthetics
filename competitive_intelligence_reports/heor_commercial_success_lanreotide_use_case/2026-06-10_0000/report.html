Executive summary

  • Commercial relevance came from reframing the comparison from labeled acquisition cost to observed utilization, preparation time, and injection-route assumptions.1
  • The modeled direction of the budget result reversed when octreotide was constrained to its labeled 30 mg every 4 weeks dose.1

Model structure | Hospital perspective

The model translated utilization share into hospital budget impact

Ortendahl et al. evaluated annual costs after shifting modeled SSA utilization from a predominantly octreotide mix to a higher lanreotide share.

Population

500 GEP-NET patients

The model began with 500 GEP-NET patients; 313 were estimated to have metastatic disease and receive an SSA.1

Share shift

Lanreotide 5% -> 30%

The alternative scenario changed utilization from 5% lanreotide and 95% octreotide to 30% lanreotide and 70% octreotide.1

Cost categories

Acquisition, preparation, administration

The model included drug costs plus preparation and administration time; it also explored failed IM injection in a scenario analysis.1

Reported base-case result

The modeled per-patient annual cost was $83,473 for lanreotide and $89,673 for octreotide; the modeled utilization shift reduced annual hospital costs by $488,615.1

03 / 10
Decisive assumption | Dosing

The budget result depended on real-world octreotide use exceeding labeled dosing

The model used labeled maximum dosing for lanreotide but incorporated above-label dose or frequency for octreotide.

Base case

64% at labeled maximum; 36% above label or more frequent

Ortendahl et al. assumed 64% of octreotide patients received 30 mg every 4 weeks, 18% received 40 or 60 mg every 4 weeks, and 18% received more frequent injections.1

Sensitivity result

Label-only octreotide dosing reversed the budget direction

When all octreotide patients were modeled at 30 mg once every 4 weeks, the same utilization shift increased costs by $878,489 instead of producing savings.1

The result does not establish clinical superiority. It demonstrates that economic conclusions can change when modeled dosing reflects observed utilization rather than label-only assumptions.

Operational pathway | Administration

Route and preparation created a second commercial-use argument

The HEOR case combined dose utilization with administration mechanics, including preparation time and a scenario involving failed IM injection.

Deep SC route

Lanreotide label

Somatuline Depot is administered as 120 mg every 4 weeks by deep subcutaneous injection for GEP-NETs.2

IM route

Octreotide LAR label

Sandostatin LAR Depot is administered intramuscularly in the gluteal region, with needle considerations for patients with greater skin-to-muscle depth.3

Scenario analysis

Failed IM injection

At a 25% failed IM injection rate requiring repeat injection, the model reported $2.24 million in annual savings with the utilization shift.1

Evidence basis for the injection-failure scenario

Boyd et al. evaluated intended gluteal IM depot octreotide injections using CT and reported improved success after nurse instruction; Ortendahl et al. used that study to construct the failed-injection scenario.4,1

Post-publication RWE | Treatment patterns

Later claims evidence reported treatment-pattern differences between long-acting SSAs

Clarke et al. evaluated commercially insured and Medicare supplemental patients newly treated with long-acting SSAs from 2015 to 2020.

Sample

241 lanreotide and 521 octreotide LAR patients

The US MarketScan analysis compared patients initiating lanreotide or octreotide LAR.5

Pattern

Lower rescue-medication and escalation measures

The authors reported lower rescue medication use, fewer dose escalations, and lower above-label dose use among lanreotide patients.5

Limitations

Observational design

Claims-based analyses can be affected by residual confounding, coding limits, and dose-estimation uncertainty; Clarke et al. reported Ipsen funding.5

Interpretation for the use case

Later RWE may support the plausibility of differential real-world treatment patterns, but it does not remove the need to disclose funding, methods, and sensitivity of conclusions to modeled assumptions.5

Balance | Countermodel

A contemporaneous payer model reached the opposite cost conclusion under label-dose assumptions

Ayyagari et al. compared octreotide LAR 30 mg with lanreotide 120 mg in metastatic gastrointestinal NETs from a payer perspective.

Ayyagari 2017

Octreotide lower-cost conclusion

The analysis reported lower treatment costs for octreotide LAR than lanreotide over 1-, 3-, and 5-year horizons, with drug acquisition cost as the main driver.6

Interpretive control

Assumptions determine the commercial evidence narrative

The countermodel illustrates that HEOR findings can support different commercial messages when dosing, perspective, administration burden, and injection-failure assumptions differ.6,1

Perspective

Hospital vs payer

Ortendahl used a hospital perspective; Ayyagari used a payer perspective.1,6

Funding

Ipsen and Novartis

Ortendahl reported Ipsen funding; Ayyagari reported Novartis funding.1,6

Use

Source-backed comparison

Both studies can be used to discuss how assumption selection shapes modeled economic value claims.1,6

Scientific guardrails | Commercial interpretation

HEOR can be commercially material when assumptions are explicit and stress-tested

The use case supports a disciplined commercial-evidence framework rather than a causal claim that HEOR alone produced product success.

1. Define the comparator

Indication and route matter

The comparison is lanreotide deep SC versus octreotide LAR IM in metastatic GEP-NET, including pancreatic NET, not pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.1,2

2. Separate label and RWE

Real-world utilization drove the result

The modeled savings depended on octreotide dose escalation and frequency assumptions; label-only dosing reversed the direction.1

3. Disclose uncertainty

Sponsor and scenario context

Funding source, model perspective, pricing inputs, and injection-failure assumptions are necessary context for interpreting the findings.1,6

Use-case conclusion

The lanreotide case illustrates how HEOR can support commercial value communication by quantifying real-world utilization and operational costs, provided that the analysis is presented with transparent assumptions, limitations, and counter-evidence.1,6
