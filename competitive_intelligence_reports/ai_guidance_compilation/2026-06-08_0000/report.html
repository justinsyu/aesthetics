Executive summary

  • Regulators are converging on context of use, lifecycle risk management, data governance, transparency, performance monitoring, and controlled change management for AI-enabled devices and AI-supported medicine development.1246
  • HTA guidance treats AI-supported evidence as acceptable only when the user justifies the method, declares AI use, preserves human accountability, validates outputs, and manages bias, cybersecurity, licensing, and real-world-data suitability risks.911
  • Professional and methods organizations provide checklists and guidance for AI-assisted evidence synthesis, HEOR, AI clinical trials, early clinical evaluation, diagnostics, and prediction models.121314151617

02 / 08
Regulators

Medicines regulators distinguish AI-supported evidence from AI-enabled device software

FDA and EMA materials frame AI in medicines around credibility of submitted evidence and lifecycle governance; FDA, Health Canada, MHRA, and IMDRF materials frame AI-enabled devices around total-product-lifecycle controls.

FDA drugs and biologics

Context-of-use credibility

FDA's drug and biologic draft guidance addresses AI-generated information used to support regulatory decisions about safety, effectiveness, or quality, and requests credibility evidence proportional to the model's context and risk.1

FDA devices

Total product lifecycle

FDA's AI-enabled device software draft guidance covers marketing submission documentation, design, development, validation, maintenance, transparency, bias, and performance monitoring across the device lifecycle.2

FDA and EMA

Shared medicine principles

FDA CDER/CBER and EMA jointly identified ten good AI practice principles for drug development, with emphasis on patient safety, reliable information, and use across the medicine lifecycle.34

EMA and HMA

Medicinal product lifecycle

EMA states that its AI reflection paper helps medicine developers and applicants use AI and machine learning safely and effectively across lifecycle stages, within EU AI, data protection, and medicines requirements.4

Health Canada

ML-enabled medical devices

Health Canada's 2026 pre-market guidance defines ML-enabled medical devices, emphasizes transparency as safety-relevant, and introduces predetermined change control plans for planned ML-system changes.6

MHRA

AI as medical device

MHRA's SaMD and AIaMD materials address qualification, classification, pre- and post-market review, clinical and technical assessment, transparency, adaptivity, vigilance, and sandbox learning through AI Airlock.7

03 / 08
HTA and payer-facing expectations

NICE and CDA-AMC require AI use in evidence generation to be justified, declared, and validated

HTA guidance is less focused on AI model novelty and more focused on whether AI-informed evidence remains transparent, auditable, relevant, and decision-grade.

NICE AI evidence position

Use AI only when it adds demonstrable value

NICE states that AI methods should be used judiciously, should augment rather than replace human involvement, and should maintain transparency, rigour, trust, and accessible reporting in guidance production.9

DisclosureDeclare AI use, method choice, how it was used, and human input.9
ValidationConduct technical and external validation, especially for higher-risk applications.9
RWE extractionReport AI methods for real-world-data extraction and curation in data-suitability assessment.9
Causal inferenceUse sensitivity analysis, method checks, and triangulation for influential AI causal methods.9
CDA-AMC position

Canadian HTA adopts a NICE-aligned stance

CDA-AMC reported that its position statement explains how it will consider AI-generated or AI-reported evidence in HTA, emphasizing appropriateness, transparency, trustworthiness, ethics, and risk-benefit balance.11

ScopePotential AI uses include evidence identification, data extraction, synthesis, trial design, clinical-data adjustment, and summaries.11
ResponsibilityUsers must weigh benefits against risks including bias, cybersecurity, and reduced oversight.11
Canadian fitCDA-AMC contextualized a NICE-aligned statement for Canada's HTA environment.11
UpdatesCDA-AMC states the position will be reviewed as significant new evidence emerges.11

NICE's separate evidence standards framework remains relevant for AI-enabled digital health technologies because it was updated to include AI and data-driven adaptive algorithms and aligns evidence requirements with regulatory expectations.10

04 / 08
Evidence synthesis and HEOR

Methods organizations translate AI use into disclosure, reproducibility, and accountability expectations

For systematic reviews, HEOR, and HTA dossiers, the practical burden is not only whether AI improves efficiency; it is whether the workflow remains auditable and methodologically defensible.

Cochrane and RAISE

Human accountability remains
central

Cochrane states that authors can use AI when research integrity is upheld; AI and automation must be disclosed, have human oversight, and authors remain accountable for final content.12

ISPOR PALISADE

ML in HEOR requires
fit-for-purpose reporting

ISPOR describes its good-practices reports as international HEOR standards and lists AI-related reports including machine learning methods and generative AI for HTA.13

Generative AI in HTA

LLM use adds
reproducibility risk

ISPOR lists generative AI for health technology assessment among HTA and policy reports, supporting structured treatment of opportunities, challenges, and policy considerations.13

Operational control

Audit trails preserve
review accountability

AI-assisted screening, extraction, synthesis, and model adaptation should document AI use, method choice, human input, validation, disclosure, and final accountability when they inform submissions or publications.912

Economic evidence

AI products need
value context

ISPOR's AI and HEOR reports support fit-for-purpose methods, transparent assumptions, uncertainty handling, reproducibility, and workflow-aware interpretation when AI informs value evidence.13

Risk controls

Cybersecurity and licensing
are HTA issues

NICE identifies cybersecurity, copyright, licensing, and intellectual-property questions as risks for AI methods used in submitted evidence, not merely back-office technology concerns.9

05 / 08
Clinical evaluation standards

Study reporting guidance now covers the major AI evidence path from protocol to deployment

The professional-standards layer defines what reviewers, journals, regulators, HTA bodies, and committees can expect to see in AI study reports.

Clinical trials

CONSORT-AI and SPIRIT-AI

The CONSORT-AI and SPIRIT-AI extensions require AI-specific reporting for trial protocols and reports, including algorithm version, input-data acquisition, AI output handling, and human-AI interaction.14

ProtocolSpecify intended use, integration into workflow, and user interaction.
Trial reportReport versioning, data inputs, outputs, errors, and operational context.
Early clinical use

DECIDE-AI

DECIDE-AI covers early-stage live clinical evaluation of AI-based decision support systems and emphasizes safety, real clinical performance, implementation environment, human factors, and replicability.15

Pilot bridgeUseful between retrospective validation and large comparative trials.
Human factorsCaptures user training, workflow, interaction, and safety considerations.
Diagnostics

STARD-AI

STARD-AI provides minimum reporting criteria for AI-centered diagnostic test accuracy studies, including dataset practices, AI index-test description, evaluation methods, algorithmic bias, fairness, applicability, and generalizability.16

Beyond AUROCReport thresholding, population, reference standard, and deployment context.
FairnessDocument bias and subgroup applicability where relevant.
Prediction models

TRIPOD+AI and PROBAST+AI

TRIPOD+AI covers reporting for studies that develop prediction models or evaluate their performance using regression or AI methods, including transparent model reporting and external validation context; PROBAST+AI supports risk-of-bias and applicability assessment for those models.17

ValidationReport calibration, discrimination, clinical utility, and external validation.
ApplicabilityAssess whether model data and use context match the decision population.
06 / 08
Practical implications

AI evidence packages should be organized around use case, risk, and accountability

The compiled guidance supports a common evidence architecture for AI-assisted claims, analyses, workflows, and product features.

Regulatory submissions

Define context of use
before evidence planning

Evidence expectations depend on whether AI informs a drug or biologic regulatory decision, functions as device software, supports manufacturing, or assists evidence generation.12

HTA dossiers

Show why AI adds
decision value

Submitting organizations should justify AI methods, present assumptions, disclose human input, validate outputs, and explain risks in language assessors can evaluate.911

AI-enabled products

Plan post-market monitoring
before launch

Device and digital-health guidance emphasizes lifecycle performance, model drift, user transparency, intended-use boundaries, change control, and post-deployment evidence.6710

Evidence synthesis

Preserve reviewer control
throughout synthesis

AI can support review workflows only when tool use is declared, human oversight is active, and methodological rigour is not compromised.12

HEOR

Document uncertainty
and reproducibility

HEOR teams should use fit-for-purpose AI methods and document assumptions, uncertainty, transparency, fairness, and reproducibility when AI informs value evidence.13

Governance

Horizontal AI rules can
apply in parallel

European Commission materials describe phased obligations for high-risk AI, while WHO provides non-binding regulatory considerations on data quality, human oversight, privacy, security, and transparency.58
