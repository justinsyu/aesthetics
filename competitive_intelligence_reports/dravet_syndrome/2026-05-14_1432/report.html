Executive summary

  • Encoded reported expanded POLARIS Phase 1/2 ETX101 data in SCN1A+ Dravet syndrome, including approximately 76% median monthly countable seizure-frequency reduction at dose level 3 from Week 5 through Week 52 and no treatment- or procedure-related serious adverse events.1
  • JAX described adenine base editing of a Dravet-causing SCN1A R613X variant in mice, with nearly 60% mutated-DNA correction, near-normal gene expression, seizure prevention, and survival improvement in treated animals.2
  • ClinicalTrials.gov posted recruiting-status registry activity for DRAPETONINE, a PET-MRI study of serotoninergic brainstem pathways in adult Dravet syndrome, and ARGUS, a Phase 3 EPX-100 study with version 34 changes to study status, description, and locations.34
01 / 06
ETX101 clinical data

POLARIS Phase 1/2 data describe seizure, adaptive-behavior, and safety findings for ETX1011

Seizure frequency

Dose-level 3 durability

Encoded reported approximately a 76% median monthly countable seizure-frequency reduction at dose level 3 from Week 5 through Week 52 in three POLARIS participants.1

Developmental measures

VABS-3 and Bayley-4 findings

Participants reaching 52 weeks of observation showed VABS-3 adaptive-behavior improvements, and patients treated before age 2 showed Bayley-4 cognitive gains through Week 52.1

Safety profile

No serious related events reported

ETX101 was described as well tolerated across four dose levels with no treatment- or procedure-related serious adverse events; treatment-related transaminase elevations were reported as clinically asymptomatic and resolved.1

Program context

The POLARIS program was described as multiple Phase 1-3 clinical trials in children and adolescents with SCN1A+ Dravet syndrome, with ENDEAVOR Part 1B enrolling ages 4 to 18 and pivotal ENDEAVOR Part 2 evaluating outcomes in ages 6 months to 4 years.1

02 / 06
Preclinical genetics

JAX described direct SCN1A R613X correction in a Dravet syndrome mouse model2

Variant and delivery

Adenine base editing

The study focused on the Dravet-causing SCN1A R613X variant and used adenine base editing delivered by a single brain injection in very young mice on postnatal day 1 or day 12.2

Molecular correction

DNA and expression effects

JAX reported that treated mice had nearly 60% correction of the mutated DNA and that almost all gene expression appeared normal despite partial DNA correction.2

Phenotype findings

Seizure and survival outcomes

The edit was described as restoring gene function and preventing seizures, while mice treated at birth had significant survival improvement.2

Post-symptom timing

Day 12 dosing signal

Mice treated on day 12 were also described as benefiting, with lasting protection into young adulthood and very low levels of unintended DNA changes or other adverse effects in the brain.2
