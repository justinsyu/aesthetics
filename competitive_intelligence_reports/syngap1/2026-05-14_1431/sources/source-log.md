- Facts extracted:
  - CAMP4 announced presentation of preclinical data for CMP-002, its lead investigational ASO therapeutic candidate for SYNGAP1-related disorder, at TIDES on May 14, 2026.
  - CMP-002 administration was reported to produce statistically significant improvement in seizure threshold and severity of chemically induced tonic-clonic seizures in SYNGAP1 haploinsufficient mice.
  - The study used pentylenetetrazol after noting that the mice were described as not exhibiting spontaneous and readily countable seizures.
  - A single CMP-002 dose was followed by PTZ induction one month after administration to juvenile mice.
  - CAMP4 stated that it expects to advance CMP-002 into a Phase 1/2 clinical trial in individuals with SYNGAP1-related disorder in the second half of 2026.
  - CMP-002 was described as an intrathecal ASO designed to bind a SYNGAP1-specific regRNA, increase SYNGAP1 gene expression, and restore SYNGAP protein toward near wild-type levels.
  - CAMP4 described SYNGAP1-related disorder as rare, haploinsufficient, caused by mutations in SYNGAP1, associated with approximately 50% of normal SYNGAP protein levels, and without approved disease-modifying therapies.
