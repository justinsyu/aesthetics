Executive summary

  • CAMP4 reported May 14 preclinical data for CMP-002, its lead investigational ASO therapeutic candidate for SYNGAP1-related disorder, at the TIDES Oligonucleotide & Peptide Therapeutics conference.1
  • CMP-002 administration was reported to produce statistically significant improvement in seizure threshold and severity in chemically induced tonic-clonic seizures in SYNGAP1 haploinsufficient mice.1
  • CAMP4 furnished the press release and an updated May 2026 corporate slide presentation in an 8-K, while the TIDES agenda listed a Dan Tardiff presentation stating that CMP-002 is advancing toward clinic for SYNGAP1-related disorder.23

CMP-002 preclinical seizure update

CMP-002 was reported to improve seizure threshold and seizure severity in a SYNGAP1 haploinsufficient mouse model1

Model
Intervention
Assay design
Reported readout
SYNGAP1 haploinsufficient mice were described as having greater seizure burden than wild-type mice after PTZ induction.1
A single dose of CMP-002 was administered before PTZ challenge in juvenile mice.1
Pentylenetetrazol was used to induce tonic-clonic seizures one month after CMP-002 administration.1
CAMP4 reported statistically significant resistance to seizure onset and a statistically significant decrease in seizure severity.1
Assay constraint

Induced-seizure model

CAMP4 reported using PTZ after noting that SYNGAP1 haploinsufficient mice do not exhibit spontaneous and readily countable seizures.1

Clinical-development timing

CAMP4 stated that it expects to advance CMP-002 into a Phase 1/2 clinical trial in individuals with SYNGAP1-related disorder in the second half of 2026.1

02 / 05
Asset and disease profile

CMP-002 is described as an intrathecal ASO designed to increase SYNGAP1 gene expression1

Mechanism

RegRNA targeting

CMP-002 is described as binding a SYNGAP1-specific regulatory RNA to increase SYNGAP1 gene expression and restore SYNGAP protein toward near wild-type levels.1

Route and prior evidence

Intrathecal ASO

CAMP4 reported intrathecal administration and described prior evidence across patient-derived neurons, a humanized haploinsufficient mouse model, a chemically induced seizure mouse model, and non-human primates.1

Unmet need

SRD profile

SYNGAP1-related disorder is described as a rare haploinsufficient CNS disorder caused by SYNGAP1 mutations, resulting in approximately 50% of normal SYNGAP protein levels, with no approved disease-modifying therapies.1

Population statement from the cited source

CAMP4 described SYNGAP1-related disorder as affecting over 10,000 individuals in the United States and characterized it with intellectual disability, epilepsy, severe behavioral problems, sleep problems, and limited communication.1
