Competitive intelligence regulatory review

Deramiocel CRL: evidence basis and relevant precedents

FDA's July 9, 2025 complete response letter identified deficiencies related to substantial evidence for a DMD cardiomyopathy claim: failed prespecified HOPE-2 endpoints, exploratory cardiac analyses, no multiplicity strategy, and a nonrandomized OLE. [S1] [S2]

ProductDeramiocel / CAP-1002
ApplicationBLA 125842
CRL dateJuly 9, 2025
CRL finding

FDA identified deficiencies in the evidence supporting the proposed indication

  • HOPE-2 and HOPE-2-OLE were submitted as the primary and supportive evidence base. [S1]
  • FDA stated HOPE-2 failed the prespecified primary endpoint and prespecified secondary endpoints. [S1]
  • The cardiomyopathy evidence relied on post hoc analyses rather than a trial designed to assess DMD cardiomyopathy. [S1]
[S1] FDA CRL, BLA 125842, July 9, 2025.
CRL finding

FDA's statistical concerns were specific

  • FDA identified 50 secondary/exploratory endpoints, including 26 cardiac MRI endpoints. [S2]
  • No prespecified hypothesis testing or multiplicity adjustment strategy controlled Type I error. [S2]
  • FDA identified exploratory analyses and the nonrandomized OLE as insufficient to establish effectiveness. [S2]
[S2] FDA CRL, BLA 125842, July 9, 2025.
Company disclosure

Capricor's public framing described the stated evidence deficiency

  • Capricor said FDA cited lack of substantial evidence and need for additional clinical data. [S3]
  • The company disclosed the package relied on HOPE-2, HOPE-2-OLE, and natural-history comparisons. [S3]
  • CMC items were referenced, but public detail is redacted; the clinical evidence issue is the most clearly identifiable regulatory risk from public sources. [S1] [S3]
[S3] Capricor press release, July 11, 2025.
Type A meeting

The post-CRL update described the evidence path

  • After the Type A meeting, Capricor said FDA aligned that HOPE-3 could serve as the additional study. [S4]
  • The updated path preserved PUL v2.0 as primary and put LVEF forward as a key secondary endpoint. [S4]
  • That sequencing supports the conclusion that prospective controlled evidence was a key deficiency identified during review. [S2] [S4]
[S4] Capricor press release, September 25, 2025.
Regulatory learning

FDA guidance described multiplicity risks relevant to the CRL

  • FDA's endpoints guidance states that false conclusions become a concern as endpoint counts rise without multiplicity adjustment. [S5]
  • The deramiocel CRL used the same logic: exploratory endpoints and nominal signals were not considered sufficient to establish effectiveness. [S2]
  • Implication: pre-specify the cardiac estimand, hierarchy, and multiplicity plan before filing for a cardiac label. [S2] [S5]
[S5] FDA Multiple Endpoints guidance page, content current April 16, 2024.
Analog precedent

NurOwn indicates that unmet need does not substitute for successful randomized endpoints

  • FDA publicly said NurOwn data did not support proposed clinical benefit after a randomized Phase 3 trial. [S6]
  • None of the randomized primary or secondary endpoints were met, despite high public interest in ALS. [S6]
  • Implication: patient urgency and biologic plausibility do not make exploratory or post hoc findings sufficient to establish substantial evidence. [S6]
[S6] FDA ALS/NurOwn update, March 2, 2021.
Analog precedent

DMD-specific precedent had described the substantial-evidence standard

  • BioMarin's drisapersen CRL in DMD stated the application was not ready for approval in its present form. [S7]
  • The company disclosed FDA concluded substantial evidence of effectiveness had not been met. [S7]
  • Implication: DMD regulatory flexibility did not remove the need for adequate evidence tied to the proposed claim. [S7]
[S7] BioMarin press release, January 14, 2016.
Synthesis

Was the CRL risk reasonably foreseeable?

Available evidence suggests the CRL risk was reasonably foreseeable at the submission-strategy level.

The precise FDA timing may have been uncertain, but the central objection was identifiable from the submitted evidence structure and prior FDA guidance: a cardiomyopathy indication needed prospectively defined, controlled evidence for cardiac benefit, not retrospective cardiac analyses derived from a neuromuscular trial that missed prespecified endpoints. [S1] [S2] [S5]

  • Pre-submission risk factor: reliance on post hoc cardiac endpoints without a controlled Type I error plan. [S2] [S5]
  • Pre-submission risk factor: use of nonrandomized OLE/natural-history comparisons as supportive evidence for a label-defining claim. [S1] [S2]
  • Less knowable from public sources: redacted CMC specifics. [S1] [S3]
  • Practical implication: align the proposed label with the trial objective, estimand, endpoint hierarchy, and control group. [S2] [S5]
  • For cell therapies, CMC/potency readiness remains important, but the public record identifies the clinical-evidence deficiency as central. [S1] [S3]
  • The post-CRL HOPE-3 path supports the need for prospective controlled data. [S4]
