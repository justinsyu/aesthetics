# KOL Intelligence, Mapping, and Targeting Offerings for Pharma

Research date: June 11, 2026  
Scope: public web evidence for pharma-relevant KOL intelligence, KOL mapping, HCP targeting, digital opinion leader analytics, investigator/site selection, expert engagement, HCP media, and expert-recruitment offerings. This is a public-source market scan; vendor demos/contracts may disclose additional data sources.

## Bottom Line

Most offerings are variations on a common architecture:

- **Public scientific layer:** publications, citations, co-authorship, clinical-trial roles, congress participation, guideline/society/editorial-board roles, grants, patents, Open Payments, news, and public digital activity.
- **Commercial/clinical layer:** claims, Rx, procedures, labs, EHR, patient journeys, referral networks, payer mix, site performance, HCP contact/identity data, and CRM/MSL engagement data.
- **Activation/private-network layer:** verified HCP communities, professional media audiences, opted-in NPI targeting, market-research panels, advisory-board platforms, first-party engagement behavior, and campaign response.

A public-data build can credibly replicate **scientific KOL discovery, transparent evidence dossiers, coauthor/trial/congress maps, public DOL mapping, Open Payments profiles, and Medicare-only clinical-volume proxies**. It cannot fully replicate **all-payer claims/Rx/referral data, private HCP-community data, authenticated media activation, proprietary identity graphs, peer nominations, primary market research, or CRM-driven next-best-action**.

## Replicability Key

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| High | Mostly reproducible with public/open data plus engineering and curation. |
| Partial | The concept is reproducible, but coverage, timeliness, identity matching, or key fields will be weaker. |
| Low | Differentiating value depends on licensed, proprietary, private, consented, or client-internal data. |

## Methods and Public Replication Paths

| Method | What vendors use it for | Public build path | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bibliometrics | Scientific authority, emerging experts, topic relevance. | PubMed/PMC E-utilities, OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, ORCID. | Author disambiguation, full-text access, field normalization. |
| Coauthorship graph | Collaboration networks, central connectors, communities. | PubMed/OpenAlex authorship graph; centrality/community algorithms. | Academic bias; weak proxy for clinical influence. |
| Trial investigator scoring | PI/site experience, trial leadership, investigator targeting. | ClinicalTrials.gov API, EU/national registries, WHO ICTRP, publication links. | Sub-investigators/site performance often missing. |
| Congress/speaker mapping | Share of scientific voice, chairs, presenters, rising experts. | Scrape public programs/abstract books and parse PDFs/HTML. | Gated/app-only/dynamic sites; weak historical durability. |
| Guideline/society/editorial roles | Standards-of-care authority. | Society pages, guideline PDFs, editorial boards, committee rosters. | Manual curation; inconsistent archives. |
| Payments/industry relationships | Consulting, speaking, research, royalties, COI profiles. | CMS Open Payments; country transparency registries where available. | U.S.-centric; relationship signal is not endorsement. |
| Claims/Rx/procedure/patient mix | Treating volume, target-list quality, local clinical influence. | CMS Medicare Physician & Other Practitioners, Medicare Part D, state APCDs where available. | Public data is lagged, Medicare-heavy, thresholded, and not all-payer. |
| Referral/care network | Peer influence and patient flow. | Public proxies: shared affiliation, coauthorship, trials, HRR/HSA geography. | True referrals need linked claims or proprietary datasets. |
| Digital opinion leader mapping | Online voice, originators vs amplifiers, sentiment. | Public X/LinkedIn/Bluesky/YouTube/blog/news/podcast data where compliant. | Platform restrictions, identity verification, private forums. |
| Peer nominations / primary research | Local/regional influence and advisory relevance. | Surveys/interviews through panels or direct outreach. | Not public; sample and recruitment bias. |
| Next-best-action | Who to engage, channel, timing, content. | Public profile plus internal CRM/email/event response if available. | Without client engagement data, recommendations are speculative. |

| Source | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|

| [CMS Medicare Physician & Other Practitioners](https://data.cms.gov/provider-summary-by-type-of-service/medicare-physician-other-practitioners) | Medicare FFS procedure/service volume. | Limited, lagged, Medicare-only. |
| [CMS Medicare Part D Prescribers](https://data.cms.gov/provider-summary-by-type-of-service/medicare-part-d-prescribers/medicare-part-d-prescribers-by-provider-and-drug) | Medicare Part D prescribing by provider/drug. | Not diagnosis-linked; suppressed small counts. |
| NIH RePORTER, USPTO/Google Patents/Lens | Grants, patents, translational expertise. | Requires disambiguation. |
| Public congress programs, society pages, guideline PDFs, editorial boards | Congress voice, committee roles, guideline authority. | High curation burden. |
| Public social/news/blog/podcast/video data | Digital influence and media visibility. | Must respect ToS/compliance; private HCP networks excluded. |

## Build-vs-Buy Implications

**Best public-data wedge:** a disease-specific KOL map with transparent evidence dossiers, scores by dimension, source links, and reproducible public methods.

**Most replicable modules:** publication/citation scoring, coauthor networks, ClinicalTrials.gov investigator history, Open Payments profiles, public congress/society/guideline roles, and public DOL maps.

**Least replicable modules:** all-payer claims/Rx, patient journeys, lab/EHR/genomics, true referral networks, verified HCP media activation, private community conversations, market-research panel outputs, peer nominations, and CRM next-best-action.

**Recommended scoring dimensions for a public clone:** scientific authority, trial leadership, congress voice, guideline/society authority, clinical-volume proxy, payment/disclosure profile, network centrality, digital influence, emerging trajectory, geography, and fit for the advisory objective.

**Important compliance/interpretation guardrail:** authorship, trial participation, disclosed payments, speaking history, or committee membership can support an evidence-backed hypothesis about expertise or influence. They should not be treated as private beliefs, endorsements, prescribing intent, or willingness to advise unless a source directly supports that interpretation.
