Scope: AI-native / AI-first startups and adjacent AI-enabled scaleups relevant to Medical Affairs, Field Medical, Medical Communications, HEOR/RWE, scientific evidence generation, congress intelligence, KOL/HCP engagement, and Medical Affairs-adjacent competitive intelligence.

Retrieval date: June 2, 2026.

AI-native confidence definitions:

- **High**: Company or product is explicitly AI-native/agentic/generative-AI-first and the Medical Affairs workflow is core to the offering.
- **Medium**: AI is central to the product, but the vendor appears to combine platform, services, incumbent data assets, or broader life-sciences workflows.
- **Adjacent**: AI-native or AI-enabled product is relevant to Medical Affairs decisions, but the primary buyer/workflow is R&D, HEOR/RWE, regulatory writing, commercial analytics, or physician-facing clinical search.
- **Low / incumbent comparison**: Useful benchmark vendor, but not primarily AI-native or startup-like.

## Shortlist Implications

- The most Medical-Affairs-native AI-first platforms are **Atrix AI, SpeerHealth, Sipherio, Sorcero, Centivence, SyncIQ, LogicFlo AI, KOL Pulse, KeyOps, Dalil.bio and Ariadne** because their public messaging explicitly maps AI to field insights, KOL/HCP engagement, Medical Affairs content, congress intelligence, scientific exchange or evidence-to-strategy workflows.
- The evidence/RWE layer is fragmented: **MadeAi, Centivence, Huma.AI/Qoniq and Atropos** each emphasize traceability, but they differ in whether the core output is literature synthesis, evidence repositories, RWE study generation, or expert-guided analysis.
- Scaleups, acquired assets and incumbents (**Within3, Alucio / BEACON, WhizAI, Aktana, H1, TikaMobile, Yseop, Causaly, Atropos, Sorcero**) matter as buyer benchmarks because pharma teams often compare AI-native startups against vendors with broader data assets, validated workflows, integrations, or existing procurement paths.
- Edge-case HCP-facing platforms such as **OpenEvidence** should be tracked as channel context: they may shape how physicians consume scientific information, but they are not substitutes for internal Medical Affairs insight, evidence, compliance or engagement systems.
