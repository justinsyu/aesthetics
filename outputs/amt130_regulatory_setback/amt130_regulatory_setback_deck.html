Competitive intelligence | AMT-130 regulatory evidence review

uniQure did not receive a CRL. FDA still sent a clear signal: external-control evidence was not enough.

The most recent AMT-130 regulatory setback was final Type A meeting minutes, not a Complete Response Letter. The avoidable lesson is broader: for an invasive CNS gene therapy in a slow, heterogeneous neurodegenerative disease, a sham-controlled pivotal path should have been treated as base case, not contingency.

Learning 1: external controls

FDA's own external-control guidance makes the AMT-130 primary-evidence strategy a known risk

Why this mattered for Huntington's disease

  • Clinical progression is slow and heterogeneous.
  • cUHDRS and TFC are clinically meaningful but observer- and trajectory-sensitive.
  • Phase I/II treated sample size was small, while the comparator came from a natural-history cohort.
  • Propensity weighting can improve balance, but it cannot replace randomization or blinding when unmeasured confounding remains plausible.
The guidance did not forbid external controls. It signaled that the design had to be fit-for-purpose and robust enough to support effectiveness.

Source: FDA draft guidance on externally controlled trials, February 2023.

06 / 12
Learning 2: CNS surgical-gene-therapy precedent

Neurosurgical CNS programs had already shown why sham control could be necessary

Relevant precedent

  • CERE-120, an AAV2-neurturin Parkinson's disease gene therapy, moved from open-label promise into a double-blind, randomized, sham-surgery controlled trial.
  • The published Lancet Neurology abstract reported no significant primary-endpoint difference versus sham control at 12 months.
  • For AMT-130, the direct lesson was not disease or vector identity. It was procedural and measurement bias: invasive brain-delivered therapies can create expectation, observer, and surgery-related effects that regulators may want controlled prospectively.
A sponsor could reasonably argue AMT-130's disease biology differs. It could not reasonably ignore the sham-control precedent for brain-delivered gene therapy.

Screenshot source: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00400634. Publication source: Marks et al., Lancet Neurology 2010, PubMed record.

Learning 3: prior alignment can be revisited

Recent single-arm programs show that FDA alignment did not eliminate design-risk exposure

Tabelecleucel: a January 2026 CRL said FDA no longer considered the previously accepted single-arm ALLELE study adequate for accelerated approval.

RP1: Replimune described prior FDA statements that a single-arm trial could be acceptable if sufficiently compelling, yet the program still received a CRL.

Sources: Pierre Fabre, Jan. 12, 2026 PDF statement; Replimune / GlobeNewswire, Apr. 10, 2026.

Learning 4: accelerated approval operations

Even when efficacy and safety are not the issue, confirmatory-trial readiness can drive CRL risk

Source: Regeneron / GlobeNewswire, March 25, 2024.

How this maps to AMT-130

  • Odronextamab's CRLs were not about efficacy, safety, trial design, labeling, or manufacturing. They were about confirmatory-trial status.
  • That precedent reinforces a conservative accelerated-approval operating model: the controlled evidence plan must be active, credible, and synchronized with the filing strategy.
  • For AMT-130, a sham-controlled trial plan should have been more than a fallback scenario once the filing package depended on external controls.
Regulators can support flexibility and still require the confirmatory or controlled-study machinery to be real before approval.

Sponsor-readiness checklist

What uniQure's sponsor could or should have stress-tested earlier

Risk area Prior evidence to look at Action that could have reduced CRL-like setback risk
External-control comparability FDA external-control guidance; AMT-130 reliance on ENROLL-HD, TRACK-HD/TRACK-ON, and PREDICT-HD sensitivity analyses. Pre-specify a controlled fallback and treat external controls as supportive unless FDA acceptance was locked at filing-grade specificity.
CNS surgical bias CERE-120 sham-controlled Parkinson's disease gene therapy trial. Start or protocol-ready a randomized double-blind sham-controlled trial earlier, even if accelerated approval was pursued.
Prior-alignment fragility Tabelecleucel and RP1 single-arm evidence disputes. Communicate alignment as conditional and reversible; avoid treating design discussions as durable approval evidence.
Accelerated-approval readiness Odronextamab CRLs tied to confirmatory-trial enrollment status. Ensure the controlled/confirmatory study is not only proposed but operationally credible before the BLA package becomes dependent on it.

Foreseeability assessment

This was foreseeable as a material regulatory risk, but not as a guaranteed FDA reversal

Foreseeable

  • Primary evidence came from a small Phase I/II surgical gene therapy program compared with external controls.
  • The indication involves slow, variable neurodegenerative progression.
  • FDA guidance and CNS neurosurgical precedents made bias and comparability obvious review risks.
  • Recent CRLs show single-arm or accelerated-approval strategies can be revisited despite prior interaction history.

Not fully predictable

  • uniQure had publicly described FDA alignment on endpoint, SAP, and external-control dataset elements before the setback.
  • RMAT and Breakthrough designations plausibly supported a view that FDA would entertain flexibility.
  • The exact Type A outcome and timing could not be known externally before the meeting minutes.
Final read: do not say "there was no way to foresee this." A more precise statement is that the strength of FDA's reversal was uncertain, while the trial-design risk was visible enough to warrant earlier controlled-study planning.

Implications

Practical takeaway for future rare-disease gene therapy programs

Base-case evidence plan

  • Use external controls to enrich, contextualize, and support sensitivity analyses.
  • Use randomization and blinding when the clinical endpoint, procedure, or disease trajectory can plausibly be biased.
  • Make the confirmatory or controlled trial operational before it becomes a post-setback rescue plan.

Investor and internal communication

  • Separate FDA agreement on analysis components from agreement that the total package is filing-grade primary evidence.
  • Label RMAT, Breakthrough, and accelerated-approval interactions as facilitative, not dispositive.
  • Stress-test public claims against worst-case FDA interpretation of adequacy and well-controlled evidence.
