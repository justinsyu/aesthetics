Mimilabs Data Catalog Opportunity Map for Wet AMD and Retinal Diseases

A retina-specific review of how public and public-derived datasets in the mimi_ws_1 catalog can support MSL, Medical Affairs, Market Access, field reimbursement, and patient-access teams.

FocusWet AMD and adjacent retinal diseases involving anti-VEGF, complement, intravitreal injection, imaging, retina-specialist access, site-of-care economics, and payer controls.

Executive Take

For wet AMD and retinal diseases, the Mimilabs catalog is most useful as a public-data connection layer around retina specialists, injection and imaging procedures, physician-administered ophthalmic drugs, medical-benefit coverage, local access, and evidence-generation targets.

It is less useful as a direct outcomes dataset: the catalog does not appear to contain chart-level visual acuity, OCT anatomy, lesion activity, laterality, injection interval, persistence, switching, or retina registry outcomes.

The key revision from the general map is to build around the retina care pathway. Public data can show where retina care is delivered, paid for, coded, and geographically constrained. Deeper claims, EHR, registry, hub, or chart-abstraction data are needed to validate clinical outcomes and treatment behavior.

Retina Intelligence Spines

  • Provider spine: NPI, taxonomy, practice addresses, group/TIN, facility affiliation, HIE endpoints, Open Payments, trials, publications, and Medicare service volume.
  • Procedure and product spine: ICD-10 diagnosis families, CPT/HCPCS for injection and imaging, NDC/RxCUI/HCPCS for ophthalmic drugs, FDA labels, adverse events, and code histories.
  • Medical-benefit payer spine: Part B utilization/spending, MA enrollment and benefit design, LCD/article/code-policy language, commercial in-network rates, provider references, and Medicaid rates/utilization.
  • Geography and access spine: county/ZCTA/tract burden proxies, older-adult enrollment, SVI/ADI/SDI, HPSA/MUA, provider supply, rurality, transportation benefits, and practice locations.
  • Evidence and influence spine: ClinicalTrials.gov sites/investigators, publications/ORCID, Open Payments, FDA safety/label changes, and payer-policy evidence citations.

Clean Before Analysis

  1. Product identity: brand, ingredient, NDC, RxCUI, HCPCS/J-code, FDA application, reference biologic or biosimilar status, route/formulation, and indication.
  2. Provider identity: retina specialists, comprehensive ophthalmologists, groups, hospital outpatient departments, ambulatory sites, and academic centers.
  3. Procedure identity: intravitreal injection, OCT/retinal imaging, angiography, office visits, drug administration, modifiers, and code edits.
  4. Geography: provider address, county/ZCTA/tract/CBSA, RUCA, MA plan service areas, and MAC jurisdictions.
  5. Time: align monthly directories, quarterly drug spending, yearly utilization files, MCD updates, and MRF refreshes.
  6. Policy text: covered diagnoses, excluded diagnoses, step requirements, documentation criteria, code groups, effective dates, and revision history.

Where Public Data Is Not Enough

  1. Visual acuity, OCT anatomy, lesion activity, laterality, fibrosis/atrophy, and durability require EHR, registry, chart abstraction, or disease-specific RWE.
  2. Persistence, discontinuation, switching, bilateral treatment, treat-and-extend intervals, missed injections, and restart patterns usually require claims/EHR/registry data.
  3. Copay assistance, hub interactions, prior authorization outcomes, abandonment, transportation barriers, and caregiver burden are not fully visible in public files.
  4. Rebates, provider acquisition cost, buy-and-bill spread, 340B margin, value-based contracts, and account-specific agreements are not observable directly from public tables.
  5. Public coding may not cleanly distinguish active wet AMD from history of wet AMD, fellow-eye disease, lesion subtype, fibrosis, atrophy, or clinical contraindications.

Where to Dig Deeper First

  1. Build one retina drug/code spine: FDA label/NDC/RxCUI -> HCPCS/J-code -> MCD LCD/article code groups -> Medicare Part B spending/utilization -> Medicaid utilization/NADAC -> commercial MRF procedure/provider economics.
  2. Build one provider spine in a test geography: NPPES ophthalmology/retina candidates -> geocoded practice locations -> Medicare injection/imaging volume -> TIN/group and health-system affiliation -> MA enrollment and SVI/ADI/RUCA context.
  3. Pick a few MAC jurisdictions and extract retina-specific LCD/article criteria, code groups, documentation requirements, and revision histories before scaling policy NLP.
  4. Validate procedure and drug code sets with a retina billing expert before presenting findings.
  5. Compare public insights against one deeper source if available: IRIS Registry, Vestrum/Verana/Komodo/Symphony/IQVIA claims, retina EHR extracts, hub data, or account-level payer criteria.

Best First Deliverable

For wet AMD, build a 10-geography "retina access and evidence action" pilot.

  1. Select 10 high-priority counties, CBSAs, or IDN catchments.
  2. Resolve retina providers, practice sites, TINs, hospital affiliations, 340B/IDN status, MA enrollment, and local vulnerability/rurality.
  3. Score each geography on retina specialist density, injection/imaging capacity, Medicare Part B drug and procedure utilization, commercial procedure rates, policy friction, trial/publication activity, older-adult access risk, and private-data gaps.
  4. Produce role-specific action:
    • MSL: which retina specialists or investigators to engage, what evidence gaps to discuss, and where access context may shape scientific exchange.
    • Medical Affairs: which evidence narratives need stronger durability, safety, switching, adherence, or outcomes support.
    • Market Access: which policy, coding, site-of-care, payer, or account economics barrier is most actionable.

This focused pilot is more useful than a national retina dashboard because it tests whether public catalog data can generate account-level hypotheses worth validating with claims, registry, EHR, hub, or payer-policy data.
