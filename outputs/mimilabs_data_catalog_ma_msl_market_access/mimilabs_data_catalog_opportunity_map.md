# Mimilabs Data Catalog Opportunity Map for Retinal Diseases Like Wet AMD

Source reviewed: https://www.mimilabs.ai/datacatalog/list  
Review date: 2026-06-10  
Scope: Public and public-derived datasets in the `mimi_ws_1` catalog that can support an opportunity map for retinal diseases such as wet age-related macular degeneration (wet AMD). This is an access, evidence, and account-intelligence map, not a clinical claims report or a prevalence estimate.

## Executive take

The Mimilabs catalog is useful for retina because many difficult public assets are already organized in one lakehouse: provider directories, CMS payment files, HCPCS and ICD-10 coding files, Medicare utilization summaries, FDA drug and adverse-event data, ClinicalTrials.gov, Open Payments, geography and deprivation indices, payer directories, 340B files, and derived linkages such as geocoded provider addresses, Placekey, and NPI/TIN relationships.

For wet AMD and adjacent retinal diseases, the catalog can map where access, economics, evidence, and site-of-care friction may exist. It cannot, by itself, prove disease prevalence, lesion activity, visual acuity outcomes, OCT anatomy, injection persistence, switching, rebates, PA approval rates, or patient-level treatment journeys. Those require deeper digging into claims, EHR, retina registry, specialty pharmacy, hub, payer policy, chart abstraction, or account-level contracting data.

The best use is a retina-specific intelligence graph around five spines:

1. Retina provider and site spine: NPI, NUCC taxonomy, CMS National Provider Directory, provider/facility affiliations, TIN, Placekey, CCN, hospital outpatient, ASC, IDN, 340B, and geocoded practice locations.
2. Retinal drug and code spine: NDC, RxCUI, active ingredient, FDA label, HCPCS/J-code, ASP NDC-HCPCS crosswalk, OPPS/PFS payment, ICD-10 diagnosis, CPT/HCPCS procedure, NCCI edits, revenue codes, modifiers, and place-of-service.
3. Payer and coverage spine: Medicare LCD/article/NCD tables, MAC jurisdiction, MA/PDP enrollment and plan geography, Marketplace and data.healthcare.gov plans, payer MRF in-network rates, Medicaid utilization and NADAC, and HPMS/state policy text.
4. Evidence and influence spine: ClinicalTrials.gov conditions, interventions, facilities, investigators, outcomes, adverse events, publications, ORCID profiles, Open Payments, FDA approvals, labels, adverse events, recalls, and device files.
5. Geography and equity spine: county, tract, ZIP/ZCTA, CBSA, HSA/HRR, RUCA, SVI, ADI, SDoH, HPSA, MUA, health centers, broadband/connectivity proxies, and Medicare/Marketplace/Medicaid enrollment context.

## Retina-specific cleaning and connection work that matters most

1. Build canonical dimensions before analysis:
   - `dim_retina_provider`: NPI, name variants, credentials, taxonomy, retina-likelihood flag, practice locations, facility affiliations, Open Payments IDs, ClinicalTrials.gov investigator names, ORCID IDs, and geocoded address keys.
   - `dim_retina_site`: practice, office, HOPD, ASC-like setting, hospital, FQHC/340B covered entity, CCN, TIN, Placekey, ownership, health-system parent, and contract/network identifiers.
   - `dim_retinal_product_code`: FDA application and label, NDC, RxCUI, active ingredient, brand/generic display names, HCPCS/J-code, ASP crosswalk, OPPS/PFS payment, MCD code references, and formulary display names where applicable.
   - `dim_retina_diagnosis_procedure`: wet AMD and adjacent retinal disease ICD-10 families, laterality-aware diagnosis fields where available, intravitreal injection/admin codes, OCT and diagnostic imaging codes, modifiers, revenue codes, and place-of-service.
   - `dim_plan_payer_geo`: CMS contract/PBP, HIOS, issuer, parent, line of business, county/state service area, Marketplace/MA/PDP/Medicaid segment, provider network, and MRF source metadata.
   - `dim_geo_access`: ZIP/ZCTA, tract, county, CBSA, HSA/HRR, RUCA, SVI, ADI, SDoH, HPSA/MUA, health-center sites, and travel/access proxies.

2. Treat medical benefit as the default for intravitreal anti-VEGF work. Part D and formulary files can be useful for adjacent affordability context, but the core wet AMD access question usually lives in Part B, medical policy, buy-and-bill economics, HOPD/office payment, and commercial medical-benefit rules.

3. Version everything. Retinal drug access is sensitive to quarter-specific ASP, HCPCS changes, label updates, LCD/article revisions, plan-year enrollment, MRF refreshes, and annual code-set updates.

4. Keep code specificity separate from clinical specificity. Public ICD-10 and HCPCS data can indicate diagnosis/procedure coding, but it cannot confirm eye laterality in practice, active CNV status, OCT anatomy, visual acuity, or response to therapy unless those fields exist in a licensed chart/EHR/registry source.

5. Use text extraction carefully. LCDs, articles, HPMS memos, state policy letters, FDA labels, ClinicalTrials.gov descriptions, and publication abstracts need structured fields for indication, treatment line, prior therapy, documentation requirement, site-of-care condition, code list, effective date, revision reason, comparator, and evidence cited.

6. Separate public signal from market reality. MRF rates, ASP files, public formularies, Medicare utilization, Open Payments, and FDA adverse-event tables are directional. They are not net cost, rebates, PA operations, patient-level adherence, or real-world visual outcomes.

## Where to dig deeper first

1. Query `mimilabs.tablestats` and `mimilabs.columnstats` for every table used in the retina map. Confirm row counts, source dates, load dates, null rates, distinct counts, and whether key fields such as NPI, TIN, HCPCS, NDC, RxCUI, county, ZIP, Placekey, plan ID, and effective date are usable.

2. Inspect `mimilabs.entity_linkages`, `mimilabs.tin_entities`, and the derived address/Placekey assets before building account views. If NPI/TIN/facility/system resolution is incomplete for retina practices, the first deliverable should be an entity-resolution QA report.

3. Pull one retinal product or analog through the drug/code/payment spine:
   - FDA NDC/label -> RxNorm -> HCPCS/NDC-HCPCS -> ASP pricing -> OPPS/PFS -> MCD LCD/article code references -> QDD/MUPPHY/MUPOHP utilization -> payer MRF where available.
   - Output: "What would block or favor adoption by site of care if used today?"

4. Pull one retina geography through the provider/access spine:
   - NPPES/CMS NPD/provider catalog -> Placekey/geocoding -> health-system and facility affiliation -> HPSA/MUA/SVI/ADI/RUCA -> Medicare/Marketplace/Medicaid plan context -> MRF/network availability.
   - Output: "Where are retina specialist supply, payer friction, and access vulnerability likely to overlap?"

5. Build one KOL graph before scaling:
   - ClinicalTrials.gov investigator/facility -> ORCID/PMC/publications -> Open Payments -> NPPES/provider location -> Medicare utilization proxies -> health-system affiliation -> access/equity context.
   - Output: ranked segments such as scientific leaders, trial-capable sites, high-volume implementation champions, access-equity champions, and emerging investigators.

6. Test coverage-policy NLP on one MAC jurisdiction and one commercial payer medical policy source. The useful output is not a long policy summary; it is a structured table of covered diagnoses, codes, documentation requirements, prior therapy, treatment interval language, site-of-care requirements, effective date, and revision reason.

7. Plan the private-data bridge explicitly. The highest-value wet AMD questions need claims/EHR/registry/SP/hub/payer data for diagnosis laterality, visual acuity, OCT findings, injection interval, bilateral treatment, persistence, switching, missed visits, PA outcomes, patient affordability, and drug-level rebates.

## Best first deliverable

Build a 10-account retinal disease "evidence-access-action" pilot rather than a broad dashboard.

1. Pick 10 retina accounts, IDNs, or geographies with plausible access or evidence relevance.
2. Resolve retina providers, sites, TINs, CCNs, health-system parents, 340B status, plan footprints, and local access vulnerability.
3. Score each account on:
   - retina specialist and injection-site supply
   - Part B and site-of-care economics
   - relevant HCPCS/ICD/CPT/OCT coding confidence
   - LCD/article or commercial medical-policy friction
   - trial, publication, and KOL activity
   - FDA label/safety/evidence gaps
   - equity/geographic access vulnerability
   - 340B/IDN/HOPD incentive context
   - explicit data gaps requiring claims, EHR, retina registry, SP/hub, payer policy, or chart review
4. Produce role-specific action:
   - MSL: who to engage, which scientific/evidence gap matters locally, and what is appropriate to discuss.
   - Medical Affairs: which evidence narrative, trial endpoint, safety topic, or real-world evidence gap should be prioritized.
   - Market Access: which payer, code, site-of-care, or account-economics barrier is actionable and what evidence is needed to validate it.

This pilot is likely more useful than a national retina dashboard because it tests whether the public catalog linkages create account-level intelligence that would change MSL, Medical Affairs, or Market Access decisions.
