Wet AMD terminology audit

Three retina companies, three different language systems

All three manufacturers anchor to wet AMD, but the surrounding vocabulary diverges sharply: Ocular emphasizes continuous local drug delivery, 4DMT emphasizes genetic medicine as backbone therapy, and EyePoint emphasizes a bioerodible sustained-delivery insert.

High-level finding

Disease language is mostly standardized; platform and positioning language is not. The convention differences signal each company’s desired category: implant, gene therapy, or sustained-release insert.

Scope: public company websites and official investor pages available on May 11, 2026. “Wet AMD-related” means pages whose visible copy, page title, or official release copy references wet AMD, neovascular AMD, AXPAXLI / OTX-TKI, 4D-150, DURAVYU / EYP-1901, or named wet AMD trials.

03 / 06
Messaging contrast

The categories they want you to hear are different

Terminology choices reveal category strategy more than disease definition.

Convention A: burden language
is universal

Ocular: emphasizes monthly anti-VEGF injections as the historical burden, then frames AXPAXLI as continuous release from ELUTYX hydrogel.1,2

4DMT: reframes burden through one-time genetic delivery and calls 4D-150 a potential “backbone therapy.”8,10

EyePoint: focuses on six-month dosing, “supplement-free” control, and sustained-release insert design.13,15

Convention B: technical vocabulary maps to product architecture

Hydrogel implant: Ocular repeats “implant,” “hydrogel,” “bioresorbable,” and “axitinib” to make the delivery material part of the brand story.

Genetic medicine: 4DMT repeats “vector,” “payload,” “transgene,” and “intravitreal genetic medicine” to signal a different therapeutic class.

Insert platform: EyePoint repeats “insert,” “Durasert E,” “bioerodible,” and “sustained delivery” to separate DURAVYU from conventional injections.
