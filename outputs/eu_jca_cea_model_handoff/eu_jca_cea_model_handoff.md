# EU JCA Treatments and CEA Model-Building Publication Leads

## Objective

Create a handoff-ready evidence map of treatments currently undergoing evaluation in the EU Joint Clinical Assessment (JCA) process, with candidate cost-effectiveness analysis (CEA), cost-utility analysis (CUA), health economic, or model-building publications that another agent can investigate for creating treatment-specific CEA models.

This file captures findings from the current conversation and should be treated as a starting evidence inventory, not a final systematic review.

## Date and Source Basis

- Current handoff date: June 10, 2026.
- EU JCA treatment list basis: European Commission JCA public materials referenced in the conversation, including the official ongoing-JCA workbook extracted June 9, 2026.
- Scope used in the prior findings: treatments listed as ongoing in the European Commission workbook. Discontinued sasanlimab was excluded. Completed tovorafenib / Ojemda was noted in prior work but is excluded from the main "ongoing" table because it is no longer undergoing evaluation.
- Disease areas in EU JCA scope from January 12, 2025: cancer medicines and advanced therapy medicinal products (ATMPs), per European Commission implementation materials.

## Methodology Used in Current Conversation

1. Identified EU JCA treatments from the European Commission ongoing-JCA workbook cited above.
2. Excluded treatments marked discontinued and separated completed JCA products from ongoing JCA products.
3. For each ongoing treatment, searched for direct CEA/CUA/model publications using treatment names, alternate names, disease context, and comparator-class terms.
4. Where no direct CEA was identified, selected the closest model-building analogues by disease, mechanism, treatment class, target population, or useful model inputs such as utility, burden, and resource-use publications.
5. Cross-checked PubMed leads and recorded PMID and DOI where available from the prior findings.
6. Checked public Tufts CEVR/CEA Registry keyword-search signals where accessible. Counts are public-search signals only and require validation by a follow-on agent.

## Caveats and Uncertainties

- The EC workbook should be re-downloaded and parsed before final use because the JCA list can change. The prior findings were based on the workbook available/extracted June 9, 2026.
- Tufts CEVR public search was treated as a keyword-level signal, not a definitive registry extraction. Public results may not expose full stable metadata without deeper registry access.
- "No direct CEA found" means no direct model was identified in the prior live searches and conversation. It is not proof that no model exists.
- Some PMIDs in the prior findings appear to be very recent or future-indexed relative to the current handoff date. A follow-on agent should verify publication metadata, abstracts, DOI resolution, and journal details directly in PubMed and publisher pages.
- Several treatments have multiple synonyms, development codes, or brand names. Missed synonyms are a material risk, especially for newer oncology and gene-therapy assets.
- Many direct CEAs may be jurisdiction-specific, often using China or US prices. Model structure may still be useful even when cost inputs are not transferable to an EU setting.
- The prior task requested live browser, PubMed, and Tufts CEVR searches; this file preserves the results already generated in the conversation rather than rerunning a full systematic search.
- For EU CEA model building, comparator choice should be aligned with final PICO scoping and EU member state standard of care, not simply the comparator used in the most convenient published model.
