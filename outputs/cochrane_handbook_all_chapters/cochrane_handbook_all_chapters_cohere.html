Operating requirements described in the Handbook

Systematic reviews contain pre-specified questions and methods documented in protocols to minimize bias.
Review teams include topic expertise, methods expertise, project management, quality assurance, and conflict-of-interest management.
Stakeholder involvement is part of review planning for decisions about scope and relevance.
PICO defines scope, and protocols define how populations, interventions, outcomes and study designs are grouped for synthesis.
Critical and important outcomes are limited in number, decision-relevant, and include adverse as well as beneficial outcomes.

Broad and narrow scopes

Broad reviews address decisions across options, while narrow reviews address more specific questions. Logic models and stakeholder engagement inform scope.

Trials registers, journal articles, regulatory documents and study reports can conflict.

Missing results and bias

Non-reporting bias arises when reporting depends on the P value, magnitude or direction of results.
Selective non-reporting and under-reporting can bias synthesis even when the study is known.
Funnel plots have setting-specific uses and limitations.

Bias and certainty

Bias is assessed at the result, study, synthesis and evidence-body levels

The Handbook separates internal validity of individual results from bias in a synthesis and certainty in a body of evidence.

Bias assessment elements

Risk-of-bias assessment addresses whether results overestimate or underestimate the true intervention effect.
Signalling-question answers and judgements are accompanied by written justifications.
Study funding and conflicts of interest are considered in directness, heterogeneity, within-study bias and missing-results bias assessments.
Risk of bias and certainty are separate concepts in the Handbook.

Decisions specified before result interpretation

Effect measures match outcome data types: dichotomous, continuous, ordinal, count/rate or time-to-event.
Comparisons and study groupings are prepared before synthesis; PICO tabulation informs what can be combined.
Meta-analysis applies when studies are sufficiently comparable and data are usable; heterogeneity remains part of interpretation.
Prediction intervals in random-effects meta-analysis communicate between-study variation when appropriate.
Sensitivity analyses examine whether findings are robust to influential decisions.

Network meta-analysis

Compares three or more interventions by combining direct and indirect evidence, but requires similarity across studies and attention to incoherence.

Interpretation reporting boundaries

Reporting emphasizes confidence intervals and exact P values rather than statistical-significance labels.
The Handbook distinguishes no evidence of an effect from evidence of no effect.
Review conclusions explain certainty, benefits, harms, values, preferences and cost while remaining distinct from clinical guidance.
Objectives, outcomes, caveats and conclusions are consistent across the main text, abstract, plain language summary and Summary of findings tables.
Reporting audiences include healthcare professionals, consumers, policy makers and methods specialists.

Reporting, updating and Overviews extend the review record

Chapters III-V
22

Reporting standards

Cochrane protocols and reviews follow PRISMA 2020 and PRISMA for Protocols.
Plain language summaries state key messages for consumers and non-expert readers.
Methods, results, caveats and conclusions are consistent across summary formats.

Updating criteria

Reviews are assessed periodically because new studies may make results out of date or misleading.
Update decisions depend on continuing relevance and whether new data or methods could affect findings.
Updates can revise scope, eligibility criteria and methods, with changes managed and reported.

Overviews and review-level questions

Overviews search for and analyse multiple systematic reviews on related questions.
The unit of search, inclusion and analysis is the systematic review rather than the primary study.
The Overview format is matched to the question and associated methodological challenges.

Living reviews
Rapidly evolving fields where research is frequently published.

Prospective meta-analysis

Often collaborative, frequently using individual participant data.
Trial variants
Cluster, crossover and multi-arm trials introduce correlations or design-specific biases.

Non-randomized studies
Randomized trials cannot answer the question or are insufficient.

IPD reviews
Aggregate data are insufficient for a high-quality or thorough review.
Analyses are pre-specified; time, funding and data-management requirements are higher.

Advanced-methods constraints

Formal sequential statistical methods are outside the main analyses for standard updated Cochrane meta-analyses.
Non-randomized studies may be justified, but biases are likely to be greater than in randomized trials.
ROBINS-I starts from a target trial and assesses pre-intervention, at-intervention and post-intervention features.
IPD can improve data quality and analysis flexibility, but creates new demands for collaboration, governance and pre-specification.

Synthesis

Recurring requirements across the Handbook

The review question, eligibility criteria, outcomes, methods and synthesis plans are pre-specified.
Bias is addressed as a multi-layer problem: within studies, across syntheses, from missing results and within the body of evidence.
Synthesis is limited to evidence that can be meaningfully synthesized; heterogeneity and indirectness are substantive considerations.
Certainty and absolute effects are communicated so decision makers can understand what the evidence does and does not support.
Review reporting, updating, equity, harms, economics, implementation and patient-important outcomes are addressed when relevant.
