Cost-effectiveness replication

Replicating the FINGER prevention model from published and supplemental inputs

A transparent cohort Markov reconstruction was developed to evaluate whether the published long-term cost-effectiveness findings could be reproduced without calibrating model parameters to reported study results.

01 / 07
Analytic objective

The replication was structured as an input-driven reconstruction

The analytic objective was to implement the model described by the publication and supplement using externally stated inputs, then compare reconstructed outputs with publication results only after model execution.

Principle

No outcome calibration

Reported costs, QALYs, incident dementia cases, and person-years were reserved for validation. They were not used to fit, scale, or tune model inputs.

Perspective

Societal costing frame

The reconstruction preserved the paper’s societal perspective, 2016 SEK cost year, 3% discount rate, and base-case willingness-to-pay threshold of 600,000 SEK per QALY.

Scope

Base case before scenarios

The first objective was base-case replication. Sensitivity analyses were not expanded until the base-case model structure and data sufficiency were assessed.

The reconstruction therefore estimates reproducibility from disclosed inputs, rather than agreement after parameter adjustment.
Methods aligned to the published model description: lifetime cohort Markov model, half-cycle correction, annual cycles, and age-specific mortality, costs, and utilities

Data extraction

Model inputs were abstracted by domain and assigned to explicit parameter tables

The supplemental file was reviewed for transition probabilities, incidence model inputs, mortality inputs, costs, utilities, intervention cost, and structural assumptions.

Inputs available in the supplement

1

Dementia incidence

EURODEM age-band cases and person-years, Poisson age coefficient, intercept, and risk multiplier for the FINGER at-risk cohort.

2

Clinical progression

Annual transition probabilities among mild, moderate, and severe dementia states from SveDem-derived estimates.

3

Economic and utility inputs

Age-specific cost and utility values at 5-year intervals, intervention cost components, and the total intervention cost.

Inputs requiring special handling

4

Mortality

Initial implementation used rounded 5-year mortality readouts. The final implementation used exact 2016 Statistics Sweden single-year life-table values.

5

Cost GLM

The supplement provides fitted cost values at 5-year ages but does not provide the GLM coefficients or equation required for exact reconstruction.

6

Rounded transition rows

Moderate and severe dementia transition rows summed to 1.01 in the rounded table and were normalized to preserve cohort mass.

Supplemental tables S1-S8 and Supplemental Figure S3 were used as the primary abstraction sources
03 / 07
Model implementation

The Markov cohort model was implemented with transparent annual state accounting

The implementation followed the reported five-state structure and applied transition, mortality, cost, and utility inputs at each annual cycle.

State space

At risk

All individuals entered the model at age 70 in the at-risk state, with age-specific annual transition to mild dementia.

Dementia

Mild to severe

Dementia progression used the observed annual transition matrix, with no direct at-risk transition to moderate dementia.

Mortality

State-specific death

General-population mortality was applied to the at-risk state, and dementia mortality hazard ratios were applied by severity.

Outcomes

Costs and QALYs

Costs and QALYs were accumulated with half-cycle correction and discounted at 3% annually.

Intervention effect specification

The FINGER intervention was represented as a relative risk of 0.9356 applied to age-specific dementia incidence. The effect was applied across the model horizon, consistent with the base-case assumption that the intervention effect persisted after the two-year program.

Implementation file: outputs/finger_replication/finger_cost_effectiveness_replication.py

Data sufficiency

Unavailable cost GLM parameters were not imputed from publication results

Model reconstruction distinguished between parameters that were directly available, parameters that could be obtained from cited public sources, and parameters that remained unavailable after review.

External mortality update

The mortality component was refined using official single-year Swedish life tables

The supplement described conversion of sex-specific death probabilities to rates, age-weighting by the proportion of men and women alive, applying dementia severity hazard ratios, and back-transforming rates to probabilities.

Source

Statistics Sweden

Annual 2016 probabilities of dying per mille and numbers living at each age were retrieved for men and women from ages 50 through 100.

Transformation

Probability to rate

Sex-specific probabilities were transformed to rates using −ln(1−p), weighted by the life-table survivor counts, and converted back after applying hazard ratios.

Effect

Closer survival replication

The updated mortality procedure reproduced survival and death outcomes closely, with a 30-year death difference of −29 versus −28 in the publication.

Validation

Incremental health outcomes were closely reproduced; absolute costs remained less certain

After model execution, outputs were compared with reported publication results to identify domains in which the reconstructed model aligned and domains affected by unavailable inputs.

Interpretation

Incremental effectiveness, dementia incidence, survival, and net benefit were consistent with the publication. The prevention strategy remained dominant in the reconstructed base case.

Primary residual limitation

Absolute costs were higher than the publication because exact cost GLM coefficients were unavailable. Cost outputs should therefore be interpreted as an approximate reconstruction.
