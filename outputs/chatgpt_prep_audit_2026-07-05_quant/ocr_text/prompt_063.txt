How clinicians should counsel patients about HIV testing
1. HIV testing is required before every injection
Patients should be told clearly that:
·They musthave a documented negativeHIV test immediatelybeforeeach injectionvisit.
·This is typically a 4th-generation HIV-1/2 antigen-antibody test.
·If recent exposure or symptoms suggest acute HIV infection,HIV RNA testing should also be done,and
injections should be delayed until infection is ruled out.
This is because long-acting cabotegravir can persist in the body for months, and starting it during
undetected infection can lead to integrase inhibitor resistance.

2. Testing happens on a fixed schedule tied to dosing
Clinicians should explain the rhythm:
·Initiation phase: HIV test before starting (and often after oral lead-in if used)
·Injection phase:
·Test before every injection every 2 months (after the initial monthly loading doses)
·If a dose is late:
·HIV testing is still required before restarting injections
This structured testing schedule is a key safety feature of injectable PrEP compared with daily oral PrEP.
