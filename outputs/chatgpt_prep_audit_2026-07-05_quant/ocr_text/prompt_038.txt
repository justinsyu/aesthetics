1)Put side effects in context of benefit (absolute risk framing)
·The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention notes PrEP reduces the risk of acquiring HIV by
about 99% from sexual exposure when taken daily.
·The World Health Organization similarly emphasizes that oral PrEP is highly effective and recommends it
for people at substantial risk of HIV acquisition .
Framing example:“Some people experience mild sideeffects early on,butthe protectionagainstHIVis
extremely high and far outweighs theserisksforpeopleat risk"
2)Normalizecommonsideeffectsandemphasizetemporality
Most side effects of oral PrEP (e.g., nausea, headache, mild Gl upset) are:
·Common but usually mild
·Transient (often resolving within days to weeks)
Guidelines from CDC recommend counseling that early side effects are expected for some patients and often
improve without stopping medication.
Key communication principle: avoid framing side effects as"warning signs" and instead present them as
anticipated andmanageablestart-up effects.
