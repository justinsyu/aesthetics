# AML Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: 2026-05-11

Disease state: Acute myeloid leukemia (AML)

Purpose: Provide a current, credible website search plan for AML competitive intelligence. This is a source and monitoring plan, not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track treatment-landscape changes in AML, including newly diagnosed fit/intensive, newly diagnosed older or intensive-ineligible, relapsed/refractory, mutation-defined subgroups, secondary AML, therapy-related AML, AML with myelodysplasia-related changes, acute promyelocytic leukemia, pediatric AML, transplant-eligible populations, and maintenance settings.
- Detect competitor events early: new trial starts, protocol amendments, enrollment pauses, primary-completion changes, topline readouts, conference abstracts, peer-reviewed publications, FDA/EMA/PMDA/NMPA/Health Canada actions, label expansions, withdrawals, safety communications, and guideline changes.
- Monitor mechanism- and biomarker-specific competition, including BCL-2, FLT3, IDH1, IDH2, menin/KMT2A/NPM1, CD33, CD47/SIRP-alpha, CD123, CD70, TIM-3, TP53-directed approaches, MDM2, IRAK4, CDK9, antibody-drug conjugates, bispecifics, cell therapies, vaccines, and transplant-conditioning regimens.
- Track evidence generation by treatment setting, regimen backbone, mutation subgroup, measurable residual disease (MRD), transplant bridging, post-transplant maintenance, response depth, survival, transfusion independence, safety, quality of life, and real-world adoption.
- Monitor market access and commercial signals, including NCCN/compendia inclusion, payer restrictions, prior authorization criteria, coding, ASP/Part B pricing, Part D formulary treatment for oral agents, HTA recommendations, patient assistance, and launch messaging.
- Build a repeatable website search process that privileges primary sources and documents uncertainty when only secondary or company-promotional information is available.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest confidence: regulator pages, official labels, trial registries, peer-reviewed publications, guideline bodies, HTA bodies, and official registry/statistics sources.
- High confidence but interpret carefully: conference abstracts and posters. They are often preliminary, may be embargoed, and may differ from final publications.
- Medium confidence: company press releases, investor presentations, product websites, and earnings transcripts. They are primary for company statements but promotional; verify efficacy, safety, trial status, and regulatory claims against labels, registries, abstracts, or publications.
- Medium-to-low confidence: reputable trade press. Use for alerting, deal context, and analyst leads, then verify with primary sources.
- Low confidence: social media, forums, Wikipedia, non-credentialed blogs, scraped trial aggregators, and AI-generated summaries. Use only as leads, not as CI evidence.
- For clinical claims, capture source date, population, line of therapy, biomarker, comparator, endpoint, follow-up duration, and whether the claim comes from an abstract, manuscript, label, or company statement.
- For safety claims, distinguish trial adverse-event rates, label warnings, FAERS signals, case reports, and regulatory safety communications.
- For access claims, distinguish FDA approval from NCCN listing, compendia support, payer coverage, formulary placement, coding/payment, and patient out-of-pocket affordability.
- For AML specifically, always tag results by mutation/subtype and fitness/treatment-intensity group. General AML claims can be misleading because competitive relevance varies sharply by FLT3, IDH1/2, NPM1, KMT2A, TP53, secondary AML, transplant status, and older/unfit status.
- Maintain an evidence log with URL, access date, publication/update date, source category, and corroborating source. If a company statement is not corroborated by a registry, regulator, conference abstract, or manuscript, mark it as unverified.
