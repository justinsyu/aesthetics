# Geographic Atrophy CI Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for geographic atrophy (GA) secondary to age-related macular degeneration (AMD). It identifies credible websites to search and monitor. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track changes in the GA treatment landscape, including approved complement inhibitors, dosing/label changes, safety updates, formulation changes, and emerging oral, gene, cell, neuroprotective, complement, and visual-cycle approaches.
- Monitor clinical development activity across approved and emerging assets, including trial starts, enrollment updates, endpoint changes, readouts, terminations, extensions, and investigator-sponsored evidence.
- Identify regulatory signals in the United States, Europe, United Kingdom, Canada, Japan, Australia, and other priority markets, including approval decisions, refusals, withdrawals, label variations, advisory committee materials, and safety communications.
- Track scientific and medical-society activity that can change diagnosis, imaging, patient selection, endpoint expectations, trial feasibility, and clinical adoption.
- Monitor market access and commercialization signals: HCPCS/J-code updates, ASP pricing, Medicare LCD/NCD language, payer prior authorization criteria, VA criteria, HTA decisions, patient support programs, launch metrics, and revenue commentary.
- Maintain an auditable source trail that prioritizes official primary sources, peer-reviewed literature, society/congress materials, and clearly labeled secondary sources.

## Source-quality notes

- Prioritize primary sources for factual claims: FDA/EMA/MHRA/PMDA/Health Canada/TGA pages, ClinicalTrials.gov/CTIS/WHO ICTRP, company SEC filings, official labels, CMS/VA/payer policy documents, and peer-reviewed journal articles.
- Treat company press releases and HCP/patient websites as primary for company positioning, but not as independent evidence of comparative benefit. Confirm clinical claims against labels, trial registry records, manuscripts, and congress abstracts.
- Treat FAERS as signal-generating only. Reports are incomplete, duplicate-prone, and cannot establish incidence or causality. Use label updates, peer-reviewed pharmacovigilance analyses, ASRS safety communications, and regulatory safety actions for stronger evidence.
- Treat payer policy language as plan-specific and time-sensitive. Always record effective date, line of business, product name, HCPCS code, diagnosis code, step criteria, continuation criteria, and archive date.
- Treat meeting abstracts as early evidence. Track whether data later appear in peer-reviewed manuscripts, regulatory submissions, labels, or company filings.
- Use disease foundations and patient organizations to understand patient language, unmet need, recruitment, and advocacy priorities. Do not use them as sole sources for efficacy, safety, or market share.
- Use secondary news and analyst-style sources for discovery and context only. Verify material claims against official company releases, SEC filings, trial registries, labels, HTA documents, or peer-reviewed publications.
- Watch terminology drift: GA may appear as "advanced dry AMD", "atrophic AMD", "non-neovascular AMD", "late-stage dry AMD", "macular atrophy", "RPE atrophy", "cRORA", or "complete retinal pigment epithelium and outer retinal atrophy".
- Record exact access date for dynamic pages such as payer policies, company HCP sites, congress programs, pricing files, and trial registry records.
- Maintain separation between "website to search" and "finding": this plan is a source inventory and query strategy, not a market landscape report.
