# Solid Tumors Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Compiled: May 11, 2026

## CI objectives

- Build a repeatable source map for broad solid-tumor oncology CI across clinical, regulatory, scientific, pipeline, market access, and commercial signals.
- Track cross-tumor and tumor-agnostic developments, including basket trials, biomarker-defined indications, precision oncology, immuno-oncology, ADCs, radioligand therapy, cell therapy, targeted therapies, and perioperative/adjuvant expansion.
- Identify current and emerging competitors by mechanism of action, biomarker, line of therapy, trial phase, sponsor, geography, regulatory status, and reimbursement/access status.
- Monitor leading indicators before formal approvals: new trial starts, protocol amendments, abstract titles, late-breaking presentations, press releases, investor decks, regulatory review milestones, advisory committee agendas, HTA scoping documents, and guideline updates.
- Preserve source quality by separating primary sources from curated secondary sources and treating company/news claims as leads that require validation against registries, labels, filings, conference abstracts, or peer-reviewed publications.

## Monitoring cadence

- Daily: FDA oncology approvals, company press releases, SEC 8-K filings, ClinicalTrials.gov new/updated recruiting trials, major oncology news sources, and conference embargo-release windows.
- Weekly: ClinicalTrials.gov/CTIS/WHO ICTRP change review, company pipeline pages, investor-event calendars, PubMed alerts, guideline-update pages, and sponsor-specific web searches.
- Biweekly: EMA medicines/CHMP updates, Health Canada updates, trial status changes, registry/epidemiology searches for new biomarker papers, and secondary news validation.
- Monthly: NCCN guideline and compendium updates, CMS ASP files, NICE/CDA-AMC/SMC/G-BA/IQWiG/HAS/PBAC/PHARMAC decisions, payer policy changes, and company investor decks.
- Quarterly: SEC 10-Q/10-K review, earnings-call transcript review, commercial performance tracking, competitive landscape refresh by modality/mechanism/biomarker, and epidemiology/market sizing refresh.
- Conference-driven: intensify monitoring from abstract-title release through poster/oral presentation and one week after the meeting. For ASCO, AACR, ESMO, SITC, ASCO GI, ASCO GU, and EORTC-NCI-AACR, capture abstract titles, full abstracts, late breakers, posters, oral slides, discussant comments, press programs, and simultaneous publications.
- Regulatory-event-driven: add daily checks around PDUFA dates, ODAC meetings, CHMP meetings, advisory committee briefing-package release, label updates, accelerated-approval conversion/withdrawal activity, and Project Orbis approvals.

## Source-quality notes

- Treat regulator databases, labels, trial registries, government cancer statistics, HTA bodies, and peer-reviewed publications as primary or authoritative sources.
- Treat conference abstracts as primary scientific disclosures but verify maturity, cutoff dates, sample size, follow-up, subgroup definitions, and whether poster/oral data differ from abstract text.
- Treat ClinicalTrials.gov and CTIS as sponsor-reported records. Validate important fields against protocols, publications, company disclosures, and regulatory filings when possible.
- Treat company press releases, investor decks, and earnings comments as useful leading indicators but not independent evidence. Confirm claims through trial registries, abstracts, labels, filings, or peer-reviewed papers.
- Treat secondary trade press and subscription databases as discovery and synthesis tools. Use them to find leads, dates, sponsors, and strategic context; cite primary sources for final claims.
- Watch for oncology-specific evidence traps: single-arm response-rate data without duration, immature OS/PFS, pooled dose-escalation cohorts, post hoc biomarker subgroups, cross-trial comparisons, changing standard-of-care comparators, region-specific labels, and access restrictions that narrow real-world use.
- For broad solid tumors, separate true tumor-agnostic claims from multi-cohort studies that include many tumor types but only support activity in a subset.
- For market access, distinguish regulatory approval from reimbursement, guideline inclusion, test availability, site-of-care feasibility, and payer policy. A broad label may still have constrained uptake if biomarker testing, infusion logistics, toxicity management, or prior authorization is difficult.
