# NSCLC Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: 2026-05-11

Disease state: Non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)

Purpose: Provide a current, credible website search plan for NSCLC competitive intelligence. This is a source and monitoring plan, not a market or clinical report.

## CI objectives

- Detect clinical practice shifts in NSCLC by tracking guideline updates, biomarker testing recommendations, treatment sequencing, perioperative therapy, stage III consolidation, and metastatic-line treatment changes.
- Identify competitive events early, including trial starts, enrollment changes, primary completion dates, topline readouts, conference abstracts, label expansions, accelerated approvals, withdrawals, and safety communications.
- Track pipeline movement by mechanism, biomarker, stage, line of therapy, and regimen type, including EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, MET, RET, NTRK, KRAS G12C, HER2/ERBB2, NRG1, PD-1/PD-L1, TIGIT, TROP2 ADCs, HER3 ADCs, bispecifics, vaccines, and radiopharmaceuticals.
- Monitor evidence quality by following peer-reviewed publications, conference abstracts, registry/real-world evidence, and health technology assessment decisions.
- Monitor market access signals, including payer policy changes, Medicare coverage, molecular testing coverage, HTA recommendations, pricing/reimbursement notices, coding, and compendia-relevant updates.
- Build a repeatable source inventory that can be searched by analyst, asset, company, biomarker, regimen, and treatment setting.

## Trial/regulatory/pipeline sources

Prioritize source triangulation:

1. Trial registry signal: ClinicalTrials.gov, CTIS, WHO ICTRP, company trial pages.
2. Regulator signal: FDA approval notification, Drugs@FDA label/reviews, EMA EPAR/CHMP, MHRA, PMDA, NMPA, Health Canada, TGA.
3. Company signal: press release, investor deck, SEC filing, earnings transcript.
4. Evidence signal: conference abstract, poster, peer-reviewed paper, guideline update.
5. Access signal: NCCN compendia, CMS, payer policy, NICE/CDA-AMC/G-BA/HAS/PBAC/SMC.

Fields to capture when searching:

- Asset name, generic name, brand name, sponsor, partner, mechanism, target, biomarker, mutation subtype, disease stage, histology, line of therapy, regimen backbone, comparator, trial ID, trial acronym, endpoints, geography, regulatory status, guideline status, payer/HTA status, and next expected milestone.

## Market access/commercial sources

Core access questions to answer through website monitoring:

- Is the asset or regimen listed in NCCN guidelines or NCCN Drugs & Biologics Compendium for the relevant NSCLC setting?
- Has CMS issued or revised an NCD, LCD, or billing article affecting therapy, molecular testing, imaging, radiation, or companion diagnostics?
- Are major commercial payers adding prior authorization, step therapy, PD-L1/biomarker restrictions, line-of-therapy restrictions, duration limits, or reauthorization requirements?
- Has an HTA body recommended, restricted, rejected, or placed the product under managed access?
- Has a new HCPCS code, ASP file entry, WAC change, NADAC benchmark, or Part D formulary signal emerged?
- Do company filings disclose launch uptake, reimbursement delays, inventory effects, gross-to-net pressure, or competitive erosion?

## Monitoring cadence

- Daily during major congress windows: ASCO, ESMO, WCLC, AACR, ELCC, SITC, ASTRO. Monitor abstract releases, embargo lifts, late-breaking sessions, plenaries, press programs, company press releases, and journal simultaneous publications.
- Daily around known regulatory action dates: FDA PDUFA dates, advisory committee meetings, CHMP meeting weeks, EMA opinion publication, MHRA/NICE publication windows, and company earnings calls.
- Weekly: FDA oncology approvals, ClinicalTrials.gov changes for priority assets, company press releases, SEC 8-Ks, ASCO/ESMO/NCCN/IASLC updates, key journals, and major industry news.
- Biweekly: trial registry sweeps by biomarker and mechanism; payer-policy portals; NCCN guideline version checks; company pipeline pages and investor decks.
- Monthly: HTA body decisions, CMS MCD/LCD updates, ASP/NADAC/HCPCS files, Part D formulary files, epidemiology and registry updates, patient advocacy/foundation research updates.
- Quarterly: company 10-Q/earnings review, pipeline comparison table refresh, market access restrictions audit, competitive event timeline refresh.
- Annually: SEER/USCS/NPCR epidemiology refresh, NCCN/ASCO/ESMO guideline baseline review, conference calendar refresh, journal impact/source list refresh, and source-quality audit.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest-confidence sources are regulators, trial registries, peer-reviewed journals, official conference abstract books, guideline bodies, CMS/HTA bodies, and company SEC filings.
- Company press releases and investor decks are primary sources for sponsor claims, but they are promotional. Confirm claims against trial registries, regulator documents, conference abstracts, labels, or peer-reviewed papers.
- Trial registries can lag reality or contain sponsor-entered errors. Track "last update posted," primary completion dates, status changes, enrollment changes, endpoint changes, and country/site activation.
- Conference abstracts may be preliminary and embargoed; do not treat abstracts as final evidence until full presentation, poster, or manuscript is available.
- Preprints, medtech blogs, and social media are low-confidence signal sources. Use them only to identify leads, then corroborate through official or peer-reviewed sources.
- Payer policies may differ by plan, line of business, state, and Medicare versus commercial benefit. Capture effective dates, revision dates, criteria, and whether the policy applies to medical or pharmacy benefit.
- HTA decisions are jurisdiction-specific. Separate clinical conclusion, economic conclusion, managed access arrangement, comparator assumptions, and eligible population.
- Guidelines may require login or may not expose full text to search engines. Record guideline version number and update date whenever available.
- For China/Japan/EU sources, translation and regional indication wording can change meaning. Confirm with official regulator pages and English summaries when available.
- Paid databases such as Evaluate, IQVIA, MMIT, and Clarivate can be useful for commercial estimates and access data, but cite the underlying public source when possible for auditable deliverables.
