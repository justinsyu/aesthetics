# Colorectal Cancer Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Current as of May 11, 2026. Scope: credible websites and search tactics for colorectal cancer competitive intelligence. This is a source plan, not a CI findings report.

## CI Objectives

- Track changes in colorectal cancer treatment standards across metastatic, adjuvant, neoadjuvant, locally advanced rectal cancer, biomarker-defined subgroups, and screening/early detection.
- Monitor competitor activity across approved therapies, late-stage pipeline agents, diagnostics, ctDNA/MRD tools, biomarker strategies, and combination regimens.
- Identify clinical data catalysts from trial registries, conference abstracts, peer-reviewed publications, regulatory decisions, and company disclosures.
- Monitor payer, HTA, reimbursement, formulary, and guideline/compendium signals that can affect access and adoption.
- Maintain a source-quality hierarchy that separates official primary sources from secondary reporting, advocacy commentary, and paid market intelligence.

## Trial, Regulatory, and Pipeline Sources

Use these sources for structured monitoring of assets, mechanisms, stages, and catalysts.

- Trial registry core fields: condition, intervention, sponsor/collaborator, phase, enrollment, study start, primary completion, locations, status, outcomes, inclusion/exclusion, biomarker eligibility, prior treatment line, and NCT/EudraCT/CTIS IDs.
- Regulatory fields: indication wording, biomarker requirement, line of therapy, accelerated versus traditional approval, companion diagnostic language, label updates, postmarketing commitments, advisory committee materials, and safety communications.
- Pipeline fields: sponsor asset name, mechanism, target biomarker, setting, combination backbone, trial acronym, data-readout timing, regulatory filing timing, and geographic filing sequence.
- Primary source order: trial registry or regulator first, company release second, conference abstract third, peer-reviewed paper fourth, secondary trade press fifth.
- CRC mechanisms and segments to tag: EGFR, VEGF/VEGFR, BRAF V600E, KRAS G12C/G12D/pan-KRAS, HER2, NTRK/RET/ALK fusions, MSI-H/dMMR, MSS/pMMR immunotherapy combinations, ctDNA/MRD, radiopharmaceuticals, antibody-drug conjugates, bispecifics, cell therapy, microbiome, and liver-directed therapy.

## Market Access and Commercial Sources

- U.S. coverage and reimbursement: CMS Medicare Coverage Database, Medicare.gov, CMS ASP files, Part B drug spending, Medicaid NADAC, NCCN Compendium, payer policies, and major PBM/formulary documents where public.
- Ex-U.S. access: NICE, CDA-AMC, G-BA, IQWiG, HAS, PBAC, EMA EPARs, MHRA approvals, PMDA, and Health Canada.
- Commercial uptake proxies: guideline inclusion, compendium category, HTA recommendation, patient access scheme, label line of therapy, biomarker testing burden, payer policy language, J-code/HCPCS status, ASP trend, Part B utilization, company sales commentary, and institutional pathway adoption.
- Screening diagnostics market access: USPSTF recommendation grade, CMS NCD 210.3, Medicare.gov coverage language, FDA device/diagnostic approvals, ACS screening guidance, payer policies, and laboratory coverage policies.
- Secondary commercial sources should be used for signal detection only; verify all material facts against primary sources before including them in any deliverable.

## Monitoring Cadence

## Source-Quality Notes

- Highest confidence: regulators, trial registries, official labels, NCCN/ASCO/ESMO/NCI guidelines, CDC/SEER/NAACCR/IARC registries, CMS/NICE/CDA-AMC/G-BA/IQWiG/HAS/PBAC payer and HTA pages.
- High but context-dependent: peer-reviewed journals, official conference abstracts, company SEC filings, earnings call transcripts, and company trial portals.
- Useful for signal detection, not final substantiation: company press releases, advocacy newsletters, The ASCO Post, oncology trade press, analyst notes, BioCentury, Endpoints, Fierce, STAT, Evaluate, Citeline, and GlobalData.
- Handle cautiously: preprints, posters without peer-reviewed manuscripts, subgroup analyses, single-arm trials, retrospective real-world studies, non-peer-reviewed slide decks, social media, patient forums, and unsourced conference rumor tracking.
- For every material claim in a later CI deliverable, triangulate at least two source types when possible: one primary source plus either a peer-reviewed/conference source or a regulator/guideline/HTA source.
- Record access date, publication/update date, geography, disease setting, biomarker subgroup, line of therapy, comparator, data maturity, and whether the source is primary, secondary, or paid.
