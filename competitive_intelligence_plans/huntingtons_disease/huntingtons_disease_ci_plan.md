# Huntington's Disease Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for Huntington's disease (HD). It identifies credible websites to search and monitor for clinical, regulatory, pipeline, market access, and commercial intelligence. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track changes in the HD treatment landscape, including symptomatic chorea therapies, oral HTT-lowering/splicing modulators, allele-selective oligonucleotides, AAV or other gene therapies, sigma-1 receptor agonists, biomarker programs, digital endpoints, and supportive-care standards.
- Detect development and competitor signals early: trial starts, protocol amendments, enrollment updates, long-term extension data, futility/success decisions, expanded access, regulatory filings, advisory committee activity, label updates, safety communications, publications, abstracts, investor disclosures, and collaboration/licensing changes.
- Monitor disease-specific infrastructure that can affect trial design and feasibility: Enroll-HD, HDClarity, HSG sites, EHDN resources, clinical outcome measures, natural-history comparators, biomarker datasets, genetic subgroups, and patient/caregiver research priorities.
- Track regulatory and access signals that shape commercial opportunity: FDA/EMA/MHRA/Health Canada/HTA actions, orphan/fast-track/RMAT/breakthrough designations, payer prior authorization criteria, Medicare negotiation, formulary tiers, patient support, and advocacy positions.
- Maintain an auditable search process that uses official primary sources first, then peer-reviewed literature and recognized secondary sources for context or lead generation.

## Trial/regulatory/pipeline sources

### Trial registry workflow

1. Search ClinicalTrials.gov weekly for HD condition terms, product names, mechanisms, and sponsors; record changes to recruitment status, estimated completion dates, sample size, endpoints, and results.
2. Cross-check WHO ICTRP monthly for records outside ClinicalTrials.gov and for duplicate or bridged IDs from regional registries.
3. Search CTIS and EU Clinical Trials Register monthly for EU trial starts, substantial modifications, trial status, and public reports.
4. Review HSG, EHDN, Enroll-HD, HDClarity, sponsor trial pages, and patient-facing recruitment pages monthly for site activation and recruitment signals not yet obvious in registry metadata.
5. Archive registry URLs, NCT/EUCT/EudraCT/ISRCTN IDs, record-history dates, and screenshots/PDFs when a material change is detected.

## Source-quality notes

- Treat registries, regulatory agencies, official labels, HTA bodies, company filings, and company press releases as primary sources for factual status, dates, endpoints, and disclosed results.
- Trial registries can lag sponsor announcements and may contain sponsor-entered data; verify material trial changes against protocol documents, results postings, publications, and company disclosures.
- Company press releases and investor decks are primary for company claims but are promotional and selective; verify efficacy/safety claims through registry results, peer-reviewed publications, conference materials, and regulatory documents when available.
- Enroll-HD and HDClarity are high-value HD infrastructure sources for natural history, biomarkers, external controls, and feasibility; document whether a sponsor's analysis uses independent statistical review, prespecified methods, and comparable populations.
- HDBuzz, disease society news, and patient-community materials are useful for plain-language interpretation and community priorities, but do not treat them as definitive evidence for clinical or regulatory conclusions.
- Peer-reviewed journals are stronger than abstracts or preprints; preprints, posters, and conference slides should be flagged as preliminary until replicated or peer-reviewed.
- FAERS and social/community reports are signal-detection tools only; they cannot estimate incidence or causality without denominator and adjudication.
- Payer policies and manufacturer assistance pages often change without durable versioning; save PDFs/screenshots and capture access dates for any material access conclusions.
- Licensed databases and trade press are useful for surveillance and market context, but every material claim should be traced back to a primary source before inclusion in a CI deliverable.
- For HD specifically, pay close attention to disease stage definitions, CAG-repeat eligibility, use of antidopaminergic/chorea medications, genetic subgroups, wild-type versus mutant HTT lowering, neurosurgical delivery burden, external-control methodology, cUHDRS/TFC/UHDRS interpretation, NfL/MRI biomarker directionality, and exclusion criteria related to prior gene therapy or RNA-targeted treatments.
