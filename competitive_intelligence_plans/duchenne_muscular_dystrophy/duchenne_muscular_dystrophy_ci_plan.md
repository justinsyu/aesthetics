# Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy CI Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD), including Becker muscular dystrophy (BMD) only where sources, registries, trials, labels, or access decisions group dystrophinopathies together. It identifies credible websites to search and monitor. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track regulatory movement in DMD: new approvals, label changes, boxed warnings, safety communications, advisory committee materials, accelerated approval confirmatory requirements, orphan designations, pediatric designations, complete response letters when public, and EU/UK/Canada/Japan status changes.
- Monitor clinical development activity across gene therapy, exon skipping, antibody-oligonucleotide conjugates, cell therapy, HDAC inhibition, corticosteroid alternatives, myosin modulation, anti-inflammatory approaches, fibrosis/cardiomyopathy strategies, and supportive-care interventions.
- Identify competitor evidence generation: pivotal and confirmatory trials, long-term follow-up, natural-history comparisons, real-world evidence, patient-reported outcomes, caregiver burden, cardiac/pulmonary endpoints, functional scales, biomarkers, postmarketing commitments, abstracts, posters, and manuscripts.
- Track care-standard and endpoint evolution: DMD care considerations, steroid use, cardiac and pulmonary management, ambulatory versus non-ambulatory definitions, North Star Ambulatory Assessment (NSAA), timed function tests, Performance of Upper Limb (PUL), dystrophin quantification, MRI/MRS, patient preference, and health-related quality-of-life measures.
- Monitor market access and commercial signals: HTA decisions, payer medical/pharmacy policies, prior authorization criteria, gene-therapy center requirements, Medicaid policies, commercial arrangements, innovative medicines fund activity, patient support programs, coding/payment updates, and treatment access initiatives.
- Build a repeatable source workflow that starts with official and primary sources, uses society/foundation sources for stakeholder context, and treats news, social media, and investment commentary as leads requiring primary-source validation.

## Source-quality notes

- Prefer primary sources for facts: FDA, EMA, MHRA, Health Canada, PMDA, ClinicalTrials.gov, CTIS, WHO ICTRP, peer-reviewed manuscripts, company SEC filings, company press releases, and official HTA documents.
- Treat company press releases as primary for what the sponsor says, but not as independent validation of efficacy, safety, or market impact. Verify with trial registry records, regulatory documents, conference abstracts, or peer-reviewed publications.
- For safety, label, and indication status, use FDA/EMA/MHRA/Health Canada/PMDA and current prescribing information before company or media pages. FAERS is signal-generating and cannot establish incidence or causality.
- For DMD gene therapy, always check the latest label, safety communications, and clinical-hold status because regulatory and risk language can change quickly.
- For accelerated approvals and conditional authorizations, track confirmatory trial status and regulator language separately from product availability.
- For mutation-specific therapies, record the exact amenable exon or mutation subgroup. Do not generalize exon 44/45/51/53 or nonsense-mutation evidence to all DMD.
- For ambulatory and non-ambulatory populations, preserve the source's exact language. Access, trial eligibility, and labels often hinge on ambulatory status, age, mutation, steroid use, cardiac/pulmonary function, and prior gene therapy.
- For registries and natural-history studies, record population source, inclusion criteria, dates, geography, missing data, endpoint definitions, and whether data are patient-reported, clinician-reported, or chart-abstracted.
- For HTA and payer sources, distinguish list price, net/commercial arrangement, coverage criteria, medical-necessity criteria, site-of-care restrictions, and actual utilization. Public payer policy does not prove paid claims.
- For Project HERCULES and foundation-generated evidence, note funders, participating manufacturers, patient-organization role, methods, and whether outputs are peer-reviewed, conference abstracts, tools, or unpublished models.
- Use secondary media, analyst notes, Reddit, investor forums, and social media only as lead-generation sources. Do not cite them as factual support unless the underlying primary document is found and reviewed.
- Date-stamp every captured source. DMD sources are especially time-sensitive because labels, safety actions, trial status, HTA recommendations, and payer policies have changed frequently.
