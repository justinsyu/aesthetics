# Head and Neck Squamous Cell Carcinoma Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: 2026-05-11

Disease state: Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC; also searched as SCCHN)

Purpose: Provide a current, credible website search plan for HNSCC competitive intelligence. This is a source and monitoring plan focused on where and how to search, not a clinical or market report.

## CI objectives

- Track treatment-practice changes across recurrent/metastatic, locally advanced, resectable, unresectable, definitive chemoradiation, adjuvant, neoadjuvant, and HPV-positive settings.
- Detect competitive events early, including new trial starts, protocol amendments, enrollment updates, primary completion changes, topline readouts, conference abstracts, approvals, label expansions, accelerated approval obligations, safety communications, and guideline updates.
- Segment competitive signals by subsite and biology: oral cavity, oropharynx, larynx, hypopharynx, nasopharynx when relevant, HPV/p16 status, HPV16-specific approaches, PD-L1 CPS, EGFR, HRAS, tumor mutational profile, prior platinum exposure, prior PD-1/PD-L1 exposure, and operability.
- Monitor therapies and mechanisms relevant to HNSCC: PD-1/PD-L1, EGFR antibodies, EGFR/MET bispecifics, TGF-beta/EGFR bispecifics, therapeutic vaccines, personalized immunotherapy, ADCs, cellular therapies, HRAS inhibitors, radiotherapy combinations, and perioperative immunotherapy.
- Monitor evidence quality through peer-reviewed publications, conference abstracts, trial registries, regulator documents, HTA decisions, claims databases, and registry/real-world sources.
- Track access and commercial signals: payer policy changes, HTA appraisals, coding, Medicare payment, guideline compendia, biomarker-testing coverage, launch sequencing, and company disclosures.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest-quality sources for factual status are primary sources: regulatory databases and labels, ClinicalTrials.gov/CTIS records, official guideline pages, peer-reviewed journals, conference abstract portals, and HTA documents.
- Trial registries are sponsor-reported and may lag real-world enrollment or internal decisions. Use them for formal status, endpoints, and dates, but validate major claims against press releases, abstracts, publications, and regulatory filings.
- FDA/EMA/HTA pages define approved indications and reimbursement decisions more reliably than brand websites, conference slides, or news coverage. When label wording matters, confirm in Drugs@FDA, DailyMed, EMA EPARs, or the Union Register.
- Guidelines and compendia are essential for treatment-positioning and payer relevance, but account access and update timing can limit visibility. Log version numbers and update dates whenever available.
- Conference abstracts are credible early evidence but often incomplete. Reconcile abstract text with oral-presentation slides, later manuscript publications, and registry updates.
- Company press releases, investor decks, and earnings-call comments are important lead sources but are selective. Validate claims against trial registries, publications, filings, labels, and independent abstracts before using them as evidence.
- Secondary commercial sources such as BioCentury, Endpoints, Fierce, Pink Sheet, Scrip, Evaluate, MMIT, and IPD Analytics are useful for awareness and interpretation, but should not be treated as primary evidence for approval status, trial outcomes, pricing, or coverage.
- Payer policies can differ by line of business, geography, benefit type, and pathway vendor. Record policy owner, effective date, revision date, indication wording, biomarker requirements, and prior-authorization criteria.
- HNSCC terminology is inconsistent across sources. Always search both `HNSCC` and `SCCHN`, plus exact subsite terms and HPV/p16 terms, to avoid missing relevant evidence.
- Nasopharyngeal carcinoma is biologically and clinically distinct from most HNSCC sources. Include it only when a source explicitly groups it with head and neck squamous cancers or when monitoring broader head and neck oncology competition.
- Registry statistics often use anatomic categories rather than HNSCC histology. Note whether a source reports oral cavity/pharynx/larynx/nasopharynx cancer broadly or specifically squamous histology.
