# Hepatocellular Carcinoma Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: 2026-05-11

Disease state: Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)

Purpose: Provide a current, credible website search plan for HCC competitive intelligence. This is a source and monitoring plan, not a clinical, market, or competitor findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track standard-of-care changes across HCC surveillance, diagnosis, staging, locoregional therapy, transplant-eligible disease, adjuvant/neoadjuvant strategies, unresectable/intermediate-stage disease, and advanced/metastatic systemic therapy.
- Detect competitive events early: trial starts, protocol amendments, enrollment changes, primary completion dates, abstract titles, late-breaking presentations, pivotal publications, label updates, safety signals, guideline changes, HTA decisions, and payer-policy revisions.
- Monitor pipeline movement by setting, regimen, biomarker, and mechanism, including PD-1/PD-L1, CTLA-4, VEGF/VEGFR, multikinase TKIs, FGFR4/FGF19, MET, TGF-beta, GPC3, AFP vaccines/TCRs, cell therapy, bispecifics, ADCs, radiopharmaceuticals, oncolytic viruses, and liver-directed combinations.
- Separate HCC from adjacent but distinct markets: cholangiocarcinoma, combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma, fibrolamellar carcinoma, liver metastases from other primary tumors, and broader liver/intrahepatic bile duct cancer statistics.
- Monitor commercial and access signals affecting adoption: NCCN category/compendium status, line-of-therapy restrictions, Child-Pugh/ECOG restrictions, bleeding/varices screening language for anti-VEGF regimens, biomarker requirements, coding, ASP trends, HTA restrictions, and public payer policies.
- Maintain a repeatable website inventory that can be searched by analyst, company, asset, mechanism, trial acronym, NCT/CTIS identifier, treatment setting, BCLC stage, Child-Pugh class, biomarker, and geography.

## Trial/regulatory/pipeline sources

Prioritize source triangulation in this order:

1. Trial registry signal: ClinicalTrials.gov, CTIS, WHO ICTRP, jRCT, ChiCTR, company trial pages.
2. Regulator signal: FDA approval notifications, Drugs@FDA, DailyMed, FDA advisory committee materials, EMA EPAR/CHMP, MHRA, PMDA, NMPA, Health Canada, TGA.
3. Guideline/compendium signal: AASLD, EASL, NCCN, ASCO, ESMO, NCCN Drugs & Biologics Compendium.
4. Evidence signal: conference abstract, poster, oral presentation, peer-reviewed paper, editorial, meta-analysis, real-world cohort.
5. Company signal: press release, investor deck, SEC filing, earnings call, medical congress booth/symposium page.
6. Access signal: CMS, payer policy, NICE, CDA-AMC, G-BA/IQWiG, HAS, PBAC, SMC.
7. Secondary signal: trade press, analyst reports, paid databases; verify material facts against primary sources.

## Source-quality notes

- Use official primary sources first: regulators, trial registries, guideline bodies, peer-reviewed journals, cancer registries, HTA agencies, payer policies, and company SEC filings.
- Treat company press releases and investor decks as primary for company claims but not as sufficient evidence for clinical magnitude, regulatory status, or market access; verify against trial registries, regulators, abstracts, labels, and publications.
- Distinguish active, recruiting, suspended, terminated, withdrawn, completed, and unknown-status trials. Trial registry status may lag sponsor reality, so corroborate with sponsor releases and conference abstracts.
- Record date accessed, publication/update date, guideline version, label revision date, trial registry "last update posted," and abstract embargo/release date for each source.
- Do not mix HCC with broader "liver cancer" unless the source explicitly maps histology. SEER and GLOBOCAN often group liver and intrahepatic bile duct cancers; use them for market context and use histology-specific sources when HCC-only estimates are required.
- Track liver-function eligibility carefully. HCC evidence and access language often depends on Child-Pugh class, ALBI grade, ECOG status, portal hypertension, varices, bleeding risk, viral hepatitis status, MASH/MASLD etiology, and transplant eligibility.
- Separate guideline recommendations from reimbursement. NCCN/ASCO/AASLD/EASL/ESMO can change clinical practice before payer policies, while NICE/CDA-AMC/G-BA/HAS/PBAC/SMC may restrict by line, liver function, comparator, or commercial arrangement.
- Confirm regional approvals independently. FDA, EMA, NMPA, PMDA, Health Canada, MHRA, and TGA may differ in indication wording, line of therapy, biomarker/AFP restrictions, combination partners, and withdrawal history.
- Use secondary news sources only for horizon scanning and context. Before using a secondary source in a deliverable, trace every material claim back to the regulator, registry, company filing/release, conference abstract, journal article, payer policy, or HTA document.
- Avoid social media, forums, and unsourced conference-reporting posts for factual CI unless they point to a primary document that can be independently verified.
