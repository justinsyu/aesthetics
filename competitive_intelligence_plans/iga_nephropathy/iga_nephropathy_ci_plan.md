# IgA Nephropathy CI Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for IgA nephropathy (IgAN), including IgA vasculitis nephritis only where guidelines, registries, trials, or stakeholder sources group it with IgAN. It identifies credible websites to search and monitor. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track the IgAN treatment landscape across approved and emerging approaches, including targeted-release budesonide, dual endothelin/angiotensin blockade, selective endothelin A antagonism, complement inhibition, APRIL/BAFF inhibition, anti-APRIL antibodies, anti-CD38 or plasma-cell-directed therapies, RNA/antisense approaches, and other kidney-protective strategies.
- Monitor clinical development activity: new trials, recruitment changes, protocol amendments, endpoint changes, posted results, open-label extensions, discontinuations, readouts, publications, and congress abstracts.
- Identify regulatory signals in the United States, Europe, United Kingdom, Canada, Japan, Australia, China, and other priority markets, including approvals, accelerated/conditional approval commitments, label expansions, safety updates, REMS changes, orphan designations, PDUFA dates, CHMP opinions, and HTA interactions.
- Track evidence-generation trends that may affect positioning: eGFR slope, UPCR/proteinuria, hematuria, Gd-IgA1, complement biomarkers, biopsy requirements, optimized supportive care, SGLT2 inhibitor background therapy, patient-reported outcomes, health-related quality of life, and real-world outcomes.
- Monitor commercial and access signals: payer prior authorization criteria, formulary placement, specialty pharmacy pathways, REMS requirements, manufacturer support programs, HTA decisions, value assessments, pricing files, VA criteria, and launch/revenue commentary.
- Maintain an auditable source workflow that starts with official primary sources, uses society/foundation sources for stakeholder context, and treats company releases, trade press, and social media as leads requiring primary-source validation.

## Source-quality notes

- Tier 1 primary sources: FDA, EMA, MHRA, Health Canada, PMDA, TGA, NMPA, ClinicalTrials.gov, CTIS, WHO ICTRP, company SEC filings, official product labels, REMS documents, HTA agencies, and peer-reviewed journal articles. Use these for factual claims.
- Tier 2 credible context sources: KDIGO, NKF, NIDDK, ASN, ISN, ERA, IgA Nephropathy Foundation, CureGN, NEPTUNE, KPMP, CURE-CKD, PubMed-indexed reviews, and major medical congress materials. Use these for guideline, stakeholder, clinical-practice, and research-context signals.
- Tier 3 secondary leads: company press releases, investor presentations, trade press, specialty news, GoodRx, Evaluate, MMIT, and transcript aggregators. Use these to identify leads, then verify against labels, registries, filings, trial records, HTA documents, or publications.
- Company materials are primary for what the company announced, but not independent evidence of clinical benefit, market share, or payer access. Cross-check clinical claims against labels, peer-reviewed publications, congress posters, and regulatory reviews.
- Payer policies are plan-specific and update frequently. Capture the policy title, policy number, version date, line of business, and URL/PDF filename. Do not generalize one payer’s criteria to the full US market.
- HTA conclusions are jurisdiction-specific. Record the population, comparator, price/commercial arrangement language, uncertainty themes, and date of appraisal.
- FAERS and spontaneous adverse-event reports are signal-generating only. Do not infer incidence, comparative safety, or causality without denominator-aware analyses and clinical review.
- ClinicalTrials.gov and CTIS records can lag actual sponsor decisions. Compare registry changes with company releases, SEC filings, and conference disclosures.
- Conference abstracts can precede peer review and may be superseded by manuscripts. Preserve abstract number, session, presentation date, presenter, sponsor, and whether the data are interim, final, post hoc, or encore.
- Social media and patient forums can reveal access friction and patient language, but should not be used as evidence for safety, efficacy, prevalence, or coverage without corroboration from credible sources.
- For every CI capture, log access date, source type, publication/update date, searched terms, and whether the finding was independently verified.
