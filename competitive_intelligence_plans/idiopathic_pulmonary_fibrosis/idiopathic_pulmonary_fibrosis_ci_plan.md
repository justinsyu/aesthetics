# Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: 2026-05-11

Disease state: Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)

Purpose: Provide a current, credible website search plan for IPF competitive intelligence. This is a source and monitoring plan, not a CI report.

Scope rule: Include only sources and extracted facts that are specific to idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Exclude adjacent pulmonary-fibrosis, progressive-fibrosis, pediatric, and broad interstitial-lung-disease records unless the source reports an IPF-specific subgroup, indication, label, endpoint, or trial record that can be cited separately.

## CI objectives

- Detect clinical practice shifts in IPF diagnosis, treatment sequencing, pulmonary rehabilitation, oxygen use, lung transplant referral, acute exacerbation management, and multidisciplinary discussion practices.
- Track competitive events early, including trial starts, enrollment updates, primary completion changes, topline readouts, conference abstracts, peer-reviewed publications, label approvals, safety updates, and market-access decisions.
- Monitor the approved and late-stage IPF treatment landscape, including nintedanib, pirfenidone, nerandomilast/Jascayd, admilparant/BMS-986278, inhaled treprostinil/Tyvaso, inhaled pirfenidone/AP01, taladegib/ENV-101, LTI-03, MTX-463, and other IPF-directed antifibrotic, anti-inflammatory, vascular, epithelial-repair, and remodeling-targeted approaches.
- Build a repeatable source inventory that separates primary regulatory/trial evidence from society guidance, sponsor disclosures, patient advocacy resources, market-access sources, and secondary news.
- Track epidemiology, registry, patient-burden, and real-world evidence signals from disease-specific foundations and registries without overstating observational data.
- Monitor market access and commercial signals, including U.S. labels, payer coverage, prior authorization criteria, HTA recommendations, launch support programs, SEC filings, earnings commentary, and pricing benchmarks.

## Monitoring cadence

### Daily to twice weekly

- FDA drug approvals, safety labeling changes, Drugs@FDA, FDA labels, and FDA news pages during expected action windows or after major Phase 3 readouts.
- ClinicalTrials.gov for recruiting status, posted results, protocol amendments, site additions, endpoint changes, and primary completion date shifts for high-priority assets.
- Sponsor press releases and SEC 8-K filings for Boehringer Ingelheim, BMS, United Therapeutics, Avalyn, Endeavor, Rein, Tvardi, Pliant, and other active IPF sponsors.
- Major industry news sources when trial readouts, regulatory actions, financing, M&A, or safety events are expected.

### Weekly

- PFF pipeline tracker, PFF news, American Lung Association updates, and disease foundation news; retain only IPF-specific items.
- PubMed saved searches for IPF randomized trials, registries, real-world evidence, safety, biomarkers, and health economics.
- Company investor relations pages, presentations, earnings calendars, and conference participation pages.
- Payer policy portals for the newest approved products or anticipated coverage restrictions.

### Monthly

- EMA EPARs, CHMP agendas/minutes, European Commission register, Health Canada, MHRA, PMDA, and TGA for ex-U.S. regulatory movement.
- HTA sources such as NICE, CDA-AMC/CADTH, G-BA, IQWiG, HAS, SMC, and PBAC.
- NIH RePORTER, foundation grants, registry publications, and investigator networks.
- Product sites and patient support programs for changes in claims, access language, copay assistance, and educational material.

### Conference-driven

- ATS International Conference: monitor abstract titles, late-breaking sessions, press program, posters, simultaneous publications, and sponsor medical-information portals.
- ERS Congress: monitor accepted abstracts, late breakers, clinical-trial sessions, and respiratory journal supplements.
- CHEST Annual Meeting: monitor clinical-practice presentations, regional subgroup analyses, and safety/real-world studies.
- PFF Summit: monitor abstract books, session recordings, sponsor presence, patient advocacy themes, registry updates, and clinical-trial education; retain only IPF-specific items.
- Before each conference: search 6-8 weeks pre-meeting for accepted abstract titles and embargo policies.
- During each conference: search daily for press releases, simultaneous publications, and medical-information posters.
- After each conference: search weekly for 4 weeks for uploaded posters, videos, and full manuscripts.

### Trigger-based

- Increase monitoring to daily when a high-priority trial reaches primary completion, when a sponsor announces topline timing, when FDA/EMA action dates approach, when a safety issue emerges, or when a product launch begins.
- For new approvals, monitor labels, REMS status, NDC listing, product site, payer policies, Medicare Part D formulary placement, specialty pharmacy criteria, and patient support pages for the first 90-180 days.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest confidence: regulatory agency pages, prescribing information, trial registries, official guideline publications, peer-reviewed journals, HTA decisions, and SEC filings.
- Strong but contextual: disease foundations, registry websites, society conference pages, NIH/NHLBI pages, and academic medical publications. Use these for disease burden, unmet need, registry trends, and clinician/patient priorities.
- Sponsor sources are primary for company intent, timelines, pipeline status, and financial disclosures, but they are promotional or investor-oriented. Reconcile sponsor statements with ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA/EMA records, peer-reviewed publications, and SEC filings.
- PFF pipeline pages are useful for IPF surveillance, but they should not replace primary trial-registry or regulatory confirmation.
- Conference abstracts and posters can be early and influential but often have limited peer review, incomplete methods, and interim datasets. Flag whether a dataset is encore, subgroup, interim, post hoc, or full primary endpoint.
- FAERS, social media, forums, and patient anecdotes are signal-generating only. Do not infer incidence, causality, comparative safety, or access prevalence without corroborating evidence.
- Secondary industry news is useful for rapid awareness and deal context, but all clinical/regulatory claims should be traced back to sponsor releases, registries, agency documents, or publications.
- Market-access sources vary by geography and date. Record the exact appraisal date, covered population, stopping criteria, discounts or patient access scheme references, and confirm that the covered population is IPF before including the source in an IPF output.
- Adjacent pulmonary-fibrosis and interstitial-lung-disease populations are not interchangeable with IPF. Exclude them from IPF reports unless IPF-specific results are separately reported and cited.
- For current-state dashboards, capture access date, source URL, last-updated date, trial ID, product name, INN/generic name, sponsor, mechanism, phase, population, endpoint, geography, and evidence type.
