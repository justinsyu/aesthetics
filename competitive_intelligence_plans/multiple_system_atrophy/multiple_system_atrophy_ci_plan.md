# Multiple System Atrophy Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for multiple system atrophy (MSA). It identifies credible websites to search and monitor for clinical, regulatory, pipeline, market access, and commercial intelligence. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track changes in the MSA treatment landscape, including disease-modifying alpha-synuclein approaches, iron-targeting agents, neurogenic orthostatic hypotension (nOH) therapies, stem-cell approaches, symptomatic autonomic care, digital endpoints, biomarkers, imaging, skin/seed-amplification diagnostics, and natural-history infrastructure.
- Detect competitor development signals early: trial starts, amendments, enrollment completion, trial termination, futility/success decisions, long-term extension updates, expanded access, regulatory filings, designations, advisory committee activity, label updates, safety communications, publications, abstracts, investor disclosures, and licensing or financing events.
- Monitor disease-specific infrastructure that can affect trial feasibility and endpoint selection: MDS MSA Study Group, Mission MSA Centers of Excellence, Global MSA Registry/GLOMSAR outputs, MSA Trust research network, NYU/TGen registries, UMSARS-related materials, natural-history datasets, biomarkers, and brain/biospecimen banking.
- Track regulatory and access signals that shape commercial opportunity: FDA/EMA/PMDA/MHRA/Health Canada decisions, orphan/fast-track/SAKIGAKE designations, payer medical/pharmacy policies, Medicare/Medicaid utilization, HTA routing, formulary restrictions, patient support programs, and advocacy positions.
- Maintain an auditable search process that prioritizes official primary sources, peer-reviewed literature, recognized societies/foundations, registries, regulator databases, HTA bodies, and company filings over promotional or unsourced reporting.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest-confidence sources: regulator databases, trial registries, peer-reviewed publications, government disease pages, official society pages, HTA decisions, payer policies, and SEC/company filings.
- Sponsor press releases and investor decks are primary for company claims but promotional by nature. Treat them as leads until verified against registry records, regulatory documents, conference abstracts, filings, or peer-reviewed publications.
- ClinicalTrials.gov, CTIS, EUCTR, and WHO ICTRP records are sponsor/investigator-submitted. Check record history, update dates, recruitment status, outcome definitions, and posted results; do not assume registry text equals regulatory agreement.
- Orphan, Fast Track, PRIME, SAKIGAKE, and similar designations do not establish efficacy or approvability. Record designation date, scope, sponsor, product name, and exact indication wording.
- FAERS and social/community sources can identify potential safety or patient-experience themes but cannot establish incidence, causality, or comparative risk.
- Advocacy sources such as Mission MSA, MSA Trust, MSA United, NORD, Dysautonomia International, and National Ataxia Foundation are strong for patient priorities, research networks, education, and recruitment context; separate advocacy framing from neutral evidence assessment.
- Preprints on medRxiv/bioRxiv should be labeled as not peer reviewed and monitored for later publication, correction, or withdrawal.
- Commercial databases such as Evaluate, Citeline, Cortellis, GlobalData, BioCentury, and DelveInsight are useful for triangulation and market context if licensed, but any high-impact claim should be traced back to primary sources.
- Market-access evidence for MSA may be sparse because there are no broadly established disease-modifying approvals. Search symptomatic nOH and autonomic-dysfunction comparators separately from disease-modifying MSA assets.
- For rare-disease prevalence and market sizing, triangulate across Orphanet, NINDS/MedlinePlus, peer-reviewed epidemiology, company assumptions, and claims data; document geography, case definition, and date.
- For international sources, search both English and local-language terms when relevant, especially Japanese sources for MSA/SAKIGAKE activity: `multiple system atrophy`, `MSA`, and `多系統萎縮症`.
