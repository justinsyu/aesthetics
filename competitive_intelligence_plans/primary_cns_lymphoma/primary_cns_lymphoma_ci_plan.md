# Primary CNS Lymphoma Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: 2026-05-11

Disease state: Primary central nervous system lymphoma (PCNSL), including primary diffuse large B-cell lymphoma of the CNS, primary vitreoretinal lymphoma (PVRL), and relapsed/refractory PCNSL where explicitly included by sources.

Purpose: Provide a current, credible website search plan for PCNSL competitive intelligence. This is a source and monitoring plan, not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track changes in the PCNSL treatment landscape across newly diagnosed, elderly/frail, transplant-eligible, transplant-ineligible, relapsed/refractory, primary vitreoretinal, immunodeficiency-associated, and secondary CNS lymphoma-adjacent populations when sources clearly separate them.
- Detect early competitor signals: new trial starts, protocol amendments, primary-completion changes, enrollment holds, readouts, conference abstracts, peer-reviewed publications, regulatory filings, advisory committee activity, label changes, safety communications, guideline updates, and pathway/compendia changes.
- Monitor mechanisms and modalities with PCNSL relevance, including high-dose methotrexate regimens, rituximab-containing chemotherapy, thiotepa-based consolidation, autologous stem-cell transplant, reduced-dose or whole-brain radiation, BTK inhibitors, IMiDs/CELMoDs, anti-CD19 antibodies, CD20xCD3 bispecifics, PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, PI3K pathway approaches, CAR-T/cellular therapies, NK-cell approaches, intrathecal/intraventricular strategies, and BBB/CNS-penetrant delivery strategies.
- Track evidence by setting and patient segment: objective response, complete response, progression-free survival, overall survival, neurocognitive toxicity, ocular response, CSF clearance, steroid response, MRI response criteria, duration of response, infection risk, hematologic toxicity, transplant feasibility, quality of life, and older-adult tolerability.
- Monitor market access and commercial signals, especially because U.S. PCNSL-specific approved therapy availability may change: NCCN/compendia positioning, VA pathways, payer policy edits, coding, ASP/WAC/NOC pricing, Part D or Part B benefit treatment, prior authorization criteria, and patient support program launch pages.
- Maintain a repeatable website search process that privileges primary sources and treats press releases, investor decks, and media articles as leads requiring confirmation against trial registries, labels, filings, conference abstracts, or peer-reviewed publications.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest confidence: regulator labels/review packages, trial registries, peer-reviewed guideline publications, government epidemiology databases, and official society guideline pages.
- High confidence but time-lagged: peer-reviewed journal articles, registry reports, HTA documents, and published conference supplements. Check study dates and whether data are interim, updated, or final.
- Primary but potentially promotional: company press releases, investor decks, earnings calls, HCP product pages, and sponsor trial pages. Treat as leads and verify against trial registries, abstracts, publications, labels, or filings.
- Useful but secondary: BioPharma Dive, Fierce Biotech, Endpoints, BioSpace, Business Wire, PR Newswire, GlobeNewswire, BioCentury, Evaluate, and GlobalData. These can identify events quickly but should not be the sole evidence base for CI conclusions.
- Payer policies and pathways are decision documents, not clinical evidence. Record the policy effective date, line of therapy, ICD-10 language, diagnosis restrictions, compendia citations, and whether criteria apply to pharmacy benefit or medical benefit.
- Conference abstracts are often preliminary. Record abstract release date, presentation date, trial cutoff date, abstract number, sponsor, NCT ID, endpoint maturity, and whether an updated poster/oral presentation supersedes the abstract.
- Trial registries are sponsor-reported and can lag real operations. Track first posted, last updated, status, enrollment, primary completion, study completion, arms, endpoints, locations, and change history.
- PCNSL terminology is inconsistent. Search all variants: "primary CNS lymphoma", "primary central nervous system lymphoma", "PCNSL", "primary diffuse large B-cell lymphoma of the CNS", "CNS-DLBCL", "primary vitreoretinal lymphoma", "PVRL", "intraocular lymphoma", and "CNS lymphoma".
- Separate primary PCNSL from secondary CNS lymphoma and systemic DLBCL with CNS involvement. Include secondary CNS lymphoma only when relevant to mechanism, CNS penetration, or competitor strategy, and label it separately.
- For safety monitoring, distinguish spontaneous-report signals from incidence. FAERS and social media can generate hypotheses but require label, trial, pharmacovigilance, or literature confirmation.
- For market access, check whether a therapy is oral, infused, intrathecal/intraventricular, cellular, transplant-related, or radiation-related because benefit channel, coding, site-of-care, and prior authorization criteria will differ.
