# Ocular Hypertension / Glaucoma CI Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for ocular hypertension, glaucoma suspect, primary open-angle glaucoma, chronic open-angle glaucoma, angle-closure glaucoma where relevant to competitive positioning, and elevated intraocular pressure (IOP). It identifies credible websites to search and monitor. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track changes in the glaucoma and ocular hypertension treatment landscape, including topical drugs, fixed-dose combinations, preservative-free formulations, sustained-release implants, laser therapy, direct selective laser trabeculoplasty, micro-invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS), trabecular/aqueous outflow devices, neuroprotection, gene/cell approaches, digital monitoring, and diagnostic technologies.
- Monitor clinical development activity across approved and emerging assets, including trial starts, recruitment status, protocol changes, endpoint changes, readouts, terminations, extension studies, investigator-sponsored trials, and real-world evidence.
- Identify regulatory signals in the United States, Europe, United Kingdom, Canada, Japan, Australia, and other priority markets, including approvals, clearances, label changes, PMA/510(k)/De Novo activity, advisory committee materials, safety communications, recalls, and post-market surveillance.
- Track scientific and medical-society activity that can change diagnosis, treatment thresholds, first-line therapy choices, surgical sequencing, progression monitoring, endpoint expectations, and trial feasibility.
- Monitor market access and commercialization signals: Medicare LCD/NCD language, CPT/HCPCS updates, ASP pricing, Part D formulary status, Medicaid utilization, commercial payer prior authorization criteria, VA criteria, HTA decisions, patient support programs, launch metrics, revenue commentary, and sales-force messaging.
- Maintain an auditable source trail that prioritizes official primary sources, peer-reviewed literature, society/congress materials, and clearly labeled secondary sources.

## Trial/regulatory/pipeline sources

### Trial registry workflow

1. Search ClinicalTrials.gov weekly using disease, mechanism, intervention, and competitor queries. Save changed NCT records and compare fields for status, enrollment, sponsor, intervention, primary endpoints, completion dates, locations, and results postings.
2. Cross-check WHO ICTRP monthly to identify non-U.S. records, duplicate/bridged trials, and trials registered only in regional registries.
3. Search CTIS and the legacy EU Clinical Trials Register monthly for EU trial starts, member-state participation, public reports, and transitioned EudraCT records.
4. Search ISRCTN monthly for UK service-delivery, laser-first, pathway, device, and investigator-sponsored glaucoma studies.
5. Around AGS, WGC, EGS, ARVO, AAO, ASCRS, and ESCRS, search the relevant abstract portals before and after abstract release for trial names, NCT numbers, company names, and product names.
6. Reconcile company press releases against registry records and regulator pages before treating a trial status or endpoint claim as confirmed.

### Regulatory workflow

- FDA drugs: monitor Drugs@FDA, FDA labels, Orange Book, Drug Trials Snapshots, FDA drug safety communications, FAERS dashboard, and FAERS quarterly files.
- FDA devices: monitor PMA, 510(k), Product Classification, MAUDE, medical device recalls, CDRH safety communications, and openFDA device APIs.
- EMA/EU: monitor EMA medicines search, EPARs, CHMP agendas/minutes where relevant, CTIS, and European Commission Union Register decisions.
- UK: monitor MHRA product information, GOV.UK MHRA announcements, NICE guideline/TA pages, and RCOphth guidance.
- Canada: monitor Health Canada Drug Product Database and Canada Drug Agency reimbursement reviews.
- Japan: monitor PMDA review reports, MHLW-related notices, Santen and other Japan-based company disclosures, and Japan trial registry records.
- Australia: monitor TGA ARTG, PBAC agenda/outcomes, and ANZCTR.
- For devices and procedures, always separate regulatory authorization from coverage/reimbursement status; a cleared/approved device may still face LCD, coding, or payer restrictions.

### Pipeline and competitor disclosure workflow

- Use ClinicalTrials.gov/ICTRP as the discovery base for active assets and procedures, then confirm with company websites, investor decks, SEC filings, and regulatory databases.
- Track mechanisms and categories separately: prostaglandin analogs, prostamides, nitric-oxide donating prostaglandins, ROCK inhibitors, EP2 agonists, beta blockers, alpha agonists, carbonic anhydrase inhibitors, fixed-dose combinations, preservative-free products, sustained-release implants, punctal/intracameral/intravitreal delivery, SLT/DSLT, MIGS, trabecular bypass, subconjunctival drainage, canaloplasty/trabeculotomy, neuroprotection, mitochondrial/metabolic approaches, gene therapy, cell therapy, and diagnostics/monitoring.
- Use company IR and SEC filings as primary sources for commercial metrics, timelines, milestone payments, risk factors, inventory, launch strategy, payer commentary, and product concentration.
- Use press-release wires and trade press as discovery feeds only; verify material claims against registries, labels, filings, regulator pages, or conference abstracts.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest confidence sources for factual status are regulator databases, official labels, clinical-trial registries, HTA bodies, CMS/VA sources, peer-reviewed journals, and company SEC filings.
- Society meeting abstracts and congress presentations are high-value early signals, but they may be preliminary, non-peer-reviewed, selectively reported, or later revised in full publication.
- Company press releases, investor decks, and earnings calls are primary for company intent, timelines, commercial claims, and disclosed metrics, but they are not independent evidence of clinical value.
- Trade press, subscription databases, price aggregators, and analyst reports are discovery and triangulation tools. Treat them as secondary unless they link to primary documents.
- FAERS and MAUDE are signal-detection sources, not incidence databases. Always document limitations: under-reporting, duplicate reports, stimulated reporting, missing denominator, confounding by disease severity, and incomplete causality assessment.
- FDA approval/clearance and payer coverage are separate questions. For glaucoma devices and procedures, confirm regulatory status, coding, Medicare LCD language, billing articles, commercial policy criteria, and actual plan-level restrictions independently.
- CMS Part D and Medicaid data are useful for public utilization and spending, but they do not disclose manufacturer rebates, commercial net price, free goods, patient assistance, or full payer mix.
- Consumer cash-price sources such as GoodRx reflect coupon/cash-pay context, not payer coverage or net revenue.
- EyeWiki, patient foundations, and patient-facing education sites are useful for terminology, patient journey, and awareness themes, but do not substitute for guidelines, labels, registries, or peer-reviewed evidence.
- For all monitored items, capture retrieval date, URL, source type, publication/update date, affected product/procedure, geography, and whether the source is primary, peer-reviewed, society/congress, payer/HTA, company, or secondary.
