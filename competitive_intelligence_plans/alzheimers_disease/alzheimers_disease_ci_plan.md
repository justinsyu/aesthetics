# Alzheimer's Disease Competitive Intelligence Source Plan

Created: 2026-05-11

This is a source and monitoring plan for Alzheimer's disease competitive intelligence. It is intentionally focused on websites to search, not on conclusions about the market, competitors, or clinical landscape.

## CI objectives

- Track changes in the Alzheimer's disease treatment landscape, including anti-amyloid antibodies, symptomatic therapies, emerging mechanisms, diagnostics, biomarkers, digital tools, and combination approaches.
- Identify competitor clinical, regulatory, safety, labeling, access, pricing, reimbursement, real-world evidence, and commercialization signals early.
- Monitor scientific and medical-society activity that can affect standard of care, diagnosis pathways, patient eligibility, infusion/MRI capacity, biomarker use, and clinical-trial feasibility.
- Maintain an auditable source trail using official primary sources first, then peer-reviewed and recognized secondary sources for synthesis.

## Trial, regulatory, and pipeline sources

### Trial registry workflow

1. Search ClinicalTrials.gov weekly for condition terms and mechanism/class terms; export or save URLs for changed records.
2. Cross-check WHO ICTRP monthly to identify non-U.S. records, duplicate registration IDs, and trials outside ClinicalTrials.gov.
3. Search CTIS monthly for EU trial status, documents, and sponsor updates.
4. Review ACTC, DIAN, ADNI, TrialMatch, and major academic center study pages monthly for recruitment and network signals that may not yet be obvious from registry changes.

## Source-quality notes

- Treat official regulatory, trial registry, government, and company filings as primary sources. Use them to verify all actionable CI claims.
- Treat peer-reviewed journals and systematic reviews as high-quality evidence sources, but check whether data are interim, subgroup, exploratory, or peer-reviewed final results.
- Treat society and foundation pages as credible for policy, education, conference, registry, and advocacy signals; distinguish advocacy interpretation from regulatory fact.
- Treat Alzforum as a strong disease-specific secondary source and discovery tool; confirm asset status in trial registries, publications, or sponsor disclosures.
- Treat medical news sites as signal generators only. Confirm trial, regulatory, financial, and commercial claims against registries, filings, labels, or company releases.
- Be cautious with product websites and HCP pages: they are useful for approved indication, safety language, and positioning, but they are promotional sources.
- Avoid using social media, forums, or unsourced investment commentary as evidence. Use them only to identify rumors or patient-access issues that require confirmation elsewhere.
- Preserve source dates, access dates, URLs, and document versions for labels, EPARs, HTA documents, CMS pages, and conference abstracts because Alzheimer's treatment access and monitoring requirements can change quickly.
