Alzheimer's disease CI report | May 11, 2026

Alzheimer's disease competition is shifting from amyloid efficacy to implementation capacity

Disease-modifying treatment is available, but uptake is constrained by diagnosis, ApoE risk stratification, MRI and infusion logistics, registry coverage, and health technology assessment. Emerging differentiation includes safety, administration, blood biomarkers, neuropsychiatric indications, and non-amyloid mechanisms.

01 / 09
Market structure

Four evidence domains define Alzheimer's disease competition

The commercial landscape is not defined by a single product class. It is shaped by treatment, diagnostics, safety monitoring, and payer evidence requirements.

Disease-modifying

Anti-amyloid launch dynamics

Leqembi and Kisunla are used in early symptomatic patients with confirmed amyloid pathology. Differentiation is increasingly related to monitoring burden, dosing schedule, geography, and real-world persistence.

Symptoms

Behavior and cognition

Agitation now has two labeled options: Rexulti and Auvelity. Psychosis remains an area of unmet medical need, with Acadia's remlifanserin in Phase 2 and a mid-2026 readout expected.

Diagnostics

Eligibility infrastructure

Amyloid confirmation, ApoE genotyping, MRI monitoring, and emerging plasma pTau217 testing determine how many patients can enter and stay in treatment.

Emerging

Mechanism breadth

The 2026 pipeline is more diversified across inflammation, tau, metabolism, synaptic biology, and neuropsychiatric symptoms, with 2026 readouts dense in Phase 2 and Phase 3.

Analytic implication

Competitive assessment should include patient identification, confirmatory testing, registry submission, MRI scheduling, site economics, product persistence, and safety management, in addition to clinical efficacy endpoints.

03 / 09
Access and reimbursement

Coverage is available, but access barriers persist

U.S. access is materially better than early Aduhelm-era uncertainty, while Europe and the UK show that regulatory authorization does not guarantee funded routine use.

United States: coverage with evidence requirements

  • Medicare Part B may cover FDA-approved anti-amyloid monoclonal antibodies for eligible patients.
  • Providers must collect evidence through a qualifying study or registry, including CMS-approved registry pathways.8
  • Patients typically owe 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after the Part B deductible, and related scans or tests can add cost exposure.9

Europe/UK: value and infrastructure are still contested

  • EMA authorized Leqembi and Kisunla in narrower ApoE-defined populations with controlled access and ARIA risk minimization measures.36
  • NICE concluded lecanemab and donanemab benefits were too small to justify additional NHS costs; donanemab draft guidance stayed negative in April 2026.1011
  • Economic objections center on drug acquisition, hospital infusion, intensive monitoring, and uncertain long-term effectiveness.

Commercial signal

Commercial uptake is measurable, but penetration remains early

The two anti-amyloid launches are beginning to show revenue scale, while the broader market remains capacity-limited and dependent on specialty-center operations.

Q1 2026 selected product sales

Leqembi
$168M
Kisunla
$124M

Biogen reported Leqembi global in-market sales of $168M in Q1 2026, up 74% year over year, including U.S. in-market sales of $86M.12 Lilly reported Q1 2026 Kisunla revenue of $124M versus $22M in Q1 2025.13

Interpretation

  • Both products are establishing early commercial baselines.
  • Growth depends on expanding eligible diagnosis rather than mass-market dementia treatment.
  • Services or products that reduce MRI, infusion, ApoE, amyloid-confirmation, or registry burden may affect adoption.
  • Subcutaneous or lower-frequency regimens may affect site economics if clinical confidence is maintained.

Monitoring priority

Track treated-patient counts, persistence, time-to-start, MRI capacity, and payer documentation friction rather than relying on revenue alone.

Mechanism mix is moving

  • Inflammation/immune dysfunction rose to about 20% of the pipeline, up from 6% a decade earlier.

Neuropsychiatric and care burden

Neuropsychiatric symptoms represent a distinct therapeutic domain

Behavioral symptoms affect caregiver burden, institutionalization risk, and care costs. Recent approvals create a separate development and commercialization domain alongside disease-modifying therapy.

Approved

Rexulti

Brexpiprazole is FDA-approved for agitation associated with dementia due to Alzheimer's disease, but carries antipsychotic class concerns including increased mortality risk in older adults with dementia-related psychosis.17

New in 2026

Auvelity

FDA approved dextromethorphan/bupropion as the first non-antipsychotic treatment for agitation associated with dementia due to Alzheimer's disease.18

Pipeline

ACP-204

Acadia is developing a selective 5HT2A inverse agonist for Alzheimer's disease psychosis; the program is recruiting and may read out in 2026.16

Competitive relevance

  • Anti-amyloid therapies only address a subset of early-stage biology; behavioral symptoms cut across later stages and caregiver burden.
  • A non-antipsychotic agitation label creates a differentiated treatment option relative to atypical antipsychotics.
  • Psychosis remains an indication with high unmet medical need and a near-term Acadia clinical catalyst.

Diagnostics and real-world evidence

Treatment pathways depend on earlier patient identification and scalable safety evidence

Blood biomarkers can unlock throughput

  • Roche reported Elecsys pTau217 data supporting amyloid rule-in/rule-out performance comparable to PET/CSF in AAIC 2025 communications.14
  • Blood-based prescreening in trials can expand community reach before expensive confirmatory imaging.
  • Faster triage may improve site conversion, lower screen-failure cost, and increase treated-patient flow.

Registries are becoming market infrastructure

  • CMS lists multiple approved CED studies and registries, including ALZ-NET and the CMS National Patient Registry pathway.8
  • ALZ-NET collects real-world clinical, safety, and imaging data for patients receiving new FDA-approved Alzheimer's therapies.2
  • Registry operations create administrative burden, but can also support evidence generation on long-term outcomes and safety.
Primary care
