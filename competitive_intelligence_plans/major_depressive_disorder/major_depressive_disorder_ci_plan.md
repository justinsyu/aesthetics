# Major Depressive Disorder Competitive Intelligence Source Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: Websites and search tactics for monitoring competitive intelligence in major depressive disorder (MDD), including treatment-resistant depression (TRD), depressive symptoms with acute suicidal ideation/behavior where relevant, and adjacent mood-disorder signals only when they affect MDD positioning. This is a source plan, not a product or competitor report.

## CI objectives

1. Track clinical development activity: new interventional trials, protocol amendments, enrollment status changes, endpoints, inclusion/exclusion criteria, phase transitions, readouts, and trial-result postings.
2. Track regulatory movement: approvals, label changes, REMS changes, advisory committee activity, complete response letters when publicly disclosed, postmarketing requirements, and safety communications.
3. Track scientific differentiation: emerging mechanisms, biomarkers, clinical outcome assessments, rapid-acting antidepressants, psychedelics/psychoplastogens, neuromodulation, digital/AI-enabled interventions, relapse prevention, anhedonia, cognition, suicidality, and TRD subpopulations.
4. Track market access and reimbursement: HTA decisions, formulary restrictions, step therapy, protected-class policy changes, Medicare/Medicaid spending, coding/payment changes for procedures such as TMS or ECT, and payer policy changes for branded products.
5. Track stakeholder voice: guideline updates, society congress abstracts, patient/foundation priorities, registry outputs, and advocacy signals that may shape unmet-need framing or evidence generation.

## Trial/regulatory/pipeline sources

### Trial monitoring

- ClinicalTrials.gov (https://clinicaltrials.gov/) is the first-pass source for interventional MDD/TRD studies. Capture NCT ID, sponsor, collaborators, phase, arms, mechanism, primary endpoint, key secondary endpoints, estimated primary completion, sites, enrollment target, eligibility criteria, and result-posting date.
- CTIS (https://www.euclinicaltrials.eu/search-for-clinical-trials/) is required for EU/EEA trial authorization monitoring after CTIS transition. Search both MDD and broader depression terms.
- WHO ICTRP (https://trialsearch.who.int/) is the cross-registry backstop for non-U.S./non-EU studies.
- NIH RePORTER (https://reporter.nih.gov/) is useful for early academic mechanisms, biomarkers, digital interventions, neuromodulation, and grant-funded trials before they become sponsor-facing assets.

### Regulatory monitoring

- FDA hub (https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-approval-process-drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases): check Drugs@FDA, labels, Drug Trials Snapshots, REMS, FAERS, Orange Book, postmarketing requirements, and weekly approvals.
- FDA advisory committee calendar (https://www.fda.gov/advisory-committees/advisory-committee-calendar): monitor Psychopharmacologic Drugs Advisory Committee, Peripheral and Central Nervous System Drugs Advisory Committee if device/neurostimulation-adjacent, and cross-cutting safety meetings.
- EMA medicines search (https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines): track EPARs, CHMP opinions, withdrawals, refusals, safety referrals, and EU labels.
- Health Canada DPD (https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/drug-product-database.html) and PMDA (https://www.pmda.go.jp/search_index.html): monitor non-U.S. regulatory sequencing and label differences.

### Pipeline monitoring

- Primary company sources: investor-relations pages, press releases, SEC EDGAR (https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/), quarterly decks, R&D days, and conference posters.
- Secondary databases: BioPharmCatalyst (https://www.biopharmcatalyst.com/), Citeline Trialtrove/Pharmaprojects (https://www.citeline.com/en/products-services/clinical/trialtrove), Cortellis (https://clarivate.com/products/cortellis/), Evaluate (https://www.evaluate.com/), GlobalData Healthcare (https://www.globaldata.com/industries-we-cover/healthcare/).
- Validation rule: do not treat secondary pipeline records as confirmed unless traceable to a registry, regulator, peer-reviewed abstract/publication, company filing, or company press release.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest-confidence sources for factual status: regulators, trial registries, company SEC filings, official product labels, peer-reviewed publications, and official HTA decisions.
- Company press releases are primary for sponsor intent and timelines but selective by design; confirm clinical claims against registry results, publications, posters, or regulatory documents.
- Trial registries can lag actual enrollment and protocol changes. Record the date accessed and compare with company disclosures and publication details.
- Conference abstracts often precede full peer review and may be embargoed or incomplete. Treat them as directional until a poster, slide deck, manuscript, or regulatory submission is available.
- FAERS and social/patient sources are signal-generating only. Do not infer incidence, causality, or comparative safety from spontaneous reports or patient forums.
- Paid databases are useful for completeness and tagging, but their fields can be stale or inferred. Triangulate important claims against primary sources.
- HTA decisions are jurisdiction-specific. NICE, CDA-AMC/CADTH, HAS, SMC, PBAC, and ICER use different comparators, cost assumptions, and willingness-to-pay frameworks.
- Patient advocacy and foundation sources are valuable for unmet need, patient language, and access barriers, but they may not be neutral on policy priorities or industry partnerships.
- For MDD, separate unipolar MDD from bipolar depression, postpartum depression, seasonal affective disorder, and nonspecific "depression" unless the source explicitly links the signal to the target population.
- For TRD, capture each source's operational definition of treatment resistance because the number/type of prior inadequate antidepressant trials can materially change market sizing and evidence interpretation.
