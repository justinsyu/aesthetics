# Chronic Graft-versus-Host Disease CI Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for chronic graft-versus-host disease (chronic GVHD, cGVHD). It identifies credible websites to search and monitor. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track changes in the chronic GVHD treatment landscape, especially FDA/EMA label changes, new approvals, supplemental indications, pediatric expansions, safety updates, and postmarketing commitments.
- Monitor clinical development activity across approved and emerging mechanisms, including BTK inhibition, JAK inhibition, ROCK2 inhibition, CSF-1R blockade, extracorporeal photopheresis, low-dose IL-2/Treg approaches, antifibrotic strategies, and combination/frontline trials.
- Identify competitor evidence generation: registrational trials, investigator-sponsored trials, real-world evidence, patient-reported outcomes, organ-specific studies, abstracts, posters, manuscripts, and registry analyses.
- Watch guideline and consensus changes that affect sequencing, line of therapy, organ-specific management, response criteria, and trial endpoint expectations.
- Track market access signals: HTA decisions, formulary and prior authorization language, Medicare/Medicaid utilization and spending, ASP/HCPCS updates for infused therapies, patient assistance, and commercial positioning.
- Build a repeatable search process that separates primary-source evidence from promotional or secondary reporting.

## Source-quality notes

- Treat FDA, EMA, MHRA, Health Canada, PMDA, DailyMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTIS, BMT CTN, CIBMTR, NICE, CADTH/CDA-AMC, and CMS as highest-quality primary sources for regulatory, trial, registry, access, and public payment data.
- Treat peer-reviewed journals and major society congress abstracts as high quality for scientific evidence, but verify whether data are preliminary, encore, interim, post hoc, or sponsor-funded.
- Treat company press releases, investor decks, earnings calls, and HCP product websites as primary for company claims and commercial intent, but not as neutral evidence. Cross-check clinical and regulatory claims against registries, labels, and publications.
- Treat payer policies as primary for that payer's access rules, but not generalizable to the whole market. Capture policy date, line of business, geography, drug benefit vs medical benefit, and whether the policy is PA, step therapy, medical necessity, or coding guidance.
- Treat secondary media as alerting only. Do not cite secondary media for approval status, trial design, data, pricing, or access unless primary sources are unavailable and the limitation is explicit.
- Use both spellings: `graft-versus-host` and `graft versus host`; use both `GVHD` and `GvHD`; include `cGVHD` and `chronic GVHD`.
- Separate chronic GVHD from acute GVHD in every search. Many sources combine GVHD indications and may mention acute GVHD products, including remestemcel-L, etanercept, infliximab, and other acute-focused interventions.
- For clinical trials, record NCT/EudraCT/CTIS IDs, sponsor, collaborator, start date, primary completion date, status, phase, intervention arms, population, prior lines, organ-specific enrollment, endpoints, and update date.
- For labels and regulatory pages, archive the label version/date because product sites may update without preserving old language.
- For conference abstracts, capture abstract number, presentation type, session date, whether the abstract is late-breaking, and whether a subsequent manuscript supersedes it.
- For market access, distinguish list price, net price, ASP, WAC, FSS, 340B, patient out-of-pocket, and payer policy. Public sources often measure different price concepts.
- For rare-disease utilization data, expect small numbers, suppression, lag, and unstable trends. Triangulate with company revenue commentary and registry/transplant-volume data rather than relying on one source.
