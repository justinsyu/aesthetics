# MPS IIIA / Sanfilippo Syndrome Type A CI Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for mucopolysaccharidosis type IIIA (MPS IIIA), also known as Sanfilippo syndrome type A. It identifies credible websites to search and monitor for clinical, regulatory, pipeline, market access, and commercial intelligence. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track therapeutic development in MPS IIIA across AAV gene therapy, ex vivo HSC gene therapy, blood-brain-barrier-penetrant enzyme replacement therapy, intracerebroventricular/intrathecal enzyme replacement, anti-inflammatory/supportive approaches, newborn screening, biomarker, and natural-history programs.
- Detect material competitor and regulatory signals: new trials, protocol amendments, status changes, enrollment updates, clinical holds, complete response letters, BLA/MAA/NDA filings, PDUFA or CHMP timing, orphan/RMAT/fast-track/rare pediatric disease/PRIME/START designations, expanded-access policies, long-term follow-up, and manufacturing updates.
- Monitor endpoints and evidence standards for neuronopathic MPS: CSF/plasma/urine heparan sulfate, neurocognitive scales, adaptive behavior, communication, motor function, neuroimaging, survival, caregiver burden, patient preference, and natural-history/external-control methodology.
- Map patient, physician, and site infrastructure: Sanfilippo and MPS foundations, registries, trial-readiness efforts, expert centers, newborn-screening programs, metabolic/genetics societies, and lysosomal storage disease conferences.
- Prepare for access and launch intelligence if a first disease-modifying therapy is approved: coding, site-of-care requirements, gene-therapy center qualification, prior authorization, Medicaid policy, commercial payer criteria, HTA reviews, manufacturer disease-awareness activity, and patient-support infrastructure.
- Maintain a source hierarchy that treats regulator, registry, company filing, peer-reviewed, and conference sources as primary or near-primary sources; use advocacy, news, and paid databases as leads that require validation.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest-confidence sources: regulator pages, trial registries, company SEC/official filings, official company press releases, peer-reviewed manuscripts, posted conference abstracts, and HTA/payer policy documents.
- Disease foundations and registries are high-value for trial awareness, patient priorities, recruitment feasibility, and burden context, but trial claims should be validated against registry, sponsor, regulator, or publication sources.
- Sponsor disease-awareness pages are useful for commercialization and diagnostic-funnel signals, but should not be treated as neutral evidence for product benefit.
- ClinicalTrials.gov and other registries can lag sponsor decisions; always compare registry status with company disclosures and regulator communications.
- Posted clinical-trial results and protocols are stronger than press releases for endpoint definitions, analysis populations, and safety tables.
- FDA and EMA designation pages confirm regulatory incentives, not efficacy or approval; record the exact designation wording and date.
- FAERS and spontaneous adverse-event databases are hypothesis-generating only; do not infer incidence or causality without denominator and adjudicated evidence.
- Conference abstracts can precede peer review and may omit detail; archive the abstract/poster and later reconcile with manuscripts, registry results, and regulator reviews.
- Secondary news, Sanfilippo News, CGTLive, and paid databases are useful for lead generation and timeline context, but key facts should be verified with primary documents.
- Historical discontinued or transferred assets, including rhHNS, LYS-SAF302, SOBI003, and ABO-102 transfer history, should remain in the CI archive because they inform endpoint, delivery, safety, and regulatory precedent.
- For global sources, preserve original URLs and translation notes; regulatory terms such as orphan designation, clinical-trial approval, reimbursement listing, and conditional approval may not be directly comparable across regions.
