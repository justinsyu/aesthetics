# Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS, motor neuron disease/MND). It identifies credible websites to search and monitor. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track changes in the ALS treatment landscape, including approved therapies, discontinued or withdrawn products, genetically targeted therapies, antisense/RNA approaches, gene therapy, neuroinflammation, stress-response biology, mitochondrial/metabolic approaches, cell therapy, biomarkers, digital endpoints, and platform-trial activity.
- Detect competitor development signals early: trial starts, amendments, enrollment completion, futility/success decisions, expanded access, regulatory filings, advisory committee materials, label updates, safety communications, publications, abstracts, and investor disclosures.
- Monitor disease-specific research infrastructure that can affect trial feasibility, endpoint selection, biomarker strategy, natural history assumptions, patient segmentation, and recruitment.
- Track regulatory and access signals that shape commercial opportunity: FDA/EMA/Health Canada/HTA decisions, orphan designations, accelerated approval commitments, payer policies, formulary restrictions, Medicare/Medicaid utilization, patient assistance, and advocacy positions.
- Maintain a repeatable, auditable search process that prioritizes official primary sources, peer-reviewed literature, credible societies/foundations, and recognized HTA/payer sources over promotional or unsourced reporting.

## Trial/regulatory/pipeline sources

### Trial registry workflow

1. Search ClinicalTrials.gov weekly using disease, drug, mechanism, sponsor, and genetic subtype queries. Save changed records and compare `Last Update Posted`, status, enrollment, primary completion date, arms, endpoints, and results.
2. Cross-check WHO ICTRP monthly for non-U.S. and regional registry coverage, especially Japan, China, Australia/New Zealand, ISRCTN, EU, and investigator-led studies.
3. Search CTIS and the EU Clinical Trials Register monthly for EU trial starts, transitioned trials, sponsor changes, and public result postings.
4. Review NEALS, HEALEY, ALS Association, ALS TDI Trial Navigator, and CDC National ALS Registry research notifications monthly to identify recruitment visibility and patient-facing messaging changes.
5. At conference abstract release windows, search trial IDs, regimen names, sponsors, and mechanisms across conference portals and company newsrooms before and after embargo dates.

### Regulatory workflow

- FDA: monitor FDA ALS pages, Drugs@FDA, FDA labels, advisory committee calendars, FAERS dashboard, orphan designations, and ACT for ALS updates.
- EMA: monitor EMA medicines search, Qalsody/Rilutek EPAR pages, CHMP agendas/minutes, orphan designations, and EU Union Register decisions.
- Canada: monitor Health Canada Drug Product Database and Canada Drug Agency reimbursement reviews.
- UK/EU HTA: monitor NICE, HAS, and G-BA for appraisals and benefit assessments.
- Japan/Australia: monitor PMDA and PBAC for review, label, and reimbursement signals.
- Product-specific bookmarks:
  - FDA Qalsody approval: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-treatment-amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-associated-mutation-sod1-gene
  - EMA Qalsody EPAR: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/qalsody
  - EMA Rilutek EPAR: https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/rilutek
  - FDA Drug Approvals and Databases: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/development-approval-process-drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases
  - DailyMed: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/

### Pipeline and competitor disclosure workflow

- Use company pipeline pages and investor news as first-pass sources for asset status, but confirm trial status in ClinicalTrials.gov/CTIS/ICTRP and material developments in SEC filings.
- Monitor both ALS-specific companies and broader neurology companies; ALS programs may appear under neurology, rare disease, genetic medicine, RNA therapeutics, neuroinflammation, or platform-trial language.
- Track trial status by both asset name and legacy code because ALS assets often change names after licensing or sponsor transfer.
- Maintain a watchlist for currently or historically relevant ALS terms:
  - Approved or commercial: `riluzole`, `Rilutek`, `Tiglutik`, `Exservan`, `edaravone`, `Radicava`, `Radicava ORS`, `tofersen`, `Qalsody`
  - Historical/withdrawn but commercially relevant: `AMX0035`, `Relyvrio`, `Albrioza`, `sodium phenylbutyrate taurursodiol`
  - Pipeline/watchlist: `DNL343`, `SAR443820`, `VHB937`, `AMX0114`, `PrimeC`, `NurOwn`, `QRL-201`, `QRL-101`, `BIIB`, `IONIS`, `C9orf72`, `SOD1`, `FUS`, `TARDBP`, `ATXN2`
  - Platform-trial regimen terms: `HEALEY ALS Platform Trial`, `Regimen`, `ABBV-CLS-7262`, `CNM-Au8`, `trehalose`, `zilucoplan`, `verdiperstat`, `DNL343`

## Market access/commercial sources

### Access and reimbursement monitoring workflow

1. Search manufacturer product/access pages monthly for patient support, copay, hub services, bridge programs, dosing logistics, and access-positioning changes.

## Source-quality notes

- Treat regulators, trial registries, labels, HTA agencies, peer-reviewed journals, and SEC/company filings as highest-confidence sources for factual status, dates, endpoints, and official claims.
- Treat company press releases, product sites, and investor decks as primary for company statements but potentially selective. Confirm trial details in registries and regulatory databases.
- Treat advocacy sources such as ALS Association, MND Association, ALS TDI, Target ALS, Answer ALS, and International Alliance as credible for patient/community, research-infrastructure, and policy signals, while noting advocacy perspectives and funding roles.
- Treat FAERS, social media, patient forums, and anecdotal access reports as signal-generating only; do not infer incidence, causality, market share, or coverage rates without stronger evidence.
- Treat medRxiv/bioRxiv as early-warning sources. Flag preprints as not peer reviewed and re-check for journal publication, retraction, major revision, and sponsor disclosures.
- For ALS, always distinguish sporadic ALS, familial/genetic ALS, SOD1-ALS, C9orf72 ALS/FTD, bulbar-onset disease, limb-onset disease, early/rapid progressors, respiratory-status eligibility, and disease-duration restrictions when interpreting trial and access sources.
- For approved products, verify current market status before reuse. Some ALS therapies and formulations have changed commercial availability over time; older approval pages can remain live after discontinuation, withdrawal, or access-program changes.
- For market access, payer policy PDFs may lag labels and may vary by plan, line of business, state, and site of care. Capture effective date, revision date, plan type, and geography.
- For conference intelligence, confirm whether abstracts are preliminary, embargoed, encore, sponsor-authored, subgroup/post hoc, or final peer-reviewed data.
- For AI/search summaries and secondary media, use them only for lead generation. Re-cite the underlying FDA, trial registry, company filing, HTA, or journal source in the CI tracker.
