# Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Current as of May 11, 2026. Scope: credible websites and search tactics for gastrointestinal stromal tumor (GIST) competitive intelligence. This is a source and monitoring plan, not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track changes in GIST standards of care across localized resectable disease, neoadjuvant/adjuvant therapy, unresectable/metastatic disease, mutation-defined treatment, drug resistance, and later-line sequencing.
- Monitor competitive events for approved and investigational GIST therapies, including trial starts, enrollment changes, primary completion dates, abstract releases, full readouts, regulatory submissions, label changes, and safety updates.
- Track mutation-specific development and positioning for KIT exon 9, 11, 13, 14, 17, and 18; PDGFRA exon 18 and D842V; SDH-deficient/wild-type GIST; NF1-associated GIST; BRAF/RAS-altered GIST; NTRK fusions; pediatric and syndromic GIST.
- Monitor access and commercial signals, including guideline inclusion, NCCN compendium status, payer prior authorization language, HTA recommendations, coding, pricing, distribution, and rare-disease patient-support programs.
- Maintain a repeatable web inventory that can be searched by disease term, asset, sponsor, biomarker, line of therapy, trial ID, conference, geography, and access decision.

## Source-quality notes

- Treat regulators, trial registries, official labels, NCCN/ASCO/ESMO/NCI, peer-reviewed journals, and HTA bodies as primary or high-authority sources.
- Treat company press releases, investor decks, SEC filings, and earnings call transcripts as primary for company intent and disclosures, but verify clinical and regulatory facts against trial registries, labels, regulators, abstracts, or publications.
- Treat patient advocacy organizations as valuable for patient experience, access friction, registry activity, mutational testing barriers, and community education; corroborate clinical claims against guidelines, labels, and peer-reviewed evidence.
- Treat conference abstracts as time-sensitive evidence signals. Confirm whether they are preliminary, encore, updated, late-breaking, or final; look for subsequent peer-reviewed publication and trial-registry consistency.
- Treat paid databases and trade press as useful for early signal detection, market-sizing hypotheses, deals, and analyst framing, but do not rely on them as sole support for clinical, regulatory, or access claims.
- For GIST specifically, avoid pooling all sarcoma sources without filtering. GIST has distinct biology, approved TKIs, resistance patterns, and mutation-dependent treatment decisions; tag sources by GIST-specific relevance.
- Track terminology variants: "gastrointestinal stromal tumor" and "gastrointestinal stromal tumour"; "GIST"; "GI stromal tumor"; brand/generic names; old development codes; U.S./EU spelling.
- Capture date accessed and page update date for every source because rare oncology pages, meeting abstracts, and payer policies change quickly.
- For mutation-specific claims, record the specimen type and testing method when available: tissue NGS, ctDNA, PCR, IHC, SDHB IHC, KIT/PDGFRA panel, or comprehensive genomic profiling.
- For market access claims, distinguish label indication, guideline recommendation, compendium listing, payer medical policy, pharmacy benefit policy, HTA recommendation, and real-world formulary placement.
