# Ovarian Cancer Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: 2026-05-11

Disease state: Ovarian cancer, including epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, primary peritoneal, high-grade serous, low-grade serous, clear cell, endometrioid, mucinous, germ cell, and sex-cord stromal tumors.

Purpose: Provide a current, credible website search plan for ovarian cancer competitive intelligence. This is a source and monitoring plan, not a clinical, commercial, or market-share report.

## CI objectives

- Track treatment-standard changes across newly diagnosed, maintenance, platinum-sensitive recurrent, platinum-resistant recurrent, low-grade serous ovarian cancer, rare histologies, biomarker-defined populations, and post-PARP/post-bevacizumab settings.
- Detect competitor events early, including new trial starts, protocol amendments, enrollment changes, primary-completion date changes, topline readouts, conference abstracts, peer-reviewed publications, regulatory submissions, approvals, label expansions, withdrawals, safety communications, and guideline updates.
- Monitor asset and mechanism movement, including PARP inhibitors, VEGF/VEGFR agents, FR-alpha/FOLR1 antibody-drug conjugates, NaPi2b/SLC34A2 ADCs, CDH6 ADCs, HER2 ADCs, WEE1 inhibitors, ATR/CHK1/DNA-damage response agents, MEK/RAF/FAK combinations in low-grade serous disease, immuno-oncology combinations, cell therapies, vaccines, radiopharmaceuticals, and endocrine strategies.
- Track biomarker and diagnostic shifts, including BRCA1/2, HRD, homologous recombination proficiency/resistance, FR-alpha expression, NaPi2b, HER2, MMR/MSI, TMB, KRAS/RAS/MAPK alterations, TP53, CCNE1 amplification, folate receptor assays, companion diagnostics, and molecular testing coverage.
- Follow evidence quality by source type: pivotal trial records, FDA/EMA review documents, guideline panels, peer-reviewed journals, congress abstracts, registry/real-world evidence, payer policies, HTA recommendations, and company disclosures.
- Monitor market access and commercial signals, including NCCN compendia status, payer prior authorization criteria, diagnostic testing coverage, coding, ASP/Part B pricing, Part D formulary treatment for oral therapies, HTA outcomes, patient assistance messaging, and launch materials.
- Build a repeatable website-search workflow that privileges official and primary sources, uses credible secondary sources only for alerts, and triangulates company claims against trial registries, labels, publications, or regulator documents.

## Source-quality notes

- Highest-confidence sources are regulators, labels, trial registries, guideline organizations, peer-reviewed journals, and official HTA/payer documents. Use these as the evidentiary backbone.
- ClinicalTrials.gov and other registries are sponsor-reported and can lag real activity. Compare `Last Update Posted`, enrollment status, primary completion date, results posting, and protocol changes against company disclosures and conference abstracts.
- FDA approval pages are reliable for U.S. indication language and approval basis, but the current prescribing information in Drugs@FDA and DailyMed should be checked for exact indication text, dosing, warnings, and companion diagnostic wording.
- EMA CHMP opinions and highlights are early signals; final legal authorization and product information should be confirmed in EMA EPARs and the European Commission Union Register.
- Guidelines and compendia may require accounts and may change without a public change log. Capture access date, guideline version, and exact recommendation language when extracting evidence.
- Conference abstracts are credible but preliminary. Check for updated posters, oral presentation slides, subsequent manuscripts, corrected abstracts, and final labels before treating findings as mature evidence.
- Company press releases, investor decks, and earnings calls are primary sources for company intent, timing, and messaging, but they are promotional and selective. Corroborate efficacy, safety, enrollment, and regulatory claims against registries, regulators, abstracts, or publications.
- Payer and HTA documents are highly relevant for access but jurisdiction-specific. Do not generalize a NICE, G-BA, PBAC, or payer decision to other markets without checking local comparators, discount assumptions, and label scope.
- Secondary news sources are useful for alerts, deal context, and commercial framing. Do not cite them as sole support for clinical efficacy, regulatory status, or guideline changes.
- Patient advocacy and foundation sites are useful for awareness, unmet-need framing, trial-navigation resources, advocacy priorities, and patient-experience signals. Use clinical/regulatory sources for treatment claims.
- Safety databases such as FAERS are signal-detection tools and cannot estimate incidence, causality, or comparative risk without formal pharmacoepidemiologic analysis.
- For ovarian cancer, always search synonymous disease terms together: `ovarian`, `fallopian tube`, and `primary peritoneal`. Many labels and trials combine these populations.
- For biomarker-defined therapies, distinguish assay eligibility from biological target expression. Track cutoff, assay name, tissue requirements, companion diagnostic status, and payer coverage separately.
