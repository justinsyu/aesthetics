# MASH Competitive Intelligence Website Search Plan

Prepared: May 11, 2026

Scope: This is a competitive intelligence source and monitoring plan for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), formerly nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), within the broader MASLD/SLD disease area. It identifies credible websites to search and monitor. It is not a CI findings report.

## CI objectives

- Track the MASH treatment landscape across approved therapies, late-stage pipeline assets, mechanisms, combinations, diagnostics, noninvasive tests, digital pathology, biopsy endpoints, fibrosis staging, cirrhosis populations, metabolic comorbidities, and lifecycle expansion.
- Detect competitor signals early: trial starts, protocol amendments, enrollment completion, readouts, discontinuations, label changes, safety updates, regulatory submissions, CHMP/FDA decisions, advisory committee activity, conference abstracts, publications, and investor disclosures.
- Monitor disease-area standards that shape development and access: AASLD/AGA/EASL guidance, MASLD/MASH nomenclature, endpoint expectations, noninvasive testing, fibrosis-risk stratification, and treatment sequencing.
- Track market access and commercial signals: payer prior authorization criteria, formulary placement, Medicare Part D dynamics, VA criteria, ICER/HEOR updates, HTA decisions, launch resources, copay programs, patient-support messaging, and field education.
- Preserve an auditable process that prioritizes official regulators, trial registries, medical societies, peer-reviewed journals, company filings, and payer/HTA sources over promotional or unsourced reporting.

## Source-quality notes

- Treat primary sources as the evidentiary base: FDA/EMA/other regulators, trial registries, peer-reviewed publications, society guidelines, SEC filings, official company disclosures, and public payer/HTA documents.
- Use older disease terminology deliberately. MASH was formerly NASH, and MASLD replaced NAFLD in many settings; trial registries, labels, abstracts, payer policies, and older publications may use any of these terms.
- Separate label claims from developmental claims. Approved indications and safety language should come from FDA/EMA labels or official product information; company sites are useful for positioning but should not override labels.
- Treat ClinicalTrials.gov and CTIS records as operational evidence, not proof of efficacy. Registry fields can lag sponsor announcements, and endpoint wording can change through amendments.
- Treat FAERS and social media as signal-generation sources only. They cannot establish incidence, causality, or comparative safety.
- Validate company press releases against trial records, conference abstracts, peer-reviewed papers, SEC filings, or regulator documents before using them as firm CI findings.
- Payer policies can be plan-specific and date-sensitive. Record policy effective date, revision date, line of business, geography, fibrosis-stage criteria, diagnostic requirements, continuation criteria, and step requirements.
- HTA and ICER assessments depend heavily on assumptions about surrogate endpoints, long-term fibrosis outcomes, discontinuation, adherence, price, and eligible population size. Capture model assumptions separately from conclusions.
- Secondary news and licensed databases are useful for discovery and triangulation, but final CI claims should cite primary sources wherever possible.
- For conference abstracts, distinguish title-only listings, full abstracts, poster PDFs, oral presentations, sponsor decks, and peer-reviewed manuscripts. Evidence weight increases after full peer review.
