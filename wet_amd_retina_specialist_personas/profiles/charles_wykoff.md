## Public role and practice setting

Evidence-backed facts:

- RCTX lists Wykoff as a board-certified medical and surgical retina specialist and ophthalmologist at Retina Consultants of Texas. The same institutional bio says he serves as Director of Research at RCTX and the Greater Houston Retina Research Foundation, and as Chairman of the Research and Clinical Trials Subcommittee at Retina Consultants of America. It also lists him as elected Deputy Chair of Ophthalmology for the Blanton Eye Institute, Houston Methodist Hospital. [S1]
- The ASRS profile lists RCTX locations in Houston-area Texas markets and professional appointments as Director of Research at Retina Consultants of Texas, Chairman of Research and Clinical Trials at Retina Consultants of America, Deputy Chair Ophthalmology at Houston Methodist's Blanton Eye Institute, and Clinical Professor Ophthalmology at Weill Cornell Medical College. [S2]
- RCA Research lists "Dr. Charles Wykoff" as Research Chair and describes RCA Research as a network with more than 50 retina research centers providing access to trials for conditions including macular degeneration and diabetic retinopathy. [S3]
- RCTX states that his research interests include angiogenesis and retinal vascular diseases including age-related macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, and venous occlusive diseases. [S1]
- ASRS states that he serves on multiple scientific and medical advisory boards, safety monitoring committees, and global steering committees across early- to late-stage development. [S2]

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- He is best modeled as a private-practice retina research-network operator: someone likely to translate trial efficacy into clinic workflow, patient selection, research-site feasibility, and payer/access consequences. This is an inference from his RCTX/RCA research leadership and high-volume private-practice setting, not a stated private position.

## Wet AMD evidence footprint

Evidence-backed facts:

- Public bios and editorial pages identify nAMD/AMD as a core area. Ophthalmology Retina's editorial board page lists Charles Wykoff, MD, PhD at Greater Houston Retina Research Foundation and includes "Neovascular age-related macular degeneration, geographic atrophy and dry age-related macular degeneration" among listed expertise areas. [S4]
- RGX-314 gene therapy: Wykoff is a co-author on the Lancet phase 1/2a dose-escalation study of subretinal RGX-314 for neovascular AMD; his affiliation is Retina Consultants of Texas. [S5]
- Sustained delivery/PDS: Wykoff is a co-author on the Ophthalmology 2023 two-year Archway phase 3 trial of the Port Delivery System with ranibizumab for nAMD. The trial compared PDS refill-exchanges every 24 weeks with monthly intravitreal ranibizumab in previously treated, anti-VEGF-responsive nAMD. [S6]
- Long-duration anti-VEGF: Wykoff was first author on the JAMA Ophthalmology phase 2 CANDELA randomized clinical trial evaluating aflibercept 8 mg versus aflibercept 2 mg in treatment-naive nAMD. [S7]
- Faricimab context: The pivotal TENAYA/LUCERNE phase 3 trials evaluated faricimab up to every 16 weeks versus aflibercept every 8 weeks in treatment-naive nAMD. Wykoff is not named in the visible PubMed author list, but this publication is important as a benchmark for the advisory simulation's durability claims. [S8]
- Sozinibercept/VEGF-C/D: Wykoff is first author on a 2025 phase 2b subgroup analysis of sozinibercept combination therapy in treatment-naive nAMD, evaluating angiographic predictors of response. The abstract reports larger visual acuity benefits in occult lesions receiving 2 mg sozinibercept plus ranibizumab compared with ranibizumab monotherapy at week 24, while noting lesion characteristics predicted response. [S9]
- Society/editorial roles: ASRS lists him as a past President of the Vit-Buckle Society, an ASRS Board of Directors member, and a founding member of the Ophthalmology Retina Editorial Board. RCTX also notes ASRS Board activity and Retina Society/Macula Society membership. [S1, S2]
- Patient/public education role: In a January 29, 2025 BrightFocus/ASRS wet AMD chat, he was introduced as RCTX Director of Research, RCA Chairman of Research, Deputy Chair at Blanton Eye Institute, Clinical Professor at Weill Cornell, and an ASRS Executive Committee/Board member. The transcript also records his public discussion of wet AMD diagnosis, anti-VEGF therapy, treatment access, step therapy, Avastin shortage/access issues, and GA in eyes with wet AMD. [S10]

## Pipeline and emerging-therapy exposure

Evidence-backed facts:

- Gene therapy: The RGX-314 phase 1/2a Lancet publication directly supports exposure to subretinal gene therapy development in nAMD. ClinicalTrials.gov describes AAVIATE (NCT04514653) as an investigational ABBV-RGX-314/RGX-314 gene therapy study for neovascular/wet AMD, testing whether a potential one-time treatment can have similar effects as current anti-VEGF standard of care. [S5, S11]
- Sustained delivery: Archway supports direct exposure to PDS with ranibizumab, including fixed refill-exchange intervals and monthly ranibizumab comparison. [S6]
- Long-duration anti-VEGF: CANDELA and PULSAR support exposure to aflibercept 8 mg/high-dose durability concepts. PULSAR showed aflibercept 8 mg achieved non-inferior visual acuity gains to aflibercept 2 mg through week 48 with extended dosing intervals in nAMD; CANDELA gives a Wykoff first-author signal in earlier high-dose aflibercept development. [S7, S12]
- Dual-pathway/combination therapy: The sozinibercept publication supports public involvement in VEGF-C/D combination strategy. Public Opthea 2025 releases subsequently reported that COAST did not meet its primary endpoint and that, after negative COAST and ShORe phase 3 results, Opthea and its development financing investors agreed to discontinue wet AMD development of sozinibercept. [S9, S13, S14]
- Value/access topics: In the BrightFocus/ASRS transcript, Wykoff publicly emphasized patient and physician choice, objected to step therapy as potentially wasting time and risking eyesight, and discussed Avastin access/shortage issues and CMS recommendations to suspend Avastin-first step therapy policies during shortage conditions. [S10]
- Complement/GA overlap: The BrightFocus/ASRS transcript includes discussion that an eye can respond well to anti-VEGF therapy while progressive vision loss may be driven by the dry/GA component, and that patients should discuss whether approved GA therapeutics may be appropriate. [S10]
- Conflict/industry relationship context: ASRS Practice Management Committee disclosure materials list Wykoff with numerous consultant and principal-investigator relationships across companies including AbbVie, Adverum, Apellis, Bayer, Genentech, IVERIC Bio, Kodiak, Novartis, Ocular Therapeutix, Opthea, Regeneron, RegenXBio, Roche, Santen, and others. Treat this as a breadth-of-exposure signal and a conflict-management watchout, not evidence of any product preference. [S15]

## Treatment-decision drivers likely to shape simulation input

Evidence-backed anchors:

- He has public roles in trial design, drug/device development, and research networks. [S1, S2, S3, S10]
- He has publication exposure across gene therapy, sustained delivery, high-dose anti-VEGF, and combination therapy. [S5, S6, S7, S9]
- He publicly supports patient/physician choice and access to the "right medicine at the right time," and publicly criticizes step therapy in wet AMD access discussions. [S10]
- He publicly frames wet AMD care around early diagnosis, anti-VEGF effectiveness, injection burden, access, and overlap with GA/dry AMD. [S10]

Analyst-derived persona hypotheses for simulation:

- Durability must change workflow, not just labeling. He is likely to ask whether a pipeline agent meaningfully reduces injection and visit burden in treat-and-extend practice, including chair time, imaging frequency, rescue injections, and call-backs.
- Safety will be adoption-limiting if it complicates routine retina care. He is likely to probe intraocular inflammation, vasculitis/occlusion, endophthalmitis, surgical/device events, steroid prophylaxis, pressure events, and whether safety monitoring erases durability gains.
- Trial endpoints must map to real patients. He is likely to challenge trial populations that exclude bilateral disease, high-frequency injection need, RAP/type 3 lesions, undertreated patients, poor responders, or older comorbid patients who make up a busy US retina clinic.
- He may be especially sensitive to access and payer policy. Public step-therapy comments suggest he would ask how a new branded/pipeline product will be used when bevacizumab, biosimilars, faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, and payer rules are in the same decision environment.
- He may be open to bold platforms if rescue paths are clear. Gene therapy/PDS exposure suggests comfort with durable innovation, but likely with detailed scrutiny of reversibility, retreatment options, non-responder management, bilateral treatment strategy, and long-term follow-up.

## Likely questions/objections in a simulated wet AMD pipeline advisory board

Clearly labeled analyst hypotheses:

- "What is the durability distribution, not just the mean interval? How many patients truly reach 12, 16, 20, or 24 weeks without rescue?"
- "How were rescue injections counted in the efficacy and burden claims?"
- "Does this reduce visits or only injections? In our clinic, imaging and monitoring visits still consume capacity."
- "What happens to patients who have persistent intraretinal fluid, large PEDs, RAP/type 3 disease, occult lesions, or previously high injection burden?"
- "What is the safety management plan for inflammation, vasculitis, pressure events, endophthalmitis, and steroid prophylaxis?"
- "If this is gene therapy or implant-based, what are the surgical/training requirements, bilateral-use strategy, and options if the first eye underperforms?"
- "How does the product compare against faricimab and aflibercept 8 mg in a modern treat-and-extend setting rather than against older fixed q8 comparators only?"
- "What access barriers will patients face, and will payer sequencing delay treatment in a way that risks vision?"
- "Are we improving absolute vision outcomes, or mostly drying anatomy and extending interval?"
- "Given sozinibercept's phase 2-to-phase 3 miss, what subgroup or biomarker rationale is robust enough to trust prospectively?"

## How to use this persona in simulation

- Assign him the "private-practice research chair / translational trialist" seat.
- Use him when the advisory board needs pressure-testing on adoption in high-volume US retina practices, trial feasibility, operational workflow, and whether a pipeline claim is robust enough for protocol changes.
- Make him constructive but demanding: open to innovation across injections, implants, and gene therapy, while asking for evidence that the benefit survives real-world patient selection, payer restrictions, safety management, and treat-and-extend practice.
- Pair him with a more academic methodologist or an access/value skeptic to create productive tension: Wykoff can bridge protocol science with clinic execution.
- Do not present him as having a fixed private preference for any branded product. His public record shows broad research and industry exposure, not a single stated prescribing hierarchy.

## Evidence gaps / watchouts

- Public profiles are promotional and may lag current titles or committee roles.
- Publication authorship and trial participation do not prove personal prescribing preference or likely voting behavior.
- Industry disclosures are extensive, time-sensitive, and incomplete unless separately audited against Open Payments and meeting-specific disclosures.
- Some relevant data are conference presentations or company releases rather than peer-reviewed papers; use them as pipeline-awareness signals, not final efficacy evidence.
- His public comments on patient choice and step therapy are evidence-backed, but any projection to a specific product access view is an inference.
- The aggregate profile in this folder appears older than some 2025 pipeline events; this profile incorporates Opthea's 2025 negative phase 3 update for simulation context.
