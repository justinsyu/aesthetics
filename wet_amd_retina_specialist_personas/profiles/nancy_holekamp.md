## Public Role and Practice Setting

Evidence-backed facts:

- Nancy M. Holekamp, MD is publicly listed as Director of Retina Services at Pepose Vision Institute in the St. Louis area [S1, S2, S3].
- Pepose Vision Institute describes itself as a patient-centered St. Louis eye-care practice with retina services and an advanced research center; its retina page states the practice is involved in research, diagnosis, and treatment of retinal diseases including macular degeneration [S2, S4].
- An OIS biography states that Holekamp was previously Professor of Clinical Ophthalmology at Washington University School of Medicine, has been a principal investigator in more than 38 national clinical trials involving AMD, retinal vascular occlusion, and diabetic retinopathy, and has continued consulting to major retina pharmaceutical companies [S3].
- Her public CV hosted by Pepose lists professional roles including Women in Retina co-founder, ASRS membership since 1995, AAO and ASRS awards, prior AAO committee/secretariat activity, and editorial-board/reviewer roles [S5].

Analyst-derived persona implication:

- For simulation, she should be modeled as a private-practice retina clinical researcher with enough academic/society history to challenge both clinical evidence quality and real-world operational fit.

## Wet AMD Evidence Footprint

Evidence-backed facts:

- Holekamp was first author on the phase 3 Archway randomized trial of the Port Delivery System with ranibizumab for neovascular AMD. PubMed lists her affiliation as Pepose Vision Institute, Chesterfield, Missouri [S6].
- Archway evaluated PDS with ranibizumab 100 mg/mL refilled every 24 weeks versus monthly ranibizumab 0.5 mg in previously treated anti-VEGF-responsive nAMD patients. The trial enrolled 418 patients and met noninferiority/equivalence for BCVA change over weeks 36/40; 98.4% of assessed PDS-treated patients did not require supplemental ranibizumab before the first refill-exchange [S6].
- The same Archway abstract reports procedure/device-relevant adverse events of special interest, including endophthalmitis, retinal detachment, vitreous hemorrhage, conjunctival erosion, and conjunctival retraction in the PDS arm [S6].
- Holekamp was first author on a prospective trial of home OCT-guided management of treatment-experienced nAMD patients. The PMC-indexed article identifies her Pepose Vision Institute affiliation and keywords including remote monitoring, personalized medicine, AI, anti-VEGF therapies, nAMD, home monitoring, and telemedicine [S7].
- She is also a co-author on a 2024 Journal of VitreoRetinal Diseases article on clinical use of home OCT data to manage nAMD, with her affiliation listed as Pepose Vision Institute, St. Louis [S8].
- ASRS 2025 wet AMD symposium materials list "Nancy Holekamp, MD, FASRS" on a presentation involving a network meta-analysis of faricimab versus aflibercept 8 mg after loading doses, connecting her public meeting presence to contemporary long-duration anti-VEGF comparison questions [S9].

Analyst-derived persona implication:

- Her public wet AMD footprint is not primarily "new molecule discovery"; it is implementation-heavy: sustained drug delivery, remote disease monitoring, treatment burden, patient adherence, and how durable-control claims translate to clinic behavior.

## Pipeline and Emerging-Therapy Exposure

Evidence-backed facts:

- Sustained delivery: Archway and later Susvimo/PDS public materials place Holekamp near the central evidence base for refillable ocular-implant anti-VEGF delivery in nAMD [S6, S10].
- Device lifecycle and safety: Roche announced on 2024-07-08 that Susvimo was being reintroduced in the US for nAMD after the end of a voluntary recall, following FDA approval of component-level updates to the ocular implant and refill needle [S10].
- Home OCT and remote monitoring: Holekamp's home OCT-guided nAMD work supports exposure to monitoring-enabled treatment individualization and AI-assisted home imaging workflows [S7, S8].
- Long-duration anti-VEGF: ASRS 2025 materials link her to current comparative questions involving faricimab and aflibercept 8 mg, although the source is a meeting PDF rather than a full peer-reviewed publication [S9].
- Complement/GA adjacency: Retinal Physician lists Holekamp as author/commentator on patient perspective in geographic atrophy and discloses consultancy to Apellis for that article. This is relevant only as patient-centered retina-care context, not as evidence of a wet AMD complement-development role [S11].

Evidence gaps:

- I did not find strong public evidence, within the reviewed sources, that Holekamp is a named principal investigator or author for wet AMD gene-therapy programs such as ixo-vec or 4D-150. Gene therapy should therefore not be over-weighted for this persona unless additional source-supported evidence is added.
- I did not find a source-supported public role in wet AMD biosimilar policy, formal guideline authorship, or payer/value assessment specific to nAMD.

## Treatment-Decision Drivers Likely to Shape Simulation Input

Evidence-backed fact pattern:

- Her PDS authorship creates a documented interest in injection-burden reduction, fixed long refill intervals, device-related safety, and supplemental-treatment rules [S6].
- Her home OCT authorship creates a documented interest in remote monitoring, daily or frequent imaging feasibility, personalized treatment timing, and separating visit burden from injection burden [S7, S8].
- Her public GA commentary emphasizes talking to patients about functional goals and lived limitations, rather than relying only on visual acuity. Although GA is not wet AMD, it supports a patient-centered decision style in retina care [S11].

Analyst-derived hypotheses for simulation:

- She would likely ask whether a pipeline therapy reduces total burden for patients and caregivers, not just the number of injections.
- She would likely distinguish "durability" from "undertreatment risk"; longer intervals would need credible monitoring and rescue criteria.
- She would likely be attentive to elderly-patient usability for home technologies and whether home OCT data can be integrated into clinic workflow without creating a new staffing bottleneck.
- She would likely scrutinize device or procedure safety, especially adverse events that are uncommon but practice-changing, such as endophthalmitis, retinal detachment, vitreous hemorrhage, conjunctival erosion/retraction, or device malfunction.
- She may be receptive to pipeline concepts that provide certainty between visits, but only if trial design and real-world logistics support that confidence.

## Likely Questions/Objections in a Simulated Wet AMD Pipeline Advisory Board

Analyst-derived, grounded in public evidence:

- "What is the evidence that this reduces patient and caregiver burden after counting monitoring visits, imaging, calls, rescue injections, and procedure time?"
- "If patients are extended, how are we detecting recurrence early enough to prevent avoidable vision loss?"
- "What proportion of patients still need rescue or interval-shortening, and how quickly can we identify them?"
- "Does the durability claim hold in previously treated, high-burden patients, or only in a selected trial population?"
- "What device-, procedure-, or inflammation-related adverse events would change adoption in a community retina practice?"
- "Can home OCT or another remote-monitoring tool safely support the dosing interval being proposed?"
- "How does this compare with current long-duration options such as faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, and PDS/Susvimo on both durability and operational complexity?"
- "What does the patient actually experience differently: fewer injections, fewer visits, fewer urgent calls, less anxiety, or just a different protocol?"

## How to Use This Persona in Simulation

- Use her as the "burden plus monitoring" expert rather than a pure mechanism-of-action reviewer.
- Give her prompts that force separation of endpoints: BCVA, anatomy, injection frequency, visit frequency, rescue frequency, home-monitoring workload, and patient confidence between visits.
- In discussions of sustained delivery, have her press on surgical/refill workflow, device complication mitigation, training, patient selection, and post-recall device confidence.
- In discussions of long-duration injections, have her compare whether the new agent offers a meaningful advantage over faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, or treat-and-extend anti-VEGF practice.
- In discussions of home OCT or AI monitoring, have her challenge whether the proposed workflow changes physician behavior and improves outcomes rather than merely producing more data.

## Evidence Gaps / Watchouts

- Do not infer private treatment preferences, formulary choices, or specific product loyalty from consulting disclosures, trial authorship, or meeting participation.
- Her public evidence base is strong for PDS and home OCT, but weaker for direct wet AMD gene-therapy leadership, biosimilars, payer policy, and formal guideline authorship.
- Some public bios and CV materials may lag current appointments; use them as public role signals rather than definitive employment verification.
- Meeting PDFs and trade publications are useful for current exposure but should be weighted below peer-reviewed publications and institutional/professional bios.
- CMS Open Payments can be used for additional disclosure research, but CMS cautions that reported payments do not by themselves identify beneficial relationships or conflicts; I did not include individual Open Payments amounts in this profile [S12].
