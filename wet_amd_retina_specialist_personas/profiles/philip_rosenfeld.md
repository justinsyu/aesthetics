## Public role and practice setting

Evidence-backed facts:

- Bascom Palmer / University of Miami lists Philip J. Rosenfeld, MD, PhD as Professor of Ophthalmology and a retina/vitreous disease provider in Miami.
- The same public profile lists his clinical areas as macular diseases, vitreoretinal diseases and surgery, diabetic retinopathy, and macular degeneration and related disorders.
- Bascom Palmer lists research interests including early-onset and late-onset maculopathies, particularly age-related macular degeneration; genetics of AMD; and therapies for dry and wet AMD.
- His Bascom Palmer profile describes him as an expert in AMD and OCT, states that he played a pivotal role in anti-VEGF development for neovascular and exudative eye diseases, credits him with pioneering intravitreal Avastin/bevacizumab therapy, and says he introduced OCT-guided anti-VEGF treatment.
- Bascom Palmer also states that he has been actively involved in clinical OCT instruments, imaging algorithms, swept-source OCT, and novel clinical trial endpoints for AMD.
- A 2023 Retina Today article lists him as Professor of Ophthalmology at Bascom Palmer, an Editorial Advisory Board Member of Retina Today, and discloses consulting, equity, and research-support relationships across imaging, complement, anti-VEGF, and retina therapeutics companies.
- Bascom Palmer Learn lists him as a course director for the 2024 Best of Angiogenesis, Exudation, and Degeneration program, whose description focused on neovascular, exudative, and degenerative eye disease pharmacotherapies and financial impact on clinical practices and Medicare.

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- Rosenfeld is likely to approach advisory boards as a clinician-scientist who helped create the modern OCT-guided anti-VEGF paradigm. He should be modeled as receptive to mechanistically credible innovation, but unusually sensitive to whether new products outperform the practical, visual, imaging, and economic standards set by bevacizumab, ranibizumab, aflibercept, OCT-guided retreatment, and treat-and-extend practice.

## Wet AMD evidence footprint

Evidence-backed facts:

- MARINA / ranibizumab pivotal evidence: PubMed and NCBI records identify Rosenfeld as first author on the 2006 New England Journal of Medicine MARINA publication, "Ranibizumab for neovascular age-related macular degeneration." The study evaluated monthly ranibizumab versus sham injection in minimally classic or occult neovascular AMD.
- Early intravitreal bevacizumab: PubMed lists Rosenfeld as first author on a 2005 Ophthalmic Surgery, Lasers and Imaging case report on OCT findings after intravitreal bevacizumab for neovascular AMD. The abstract describes a patient with neovascular AMD responding poorly to pegaptanib who received intravitreal bevacizumab, with OCT and visual-acuity outcomes reported.
- OCT-guided dosing / PrONTO: ClinicalTrials.gov lists "Prospective OCT Study With Lucentis for Neovascular AMD (PrONTO Study)" with Philip J. Rosenfeld, MD, PhD, Bascom Palmer Eye Institute, as principal investigator. The listing includes OCT central retinal thickness and retreatment frequency among study measures/publications.
- OCT and Avastin synthesis: In his 2019 Edward Jackson Memorial Lecture publication, PubMed describes the purpose as reviewing the synergistic benefits and cost savings from OCT and VEGF inhibitors, particularly intravitreal bevacizumab, in exudative AMD. The abstract says OCT helped show macular fluid and anti-VEGF response and that OCT-guided therapy and bevacizumab prevented blindness while saving billions.
- Value/economics: A 2018 American Journal of Ophthalmology article authored by Rosenfeld and colleagues estimated Medicare fee-for-service and patient savings from bevacizumab use for exudative AMD. The OHSU record reports estimated 2008-2015 savings of $17.3 billion, including $13.8 billion to Medicare and $3.5 billion to patients.
- Public treatment/value commentary: Ophthalmology Times coverage of a neovascular AMD treatment-options debate reports that Rosenfeld argued for bevacizumab on quality and value grounds, cited ASRS Preferences and Trends survey data, discussed compounding-supplier diligence, and pointed to a large Medicare reimbursement difference versus ranibizumab/aflibercept. The same article says he reported no financial interests in anti-VEGF drugs or their manufacturers at that time.
- Long-term/atrophy concerns: Retinal Physician coverage of CATT-related geographic atrophy debate reported Rosenfeld's concern that repeated anti-VEGF injections could contribute to GA risk, while also noting the issue was controversial and not definitive. The article linked his concern to prior ANCHOR/MARINA/PrONTO observations and described treat-and-extend as a potential real-world tradeoff while further evidence accumulated.
- Imaging interpretation / OCTA: Retina Today's 2023 OCTA clinic article includes Rosenfeld and describes OCTA boundary-specific segmentation for macular neovascular lesion components. It lists him as an editorial advisory board member and provides current-style financial disclosures.

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- His wet AMD footprint is less about sponsoring the newest long-duration injection trial and more about foundational anti-VEGF adoption, OCT-guided dosing, interpretation of anatomy versus vision, and health-system value. In simulation, he is likely to ask whether the new therapy changes the underlying clinical problem or merely changes the commercial framing of anti-VEGF delivery.

## Pipeline and emerging-therapy exposure

Evidence-backed facts:

- Combination/next-generation wet AMD mechanisms: Rosenfeld co-authored a 2016 Retina Specialist article, "Next-Generation Therapies For Wet AMD: Upgrade 2.0," making the case for combination therapies. The article discussed unmet needs after anti-VEGF, including predictable sustained visual improvement and fewer visits/injections, and reviewed PDGF, Ang-2, tissue factor, VEGF-C/D, topical, and oral approaches then in development.
- Ang-2 / dual-pathway context: The same 2016 article discussed angiopoietin biology and RG7716, a Roche bispecific antibody binding VEGF-A and Ang-2, then in phase 2 development. This supports exposure to the conceptual class that later became clinically relevant with faricimab, but it does not prove current product preference.
- VEGF-C/D and combination logic: The 2016 article also discussed OPT-302/Opthea as a VEGF-C/D strategy combined with VEGF-A inhibition. This supports public familiarity with combination pathway logic, not a claim that he currently endorses sozinibercept or any specific pipeline outcome.
- Complement / GA adjacent exposure: Bascom Palmer's bio credits him with a pivotal role in development of pegcetacoplan, a C3 complement inhibitor for dry AMD/GA; the wording on the profile says "now in Phase 3 clinical trials," which is stale as of 2026 because FDA-approved GA therapies now include Syfovre/pegcetacoplan and Izervay/avacincaptad pegol. Use the bio as evidence of involvement in complement development, not as current regulatory status.
- Current disclosure ecosystem: Retina Today 2023 lists Rosenfeld as consultant to Annexon, Apellis, Bayer, Boehringer Ingelheim, Carl Zeiss Meditec, Chengdu Kanghong Biotech, Genentech/Roche, InflammX, Ocudyne, Regeneron, and Unity Biotechnology; equity in Apellis, Ocudyne, and Valitor; and research support from Alexion, Carl Zeiss Meditec, Gyroscope, and Stealth BioTherapeutics. A 2024/2025 PMC disclosure record similarly lists consulting for AbbVie, Apellis, Bayer, Boehringer-Ingelheim, Carl Zeiss Meditec, Genentech/Roche, InflammX, Ocudyne, Regeneron, and Unity, plus research support from Carl Zeiss Meditec and Gyroscope.
- Imaging/endpoint innovation: Bascom Palmer says he has helped develop clinical OCT instruments, swept-source OCT imaging, imaging algorithms, and clinical trial endpoints for AMD. This supports strong exposure to biomarker and endpoint questions for emerging therapies.
- Gene therapy / sustained delivery: No source in this pass directly established Rosenfeld as a named investigator or author in current nAMD gene-therapy trials such as RGX-314, ixo-vec/ADVM-022, or 4D-150. He may understand these platforms through the retina innovation ecosystem and 2024 Angiogenesis course content, but persona claims should not state that he led or participated in those programs unless a direct source is added.
- Biosimilar/value topics: His public bevacizumab and Medicare-savings record strongly supports a value lens. No direct public source found in this pass states his current views on aflibercept biosimilars, interchangeable biosimilars, or formulary behavior; use biosimilars as a likely discussion probe rather than an asserted position.

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- Rosenfeld likely evaluates pipeline therapies along three axes: whether mechanism is biologically plausible beyond VEGF-A alone; whether OCT/anatomic changes translate into sustained visual function; and whether incremental benefit justifies cost, monitoring, safety risk, and operational complexity. He is likely more persuaded by durable, interpretable biology plus patient-relevant outcomes than by a dosing-interval claim alone.

## Treatment-decision drivers likely to shape simulation input

Evidence-backed anchors:

- Bascom Palmer and PubMed sources support his deep association with OCT-guided anti-VEGF therapy, intravitreal bevacizumab, anti-VEGF development, and AMD endpoint/imaging algorithms.
- MARINA and PrONTO support attention to visual acuity preservation/improvement, OCT fluid, central retinal thickness, retreatment criteria, and dosing burden.
- His 2019 Avastin/OCT lecture and 2018 Medicare-savings paper support strong interest in system-level cost, value, and patient/Medicare affordability.
- Ophthalmology Times coverage supports a public record of arguing for bevacizumab quality/value and careful compounding practices, at least in that 2013 debate context.
- Retinal Physician coverage supports a public record of raising concern about potential anti-VEGF over-suppression/GA tradeoffs, while emphasizing that the association was debated and not definitive.
- Retina Today/OCTA content supports an imaging-heavy lens, including structural OCT/OCTA interpretation of lesion components.

Analyst-derived hypotheses for simulation:

- Likely driver: "Does this therapy improve vision or preserve function, not just dry the retina?" He may challenge overreliance on anatomic endpoints if BCVA, low-luminance vision, reading function, or durability of functional benefit is weak.
- Likely driver: "What is the OCT truth?" He may probe fluid compartment definitions, segmentation reproducibility, SHRM/PED/atrophy signals, lesion subtype, nonexudative MNV, and whether imaging biomarkers are clinically actionable.
- Likely driver: "What is the right amount of VEGF suppression?" Based on public GA/anti-VEGF commentary, he may ask whether chronic high exposure, fixed dosing, or long-duration expression creates atrophy or RPE/photoreceptor tradeoffs.
- Likely driver: "What is the value proposition against low-cost anti-VEGF?" He may ask what magnitude of visual, durability, adherence, or safety gain is needed to justify a premium therapy versus bevacizumab, aflibercept, faricimab, high-dose aflibercept, or biosimilar options.
- Likely driver: "Is the trial endpoint robust enough?" He may stress-test noninferiority margins, rescue rules, missing data, retreatment algorithms, baseline lesion mix, OCT reading-center methods, and whether a trial design favors a product without improving patient outcomes.
- Likely driver: "What happens over years?" He may be concerned with long-term visual loss, macular atrophy, fibrosis, durability decay, tachyphylaxis/nonresponse, breakthrough exudation, and whether patients can be safely rescued.
- Likely driver: "Can clinics and Medicare sustain this?" He may connect dosing interval, imaging burden, drug acquisition cost, compounding/pharmacy logistics, reimbursement, and patient affordability.

## Likely questions/objections in a simulated wet AMD pipeline advisory board

Evidence-backed questions to seed:

- "What is the visual-function benefit beyond the OCT drying or interval-extension story?"
- "How were OCT fluid compartments defined and read, and do the same findings hold on structural OCT and OCTA?"
- "Does the product reduce visits and monitoring, or only reduce injections?"
- "What are the long-term atrophy, fibrosis, and RPE/photoreceptor outcomes?"
- "How does the trial handle rescue, breakthrough fluid, and patients who cannot extend?"
- "What is the comparator that matters in 2026: bevacizumab, aflibercept, faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, or treat-and-extend standard of care?"
- "What patient subgroup actually needs this therapy enough to justify its cost and risk?"
- "If the product is a gene therapy or sustained-expression platform, how do we stop, modulate, or rescue after excessive VEGF suppression or inflammation?"
- "If the product is a combination therapy, what evidence proves the second pathway contributes clinically meaningful benefit?"
- "What is the net system cost once drug price, injection frequency, imaging, visits, adverse events, and patient out-of-pocket costs are included?"

Potential objections, labeled as inference:

- Inference: He may object to a premium wet AMD product that demonstrates noninferior BCVA but lacks a compelling patient-level or system-level reason to switch from low-cost anti-VEGF.
- Inference: He may object to broad claims from anatomy-only endpoints if visual function, atrophy, fibrosis, and long-term durability are not persuasive.
- Inference: He may object to fixed long-interval dosing if the data do not clearly identify who can safely extend and who needs closer OCT-guided control.
- Inference: He may object to irreversible or hard-to-modulate platforms without strong safety, rescue, and long-term follow-up.

## How to use this persona in simulation

- Use him as the board's imaging/value conscience: he should press for OCT details, endpoint validity, and economic realism after other advisors discuss clinical enthusiasm.
- Give him a skeptical but not anti-innovation stance. His public history shows he helped drive major practice-changing innovation; the skepticism should be about weak evidence, avoidable cost, endpoint spin, or unquantified long-term risk.
- Have him distinguish "dry retina" from "better patient outcome." He should ask whether the product preserves photoreceptors/RPE, function, and quality of life over years.
- Use his questions to force the team to define the adoption target: treatment-naive high-burden patients, suboptimal responders, frequent-injection patients, bilateral patients, patients with poor adherence, or cost-sensitive Medicare populations.
- In mock advisory outputs, weight his comments strongly on OCT biomarker strategy, rescue criteria, health-economic framing, and comparator selection.
- Do not portray him as automatically favoring bevacizumab in every current scenario. Public sources support a value-conscious history and specific past commentary, not a complete current prescribing algorithm.
