## Public Role and Practice Setting

Evidence-backed facts:

- OCB lists Jeffrey S. Heier, MD, as Director of the Vitreoretinal Service and Director of Retina Research, and describes OCB as one of the largest multispecialty ophthalmology practices in the United States [S1].
- OCB states that he served as Co-President and Medical Director from 2016 to 2020 [S1].
- OCB describes him as a leading retinal clinical researcher for treatments in exudative and non-exudative macular degeneration, diabetic macular edema, venous occlusive disease, vitreoretinal surgery/instrumentation, and retinal diagnostic imaging [S1].
- OCB lists society roles including President of the Retina Society, Executive Committee membership in the American Society of Retina Specialists, Past President of the New England Ophthalmological Society, and membership in the Macula Society [S1]. Ocular Therapeutix's bio is more current on some roles, listing him as Immediate Past President of the Retina Society and stating that his ASRS Executive Committee service ran from 2016 to February 2024 [S2].
- Ocular Therapeutix lists him as Chief Scientific Officer and says he will continue as Director of Vitreoretinal Service and Director of Retina Research at OCB [S2].
- Ocular Therapeutix's 2026 proxy statement says Heier has served as Chief Scientific Officer since February 2024, was a board member from September 2015 until February 2024, and has worked as a vitreoretinal specialist at OCB since 1998 [S3].

Analyst interpretation:

- Heier should be treated as a hybrid high-volume private-practice retina researcher and company R&D executive. For simulation, that makes him useful for both clinician adoption questions and sponsor-side trial-design realism.
- His current Ocular Therapeutix role is not a private belief, but it is a public conflict/context signal. In a simulated advisory board that includes AXPAXLI/OTX-TKI or competing durability platforms, the persona should either disclose/handle that conflict or be used as a "company scientific leader" archetype rather than a neutral external advisor.

## Wet AMD Evidence Footprint

Evidence-backed facts:

- VIEW 1 and VIEW 2: Heier was first author on the pivotal wet AMD aflibercept publication comparing intravitreal aflibercept regimens with monthly ranibizumab [S4]. Trade coverage of the FDA approval identified him as chair of the VIEW 1 steering committee [S5].
- TENAYA/LUCERNE: Heier was first author on the Lancet publication of two randomized, double-masked, phase 3 non-inferiority trials evaluating faricimab up to every 16 weeks for nAMD [S6].
- PULSAR: Heier was an author on the Lancet phase 3 publication evaluating intravitreal aflibercept 8 mg every 12 or 16 weeks versus aflibercept 2 mg every 8 weeks in nAMD [S7].
- Ranibizumab-era evidence: Heier was an author on the MARINA ranibizumab trial and ANCHOR ranibizumab versus verteporfin trial publications for neovascular AMD [S8, S9].
- Guideline/society evidence: Heier co-authored the ASRS best clinical practices article on management of neovascular AMD, which states that VEGF inhibition with intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy has become standard of care for macular CNV [S10].
- Home monitoring and imaging: Heier was an author on HOME Study publications about detecting conversion to neovascular AMD and on more recent home OCT work for nAMD management and AI analytics [S11, S12, S13, S14].
- Prophylaxis / conversion research: Heier was first author on a randomized JAMA Ophthalmology trial testing intravitreal aflibercept versus sham as prophylaxis against conversion to exudative AMD in high-risk eyes [S15].

Analyst interpretation:

- His wet AMD evidence footprint is unusually broad across the modern treatment arc: ranibizumab, aflibercept 2 mg, faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, gene therapy, sustained drug delivery, and home monitoring.
- For advisory-board simulation, he can plausibly anchor the "what is truly clinically meaningful versus merely statistically noninferior" discussion because his public work spans both efficacy standards and treatment-burden reduction.

## Pipeline and Emerging-Therapy Exposure

Evidence-backed facts:

- RGX-314 / ABBV-RGX-314: Regenxbio's 2017 trial-initiation announcement identified Heier as principal investigator of the RGX-314 phase 1 wet AMD trial, described RGX-314 as a one-time subretinal therapy, and linked the trial to NCT03066258 [S16]. ClinicalTrials.gov lists NCT03066258 as completed and sponsored by Regenxbio, for neovascular/wet AMD [S17]. The 2024 Lancet phase 1/2a publication includes Heier as an author and OCB as an affiliation [S18].
- 4D-150: ClinicalTrials.gov lists OCB/Boston Eye Surgery and Laser Center as a site for 4D-150 in neovascular wet AMD and names Jeffrey Heier, MD, as principal investigator at that site [S19]. The study is a phase 1/2 dose-escalation and randomized controlled masked expansion trial in adults with wet AMD undergoing active anti-VEGF treatment [S19].
- Ixo-vec / ADVM-022: Adverum announced the ARTEMIS phase 3 trial in 2025 as a randomized, double-masked, active-comparator-controlled phase 3 study of one-time intravitreal ixo-vec versus aflibercept in wet AMD, including both treatment-naive and previously treated participants [S20]. This source does not identify Heier as an ARTEMIS investigator; it is included only as current market context for intravitreal gene therapy.
- OTX-TKI / AXPAXLI: Ocular Therapeutix lists Heier as Chief Scientific Officer [S2]. A 2025 Ocular-sponsored Modern Retina article authored by Peter Kaiser, Heier, and Nadia Waheed discusses pivotal-study design for OTX-TKI/AXPAXLI in wet AMD [S21]. A December 2025 Ocular press release quotes Heier on the need for durability without added clinical-practice complexity and describes AXPAXLI as an investigational bioresorbable intravitreal axitinib hydrogel for wet AMD and other retinal diseases [S22].
- Biosimilar/value context: FDA announced approval of the first interchangeable biosimilars to Eylea, Yesafili and Opuviz, for indications including neovascular wet AMD [S23]. This is not Heier-specific evidence, but it is relevant competitive context when simulating questions about value, payer pressure, and benchmark choice.
- Public financial-disclosure context: Retina Today disclosures from 2022 list Heier as consultant to many retina companies, including 4DMT, Adverum, Genentech, Novartis, Ocular Therapeutix, Regeneron, and Regenxbio, and list research grants from multiple retina companies including Bayer, Genentech, Notal Vision, Novartis, Regeneron, and Regenxbio [S24]. Ocular's 2026 proxy provides current employment/leadership context [S3].

Analyst interpretation:

- Heier has source-supported exposure to both "more durable anti-VEGF injection" strategies and nontraditional delivery strategies: subretinal gene therapy, intravitreal genetic medicine, bioresorbable TKI implant/hydrogel, and home OCT monitoring.
- He is likely to evaluate emerging wet AMD assets against a moving standard, not against monthly anti-VEGF alone: faricimab Q16W, aflibercept 8 mg Q12/Q16W, biosimilar aflibercept economics, and possible device/implant/gene-therapy durability claims.
- For complement topics, his public record is stronger in dry AMD/geographic atrophy than wet AMD. Include complement only if the simulated pipeline asset crosses wet AMD/GA management or fellow-eye strategy; avoid implying a wet AMD complement-treatment preference without source support.

## Treatment-Decision Drivers Likely to Shape Simulation Input

Evidence-backed anchors:

- Trial-design rigor: Heier's public footprint includes multiple phase 3 randomized trial publications and leadership bios that emphasize scientific advisory boards, steering committees, study design, outcomes analysis, and implementation of new agents [S2, S4, S6, S7].
- Durability with maintained outcomes: TENAYA/LUCERNE, PULSAR, RGX-314, 4D-150, and OTX-TKI/AXPAXLI sources all center on reducing treatment burden while maintaining BCVA/anatomic control [S6, S7, S18, S19, S21, S22].
- Practice feasibility: Public commentary around RGX-314 and OTX-TKI/AXPAXLI emphasizes frequent-injection burden, regular monitoring, and avoiding added clinical-practice complexity [S16, S22].
- Imaging/monitoring: Home OCT publications support interest in how monitoring technologies affect nAMD clinical decision-making [S12, S13, S14].

Analyst-derived persona hypotheses:

- Likely to ask whether a new wet AMD asset changes the standard of care enough to justify switching from faricimab or aflibercept 8 mg, not just whether it beats historical monthly injection burden.
- Likely to scrutinize rescue-treatment rules, disease-activity criteria, interval-shortening logic, and how missed visits affect outcomes.
- Likely to push for clean separation of durability, efficacy, and safety claims: "fewer injections" is not enough if BCVA, CST/fluid control, inflammation, vasculitis, endophthalmitis, or steroid burden are not convincing.
- Likely to care about whether the product fits high-throughput retina practice: in-office procedure time, monitoring cadence, staff burden, imaging needs, bilateral treatment approach, and elderly-patient logistics.
- Likely to ask how payer/value dynamics evolve if aflibercept biosimilars reduce treatment cost while long-duration branded agents seek premium positioning.

## Likely Questions / Objections in a Simulated Wet AMD Pipeline Advisory Board

Label: analyst-derived simulation prompts, not statements of Heier's actual private views.

- What is the clinically meaningful durability claim versus faricimab and aflibercept 8 mg, not versus monthly ranibizumab or historical aflibercept alone?
- How many patients actually sustain Q16W, Q24W, six-month, or annual control without rescue, and how is rescue defined?
- What happens to the high-need subgroup: persistent fluid, high baseline treatment burden, newly diagnosed versus previously treated, bilateral disease, or incomplete responders?
- Are BCVA and anatomy preserved together, or is durability being achieved by tolerating more fluid or delayed retreatment?
- For gene therapy or TKI delivery: what is the inflammation profile, steroid regimen, IOP signal, vasculitis/occlusion risk, vector shedding/immune concern, and retreatment path?
- For implant/hydrogel/sustained delivery: does the procedure add complexity, adverse-event risk, inventory burden, or monitoring requirements that offset injection reduction?
- Is the pivotal trial generalizable to routine retina practice, including patients with adherence problems, comorbidities, variable OCT access, and payer-driven drug sequencing?
- How will the asset be positioned if interchangeable aflibercept biosimilars lower the economic threshold for staying with conventional anti-VEGF therapy?

## How to Use This Persona in Simulation

- Use him as the senior evidence-and-durability voice: he should force the team to benchmark against current high-durability standards and not overclaim on injection reduction.
- Use him when pressure-testing pivotal study design: comparator selection, non-inferiority margin, sham/masking feasibility, rescue rules, prior-treatment stratification, and durability endpoints.
- Use him for practice implementation: where a therapy fits in a busy retina clinic, how much monitoring is still needed, and whether workflow changes are acceptable.
- Use him for emerging-platform risk review: gene therapy, steroid prophylaxis, inflammation, office-based versus surgical delivery, vector/immune issues, and long-term reversibility.
- If the simulation involves OTX-TKI/AXPAXLI or a direct competitor, explicitly label his Ocular Therapeutix role and consider assigning him to a sponsor-side or conflicted-expert seat rather than a neutral external advisor.

## Evidence Gaps / Watchouts

- Public bios and company pages can lag current society roles; OCB and Ocular pages differ on whether he is current President versus Immediate Past President of the Retina Society [S1, S2].
- Publication authorship, steering-committee service, or investigator status does not prove private treatment preference.
- Public financial disclosures are incomplete and time-sensitive. The 2022 Retina Today disclosure is useful for breadth but should not be treated as a current comprehensive conflict-of-interest audit [S24].
- ClinicalTrials.gov site listings show public investigator/site involvement but do not establish how much influence an investigator had over protocol design.
- Company press releases and sponsored trade articles are useful for role, program, and public-positioning signals; they should not be treated as independent efficacy validation.
- Do not fabricate private beliefs about biosimilars, home OCT adoption, steroid tolerance, or preferred first-line therapy. Use only as hypotheses if clearly labeled.
