## Public Role And Practice Setting

Evidence-backed facts:

- Retina Consultants of Texas describes Brown as a medical and surgical retina specialist and clinical trial specialist who has pioneered research and treatment in AMD, diabetic retinopathy, and retinal vein occlusion, with more than 300 scientific papers and abstracts [S1].
- The same practice profile lists his locations as Bellaire, Brenham, Bryan/College Station, Memorial, and Northwest/Cypress, and states that Retina Consultants of Texas is a retina-only ophthalmology practice with four FDA-regulated research centers and the Texas Retina Research Foundation [S1].
- Retina Consultants of Texas and Retina Consultants of America state that Brown is director of the Greater Houston Retina Research Center, clinical professor of Ophthalmology at Baylor College of Medicine, and Vice-Chair for research at the Blanton Eye Institute at Houston Methodist Hospital [S1, S2].
- Retina Consultants of America lists him as Medical Leadership Board Co-Chair and Board Member [S2]. A March 23, 2026 Cencora release identifies him as Chief Medical Officer, RCA, in connection with Cencora's planned acquisition of EyeSouth Partners' retina business [S3].
- RCA materials state that Brown serves on the board of directors of the ASRS and is an elected member of Club Jules Gonin, The Macula Society, and The Retina Society [S2].

Analyst-derived persona implication:

- Brown should be modeled as a hybrid private-practice research leader and national retina-network leader, not as a purely academic investigator. His simulation value is likely highest when assessing whether a wet AMD pipeline asset can be translated into retina-network clinical operations, research workflows, and patient selection.

## Wet AMD Evidence Footprint

Evidence-backed facts:

- MARINA: Brown was a co-author on the 2006 NEJM ranibizumab trial in neovascular AMD. PubMed records a multicenter, 2-year, double-blind, sham-controlled study in 716 patients with minimally classic or occult CNV. The abstract reports that 94.5% and 94.6% of ranibizumab 0.3 mg and 0.5 mg groups lost fewer than 15 letters at 12 months vs 62.2% with sham, and that the benefit was maintained at 24 months [S4].
- ANCHOR: Brown was first author on the 2006 NEJM trial of ranibizumab vs verteporfin for predominantly classic neovascular AMD. PubMed records a 423-patient, 2-year, multicenter, double-blind study. At 12 months, 94.3% and 96.4% of ranibizumab 0.3 mg and 0.5 mg groups lost fewer than 15 letters vs 64.3% with verteporfin; mean visual acuity increased in ranibizumab arms and decreased in the verteporfin arm [S5].
- VIEW 1/VIEW 2: Brown was a named author on the phase 3 aflibercept program comparing aflibercept regimens with monthly ranibizumab in wet AMD. The 2012 PubMed abstract describes 2,419 patients and reports that all aflibercept groups were noninferior and clinically equivalent to monthly ranibizumab for maintaining vision at week 52, with similar anatomic improvements and similar ocular/systemic adverse events [S6].
- VIEW 96-week follow-up: Brown was also an author on the 96-week VIEW publication evaluating second-year variable dosing after first-year fixed dosing [S7].
- HAWK/HARRIER: Brown was a named author on the phase 3 brolucizumab trials in nAMD. PubMed records 1,817 untreated patients and reports brolucizumab noninferiority to aflibercept in BCVA change at week 48, greater than 50% of brolucizumab 6 mg eyes maintained on q12w dosing through week 48, and anatomic outcomes favoring brolucizumab [S8].
- ASRS best-practice role: ASRS/JVRD materials list Brown as an author on "Current Best Clinical Practices - Management of Neovascular AMD" [S9].
- Investigator-initiated recalcitrant wet AMD work: ClinicalTrials.gov identifies NCT01543568/TURF as sponsored by David M. Brown, M.D., with Brown as sponsor-investigator/responsible party through Greater Houston Retina Research, collaborator Regeneron Pharmaceuticals, and actual enrollment of 46 patients [S10]. The corresponding Br J Ophthalmol PubMed abstract lists Wykoff, Brown, Maldonado, and Croft as authors and reports monthly aflibercept in recalcitrant exudative AMD after multiple ranibizumab injections [S11].

Analyst-derived persona implication:

- Brown's public record anchors him in the shift from photodynamic therapy to anti-VEGF, then to extended durability and anatomic-drying claims. In simulation, he should compare any proposed wet AMD pipeline asset against the evidentiary bar set by MARINA/ANCHOR/VIEW and the adoption lessons from brolucizumab.

## Pipeline And Emerging-Therapy Exposure

Evidence-backed facts:

- Gene therapy and sustained anti-VEGF expression: Brown was a co-author on the 2024 Lancet phase 1/2a dose-escalation study of subretinal RGX-314 for nAMD. The abstract states that frequent anti-VEGF injections reduce risk of rapid/severe vision loss but undertreatment leads many patients to lose vision over time, and describes RGX-314/ABBV-RGX-314 as an AAV8 vector expressing an anti-VEGF-A antigen-binding fragment after a single subretinal injection [S12].
- RGX-314 surgical delivery signal: The Lancet abstract describes subretinal injection by wet-laboratory-trained vitreoretinal surgeons and reports that higher doses produced sustained aqueous RGX-314 protein concentrations with stable or improved BCVA and central retinal thickness with few or no supplemental anti-VEGF-A injections in most participants [S12].
- RCTX profile states Brown has developed subretinal gene therapy techniques used for AMD and inherited retinal dystrophies [S1].
- Long-duration anti-VEGF / durability: Brown's HAWK/HARRIER authorship connects him to q12w brolucizumab testing and anatomic superiority claims vs aflibercept in treatment-naive nAMD [S8].
- Recalcitrant/persistent-fluid switching: TURF tested aflibercept in incomplete responders to multiple ranibizumab injections. The PubMed abstract reports stable mean visual acuity, significant CST improvement, and that monthly aflibercept was required in most patients [S11].
- PDGF/combination exposure and mechanism commentary: Houston Methodist Scholars records Brown's 2021 Ophthalmology reply about the CAPELLA anti-PDGFR beta plus aflibercept study. The record states the authors believed CAPELLA supported lack of clinical benefit from anti-PDGFR beta plus intravitreal aflibercept in the general nAMD population, while leaving open possible future subgroup uses as understanding of PDGF improves [S13].
- Disclosed industry relationships: The same Houston Methodist record lists Brown as a consultant to multiple ophthalmology companies including Novartis, Adverum, Regeneron, Bayer, Ocular Therapeutix, Kodiak, Genentech, Annexon, Heidelberg, and Celltrion, with grants from several of those companies outside the submitted work [S13].
- Scientific advisory exposure: Ocular Therapeutix lists David Brown, MD, Chief Medical Officer, Retina Consultants of America, on its Global Scientific Advisory Board [S14].
- Public pipeline discussion: A Retina Consultants of Texas continuing-education post states Brown discussed three wet AMD treatments under research: KSI-301, port delivery system with ranibizumab, and RGX-314 gene therapy [S15].

Analyst-derived persona implication:

- Brown should not be represented as reflexively pro-gene therapy or pro-durability. His public record supports exposure to gene therapy, sustained delivery, and long-interval anti-VEGF programs, but the advisory simulation should make him demand safety, surgical feasibility, retreatment strategy, and evidence of real treatment-burden reduction.

## Treatment-Decision Drivers Likely To Shape Simulation Input

Evidence-backed anchors:

- Visual function remains central: MARINA and ANCHOR used loss of fewer than 15 ETDRS letters as primary endpoint and reported both prevention of loss and meaningful gains [S4, S5].
- Anatomy matters, but not alone: VIEW and HAWK/HARRIER reported anatomic outcomes alongside BCVA and durability endpoints [S6, S8].
- Burden reduction has to be credible: VIEW framed every-2-month aflibercept as potential reduction in injection and monitoring burden vs monthly ranibizumab [S6]; RGX-314 framed single-administration sustained VEGF-A suppression as an approach to reduce treatment burden [S12].
- Recalcitrant disease matters: TURF focused on patients with persistent retinal or subretinal fluid after multiple ranibizumab injections and tested whether aflibercept could maintain prior visual gains and improve anatomy [S10, S11].
- Safety can reshape adoption: HAWK/HARRIER's original abstract reported generally similar safety [S8], but Brown's brolucizumab involvement should be treated in the context of later class-level scrutiny around inflammation/vasculitis when discussing adoption risk. This profile does not attribute a private view to Brown.

Analyst-derived simulation hypotheses:

- Likely to ask for BCVA distribution, not only mean change, especially proportions losing or gaining 15 letters.
- Likely to probe rescue-treatment rules, retreatment thresholds, and whether durability claims survive real-world nonadherence and high-volume clinic operations.
- Likely to scrutinize OCT fluid compartments, central subfield thickness, pigment epithelial detachment response, and the clinical meaning of "drying" if vision is similar.
- Likely to distinguish treatment-naive pivotal-trial evidence from previously treated, persistent-fluid, or high-burden populations.
- Likely to press gene-therapy sponsors on surgical learning curve, vector dose-response, inflammation/pigmentary changes, steroid/prophylaxis needs, and how irreversible or long-lived exposure changes risk tolerance.
- Likely to evaluate network-level feasibility: patient identification, OR/procedure capacity if surgical, chair time if injectable, billing/access, monitoring cadence, and trial-site scalability.

## Likely Questions / Objections In A Simulated Wet AMD Pipeline Advisory Board

Analyst-derived questions grounded in public evidence:

- "What is the visual acuity distribution, not just the mean, and how many patients lose 15 letters or gain 15 letters?"
- "Does the product maintain vision while reducing injections, or does it trade visits for more monitoring, steroids, surgery, or rescue ambiguity?"
- "Which patient is the target: treatment-naive, well-controlled extended patients, persistent-fluid incomplete responders, or high-burden frequent injectors?"
- "How do the OCT and fluid outcomes map to patient-relevant vision outcomes?"
- "What is the rescue algorithm, and would a retina clinic actually follow it?"
- "For gene therapy, what is the surgical technique, dose-related safety pattern, immune/inflammatory monitoring plan, and reversibility strategy?"
- "If anatomy is better but vision is noninferior, what justifies switching from established anti-VEGF options?"
- "How should the sponsor communicate safety after prior wet AMD durability products where adoption was affected by inflammation concerns?"
- "How will this work across a national retina network with varying research infrastructure, patient flow, and payer access?"

## How To Use This Persona In Simulation

- Use Brown as the "pivotal-trial memory" voice. He should benchmark claims against MARINA/ANCHOR/VIEW-level evidence quality and ask whether the new product changes clinical practice beyond a noninferiority label.
- Use him as a surgical and operational reality check for subretinal gene therapy or implanted/sustained delivery approaches.
- Use him to test messages about persistent fluid and difficult-to-dry eyes, because TURF directly examined recalcitrant exudative AMD after multiple ranibizumab injections.
- Use him to challenge overclaiming around durability. He should require the sponsor to specify how many patients maintain extended intervals, what triggers interval shortening or rescue, and what happens after year 1 or year 2.
- Use him as a source of industry-aware skepticism. Public disclosures show broad consulting/grant relationships, so simulation should model familiarity with multiple company pipelines, while avoiding any invented loyalty or preference.

## Evidence Gaps / Watchouts

- Do not infer Brown's current private treatment preferences, preferred first-line agent, biosimilar posture, or payer/access views unless supported by a specific public statement.
- Public sources establish broad industry relationships but do not by themselves establish current advisory-board commitments, active contracts, or product preferences.
- RCTX/RCA biographies are institution/practice-authored and useful for role and scope, but promotional language should be treated as positioning rather than independent validation.
- PubMed records prove authorship and trial evidence, not necessarily Brown's exact operational responsibility within multicenter studies, unless he is first author, sponsor-investigator, or explicitly named in the registry.
- HAWK/HARRIER evidence should be interpreted with post-approval safety context in mind; the cited pivotal paper's abstract alone is not sufficient to characterize later brolucizumab safety debates.
- Complement inhibitors, biosimilars, and value topics were not included as Brown-specific drivers except where public evidence supported broader industry exposure or network-level feasibility. No public source in this pass established a Brown-specific complement or biosimilar position.
