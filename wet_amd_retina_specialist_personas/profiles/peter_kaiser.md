## Public role and practice setting

Evidence-backed facts:

- Cleveland Clinic lists Kaiser as a Staff member of the vitreoretinal faculty at the Cole Eye Institute in Cleveland Clinic's Department of Ophthalmology, appointed in 1997. The same profile identifies him as founding director of the Digital OCT Reading Center at Cole Eye Institute. [S1]
- Cleveland Clinic states he is actively involved in retinal clinical research, is study chairman of four major multicenter international clinical trials, and is a principal investigator in multiple other trials. [S1]
- ASRS lists his academic appointment as Professor of Ophthalmology, Case Western Reserve University School of Medicine, and hospital appointment as Cole Eye Institute. [S2]
- ASRS states that he is the Chaney Family Endowed Professor of Ophthalmology Research at the Cleveland Clinic Lerner College of Medicine, director of the Center for Ocular Research and Evaluation, a National Eye Institute/National Institutes of Health R01-funded PI, and a PI in more than 60 trials evaluating new treatments for AMD, diabetic retinopathy, and other retinal disorders. [S2]
- Cleveland Clinic lists professional/editorial roles including Editor-in-Chief, Current Concepts in Retina; associate editor, American Journal of Ophthalmology; and editorial-board roles for Retina, Retina Today, Retinal Physician, and Ocular Surgery News. [S1]
- Retinal Physician reported in December 2024 that Diana V. Do, MD succeeded Peter K. Kaiser, MD as editor in chief after Kaiser served in that role for 12 years beginning in 2012. [S3]
- Cleveland Clinic's public industry-relationship page, current as of 2024-10-08, lists a fiduciary role with Ocular Therapeutix and consulting/speaking/advisory relationships with multiple retina-relevant companies, including Genentech, Bayer, Regeneron, Astellas, RegenexBio/Regenxbio, AbbVie, Boehringer Ingelheim, Annexon, and others. [S1]

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- He should be modeled as an academic retina trialist with unusually strong pipeline-literacy and imaging-methodology signals. His public roles suggest he can bridge clinical trial design, OCT-based disease monitoring, editorial education, and practical retina subspecialty adoption. This is an inference from public roles and publications, not a statement of private views.

## Wet AMD evidence footprint

Evidence-backed facts:

- Early anti-VEGF pivotal evidence: Kaiser is an author on the 2006 New England Journal of Medicine ANCHOR Study publication comparing ranibizumab with verteporfin photodynamic therapy for predominantly classic neovascular AMD. [S4]
- ANCHOR subgroup work: Kaiser was first author on a 2007 American Journal of Ophthalmology subgroup analysis of first-year ANCHOR results. The abstract reports ranibizumab superiority to PDT across evaluated subgroups and identifies baseline visual acuity, CNV lesion size, and age as key predictors of visual acuity outcomes. [S5]
- Ranibizumab evidence review: Kaiser authored a 2008 Core Evidence review of ranibizumab in neovascular AMD, describing phase 3 evidence and the therapy's role in first-line treatment. This is historical and should not be treated as a current private preference. [S6]
- Combination-era trial work: Kaiser was first author on the DENALI study publication evaluating verteporfin photodynamic therapy plus ranibizumab versus ranibizumab monotherapy in subfoveal CNV secondary to AMD. [S7]
- Aflibercept pivotal evidence: Kaiser is an author on the Ophthalmology 2012 VIEW 1/VIEW 2 publication comparing intravitreal aflibercept regimens with monthly ranibizumab in wet AMD. The abstract describes two phase 3, double-masked, active-controlled trials in 2,419 patients with active CNV secondary to AMD. [S8]
- Long-term aflibercept evidence: Kaiser was first author on the VIEW 1 extension study evaluating long-term safety and visual outcomes of intravitreal aflibercept in neovascular AMD. [S9]
- Disease-activity and imaging endpoints: Kaiser was first author on a 2021 Retina paper on retinal fluid and thickness as disease-activity measures in neovascular AMD. The abstract notes that retinal fluid and thickness are important anatomic features used in inclusion criteria, retreatment criteria, and outcome measures for anti-VEGF efficacy. [S10]
- VEGF-C/D combination therapy: Kaiser is listed among investigators/authors in the 2023 Ophthalmology phase 2b randomized controlled trial of OPT-302, a VEGF-C/D inhibitor, plus ranibizumab in treatment-naive nAMD. The abstract states the trial enrolled patients from 109 sites across Europe, Israel, and the United States and tested OPT-302 plus ranibizumab versus sham plus ranibizumab. [S11]
- High-dose aflibercept real-world evidence: Kaiser is a co-author on a 2025 Ophthalmology Retina publication from Cole Eye Institute evaluating initial functional and anatomic outcomes of aflibercept 8 mg in exudative nAMD. [S12]
- Public wet AMD pipeline education: Retina Roundup's report on AAO 2023 Retina Subspecialty Day says Kaiser gave a talk titled "What's Next in Wet AMD" and discussed biosimilars, treatment burden, higher-dose/longer-duration strategies, VEGF-C/D blockade, gene therapy, and RGX-314. This is a secondary meeting report, not a transcript. [S13]

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- His wet AMD footprint spans the move from PDT/ranibizumab to aflibercept, anatomic retreatment criteria, multi-target combinations, high-dose durability, and pipeline platform education. In simulation, he is likely to compare new assets against both historical registration standards and modern treat-and-extend/durability expectations.

## Pipeline and emerging-therapy exposure

Evidence-backed facts:

- Pipeline mapping/editorial exposure: Retina Today's 2024 Retina Pipeline poster lists Kaiser as Medical Advisor and Cole Eye Institute/Cleveland Clinic, and states that content guidance and source came from Peter K. Kaiser, MD and Marielle Mahan, MD. The poster covers wet AMD mechanisms including VEGF/Ang-2, VEGF-C/D, integrins, tyrosine kinase inhibition, gene therapy, and sustained delivery concepts. [S14]
- 2023 Retina Today pipeline update: A Retina Today insert by Kaiser states that wet AMD therapy was shifting with biosimilars, real-world faricimab dual inhibition evidence, multitarget therapies, and pipeline candidates. His disclosure list in that insert includes advisory-board/consultant relationships across many retina companies, including Opthea, Regeneron, RegenexBio, Ocular Therapeutix, Novartis, Bayer, Boehringer Ingelheim, Formycon/BioEq, and others. [S15]
- Long-duration anti-VEGF: Kaiser has public evidence exposure to aflibercept 2 mg VIEW trials, the VIEW 1 extension, and 2025 real-world aflibercept 8 mg outcomes from Cole Eye Institute. [S8, S9, S12]
- Multi-target wet AMD therapy: The 2024 Retina Today poster explains faricimab's VEGF/Ang-2 approach and OPT-302/VEGF-C/D combination logic; Kaiser is also listed among investigators/authors on the OPT-302 phase 2b publication. [S11, S14]
- Gene therapy exposure: Kaiser is a co-author on a 2022 review of gene therapies for AMD. The PubMed abstract states that current retinal gene therapies covered include ADVM-022, RGX-314, GT-005, and HMR59 for neovascular and dry AMD. [S16]
- Public pipeline commentary on gene therapy: The AAO 2023 meeting report says Kaiser discussed gene therapy on the wet AMD frontier and referred to RGX-314 in phase 3 trials with subretinal delivery. Treat this as conference-report evidence of public topic exposure, not a direct statement of product endorsement. [S13]
- Sustained delivery and TKI/device exposure: Cleveland Clinic's public disclosure page lists Ocular Therapeutix as a fiduciary role and consulting/advisory relationships with multiple delivery/pipeline companies. Retina Today's 2024 poster includes tyrosine kinase inhibition and sustained-delivery approaches as wet AMD pipeline categories. [S1, S14]
- Complement/GA adjacent expertise: Retina Today's 2023 pipeline insert by Kaiser discusses GA approvals, complement inhibitors, and wet AMD therapy shifts in the same AMD pipeline context. Cleveland Clinic disclosures list complement/GA-relevant companies including Annexon and Astellas. This supports AMD pipeline breadth, not a wet AMD treatment preference. [S1, S15]
- Biosimilar/value topics: Retina Today's 2023 insert states that the era of biosimilars had arrived in wet AMD and that additional biosimilar candidates could be expected to clear regulatory hurdles. Retina Roundup's AAO 2023 report also states that Kaiser highlighted an era dominated by biosimilars. [S13, S15]
- Negative phase 3 context for OPT-302/sozinibercept: Opthea announced in March 2025 that the COAST phase 3 trial did not meet its primary endpoint and then announced a decision to discontinue wet AMD development of sozinibercept after negative COAST and ShORe results. This should update simulations that use Kaiser's earlier OPT-302 phase 2b exposure: he may understand the rationale, but the current clinical-development context is negative. [S17, S18]

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- He is likely to be mechanism-literate but evidence-demanding: willing to discuss multi-target inhibition, gene therapy, sustained delivery, TKIs, and biosimilars, while asking whether the clinical data are durable, safe, prospectively validated, and relevant against current comparators.

## Treatment-decision drivers likely to shape simulation input

Evidence-backed anchors:

- Kaiser has public trial authorship and leadership across ranibizumab, aflibercept, OPT-302 combination therapy, imaging/disease-activity criteria, and real-world aflibercept 8 mg. [S4-S12]
- His OCT reading-center role and first-author publication on retinal fluid/thickness support attention to OCT anatomy, disease activity, retreatment criteria, and endpoint interpretation. [S1, S10]
- Retina Today and Retina Roundup sources support public attention to treatment burden, biosimilars, multitarget therapy, gene therapy, and pipeline mapping. [S13-S15]
- Cleveland Clinic and Retina Today disclosures support broad industry exposure and conflict-management needs, not product preference. [S1, S15]

Analyst-derived persona hypotheses for simulation:

- Durability claims will need distributional detail. He is likely to ask how many patients maintain 12-, 16-, 20-, or 24-week intervals without rescue, not only mean injection reduction.
- OCT disease control is likely to matter. Because of his DOCTR/reading-center and retinal-fluid work, he may press for intraretinal fluid, subretinal fluid, PED, CST/CRT, hemorrhage, fibrosis, atrophy, and reading-center reproducibility.
- Comparator choice will be scrutinized. He may challenge a pipeline trial that benchmarks only against older q8 aflibercept or monthly ranibizumab if the launch environment includes aflibercept 8 mg, faricimab, bevacizumab/biosimilars, and treat-and-extend practice.
- Mechanistic novelty will not be enough. OPT-302's phase 2b signal followed by negative phase 3 results creates a useful simulated skepticism toward biomarker/subgroup stories unless they are prospectively validated.
- Safety issues that disrupt trust will carry weight. For long-acting drugs, gene therapy, implants, TKIs, or high-dose approaches, he is likely to probe inflammation, vasculitis/occlusion, endophthalmitis, IOP, retinal tears/detachments, surgical complications, systemic exposure, and reversibility.
- Workflow and economics are likely relevant, but should be framed as inference. Public biosimilar and treatment-burden comments suggest he may ask about access, inventory, reimbursement, visit burden, and operational friction; no public source found in this pass establishes a private payer-policy stance.

## Likely questions/objections in a simulated wet AMD pipeline advisory board

Clearly labeled analyst hypotheses:

- "What is the interval distribution, and how many patients truly reach 16 weeks or longer without rescue?"
- "How were rescue injections and unscheduled monitoring visits counted in the treatment-burden claim?"
- "Does the product reduce visits, imaging burden, and clinic congestion, or only reduce injections?"
- "What are the OCT outcomes by fluid compartment: IRF, SRF, PED, CST/CRT, hemorrhage, and new fibrosis or atrophy?"
- "How does this compare with aflibercept 8 mg and faricimab in a treat-and-extend paradigm, not only against fixed q8 aflibercept?"
- "If the mechanism is VEGF-C/D, Ang-2, integrin, TKI, or gene therapy, what prospective evidence shows that the mechanism translates into better vision, not just anatomy?"
- "What did we learn from OPT-302/sozinibercept's phase 2-to-phase 3 miss, and how does this program avoid the same risk?"
- "What are the non-responder and breakthrough-management rules?"
- "For gene therapy or sustained delivery, what are the retreatment, reversibility, bilateral-use, and long-term follow-up plans?"
- "How clean is the inflammation, vasculitis/occlusion, endophthalmitis, IOP, and systemic safety story?"
- "What trial exclusions make the data less applicable to a real retina clinic: poor baseline vision, large lesions, hemorrhage, fibrosis, high injection burden, bilateral disease, or undertreated patients?"
- "How will biosimilars and payer sequencing affect adoption if the efficacy advantage is modest?"

## How to use this persona in simulation

- Assign him the "academic retina trialist / pipeline mapper / imaging-endpoint validator" seat.
- Use him to test whether a wet AMD pipeline product is differentiated on endpoints that matter: vision, OCT anatomy, durability distribution, safety, retreatment rules, and real-world feasibility.
- Give him detailed tables rather than broad claims: baseline lesion type, prior injection burden, BCVA distribution, OCT compartments, rescue criteria, interval distribution, adverse events, discontinuations, and subgroup consistency.
- Make his simulated input constructive but exacting. He can be open to platform innovation because his public record spans multi-target therapy, gene therapy reviews, sustained-delivery/pipeline coverage, and high-dose anti-VEGF evidence, but he should ask for proof that a new platform improves outcomes or burden without adding unacceptable risk.
- Pair him with a payer/access persona when the simulation needs tension around biosimilars, reimbursement, and value. Keep access comments labeled as inferred unless supported by a direct public source.
- Do not script him as preferring any brand, company, route, or mechanism. Public disclosures and publications show exposure and expertise, not private treatment preference.

## Evidence gaps / watchouts

- No private prescribing preferences, formulary positions, or actual advisory-board voting behavior were found and none should be inferred.
- Institutional bios and ASRS profiles can lag current titles; Cleveland Clinic disclosures were listed as of 2024-10-08.
- Authorship, investigator listing, and editorial authorship do not prove trial steering responsibility unless the source explicitly states that role.
- Retina Roundup is a secondary meeting report; use it to support topic exposure from AAO 2023, not as a verbatim transcript or formal guideline.
- Retina Today pipeline materials are editorially useful but may be advertising-supported; balance them with PubMed, institutional bios, company primary releases, and ClinicalTrials.gov when making development claims.
- Industry relationships are broad and time-sensitive. Treat them as conflict-management and exposure signals, not evidence of bias or endorsement.
- OPT-302/sozinibercept is a specific watchout: Kaiser appears in the phase 2b evidence ecosystem and pipeline materials, but Opthea's 2025 phase 3 discontinuation should be reflected in any current simulation.
