## Public role and practice setting

Evidence-backed facts:

- California Retina Consultants lists Dante J. Pieramici, MD, at its Santa Barbara office and describes him as a retina specialist focused on diseases and surgery of the retina and vitreous [S1].
- The same California Retina Consultants biography states that he is Director of Clinical Research and Partner at California Retina Consultants, Director of the California Retina Research Foundation, a member of the Medical Leadership Board of Retina Consultants of America, author of more than 165 peer-reviewed articles, and a clinical investigator, principal investigator, or reading-center investigator in numerous NIH and company-sponsored clinical trials [S1].
- ASRS lists him as Dante Pieramici, MD, FASRS, affiliated with California Retina Consultants in Santa Barbara, California [S2].
- California Retina Research Foundation describes its mission as improving care for retinal diseases through clinical research and education, and lists retina clinical trials across AMD, diabetic retinopathy, retinal vein occlusion, and other conditions [S3].
- ClinicalTrials.gov records for wet AMD gene-therapy studies list California Retina Consultants as a recruiting study site, supporting an active private-practice research setting for retina pipeline studies [S9].

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- Model him as a private-practice retina research leader rather than a purely academic investigator. His likely advisory value is translating wet AMD pipeline evidence into questions about clinic operations, procedure burden, patient selection, and whether trial-defined durability is usable in a busy retina practice. This is inferred from his CRC/CRRF roles and clinical-trial publication footprint, not from a stated private belief.

## Wet AMD evidence footprint

Evidence-backed facts:

- RGX-314 subretinal gene therapy: Pieramici was a coauthor on the Lancet 2024 phase 1/2a dose-escalation study of ABBV-RGX-314/RGX-314 in previously treated neovascular AMD. The publication describes a subretinal gene-therapy approach intended to provide continuous anti-VEGF Fab expression after a one-time administration [S4].
- PDS with ranibizumab / Archway: Pieramici was a coauthor on the Ophthalmology 2022 Archway phase 3 publication evaluating the Port Delivery System with ranibizumab 100 mg/mL with fixed 24-week refill-exchanges versus monthly intravitreal ranibizumab in nAMD [S5].
- PDS supplemental-treatment evidence: Pieramici was a coauthor on a 2024 Ophthalmology Retina analysis of supplemental intravitreal ranibizumab injections in Archway eyes treated with PDS [S6].
- PDS procedural and safety relevance: Pieramici was first author on a 2022 publication describing PDS implant insertion procedure clinical pearls and was a coauthor on a 2022 Ophthalmology Retina paper on management of key ocular adverse events in PDS-implanted patients [S7, S8].
- 4D-150 PRISM wet AMD trial: ClinicalTrials.gov lists PRISM as a phase 1/2 study of intravitreal 4D-150 in adults with wet AMD, with active anti-VEGF treatment before enrollment and supplemental aflibercept criteria in the study design. The record lists California Retina Consultants in Oxnard with Dante Pieramici, MD, as principal investigator [S9].
- 4DMT identified Pieramici as a clinical investigator and advisor when announcing positive interim PRISM data for 4D-150 in wet AMD. The company release includes his public comments on durability, injection burden, safety, and moving toward phase 3 development [S10].
- Public ophthalmology trade coverage has associated Pieramici with 4D-150 PRISM wet AMD data presentations and discussion of durability and injection burden [S11].

Guideline/society/editorial roles:

- ASRS membership/profile evidence supports professional society standing as an FASRS retina specialist, but the sources reviewed did not identify a current formal wet AMD guideline-author role for him [S2].
- No source reviewed showed that he held a current AAO or ASRS wet AMD guideline chair role. Treat him as a trialist/KOL persona, not as a guideline-author persona unless new evidence is added.

## Treatment-decision drivers likely to shape simulation input

Evidence-backed anchors:

- Public evidence ties him to durability-focused wet AMD development: subretinal gene therapy, intravitreal gene therapy, and refillable PDS delivery [S4, S5, S6, S7, S9, S10].
- PRISM evidence supports interest in intravitreal gene therapy that may reduce ongoing anti-VEGF injection burden, while still requiring supplemental anti-VEGF rules and long-term follow-up [S9, S10].
- Archway and PDS procedural/adverse-event publications support familiarity with implant/refill procedures and device-related safety considerations [S5, S6, S7, S8].
- CRC/CRRF role supports practical sensitivity to private-practice trial operations, recruitment, protocol feasibility, and study-site execution [S1, S3].

Analyst-derived persona hypotheses for simulation:

- Durability claims will need patient-level clarity. He is likely to ask how many patients are injection-free, rescue-free, or maintain extended intervals, not just mean injection reductions.
- Safety will be a gating issue for gene therapy and implant delivery. He is likely to probe intraocular inflammation, surgical/procedural complications, steroid prophylaxis, retinal vasculitis/occlusion signals, endophthalmitis, device events, and long-term surveillance.
- Rescue criteria will matter. He may challenge whether supplemental anti-VEGF rules match real-world retina decision-making and whether undertreatment risk is being hidden by protocol definitions.
- Patient selection will be central. He may focus on high-burden but anti-VEGF-responsive patients, treatment-experienced versus treatment-naive populations, bilateral disease, patients with persistent fluid, and patients unlikely to tolerate procedural or long-term monitoring complexity.
- Reversibility and retreatment are likely objections. For one-time or long-acting platforms, he is likely to ask what happens in nonresponders, partial responders, inflammatory events, or patients who need future therapy in the same eye.
- He may be open to ambitious durability platforms if the safety, rescue, and operational story is credible. This is an inference from his publication and trial exposure, not evidence of a private treatment preference.

## Likely questions/objections in a simulated wet AMD pipeline advisory board

Clearly labeled analyst hypotheses:

- "What proportion of patients are truly rescue-free at 6, 12, and 24 months, and how many still need frequent supplemental injections?"
- "How were rescue injections triggered, and would a retina specialist in practice have treated earlier?"
- "What is the inflammation profile by dose, steroid regimen, and time from administration?"
- "For gene therapy, what is the plan for nonresponders, waning effect, bilateral treatment, and patients who later need a different anti-VEGF approach?"
- "For an implant or depot platform, what are the procedure requirements, refill logistics, endophthalmitis/device risks, and implications for high-volume clinic flow?"
- "Which patients should not receive this therapy, even if they are high-burden wet AMD patients?"
- "Is the comparator current enough for 2026 practice, including faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, biosimilar pressure, and treat-and-extend expectations?"
- "Can the sponsor show a clinically meaningful reduction in visits and injections, not only a favorable protocol endpoint?"

## How to use this persona in simulation

- Seat him as a senior private-practice retina clinical investigator with direct exposure to wet AMD durability platforms.
- Give him questions that connect trial data to clinic implementation: procedure burden, rescue criteria, monitoring cadence, adverse-event management, patient counseling, and real-world scheduling.
- Use him to pressure-test gene-therapy and sustained-delivery claims. He should not merely ask whether the product works; he should ask which patients benefit enough to justify irreversible or procedural complexity.
- Keep his tone evidence-oriented and practical. He can be innovation-aware without being scripted as an unquestioning gene-therapy advocate.
- Do not assign him a private brand preference or prescribing hierarchy. Public evidence supports trial involvement and advisory relevance, not undisclosed treatment preferences.
