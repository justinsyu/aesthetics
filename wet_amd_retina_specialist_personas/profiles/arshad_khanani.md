## Public role and practice setting

Evidence-backed facts:

- ASRS lists Arshad M. Khanani, MD, MA, FASRS at Sierra Eye Associates, Reno, Nevada, as Director of Clinical Research and lists an academic appointment as Clinical Professor, University of Nevada, Reno School of Medicine [S1].
- Sierra Eye Associates states that its clinical-trials department has grown under the direction of founding partner Arshad Khanani, MD, MA, and describes the site as a nationally recognized clinical research site with active studies in AMD, diabetic retinopathy, and retinal vein occlusion [S2].
- PubMed affiliations for his wet AMD publications repeatedly connect him to Sierra Eye Associates and University of Nevada, Reno School of Medicine [S3, S4, S5, S6].
- ASRS listed Khanani as Meeting Chair for Clinical Trials at the Summit 2023; the meeting description says it brings experts together to discuss ongoing trials, latest data, and clinical-trial strategy [S7].
- Retina Today described Clinical Trials at the Summit as the brainchild of Khanani and Charles Wykoff and as a conference focused on clinical-research logistics and trials for new treatments, devices, delivery approaches, and gene therapy [S8].
- In a 2024 Modern Retina interview, Khanani identified himself as Director of Clinical Research at Sierra Eye Associates and program committee co-chair for CTS 2024. He described the meeting as an open forum for development stakeholders, including retina KOLs, industry executives, investors, and others involved in new-drug development [S9].

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- Model him as a private-practice clinical-trials operator, not as a purely academic laboratory scientist. His likely simulation value is translating sponsor data into questions about study execution, real-world clinic flow, patient selection, and whether a durability claim will survive routine retina practice. This is inferred from his Sierra Eye/CTS/clinical-trial footprint, not a stated private belief.

## Wet AMD evidence footprint

Evidence-backed facts:

- Ixo-vec/OPTIC: Khanani was first author on the EClinicalMedicine 2024 OPTIC phase 1 publication evaluating intravitreal ixoberogene soroparvovec in nAMD. The study enrolled anti-VEGF-responsive nAMD patients, evaluated safety as the primary endpoint, and reported stable vision/anatomy with large reductions in annualized anti-VEGF injections over two years; inflammation was responsive to topical corticosteroids in the reported cohorts [S3].
- Ixo-vec/LUNA: ClinicalTrials.gov lists LUNA as a randomized, double-masked phase 2 study of ADVM-022/ixo-vec in anti-VEGF treatment-experienced nAMD, with two ixo-vec doses and corticosteroid prophylaxis regimens; enrollment is listed as 69 actual participants and status active, not recruiting [S10]. Adverum publicly identified Khanani as principal investigator in LUNA and as having more than five years of investigator experience across multiple ixo-vec wet AMD trials [S11].
- Faricimab/TENAYA-LUCERNE: Khanani was first author on the Ophthalmology 2024 two-year TENAYA/LUCERNE phase 3 nAMD publication. The trials compared faricimab up to Q16W with aflibercept Q8W and reported comparable BCVA change through two years, with many faricimab patients on Q12W or longer intervals at week 112 [S4].
- Faricimab anatomy/PED: Khanani was a co-author on a 2026 TENAYA/LUCERNE post hoc analysis of PED outcomes with faricimab versus aflibercept during head-to-head dosing in nAMD [S12].
- PDS with ranibizumab/Ladder: Khanani was first author on the phase 2 Ladder end-of-study report for the Port Delivery System with ranibizumab in nAMD. The study compared PDS ranibizumab formulations with monthly ranibizumab and reported comparable vision/anatomic outcomes for the 100 mg/ml PDS arm with fewer ranibizumab treatments [S5].
- PED management: Khanani was first author on a Retina 2018 review of PED management in nAMD, which concluded anti-VEGF treatment should focus on vision gains rather than PED resolution because anatomic PED improvement often does not correlate with functional improvement [S6].
- Treat-and-extend real-world outcomes: Khanani was first author on a single-center retrospective five-year nAMD series using a treat-and-extend anti-VEGF regimen in eyes with baseline vision 20/60 or better [S13].
- Society/meeting footprint: ASRS annual-meeting materials list Khanani as a wet AMD symposium panelist in 2025, and ASRS wet AMD symposium materials include ixo-vec OPTIC/LUNA content associated with Khanani [S14, S15].

## Pipeline and emerging-therapy exposure

Evidence-backed facts:

- Intravitreal gene therapy: Ixo-vec/OPTIC and LUNA directly support wet AMD gene-therapy exposure [S3, S10, S11]. Adverum's ARTEMIS ClinicalTrials.gov record describes a phase 3, randomized, double-masked, active-comparator-controlled study of a single intravitreal ixo-vec injection versus aflibercept in treatment-naive and treatment-experienced nAMD, with status active, not recruiting as of the May 2026 record [S16]. This ARTEMIS source is included as current ixo-vec program context, not as direct proof of Khanani's ARTEMIS investigator role.
- 4D-150: ClinicalTrials.gov lists PRISM (NCT05197270) as a recruiting phase 1/2 study of intravitreal 4D-150 gene therapy in adults with wet AMD undergoing active anti-VEGF treatment; the Sierra Eye Associates Reno site lists Arshad Khanani, MD, MA, as principal investigator [S17]. 4DMT also described him as lead principal investigator in PRISM and quoted him on treatment burden, single-injection potential, and phase 3 contribution [S18].
- Broader gene-therapy discussion: Eyetube's 2025 EURETINA coverage lists Khanani discussing wet AMD gene-therapy advances including sura-vec, ixo-vec, and 4D-150 [S19]. Treat this as a public education/meeting-coverage signal, not peer-reviewed efficacy evidence.
- Sustained delivery: Ladder PDS first authorship supports exposure to implant/sustained-delivery development and refill-criteria tradeoffs in nAMD [S5].
- Long-duration anti-VEGF / bispecific durability: TENAYA/LUCERNE first authorship and PED analyses support direct involvement in faricimab durability and anatomy discussions [S4, S12].
- TKI depot / AXPAXLI: Ophthalmology Times reported that Khanani was Director of Clinical Research at Sierra Eye Associates and Steering Committee Chair for Ocular Therapeutix's SOL Program when commenting on SOL-1 wet AMD topline results [S20]. Ocular's February 17, 2026 release reported SOL-1 phase 3 wet AMD results for AXPAXLI versus aflibercept, including Week 36 and Week 52 vision-maintenance and rescue-free endpoints [S21]. Ocular's May 5, 2026 business update states SOL-1 was conducted under an FDA Special Protocol Assessment, that SOL-R remains on track for 1Q 2027 topline data, and that an AXPAXLI NDA submission remains planned subject to FDA discussions [S22].
- Complement/GA overlap: Khanani was first author on the GATHER2 two-year avacincaptad pegol phase 3 GA publication, a complement C5 inhibitor study in dry AMD/GA rather than wet AMD [S23]. Use this only when a wet AMD simulation touches fellow-eye GA, dual wet/GA management, or complement safety, not as evidence of a wet AMD complement preference.
- Biosimilar/value context: Khanani is a co-author on a 2026 PubMed-indexed aflibercept biosimilar safety publication for Pavblu/aflibercept-ayyh [S24]. FDA's aflibercept biosimilar approvals for wet AMD provide market/access context [S25]. These sources support familiarity with biosimilar/value topics, not a stated payer or prescribing preference.

## Treatment-decision drivers likely to shape simulation input

Evidence-backed anchors:

- His wet AMD public record emphasizes durability platforms: intravitreal gene therapy, PDS/sustained delivery, faricimab extended dosing, 4D-150, and AXPAXLI/OTX-TKI [S3, S4, S5, S17, S18, S20, S21].
- His OPTIC/LUNA and 4D-150 evidence base repeatedly includes inflammation, corticosteroid prophylaxis, vector dose, supplemental anti-VEGF injections, and long follow-up as key development issues [S3, S10, S17, S18].
- His PDS, faricimab, PED, and treat-and-extend publications support attention to BCVA, CST/fluid, PED response, treatment intervals, and the mismatch between anatomy and function [S4, S5, S6, S12, S13].
- His CTS role supports interest in trial design, regulatory guidance, stakeholder alignment, and practical clinical-trial execution [S7, S8, S9].
- His AXPAXLI/SOL role supports attention to rescue criteria, superiority/noninferiority design, anatomic stability, and FDA-aligned endpoints [S20, S21, S22].

Analyst-derived persona hypotheses for simulation:

- Durability must be operationally meaningful. He is likely to ask whether the product reduces both injection burden and unpredictable urgent visits, not only average dosing interval.
- Safety management will be central for gene therapy and depot products. He is likely to probe intraocular inflammation, steroid regimen burden, retinal vasculitis/occlusion, hypotony, floater/drug-particle issues, pressure effects, endophthalmitis, implant/device events, and long-term follow-up.
- Rescue criteria matter. He is likely to challenge sponsors on how supplemental anti-VEGF was triggered, counted, and communicated, especially if rescue thresholds differ from treat-and-extend practice.
- Patient selection is likely to be a recurring theme. He may focus on anti-VEGF responders, high-burden patients, treatment-naive versus previously treated patients, persistent fluid, PED phenotype, bilateral disease, fellow-eye strategy, and patients at risk of undertreatment.
- Modern comparators are expected. He is likely to benchmark new wet AMD products against faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, aflibercept biosimilars/access dynamics, gene-therapy competitors, and possible TKI depot entrants, rather than against monthly ranibizumab-era expectations alone.
- He may be receptive to ambitious platforms if the benefit-risk profile is clear. Gene-therapy and sustained-delivery exposure suggests openness to innovation, but likely with detailed scrutiny of reversibility, re-dosing, steroid taper, nonresponder management, bilateral use, and how long safety surveillance must continue.

## Likely questions/objections in a simulated wet AMD pipeline advisory board

Clearly labeled analyst hypotheses:

- "What percentage of patients are truly injection-free or rescue-free at each timepoint, and what is the tail of the durability curve?"
- "How do the rescue criteria compare with what retina specialists would actually do in treat-and-extend practice?"
- "Does this reduce total visits and OCT monitoring, or just injections?"
- "What happens to patients with persistent intraretinal fluid, large PEDs, poor drying, bilateral disease, or high pretrial injection burden?"
- "For gene therapy, what steroid regimen is required, how often does inflammation occur after taper, and what is the long-term plan for re-dosing or second-eye treatment?"
- "For depot or TKI approaches, what are the procedure, floater, migration, pressure, endophthalmitis, and rescue-management implications?"
- "Is the endpoint aligned with FDA expectations and with what clinicians/payers consider meaningful?"
- "How should we compare this to faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, biosimilar aflibercept, and emerging gene-therapy competitors?"
- "Are anatomic gains translating into vision, or are we over-reading fluid and PED changes?"
- "What trial population should be used to avoid a clean but commercially narrow result?"

## How to use this persona in simulation

- Assign him the "pipeline-forward private-practice trialist" seat.
- Use him to pressure-test whether a wet AMD asset can move from promising trial data to repeatable clinical adoption in busy US retina practices.
- Let him ask detailed questions about steroid prophylaxis, rescue injections, endpoint hierarchy, patient selection, bilateral treatment, long-term safety, and sponsor-regulator alignment.
- Pair him with a payer/access persona when discussing biosimilars, prior authorization, or product sequencing; his own public record supports biosimilar and clinical-trial exposure but not a specific access-policy stance.
- Pair him with a safety skeptic for gene therapy or device/depot discussions; his public involvement gives him enough familiarity to ask technical questions rather than broad objections.
- Do not portray him as privately favoring any branded therapy. His record shows broad sponsored-trial and publication exposure across competing platforms.

## Evidence gaps / watchouts

- Public bios, company releases, and meeting coverage may be promotional, selective, or lag current titles.
- Trial authorship, PI status, steering roles, and quoted company comments show public involvement and expertise; they do not prove private prescribing preference or advisory-board voting behavior.
- Company-reported pipeline results should be treated as sponsor communications until peer-reviewed and/or regulator-reviewed.
- OPTIC/LUNA/PRISM gene-therapy data create strong exposure signals, but long-term safety, bilateral use, re-dosing, and real-world steroid management remain watchout areas for simulation.
- Complement evidence is GA-focused, not wet AMD-focused. Use it only for wet AMD/GA overlap.
- Biosimilar evidence supports value/access awareness but not a stated payer-policy position.
- Industry relationships are likely broad given his trial footprint, but this profile did not conduct a full Open Payments or meeting-specific disclosure audit.
