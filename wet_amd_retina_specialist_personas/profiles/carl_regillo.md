## Public role and practice setting

Evidence-backed facts:

- Wills Eye lists Carl D. Regillo, MD, FACS, FASRS as Chief/Director of the Retina Service at Wills Eye Hospital and Professor of Ophthalmology at Thomas Jefferson University.
- His Wills Eye bio says he joined Mid Atlantic Retina and the Retina Service of Wills Eye Hospital in 1998.
- The same bio describes him as principal investigator for numerous major international clinical trials investigating new treatments for macular degeneration, diabetic retinopathy, and other retinal conditions.
- Wills Eye also identifies him as founder and former Director of the Wills Eye Clinical Retina Research Unit, prior Chairman of the Wills Eye Institutional Review Board, prior Chairman of the American Academy of Ophthalmology Basic and Clinical Science Course Retina Section committee, and former Wills Retina Fellowship Program Director.
- Wills Eye states he has authored more than 200 scientific papers, published 9 major textbooks, served on multiple ophthalmology editorial boards, and received AAO and ASRS awards.

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- He is likely to frame advisory-board discussion from the perspective of an academic clinical trialist embedded in a busy tertiary/practice retina environment. That suggests emphasis on whether a new wet AMD option is not only statistically noninferior or durable, but also usable in real-world retina clinics with older patients, bilateral disease, comorbidities, staff constraints, prior-treatment heterogeneity, and monitoring burden.

## Wet AMD evidence footprint

Evidence-backed facts:

- Sustained delivery / PDS: Wills Eye reported that, after FDA approval of Genentech's Susvimo for neovascular or "wet" AMD, Regillo "played a key role" as principal investigator for both LADDER phase 2 and ARCHWAY phase 3 studies. The same Wills Eye article describes Susvimo as a permanent refillable eye implant for sustained ranibizumab delivery.
- Archway: PubMed lists Regillo as first author on the 2-year Archway phase 3 publication of the Port Delivery System with ranibizumab for nAMD. The PubMed abstract states the purpose was to report 2-year Archway results.
- Archway design: The earlier Archway publication is a randomized phase 3 PDS with ranibizumab nAMD study; aggregate sources in the project already cite the study as an open-label randomized trial comparing PDS refill exchanges every 24 weeks with monthly ranibizumab.
- Faricimab: PubMed lists Regillo as an author on the Lancet 2022 TENAYA/LUCERNE primary-results paper. The abstract describes two randomized, double-masked, phase 3 noninferiority trials across 271 sites in treatment-naive nAMD, comparing faricimab up to every 16 weeks against aflibercept every 8 weeks.
- Faricimab year 2: PubMed lists Regillo among investigators/authors for the TENAYA/LUCERNE 2-year paper. The abstract reports comparable 2-year BCVA change between faricimab and aflibercept groups and that most faricimab-treated patients achieved extended dosing intervals at week 112.
- Treat-and-extend aflibercept: PubMed lists Regillo as an author on a prospective clinical trial of treat-and-extend aflibercept for neovascular AMD from Wills Eye/Mid Atlantic Retina.
- Earlier anti-VEGF evidence: A Houston Methodist record for the PIER study lists Regillo on a phase IIIb randomized, double-masked, sham-controlled trial evaluating ranibizumab monthly for three months and then quarterly in subfoveal CNV secondary to AMD.
- Patient/society education: The American Society of Retina Specialists AMD patient page includes Carl D. Regillo, MD, FACS among page contributors/authors, supporting a public education role in AMD.

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- Because his public record spans early anti-VEGF dosing studies, treat-and-extend, faricimab durability, and PDS/Susvimo, he is likely to compare any wet AMD pipeline product against several reference points: visual acuity maintenance, anatomy/fluid control, injection reduction, visit reduction, rescue criteria, and safety over multiple years.

## Pipeline and emerging-therapy exposure

Evidence-backed facts:

- Sustained delivery / device-enabled anti-VEGF: Wills Eye explicitly links Regillo to LADDER and ARCHWAY PDS/Susvimo studies in nAMD, and his public quote in that article emphasizes sustained release, reduced treatment burden, and maintaining vision gains.
- Long-duration anti-VEGF / dual-pathway inhibition: Regillo is listed on TENAYA/LUCERNE faricimab publications evaluating faricimab durability up to every 16 weeks and treat-and-extend dosing through year 2.
- Gene therapy: An Adverum OPTIC wet AMD clinical-data presentation acknowledges Carl Regillo among investigators/study teams for ADVM-022, supporting exposure to intravitreal anti-VEGF gene therapy development. A PubMed-listed Lancet 2024 RGX-314 subretinal gene-therapy paper in nAMD identifies the therapy as an AAV8 vector expressing an anti-VEGF-A Fab; Regillo appears in the broader public disclosure ecosystem as consultant/grant recipient for Regenxbio, but this specific PubMed abstract should not be used alone to claim he led RGX-314 unless the full author/investigator list confirms it.
- TKI / bioerodible insert exposure: A 2024 EyePoint DAVIO-2 slide deck names Regillo in the context of wet AMD TKI insert work and lists relevant financial disclosures, supporting exposure to sustained delivery via small-molecule insert platforms.
- Complement / GA adjacent topics: Regillo is first author/corresponding author on a PMC article giving expert-panel recommendations for GA identification and management; the article notes practical scenarios such as concomitant nAMD therapy when considering GA treatments. This is adjacent, not direct wet AMD efficacy evidence.
- Biosimilar/value context: FDA announced approval of Yesafili and Opuviz as interchangeable aflibercept biosimilars to Eylea for wet AMD and other retinal indications, and notes biosimilars may cost less than reference products. No source found in this pass directly attributes a public Regillo position on aflibercept biosimilar use, so any value/biosimilar view should be treated as a discussion probe, not a persona belief.
- Industry relationships: A Retina Today article lists Regillo as consultant to multiple retina companies including 4DMT, Adverum, Genentech/Roche, Opthea, Regeneron, Regenxbio, and others, and grant support from several companies including 4DMT, Adverum, Genentech/Roche, Opthea, Regeneron, and Regenxbio. These are evidence of research/consulting exposure, not proof of product preference.

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- He is likely fluent across the current durability landscape: high-dose/longer-interval injections, Ang-2/VEGF dual inhibition, refillable implants, intravitreal and subretinal gene therapy, and bioerodible inserts. His likely differentiation lens is not "durability at all costs" but "durability with acceptable ocular safety, manageable follow-up, credible rescue, and a practical route to adoption."

## Treatment-decision drivers likely to shape simulation input

Evidence-backed anchors:

- Public statements around Susvimo emphasize reducing treatment burden while maintaining vision gains and delivering an effective anti-VEGF in sustained-release fashion.
- PDS/Susvimo work inherently raises device/procedure issues, refill logistics, ocular surgery risk, and endophthalmitis risk; these are central to public PDS discussions and labeling/trade coverage.
- TENAYA/LUCERNE and treat-and-extend work support attention to dosing interval extension, BCVA noninferiority, anatomy, disease-activity criteria, and long-term safety.
- GA expert-panel work supports attention to practical patient scenarios, including patients who may have both GA and nAMD treatment needs.

Analyst-derived hypotheses for simulation:

- Likely driver: "Does the product reduce total patient and clinic burden, or only injection count?" He may ask about visit frequency, imaging frequency, refill/procedure time, monitoring between doses, and rescue protocols.
- Likely driver: "What is the durability responder profile?" He may want baseline predictors, fluid compartments, lesion subtype, prior treatment history, and how to identify patients who can safely extend.
- Likely driver: "How clean is the safety story?" He may press on intraocular inflammation, vasculitis, endophthalmitis, implant/procedure complications, pressure events, retinal tears/detachments, and systemic vascular safety.
- Likely driver: "What happens after failure or breakthrough?" He may ask whether rescue anti-VEGF works, whether retreatment compromises future options, and how quickly anatomy/vision recover.
- Likely driver: "Is the endpoint clinically meaningful?" He may challenge surrogate-heavy or anatomy-only claims if visual acuity, durability distribution, or patient-relevant burden reduction is weak.
- Likely driver: "Can a retina practice operationalize it?" He may test training needs, OR/procedure-room requirements, inventory, reimbursement, bilateral treatment strategy, and scheduling.

## Likely questions/objections in a simulated wet AMD pipeline advisory board

- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written question: "What proportion of patients achieve 12-, 16-, 20-, or 24-week control without extra visits or rescue, and what were the exact rescue criteria?"
- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written question: "How does visual acuity hold up in the patients who need interval shortening or supplemental treatment?"
- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written question: "What is the anatomic profile: central subfield thickness, intraretinal fluid, subretinal fluid, PED, hemorrhage, and new fibrosis or atrophy signals?"
- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written question: "How does the safety profile compare with repeated intravitreal injections and with other durability platforms such as faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, Susvimo/PDS, gene therapy, and TKI inserts?"
- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written question: "If this is a device, implant, gene therapy, or procedural product, what is the learning curve and how many patients would a practice realistically treat in the first year?"
- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written question: "For previously treated patients, how high was the pre-enrollment injection burden, and did the product reduce both injections and visits?"
- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written question: "What are the exclusion criteria that make the pivotal data less applicable to the real-world wet AMD population?"
- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written objection: "A mean interval or mean injection reduction can hide a meaningful non-responder tail; show the distribution and rescue outcomes."
- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written objection: "A marginal durability gain may not justify new safety concerns, new procedures, or reimbursement friction."
- Evidence-backed topic, analyst-written objection: "For a one-time or long-acting therapy, reversibility and post-failure management are as important as the initial durability claim."

## How to use this persona in simulation

- Use him as a senior retina clinical-trialist and implementation realist.
- Give him high influence on trial-design critique, endpoint interpretation, safety thresholds, durability claims, and adoption feasibility.
- Prompt him with concrete trial tables rather than broad mechanism claims: baseline disease severity, prior injection history, treatment intervals, rescue rules, BCVA distribution, OCT outcomes, adverse events, and discontinuations.
- Let him pressure-test whether a pipeline therapy is differentiated against current options: aflibercept 8 mg, faricimab, PDS/Susvimo, biosimilars where relevant, and emerging gene therapy/TKI platforms.
- Keep his simulated comments evidence-grounded. Do not assert that he "prefers" a brand, company, mechanism, or route unless a public source explicitly supports that statement.
- Useful simulated role: "asks the questions that determine whether impressive durability data can become a repeatable clinic protocol."

## Evidence gaps / watchouts

- No private beliefs, actual prescribing preferences, formulary positions, or advisory-board opinions were found or should be inferred.
- Disclosures show broad industry engagement; they should be used to understand exposure and possible familiarity, not to imply bias or endorsement.
- PubMed author lists and investigator acknowledgments do not always distinguish steering committee, site PI, author, consultant, and presenting investigator roles. Use precise labels.
- Gene therapy exposure is supported for ADVM-022 OPTIC via investigator acknowledgment; do not overstate as leadership without additional public confirmation.
- Complement/GA work is relevant to AMD comorbidity and retinal pipeline literacy, but it is not direct wet AMD treatment-preference evidence.
- Biosimilar/value topics are market-relevant, but no direct public Regillo position was identified in this pass.
- Some trade-publication PDFs and conference materials may be sponsor-supported; they are useful for disclosures and public presentation topics but should be balanced against PubMed, institutional, FDA, and ClinicalTrials.gov evidence.
