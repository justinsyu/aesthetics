## Wet AMD evidence footprint: pivotal trials/publications, guideline/society/editorial roles if relevant, trial leadership

Evidence-backed facts:

- HARBOR ranibizumab: Ho was first author on the 24-month HARBOR publication evaluating 0.5 mg or 2.0 mg ranibizumab in subfoveal neovascular AMD. PubMed lists his affiliation as Mid Atlantic Retina, Wills Eye Hospital [S4].
- RGX-314 subretinal gene therapy: Ho was a co-author on the Lancet 2024 phase 1/2a dose-escalation study of subretinal RGX-314 gene therapy for nAMD. The PubMed abstract describes an AAV8 vector expressing an anti-VEGF-A antigen-binding fragment, enrolled previously treated nAMD patients across eight US sites, and reported 2-year safety, BCVA, anatomy, protein-expression, and supplemental anti-VEGF outcomes [S5].
- RGX-314 public presentation leadership: A Regenxbio Retina Society 2021 deck is titled "Subretinal Delivery of RGX-314 for Neovascular AMD: End of Phase I/IIa Study Results" and names Allen Ho, MD, FACS as presenter. The deck describes 42 dosed patients, five dose cohorts, safety/tolerability, BCVA, CRT, rescue anti-VEGF use, and pivotal-program implications [S6].
- ONYX nesvacumab plus aflibercept: A PMC-indexed phase 2 ONYX randomized trial lists Allen C. Ho as an author and evaluates intravitreal nesvacumab, an anti-Ang-2 antibody, plus aflibercept in neovascular AMD [S7].
- Faricimab real-world evidence: A Wills Eye/Mid Atlantic Retina retrospective series in Ophthalmology Retina lists Allen C. Ho as a co-author and assessed anatomic and functional outcomes after at least four faricimab injections in previously treated nAMD [S8].
- Early detection/good baseline vision: PubMed lists Ho as first author on "The Potential Importance of Detection of Neovascular Age-Related Macular Degeneration When Visual Acuity Is Relatively Good," supporting interest in early detection, baseline vision, and visual prognosis in nvAMD [S9].
- Prophylactic/fellow-eye AMD context: PubMed lists a multicenter randomized clinical trial of quarterly ranibizumab versus sham to prevent conversion to nAMD in vulnerable fellow eyes with intermediate AMD and contralateral nAMD. The abstract concluded quarterly ranibizumab did not reduce 24-month conversion versus sham in that study [S10]. This is not a Ho-authored paper in the PubMed record reviewed here, but it is relevant context for advisory-board discussion of prevention, fellow-eye risk, and conversion endpoints.
- Refractory/PED-related practice evidence: Wills Eye's Farber research-faculty publication list includes a Ho co-authored report on alternating biweekly ranibizumab and bevacizumab for refractory nAMD with pigment epithelial detachment, supporting public exposure to difficult-to-dry/refractory nAMD scenarios [S11].
- Society/editorial role relevant to wet AMD: Wills Eye lists him as Retina Society Past President, active in ASRS/Macula Society/AAO, Editor-in-Chief of Current Opinion in Ophthalmology, and Chief Medical Editor of Retina Today [S1]. These roles support broad visibility into wet AMD trial data and emerging treatment debates, though they do not establish a specific treatment preference.

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- His wet AMD evidence footprint suggests he will evaluate new therapies against several eras of nAMD evidence: fixed/monthly ranibizumab, higher-dose anti-VEGF exploration, disease-activity/rescue criteria, dual-pathway Ang-2/VEGF approaches, real-world switching to faricimab, and gene therapy designed to reduce injection burden. He is likely to ask whether an asset improves the tradeoff among vision, anatomy, durability, safety, and clinic workflow.

## Pipeline and emerging-therapy exposure

Evidence-backed facts:

- Gene therapy, subretinal anti-VEGF expression: Ho's RGX-314 phase 1/2a publication and Retina Society presentation directly support exposure to wet AMD gene therapy, subretinal surgical delivery, dose escalation, protein expression, rescue anti-VEGF criteria, and long-term safety monitoring [S5, S6].
- Gene therapy, broader retina commercialization/advisory exposure: Wills Eye reported in 2025 that Ho was named to Nanoscope Therapeutics' Visionary Advisory Committee, described as a group focused on retina treatment and commercialization for patients with severe vision loss from retinal degenerative diseases. This is not wet AMD-specific, but it supports broader retina advanced-therapy/commercialization exposure [S12].
- Anti-Ang-2 pathway: The ONYX trial supports Ho exposure to Ang-2 biology in nAMD through nesvacumab plus aflibercept, distinct from but mechanistically adjacent to faricimab's Ang-2/VEGF dual inhibition [S7].
- Faricimab/long-duration anti-VEGF switching: The Wills Eye/Mid Atlantic faricimab series supports exposure to real-world outcomes in previously treated nAMD patients after switching to faricimab, including CFT, fibrovascular PED height, and VA endpoints [S8].
- Aflibercept 8 mg / longer-duration anti-VEGF: A 2023 Insight article quoted Ho on FDA clearance of Eylea HD, identifying him as Wills Eye Director of Retina Research and Co-Director of Retina Service. The article states the approval was based on PULSAR and PHOTON pivotal trials and quotes him as saying the option builds from Eylea's established efficacy and safety profile [S13]. This is a trade-publication quote, not a peer-reviewed publication or proof of prescribing preference.
- Sustained delivery / device and procedural delivery: Wills Eye's bio states he serves on the FDA Ophthalmic Devices Committee and on surgical-device/pharmaceutical scientific advisory boards pursuing macular and vitreoretinal treatments [S1]. His RGX-314 work also required subretinal surgical delivery, making route, device, and procedure feasibility directly relevant [S5, S6].
- Industry relationship breadth: The RGX-314 Retina Society deck discloses Ho relationships including consultant/advisor and/or grant support with Adverum, Alcon, Apellis, Genentech, Gyroscope, Iveric, Janssen/J&J, Lineage, MeiraGTx, Notal, Ocular Therapeutix, Regeneron, Regenxbio, and others, plus equity/stock owner disclosures for several companies [S6]. An SEC-filed Regenxbio exhibit from 2019 similarly lists broad grants, scientific-advisor roles, board role at Ocular Therapeutix, and equity disclosures [S14]. Use these as public exposure/disclosure signals, not as evidence of endorsement.
- Complement/GA adjacent exposure: Public disclosures and Wills areas of interest include AMD and companies active in GA/complement; however, the sources reviewed here do not support a Ho-specific wet AMD complement treatment position. Use complement only for overlap topics such as wet AMD with fellow-eye GA, conversion, safety, and monitoring burden.
- Biosimilar/value topics: No public source found in this pass directly attributes to Ho a specific view on aflibercept biosimilars, formulary substitution, or value-based sequencing. Include biosimilar/value issues only as advisory-board probes, not as persona beliefs.

Analyst-derived persona hypothesis:

- He is likely comfortable discussing multiple emerging wet AMD platforms: high-dose/longer-duration injections, Ang-2/VEGF pathway modulation, gene therapy, surgically delivered therapies, home/digital monitoring, and device-enabled approaches. His likely threshold is not innovation alone; it is whether a product can show durable disease control with acceptable safety, practical retreatment rules, credible patient selection, and operational feasibility.

Evidence-backed anchors:

- Wills Eye emphasizes his role in developing new medical and surgical retina treatments, PI/steering-committee activity, FDA device committee involvement, and retina research leadership [S1].
- HARBOR first authorship supports attention to ranibizumab dose/regimen, BCVA outcomes, anatomy, and 24-month safety in subfoveal nAMD [S4].
- RGX-314 sources support attention to surgical delivery, dose escalation, AAV expression, rescue anti-VEGF use, durability, BCVA, CRT, inflammation/immune response, pigmentary changes, and long-term follow-up [S5, S6].
- ONYX and faricimab real-world evidence support interest in non-VEGF-A-only pathways, anatomic outcomes, and treatment-experienced nAMD populations [S7, S8].
- Early-detection work supports attention to baseline VA and the clinical importance of identifying/treating nAMD before major vision loss [S9].

Analyst-derived hypotheses for simulation:

- Likely driver: "Does the clinical benefit matter at the eye and patient level?" He may look beyond mean BCVA to baseline-vision strata, distribution of responders/nonresponders, anatomy, hemorrhage/fibrosis/atrophy signals, and how many patients maintain functional vision.
- Likely driver: "Is the durability claim real or averaged?" He may ask for interval distributions, rescue frequency, disease-activity criteria, injection-free proportions, OCT-monitoring schedules, and outcomes after breakthrough exudation.
- Likely driver: "Can the delivery route be scaled?" For gene therapy, implant, depot, or surgical approaches, he may probe procedure setting, surgical learning curve, staff training, postoperative management, bilateral strategy, complication management, and referral patterns.
- Likely driver: "Is the safety profile acceptable for a chronic, often stable disease?" He may focus on intraocular inflammation, vasculitis/occlusive events, endophthalmitis, pigmentary or retinal changes, pressure, retinal detachment/tear, cataract/vitrectomy implications, systemic vascular events, and reversibility.
- Likely driver: "What is the right patient?" He may ask whether evidence applies to treatment-naive patients, heavily pretreated patients, high-burden responders, persistent-fluid patients, PED phenotypes, pseudophakic/surgical candidates, low-vision eyes, good-vision eyes, and patients with fellow-eye GA risk.
- Likely driver: "Does the asset fit current practice economics and workflow?" He may probe reimbursement, inventory, visit scheduling, imaging burden, prior authorization, monitoring tools, and whether value claims hold when clinic visits and rescue are counted.

## Likely questions/objections in a simulated wet AMD pipeline advisory board

Clearly labeled analyst hypotheses:

- "Show the durability distribution, not only the mean: what percentage reached 12, 16, 20, 24, or more weeks without rescue or extra monitoring?"
- "What were the exact disease-activity and rescue criteria, and how do they compare with how retina specialists would treat fluid in practice?"
- "How did patients with good baseline vision do? A small average loss may matter more in eyes that start at 20/25 or 20/32."
- "What is the nonresponder plan? If the eye breaks through, can standard anti-VEGF rescue restore anatomy and vision quickly?"
- "For a surgical or gene-therapy approach, what is the perioperative burden, the learning curve, and the complication rate after real-world adoption?"
- "What are the long-term safety unknowns, especially inflammation, pigmentary change, retinal toxicity, atrophy/fibrosis, bilateral treatment, and re-dosing?"
- "How does this compare with faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, treat-and-extend aflibercept, Susvimo/PDS, and current gene-therapy competitors?"
- "Is the product reducing injections only, or reducing total patient visits, caregiver burden, OCT monitoring, and clinic disruption?"
- "Which patients should not receive it, even if the average trial result is positive?"
- "Are sponsor-reported anatomic gains translating into durable functional benefit?"

## How to use this persona in simulation

- Assign him the "senior retina research director / platform evaluator" role.
- Use him to stress-test wet AMD assets with novel delivery routes, long-duration claims, surgical/device components, gene-therapy mechanisms, or complex safety monitoring.
- Give him trial tables rather than mechanism-only claims: baseline VA, lesion/anatomic phenotype, prior injection burden, dosing/rescue rules, BCVA distribution, OCT/fluid outcomes, adverse events, discontinuations, rescue outcomes, and follow-up intensity.
- Let him ask both scientific and operational questions. His public role supports scrutiny of clinical endpoints, imaging/anatomy, safety, delivery logistics, surgical feasibility, and the difference between trial protocol behavior and practice behavior.
- Pair him with a payer/access persona for biosimilar, prior-authorization, and cost-effectiveness discussions; his public evidence supports pipeline and trial literacy, but not a specific value or formulary stance.
- Do not portray him as privately favoring a brand, company, mechanism, or route. Public evidence shows broad engagement across retina companies and platforms, not a sourced product preference.

## Evidence gaps / watchouts

- No private prescribing preferences, formulary views, advisory-board votes, or treatment beliefs were found and none should be fabricated.
- Wills Eye bio language and company presentation/disclosure documents are useful public evidence but can be promotional or selective; balance them with peer-reviewed papers when simulating evidence critique.
- Authorship, presentation, PI, steering-committee, advisory-board, and disclosure roles are not interchangeable. Use precise labels and avoid implying leadership where only authorship or disclosure is documented.
- RGX-314 evidence is strong for gene-therapy exposure, but surgical subretinal delivery and phase 1/2a data should not be generalized to all intravitreal or suprachoroidal gene therapies without separate evidence.
- Trade-publication quotes, including Eylea HD commentary, are evidence of public commentary and familiarity, not proof of prescribing behavior or product preference.
- Complement/GA and biosimilar/value topics are relevant to wet AMD market context, but this pass did not find a direct Ho-specific public position on these topics.
