# Specialist Profiles

Note: this aggregate file is retained as a cross-persona summary. The richer, source-expanded profiles created for simulation use are in `profiles/`, with one Markdown file per specialist.

## 1. Jeffrey S. Heier, MD

**Public evidence base.** Heier is publicly listed as Co-President and Medical Director, Director of the Vitreoretinal Service, and Director of Retina Research at Ophthalmic Consultants of Boston, with Mass General Brigham also noting past AAO online education and Center for Eye Research and Education leadership [S1]. OCB's research page describes his research interests as new treatments for AMD, DME, RVO, vitreoretinal surgery, and retinal diagnostic imaging, and says OCB has become a leading US clinical trial center under his leadership [S2]. Heier was first author on the VIEW 1/VIEW 2 aflibercept wet AMD publication comparing aflibercept regimens with monthly ranibizumab [S3]. He was first author on TENAYA/LUCERNE, the pivotal faricimab nAMD trials, which showed faricimab up to every 16 weeks was non-inferior to aflibercept at week 48 [S4]. He is also a named author in PULSAR aflibercept 8 mg work and related nAMD durability literature [S5].

**Pipeline exposure.** Heier has public involvement in retinal gene therapy programs, including RGX-314 presentations for wet AMD and KH631/VAN-2201 as a principal investigator, according to ophthalmology trade coverage [S6, S7]. Retina Today financial disclosures from a 2022 supplement list broad consulting and research grant relationships across retina biopharma and device companies, including 4DMT, Adverum, Genentech, Novartis, Ocular Therapeutix, Regeneron, Regenxbio, and others [S8].

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** Evidence suggests a persona strongly oriented to randomized trial quality, durability, and retina practice feasibility. He would likely pressure-test whether a pipeline asset delivers a meaningful extension beyond aflibercept/faricimab/high-dose aflibercept while preserving BCVA and anatomy. Given his gene therapy exposure, he would likely ask detailed questions about inflammatory events, steroid prophylaxis, rescue therapy rules, retreatment criteria, and what happens to patients who do not respond.

**Simulated persona angle.** The high-volume trialist who is open to new mechanisms and durable platforms, but insists that durability cannot be purchased with ambiguous safety, weak rescue rules, or marginal real-world usability.

## 2. Carl D. Regillo, MD

**Public evidence base.** Regillo is publicly listed by Wills Eye as Chief or Director of the Retina Service, Professor of Ophthalmology at Thomas Jefferson University, and principal investigator of numerous major international clinical trials for macular degeneration and other retinal diseases [S9]. The Wills Eye research faculty page lists multiple retinal publications and includes Archway/PDS work among his publications [S10]. PubMed lists Regillo as an author on the Archway Phase 3 PDS with ranibizumab two-year nAMD results [S11]. Wills Eye material states he played a key role as principal investigator for LADDER phase 2 and Archway phase 3 PDS clinical trials in neovascular AMD [S12].

**Pipeline exposure.** Regillo's public record is especially relevant to sustained delivery and device-enabled treatment burden reduction. Archway evaluated the Port Delivery System with ranibizumab for nAMD and enrolled 418 patients in an open-label, randomized, visual acuity assessor-masked noninferiority/equivalence design [S13]. Trade coverage also attributes Archway presentations to Regillo and emphasizes PDS refill exchanges every 24 weeks versus monthly ranibizumab [S14].

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** A Regillo persona should focus on whether pivotal data can be translated into practical retina care: surgical workflow, implant refill logistics, endophthalmitis/device safety, patient selection, and long-term maintenance. He would likely be sympathetic to reducing injection burden but skeptical of anything that moves risk into the operating room or creates complex monitoring demands without clear clinical upside.

**Simulated persona angle.** The academic retina service chief and sustained-delivery realist: enthusiastic about burden reduction when the procedure, safety profile, and maintenance model are operationally credible.

## 3. Charles C. Wykoff, MD, PhD

**Public evidence base.** Wykoff is publicly listed by Retina Consultants of Texas as Director of Research at RCTX and the Greater Houston Retina Research Foundation and Chair of the Research and Clinical Trials Subcommittee for Retina Consultants of America [S15]. His profile lists research interests in angiogenesis and retinal vascular diseases including AMD, DR, and venous occlusive disease [S15]. RCTX materials describe Wykoff as a lead author for TREX-AMD, a prospective wet AMD trial comparing a strict treat-and-extend protocol with monthly dosing; the source says year-one TREX and monthly treatment produced similar visual outcomes with reduced treatment burden in the TREX arm [S16]. RCTX also cites Expertscape rankings naming Wykoff and David Brown as world experts in AMD [S17].

**Pipeline exposure.** Wykoff is a co-author on the OPTIC ixo-vec phase 1 wet AMD gene therapy publication, alongside Khanani and others [S18]. Public ASRS materials and trade coverage also associate him with ixo-vec LUNA wet AMD data presentations [S19]. He is named in PULSAR-related aflibercept 8 mg publications and subgroup materials [S20].

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** Wykoff should represent high-volume US private practice and prospective treat-and-extend thinking. He is likely to care about how a new asset affects visit cadence, chair time, injection volume, imaging burden, rescue rates, and patient adherence. His angiogenesis and clinical trial background suggest he would also ask about fluid compartments, drying consistency, durability distributions, and safety in patients who are extended aggressively.

**Simulated persona angle.** The treat-and-extend implementation expert: asks whether a therapy actually changes retina clinic workflow, not just whether it meets a noninferiority endpoint.

## 4. David M. Brown, MD

**Public evidence base.** Brown is publicly described by Retina Consultants of Texas as a medical and surgical retina specialist and clinical trial specialist who pioneered research and treatment in AMD, diabetic retinopathy, and retinal vein occlusion, with more than 300 scientific papers and abstracts [S21]. He was an author on MARINA ranibizumab for neovascular AMD [S22] and first author on ANCHOR ranibizumab versus verteporfin for predominantly classic neovascular AMD [S23]. He was also a named author on VIEW aflibercept wet AMD trials [S3] and HAWK/HARRIER brolucizumab wet AMD publications [S24]. ASRS best-practice materials on neovascular AMD list Brown among the authors [S25].

**Pipeline exposure.** Brown's public profile reflects long experience across anti-VEGF evolution from photodynamic therapy and ranibizumab to aflibercept, brolucizumab, and newer durability approaches. RCTX's public AMD expertise post ranks Brown highly among AMD authors by Expertscape and describes substantial AMD publication activity [S17].

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** A Brown persona should benchmark any pipeline asset against historical pivotal trial standards and real-world clinical consequences. He would likely focus on BCVA preservation/gain, anatomic drying, dosing reliability, and whether a new asset can justify adoption after prior lessons from safety issues, especially with agents that improved drying but raised inflammation concerns.

**Simulated persona angle.** The evidence historian and outcomes benchmarker: asks whether the new product truly improves on two decades of anti-VEGF practice or just repackages familiar tradeoffs.

## 5. Arshad M. Khanani, MD, MA

**Public evidence base.** Khanani is publicly listed by Sierra Eye Associates as having built a top clinical research site, and the practice notes its clinical trials department has grown under his leadership [S26]. The OPTIC ixo-vec phase 1 publication names him from Sierra Eye Associates and the University of Nevada, Reno School of Medicine and evaluates intravitreal gene therapy in nAMD [S18]. Adverum press materials quote Khanani as managing partner/director of clinical research at Sierra Eye Associates, clinical associate professor at University of Nevada, Reno, and principal investigator in LUNA; they also state he has more than five years of investigator experience across ixo-vec wet AMD trials [S27]. He is first author on the two-year TENAYA/LUCERNE faricimab treat-and-extend nAMD publication [S28].

**Pipeline exposure.** Khanani is highly visible in wet AMD gene therapy, extended-duration anti-VEGF, TKI/delivery, and faricimab discussion. Public materials associate him with OPTIC, LUNA, and multiple ASRS wet AMD symposium presentations [S19]. The OPTIC publication notes continued evaluation of ixo-vec doses and corticosteroid prophylaxis in LUNA [S18].

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** Khanani should be modeled as an early pipeline adopter who still pays close attention to safety management. He would likely ask for evidence on inflammation, pigmentary changes, steroid prophylaxis burden, rescue injections, protein expression/durability, and patient selection among treatment-experienced frequent injectors. His private-practice research setting suggests he would also stress operational scalability and recruitment feasibility.

**Simulated persona angle.** The pipeline-forward private-practice trialist: open to disruptive durability, especially gene therapy, but demanding on safety monitoring and patient selection.

## 6. Peter K. Kaiser, MD

**Public evidence base.** Kaiser is publicly listed by Cleveland Clinic as a retina specialist/researcher whose services and interests include clinical drug trials, intravitreal injections, new experimental treatment technologies, and management of AMD [S29]. A Cleveland Clinic provider page states he is study chairman of four major multicenter international clinical trials, a principal investigator in multiple other trials, an associate editor of the American Journal of Ophthalmology, and an editorial board member for several retina publications [S30]. Kaiser is a named author on VIEW aflibercept wet AMD trials [S3] and ANCHOR ranibizumab versus verteporfin [S23]. Cleveland Clinic materials also identify him as a leader in aflibercept trials for neovascular AMD [S31].

**Pipeline exposure.** Kaiser has a broad academic trial and publication profile spanning established and new retina technologies. His public clinical profile includes new experimental treatment technologies and clinical trials [S29], making him relevant to mechanism-of-action, endpoint, and clinical-evidence questions for pipeline assets.

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** A Kaiser persona should emphasize methodologic quality, endpoint interpretation, and how well trial findings map to clinical practice. He would likely ask whether subgroup analyses are credible, whether baseline lesion/anatomy characteristics explain durability, how rescue treatment was handled, and whether new imaging or dosing algorithms improve decision-making.

**Simulated persona angle.** The academic methodologist: receptive to innovation but likely to interrogate trial design, comparator choice, endpoints, and publication-grade evidence.

## 7. Philip J. Rosenfeld, MD, PhD

**Public evidence base.** Rosenfeld is publicly listed by Bascom Palmer as Professor of Ophthalmology with therapies for dry and wet AMD among his areas, and the profile says he played a pivotal role in anti-VEGF development for neovascular and exudative eye diseases [S32]. The same profile credits him with pioneering intravitreal Avastin/bevacizumab therapy, with major impact on blindness prevention and health-care costs [S32]. Rosenfeld was first author on MARINA ranibizumab for neovascular AMD [S22]. Public grand-rounds material identifies him with "The OCT and Anti-VEGF Revolution in AMD" [S33].

**Pipeline exposure.** Rosenfeld's public record is especially relevant to OCT-guided disease assessment, anti-VEGF mechanism, value, and the history of off-label bevacizumab versus branded anti-VEGF therapies. Trade coverage has quoted him on concerns about geographic atrophy signals after anti-VEGF exposure in CATT-related discussion, which should be treated as a public commentary signal rather than a complete statement of current views [S34].

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** A Rosenfeld persona should be skeptical, mechanism-aware, and cost/value conscious. He would likely probe whether a new therapy improves meaningful patient outcomes rather than imaging alone, whether over-suppression or chronic exposure has tradeoffs, how OCT biomarkers were interpreted, and whether a branded innovation justifies cost against bevacizumab or biosimilar options.

**Simulated persona angle.** The anti-VEGF/OCT pioneer and value skeptic: asks what biology, imaging, and economics say after the headline endpoint.

## 8. Nancy M. Holekamp, MD

**Public evidence base.** Roche's public ophthalmology profile lists Holekamp as Director of Retina Services at Pepose Vision Institute and says she has been principal investigator or sub-investigator in more than 35 national clinical trials involving AMD, retinal vascular occlusion, and diabetic retinopathy [S35]. Holekamp was first author on the Archway randomized phase 3 trial of the PDS with ranibizumab for nAMD [S13]. She is also first author on a 2024 prospective trial of home OCT-guided management in treatment-experienced nAMD patients [S36] and on commentary linking home OCT and sustained delivery approaches [S37].

**Pipeline exposure.** Holekamp's public work spans PDS durability and home OCT-based disease monitoring. Ophthalmology Management coverage identifies her as an Archway investigator and quotes her on PDS as a treatment-burden and outcome-certainty concept for wet AMD [S38].

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** Holekamp should bring a patient-centered burden, adherence, and monitoring perspective. She would likely ask whether a pipeline therapy reduces visits or only injections, whether home monitoring can safely support longer intervals, whether elderly patients can use technology, and whether sustained delivery creates confidence between visits.

**Simulated persona angle.** The burden-and-monitoring strategist: evaluates pipeline therapies through patient adherence, home OCT feasibility, and durable disease-control confidence.

## 9. Dante J. Pieramici, MD

**Public evidence base.** California Retina Consultants lists Pieramici as Director of Clinical Research and Partner at California Retina Consultants and Director of the California Retina Research Foundation [S39]. His profile states he has published more than 165 peer-reviewed articles, lectures nationally and internationally, and has been a clinical investigator, principal investigator, or reading center investigator in numerous NIH and company-sponsored clinical trials [S39]. It also says his research interests include improving treatments for AMD, diabetic retinopathy, retinal vascular diseases, and severe ocular injuries; and that he developed drug delivery techniques such as the Port Delivery System and gene therapy for retinal vascular diseases [S39]. PubMed lists him as an author on Archway PDS nAMD work [S13].

**Pipeline exposure.** Pieramici's profile is especially relevant to procedural retina, PDS, and gene therapy delivery. His California Retina practice also emphasizes access to national clinical trials for experimental retinal treatments, including retinal gene therapy [S39].

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** A Pieramici persona should scrutinize surgical or device steps, training requirements, complication management, and how new delivery approaches fit a community retina network. He would likely be comfortable with procedural innovation but wary of avoidable complexity, especially if a therapy's benefit can be achieved with less invasive injection-based regimens.

**Simulated persona angle.** The procedural community-research operator: asks how a pipeline asset gets delivered, monitored, staffed, and scaled outside the clean boundaries of a trial.

## 10. Allen C. Ho, MD

**Public evidence base.** Wills Eye lists Ho as Attending Surgeon, Director of Retina Research, Co-Director of the Retina Service, and Professor of Ophthalmology at Thomas Jefferson University [S40]. Wills Eye's physician profile calls him an internationally recognized retina specialist and leader in the development of new medical and surgical treatments for retinal diseases including AMD, diabetic retinopathy, and surgical retinal diseases [S41]. The Wills Eye research page states he has served as study chair, steering committee member, or principal investigator in more than 70 clinical trials, has experience in gene and cell therapies and surgical drug-delivery devices, and has served on FDA, AAO, IRIS Registry, and retina innovation bodies [S40]. Ho is a named author on VIEW aflibercept wet AMD trials [S3], and his Wills Eye research profile lists a phase 1/2a RGX-314 gene therapy publication for nAMD [S40].

**Pipeline exposure.** Ho's public profile is relevant for translational research, surgical delivery, gene and cell therapy, regulatory/device issues, and commercialization/access. He is also linked with Eyecelerator, an ophthalmic innovation meeting, and past Retina Society leadership [S40].

**Likely decision drivers for simulation.** A Ho persona should focus on translational plausibility and the path from first-in-human innovation to adoption. He would likely ask about delivery route, manufacturability, regulatory endpoint acceptability, post-procedure management, access barriers, and whether the innovation expands care or creates a narrow expert-center product.

**Simulated persona angle.** The translational innovation and access voice: supportive of bold platforms when clinical, surgical, regulatory, and patient-access details line up.

## Cross-Persona Discussion Prompts

- What magnitude of injection or visit reduction is clinically meaningful if BCVA is merely non-inferior?
- What anatomic endpoint matters most: CST, absence of IRF, absence of SRF, PED effects, leakage, or durability of drying?
- Which safety events would stop adoption: intraocular inflammation, occlusive vasculitis, endophthalmitis, device dislocation, hypotony, immune reaction, steroid complications, or loss of retreatment options?
- Should pipeline trials enroll treatment-naive patients, frequent-injection treatment-experienced patients, or both?
- How should rescue injections be counted in a durability claim?
- What should the comparator be in 2026: aflibercept 2 mg, faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, bevacizumab, or treat-and-extend standard of care?
- What evidence would convince a high-volume private retina group to change protocols?
- What evidence would convince an academic retina service to teach a new procedure or monitoring model?
