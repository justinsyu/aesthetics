# Persona Creation Strategy for Wet AMD Advisory Boards

## Purpose

Use this strategy to create, update, and audit retina specialist personas for simulated advisory boards focused on pipeline wet AMD/neovascular AMD treatments. The goal is not to impersonate a physician or claim private beliefs. The goal is to build evidence-anchored simulation inputs that reflect how a specialist's public roles, research history, practice context, and disease-area exposure may shape the questions they would plausibly raise.

## Core Principles

1. Separate facts from simulation hypotheses.
   - Facts include public role, affiliation, trial authorship, investigator role, society role, stated research interest, publication, disclosed relationship, or public quote.
   - Simulation hypotheses include likely objections, adoption thresholds, advisory-board stance, and discussion prompts.
   - Every hypothesis should cite the public facts that informed it.

2. Prefer evidence that is close to the behavior being simulated.
   - Strongest: wet AMD trial publications, protocol records, investigator roles, guideline/best-practice authorship, public clinical-research roles, and quoted commentary on wet AMD treatment decisions.
   - Useful but weaker: institutional bios, conference faculty pages, trade articles, company press releases, disclosure pages, and research-center descriptions.
   - Treat company and trade sources as role/exposure evidence unless independently supported by peer-reviewed or registry evidence.

3. Represent the advisory-board tension, not only the resume.
   - A useful persona should create productive friction in discussion: efficacy versus safety, durability versus monitoring, injection reduction versus visit reduction, anatomic drying versus vision outcomes, procedure burden versus clinic burden, and innovation versus payer/value constraints.

4. Do not overfit or overclaim.
   - Publication authorship does not prove a private treatment preference.
   - Trial participation does not imply product endorsement.
   - Financial disclosures indicate exposure or potential conflict context, not belief.
   - Institutional titles and disclosures can change; record retrieval date and source limitations.

## Standard Workflow

### 1. Define the advisory-board objective

Before profiling, define the pipeline decision being simulated:

- Asset type: injectable anti-VEGF, bispecific, TKI, sustained-release implant, gene therapy, cell therapy, biosimilar, combination regimen, or monitoring-enabled care model.
- Target population: treatment naive, treatment experienced, frequent injectors, poor responders, high-fluid burden, bilateral disease, elderly/frail patients, or payer-constrained populations.
- Decision being tested: clinical-trial design, target product profile, launch positioning, adoption barriers, safety monitoring, payer/value story, or evidence-generation plan.

### 2. Select specialists with explicit criteria

Document inclusion criteria before ranking names. For wet AMD, useful criteria include:

- Wet AMD/nAMD publication or pivotal trial involvement.
- Investigator or steering role in pipeline-relevant studies.
- Academic, private-practice, surgical, imaging, monitoring, payer/value, or society perspective.
- Practice setting relevance to the simulated decision.
- Ability to represent a distinct debate role, not just prestige.

Document exclusions and tradeoffs:

- US-only versus global scope.
- Academic versus private-practice mix.
- Surgical/device-heavy versus injection-heavy lens.
- Current active clinician versus historical field shaper.
- Public-source availability.

### 3. Build an evidence dossier for each specialist

Capture sources in a table with retrieval date, source class, and supported claims. Recommended source classes:

| Source class | Use | Cautions |
|---|---|---|
| Institutional bio | Current role, practice setting, stated research areas | May be promotional or outdated |
| PubMed/PMC | Trial authorship, endpoints, publication history | Authorship does not prove viewpoint |
| ClinicalTrials.gov | Investigator/site/trial context where visible | Records can lag or omit site-level details |
| Society/guideline materials | Leadership, best-practice influence, public education role | May not reflect current role |
| Conference/trade coverage | Public quotes, presentation themes, pipeline exposure | Use as exposure/commentary, not efficacy proof |
| Company disclosures/press releases | Investigator quote, advisory role, product exposure | Potential promotional framing |
| Open Payments/disclosures | Relationship context | Requires careful date/company normalization |

### 4. Translate evidence into persona dimensions

For each specialist, fill these dimensions:

- Practice context: academic center, private retina group, hybrid research site, surgical retina, imaging/monitoring center, referral center.
- Evidence orientation: pivotal-trial methodologist, real-world implementer, historical anti-VEGF benchmarker, device/procedure evaluator, imaging biomarker expert, early pipeline adopter.
- Likely adoption threshold: what would need to be true before the persona would recommend meaningful use.
- Safety sensitivity: inflammation, vasculitis, endophthalmitis, device complications, steroid burden, immune response, retinal atrophy, loss of rescue options.
- Operational sensitivity: injection volume, visit cadence, imaging burden, surgical scheduling, staffing, training, elderly patient usability, payer controls.
- Comparator frame: bevacizumab, aflibercept 2 mg, faricimab, aflibercept 8 mg, PDS/Susvimo, biosimilars, or treat-and-extend standard of care.
- Evidence gaps: what cannot be inferred from public information.

### 5. Write the profile in a repeatable structure

Each profile should include:

- Persona snapshot table.
- Public role and practice setting.
- Wet AMD evidence footprint.
- Pipeline and emerging-therapy exposure.
- Treatment-decision drivers, explicitly labeled as evidence-backed or analyst-derived.
- Likely advisory-board questions and objections.
- How to use the persona in simulation.
- Evidence gaps and watchouts.
- Source table with URLs and retrieval date.

### 6. Convert profile into simulation behavior

Use the persona to shape prompts, not to make claims about the physician. Good simulation prompts use language such as:

- "Given this persona's public trial and practice background, pressure-test..."
- "Ask as a high-volume private-practice trialist who is likely to care about..."
- "Raise concerns that would plausibly follow from this evidence footprint..."

Avoid language such as:

- "Dr. X believes..."
- "Dr. X would prescribe..."
- "Dr. X prefers..."
- "Dr. X opposes..."

## Update Cadence

Update personas when any of the following occurs:

- New phase 2/3 wet AMD data are presented or published.
- A pivotal trial changes status, completes enrollment, fails, or reports safety findings.
- A specialist changes affiliation, role, society leadership, or disclosed industry relationship.
- A new comparator becomes clinically relevant, such as a long-duration anti-VEGF, gene therapy, implant, biosimilar, or monitoring platform.
- The advisory-board objective changes.

For each update, preserve prior source-supported facts unless superseded. Add an update note with date, source, and changed implication.

## Quality Checklist

- The profile has a retrieval date.
- Every factual claim has a source.
- Every inferred persona statement is labeled as analyst-derived.
- The profile contains at least one adoption driver, one safety concern, one operational concern, and one evidence-standard concern.
- The profile includes evidence gaps and avoids private-belief claims.
- The profile can be used directly to generate advisory-board prompts.
- The profile's source table distinguishes peer-reviewed/registry evidence from company or trade commentary.

## Recommended Simulation Outputs

For each pipeline asset, use the personas to generate:

- Opening reaction to the target product profile.
- Top five evidence questions.
- Most concerning safety or operational barrier.
- Comparator the persona would use mentally.
- Adoption threshold by patient segment.
- Trial-design critique.
- Evidence-generation recommendation.
- Payer/access objection if relevant.
- Final simulated vote: enthusiastic, cautiously supportive, evidence needed, niche-only, or unlikely to adopt.
