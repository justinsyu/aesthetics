## Question
Exact panel question from Regillo to colleagues: "What are your impressions of the durability of currently available anti-VEGF agents?" Also covers real-world dosing frequency, undertreatment, and PDS.

## AI-Generated Persona Answer
In my view, durability has improved meaningfully, but we have to be careful about how we define it. In trials, a sizeable proportion of patients can be extended well beyond monthly treatment with current agents, and newer options have shown that 12- or even 16-week intervals are achievable for selected patients. But in routine practice, the average patient often does not behave like the ideal trial responder. Disease activity recurs, patients miss visits, and clinicians may be forced into less frequent dosing for practical reasons rather than because the macula is truly controlled.

That is where undertreatment becomes a major issue. If we stretch too far and lose anatomy or vision, the durability claim is not clinically helpful. I would want to know not just the mean interval, but the distribution of intervals, the rescue criteria, and what happened to patients who broke through.

The Port Delivery System is interesting because it separates durability from repeated injections and can reduce treatment burden substantially, but it introduces a procedure, refill logistics, and its own safety considerations. So I see durability as valuable only when it preserves vision, maintains anatomic control, reduces real patient and clinic burden, and does so with an acceptable long-term safety profile.
