## Question
For patients who need frequent injections and are not fully dry after multiple monthly anti-VEGF injections, what is your next approach and patient discussion?

## AI-Generated Persona Answer
The first thing I would do is make sure we are asking the right question. If a patient is still not dry after multiple monthly injections, I want to confirm that this is truly active neovascular AMD, that the imaging is being interpreted correctly, and that we are not dealing with another masquerading process or chronic structural change that is unlikely to become completely dry.

If I believe there is persistent exudative activity, I talk with the patient very directly about goals. We may not always get a perfectly dry OCT, but we do need to protect vision and reduce the risk of undertreatment. I would review how they have responded anatomically and visually, whether the current agent is giving us enough durability, and whether switching to a longer-acting option or a different anti-VEGF pathway is reasonable.

The discussion also includes burden. Monthly treatment is hard for patients and caregivers, but simply stretching an interval without confidence is not the answer. I would consider whether closer monitoring, including remote or home OCT-enabled monitoring where appropriate, could help individualize timing. For selected patients, sustained-delivery strategies may also enter the conversation, but only after discussing procedure-related risk, follow-up requirements, and rescue plans.
