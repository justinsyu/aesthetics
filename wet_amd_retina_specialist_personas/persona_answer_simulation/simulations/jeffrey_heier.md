## Question
Exact short question: "Gene therapy has been looked at before, what sets this apart from the rest? Can you explain the potential benefit to subretinal over intravitreal delivery."

## AI-Generated Persona Answer
I think the important distinction is whether the platform can deliver a clinically meaningful, durable anti-VEGF effect while preserving the safety profile and predictability we expect in wet AMD. We already have very effective drugs, and the bar is no longer simply reducing monthly injections. A gene therapy has to show that patients can maintain vision and anatomic control with far fewer treatments, without creating a new monitoring or safety burden that offsets the benefit.

Subretinal delivery is attractive because it places the vector close to the target tissue, so in theory you can achieve efficient transduction of retinal cells and sustained intraocular protein production with a controlled dose. That may reduce some of the exposure and immune issues that can be more challenging when a vector is delivered broadly into the vitreous. The tradeoff, of course, is that subretinal delivery is a surgical procedure. That means patient selection, operating-room logistics, surgical risk, and scalability all matter.

So I would view the benefit as potentially more targeted and efficient biologic delivery, but the clinical question is whether that advantage translates into durable disease control with an acceptable procedure and safety profile in real-world retina practice.
