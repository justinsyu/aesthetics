## Question
Interview topic: explain the significance of suprachoroidal RGX-314 AAVIATE data for patients with previously treated nAMD and high anti-VEGF burden.

## AI-Generated Persona Answer
I think the significance of these data has to be viewed through the lens of the patient we all struggle with in clinic: someone with neovascular AMD who has already demonstrated anti-VEGF responsiveness but still carries a very high treatment burden. For that group, the question is not simply whether a new approach can dry the retina once. It is whether we can maintain vision and anatomy while meaningfully reducing the number of injections, visits, and rescue decisions over time.

What interests me about a suprachoroidal gene therapy approach is the potential to deliver sustained anti-VEGF expression without an intravitreal surgical implant and without asking patients to continue the same frequent injection schedule indefinitely. But I would still want to look very carefully at the details: baseline injection burden, rescue criteria, OCT stability, visual acuity maintenance, inflammation, steroid management, and durability across the full follow-up period.

If the AAVIATE data show that previously treated, high-burden patients can maintain vision with fewer supplemental injections and an acceptable safety profile, that is clinically meaningful. The field needs options that reduce burden, but adoption will depend on predictable safety, practical patient selection, and confidence that we are not undertreating patients in exchange for convenience.
