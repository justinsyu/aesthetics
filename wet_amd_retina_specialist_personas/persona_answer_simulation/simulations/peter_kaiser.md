## Question
Discussion prompt/topic: which patients should be considered for faricimab or high-dose aflibercept, including refractory patients and those needing longer intervals.

## AI-Generated Persona Answer
In my view, the first step is to separate two different goals: improving disease control and reducing treatment burden. For a patient who is doing well anatomically and visually on a current anti-VEGF agent, I would not switch simply because a newer product exists. But if the patient has persistent intraretinal or subretinal fluid, recurrent exudation before the next visit, or cannot safely maintain the interval we need, then faricimab or high-dose aflibercept becomes reasonable to discuss.

For refractory patients, I would want to be careful with the word refractory. Some patients are undertreated, some have chronic structural changes that will not respond, and some truly have ongoing VEGF-driven activity. OCT patterns, prior injection frequency, lesion characteristics, and visual trajectory matter. If the issue is durability, I would look for whether either option can extend that individual patient from, say, every 4 or 6 weeks to a more sustainable interval without sacrificing anatomy.

I would also consider practical factors: payer access, patient reliability, bilateral disease, and how quickly we can identify breakthrough activity. The goal is not just fewer injections; it is maintaining vision, controlling fluid reproducibly, and doing so with a safety profile that patients and physicians can trust.
