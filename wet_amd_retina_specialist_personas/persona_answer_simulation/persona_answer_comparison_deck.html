Recurring strong-match patterns

Persona context was most predictive for clinical judgment and tradeoff language

Burden + durability

High-frequency treatment burden was consistently recovered

Simulations repeatedly framed durable therapies, PDS, home OCT, gene therapy, and high-dose anti-VEGF around reducing burden without sacrificing vision.

Safety + monitoring

Tradeoffs were usually plausible and persona-consistent

Generated answers emphasized inflammation, infection, procedure risk, rescue criteria, monitoring, and breakthrough activity when relevant.

Patient selection

Simulations captured who should be considered first

High-burden, treatment-responsive, frequent-injection, refractory, or access-constrained patient frames appeared across strong matches.

Best-recovered clinical stances

  • Gene therapy as a selected-patient foundation, not a broad injection replacement.
  • Anti-VEGF durability as useful only if anatomy and vision remain controlled.
  • Access policies and step therapy as real risks when they delay vision-preserving care.

Why the persona signal helped

  • Profiles encoded treatment philosophy, practice priorities, trial involvement, and likely adoption thresholds.
  • Opinion questions rewarded reasoning style more than factual recall.
  • Clinical archetypes remained stable across different interview topics.
04 / 08
Recurring gaps

Source-specific details were the main failure mode

Gaps generally reflected information that a clean persona would not necessarily contain unless the profile encoded the original interview's evidence context.

Trial contours

Named data details were often absent

Partial rows missed details such as first suprachoroidal trial framing, over 70% burden reduction, 6-month dose-level findings, and LADDER/ARCHWAY support.

Product specifics

Product and platform names were under-specified

Some simulations captured mechanism or class logic but omitted RGX-314, REGENXBIO AAV8, neutralizing antibodies, or 2 mg to 8 mg switching details.

Protocol details

Management algorithms lost granularity

Recorded answers included timing rules, fluid-priority distinctions, and specific retreatment intervals that simulations often paraphrased generically.

Plausible additions

Generated answers sometimes added reasonable but unrecorded cautions

Examples included route-specific surgical concerns, payer factors, or broader workflow cautions that fit the persona but were not central to the recorded answer.

Implication: persona-only simulation can test viewpoint fidelity, but benchmark scoring should not penalize missing hidden factual details unless those details are present in the persona context.

Calibration implications

The benchmark supports a two-layer persona workflow

Persona simulation should preserve judgment style, while source-specific retrieval supplies trial facts and named details.

Keep persona-only prompts for viewpoint testing

  • Use subjective, opinion-oriented questions with no single right answer.
  • Score for posture, prioritization, tradeoff language, and likely adoption thresholds.
  • Track plausible additions separately from factual errors.

Add retrieval context for fact-sensitive simulations

  • Provide trial names, product labels, data cuts, or mechanism notes when the question requires them.
  • Separate "what the persona would think" from "what the expert actually cited in that interview."
  • Use answer-key details as calibration targets, not hidden facts the model should infer.
Score design

Split scores

Rate conceptual stance and source-specific detail separately.

Persona upkeep

Add evidence hooks

Encode known trial involvement and signature phrases as structured profile fields.

Benchmark growth

Balance topics

Include access, monitoring, durability, mechanism, and real-world adoption prompts.
