CW-005Payer / treatment selection

Charles C. Wykoff, MD, PhD

BrightFocus Foundation Macular Chat
Question

How does a provider choose among wet AMD treatments, including patient access and step-therapy considerations?

Actual answer summary

Wykoff emphasized patient and physician choice, timely access, and matching the right medicine to the patient. He said Avastin can work but is off-label and requires repackaging, opposed mandatory step therapy because it can delay care and risk vision, and advised patients to understand insurance constraints.

Why this tests a persona

Strong discriminator for payer pressure, access friction, and willingness to push back on policy constraints when vision is at risk.

PK-014Adoption / risk tolerance

Peter K. Kaiser, MD

HCPLive biosimilars interview transcript
Question

What challenges and concerns might physicians face when using biosimilars for retinal diseases?

Actual answer summary

Kaiser said adoption depends on trust in FDA analytics, safety, clinical outcomes, and personal experience with the product. He flags antidrug antibodies and intraocular inflammation as issues ophthalmologists now watch closely, while noting ranibizumab biosimilar trials did not show higher ADA or IOI rates versus reference ranibizumab.

Why this tests a persona

Tests how the persona weighs regulatory evidence, immune/safety risk, real-world comfort, and economic pressure.

PR-20Monitoring / treatment selection

Philip J. Rosenfeld, MD, PhD

Anti-VEGF Q&A article
Question

How do you decide on retreatment after anti-VEGF therapy: visual acuity, OCT, or fluorescein angiography?

Actual answer summary

Rosenfeld relies heavily on qualitative OCT for retreatment and treats for any macular fluid until the macula is dry. He then follows monthly to learn the patient's fluid-free interval and returns just before expected recurrence, or uses treat-and-extend injections while dry to define and maintain that interval.

Why this tests a persona

Clear decision rule for OCT-driven retreatment; useful for testing precision, fluid tolerance, and protocol logic.

NH-12Patient selection / treatment strategy

Nancy M. Holekamp, MD

HCPLive peer exchange transcript
Question

For patients who need frequent injections and are not fully dry after multiple monthly anti-VEGF injections, what is your next approach and patient discussion?

Actual answer summary

Holekamp said she first rechecks the diagnosis and considers masqueraders before labeling a patient as a high-VEGF-need incomplete responder. She emphasized continued maintenance/treat-and-extend, patient communication about chronic therapy, and greater concern for intraretinal than subretinal fluid.

Why this tests a persona

Tests differential diagnosis, incomplete-responder management, fluid tolerance, and patient-facing explanation style.

ACH-002Adoption / treatment selection

Allen C. Ho, MD

New Retina Radio transcript
Question

Discuss whether retinal gene therapy is working, including anti-VEGF biofactory approaches for wet AMD, and where it may fit relative to injections.

Actual answer summary

Ho said retinal gene therapy is moving from rare inherited disease into common retina diseases such as wet AMD, with evidence that anti-VEGF-like proteins can be produced durably. He does not expect injections to disappear; he sees gene therapy as a possible foundation therapy for selected high-burden patients, with injections still used as needed.

Why this tests a persona

Tests adoption threshold for disruptive therapy and whether the persona avoids overclaiming replacement of injections.

AK-004Evidence / patient selection

Arshad M. Khanani, MD, MA

HCPLive gene therapy interview
Question

Explain the significance of suprachoroidal RGX-314 AAVIATE data for high-burden previously treated nAMD patients.

Actual answer summary

Khanani called AAVIATE important because it was the first suprachoroidal gene-therapy trial and showed early tolerability plus meaningful treatment-burden reduction while maintaining anatomy and vision. He stressed that RGX-314 is not curative, but a one-time in-clinic approach could reduce repeated anti-VEGF injections.

Why this tests a persona

Tests enthusiasm control: recognizes burden reduction and novelty while avoiding cure claims.

CW-011Risk / patient selection

Charles C. Wykoff, MD, PhD

Retinal Physician Q&A
Question

When would an eye with wet AMD not receive treatment?

Actual answer summary

Wykoff said active wet AMD may sometimes be observed or dosed infrequently when there is a disciform scar, limited visual potential, a well-sighted fellow eye, or near end of life. He emphasized individualized decisions rather than a single universal stopping rule.

Why this tests a persona

Tests treatment futility thresholds, patient-context reasoning, and willingness to depart from reflexive treatment.

PR-23Risk / shared decision

Philip J. Rosenfeld, MD, PhD

Anti-VEGF Q&A article
Question

In a patient with prior cerebrovascular accident or myocardial infarction and occult CNV, what treatment would you choose?

Actual answer summary

Rosenfeld said prior CVA or MI does not change his treatment choice. He presents options and finds patients generally accept even a small possible vascular risk to avoid likely blindness or vision loss, framing the decision as shared risk-benefit judgment.

Why this tests a persona

Tests medically complex risk communication and whether the persona balances small systemic risks against likely vision loss.

CDR-011Evidence / operations

Carl D. Regillo, MD

Retina Today supplement transcript
Question

What are your impressions of the durability of currently available anti-VEGF agents?

Actual answer summary

Regillo said durability varies by patient but is broadly similar across agents, with many patients extending beyond 8 weeks but fewer reaching 12 to 16 weeks. He emphasized that durability definitions depend on tolerated exudation thresholds, and described PDS as technically more demanding than injection but well tolerated and supported by LADDER/ARCHWAY maintenance data.

Why this tests a persona

Tests realistic durability expectations, exudation tolerance, and procedural adoption realism.

Source: supplements.retinatoday.com/articles/treatment-adherence-in-neovascular-amd-2/
DJP-013Operations / risk tolerance

Dante J. Pieramici, MD

HCPLive peer exchange transcript
Question

How do emerging options such as PDS, bispecific therapy, home OCT, and durability-focused therapies address wet AMD burden?

Actual answer summary

Pieramici said durability is the biggest unmet need because anti-VEGF works when patients adhere, but maintaining frequent care is difficult. He viewed PDS as a potential burden reducer while recognizing a small absolute, higher relative infection risk, and saw future durability plus home OCT as ways to reduce burden.

Why this tests a persona

Tests durability-versus-safety tradeoff, procedural realism, and openness to monitoring-enabled care models.
